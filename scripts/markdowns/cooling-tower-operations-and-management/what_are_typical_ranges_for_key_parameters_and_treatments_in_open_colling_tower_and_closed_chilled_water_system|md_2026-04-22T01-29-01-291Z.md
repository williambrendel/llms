# What are typical target ranges for key parameters and treatments in cooling tower and closed chilled water systems?

## Executive Summary

Open cooling towers require polymer dispersants at 5-30 ppm and phosphonate inhibitors at 2-5 ppm, while closed loop systems need nitrite levels of 600-1000 ppm or molybdate at 125 ppm depending on operating temperature and water chemistry.

## Overview

A cooling tower running at 4 cycles of concentration can see calcium carbonate scaling within hours if polymer dispersant drops below 5 ppm. The chemistry programs that prevent this disaster vary dramatically between open and closed systems, with closed loops requiring 100-200 times higher inhibitor concentrations than their open counterparts. Understanding these target ranges prevents equipment failure and extends system life.

## New System Startup and Passivation

New cooling systems require aggressive cleaning before normal operation. The cleaning formulation combines multiple components at high concentrations: 60 ppm orthophosphate, 20 ppm polyacrylate, 10 ppm azole, 50 ppm sodium gluconate, 400 ppm non-ionic surfactant, and 50 ppm phosphate-based scale inhibitor.

During passivation, inhibitor film formation takes 14 to 21 days across all chemistry types. Polyphosphate requires 40-60 ppm during this period, orthophosphate needs 20-30 ppm, and azole demands 10-20 ppm. All passivation occurs within pH 6.5 to 7.9 for optimal film development.

## Open Loop Tower System Parameters

Open cooling towers accept various makeup water sources: potable water, reclaimed water, softened water, or pH-controlled water with acid feed. Each source affects the required treatment chemistry and monitoring frequency.

Testing frequency varies by parameter criticality and system risk. The monitoring schedule ensures operators catch chemistry deviations before they cause equipment damage.

## Scale and Deposit Inhibitor Dosages

Different inhibitor families target specific scaling mechanisms at precise dosage ranges:

| **Chemistry family** | **Examples / descriptors** | **Primary role** | **Typical target residual (ppm active in recirculating water)** | **Comments** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Phosphonates** | PBTC, HEDP, ATMP | Threshold scale inhibition; helps manage CaCO₃/CaPO₄; works with polymers | **~2–5 ppm** (active phosphonate) |     |
| **Carboxylated polymers (polycarboxylates)** | Polyacrylate(PAA),polymethacrylate, polymaleic acid (PMA) | Crystal modification + dispersion; deposit control | **~5–30 ppm** (active polymer) | Common "polymer dispersant" control band for towers. |
| **Sulfonated polymers (anionic)** | Sulfonated polystyrene; acrylic-sulfonic copolymers (e.g., PA-SA type) | High tolerance at high pH/hardness; strong dispersancy, Ca/Fe tolerance | **~3–5 ppm** (some sulfonated inhibitors for scale control) to **~20–30 ppm** (some sulfonated dispersant programs) | can prevent scale at pH 8.5–9.5 under stressed indices. |
| **Carboxylated–sulfonated terpolymers (anionic terpolymers)** | Acrylic acid / sulfonated monomer / third monomer terpolymers (often positioned for CaCO₃ + CaPO₄ + iron control) | Broad "stressed water" deposit control; better Ca tolerance and phosphate/iron stabilization | **~5–10 ppm** (typical) to **~20–30 ppm** in high-stress programs |     |
| **Carboxylate–sulfonate–nonionic (mixed-function) polymers** | Polymers described as having **strong acid (sulfonate), weak acid (carboxylate), and nonionic groups** | Designed to work at high pH/hardness/alkalinity; enhanced dispersion + Ca tolerance | **Commonly falls in the same "polymer dispersant" band: ~5–30 ppm** | These are marketed specifically for **high pH/high hardness** conditions standard polymer dispersant residual ranges. |
| **Non-ionic polymers / non-ionic dispersants** | Non-ionic surfactant/polymer dispersants used for fouling control (less common as the primary scale inhibitor) | Wetting/dispersion; can complement anionic polymers, help with organic fouling | product-specific) |     |

Phosphonates work at the lowest concentrations because they provide threshold inhibition - preventing crystal nucleation rather than managing existing crystals. Polymers require higher doses because they modify crystal structure and disperse particles that have already formed.

## Closed Loop Corrosion Control Programs

Closed loop systems use five main corrosion control approaches: sulfite-caustic, nitrite-azole, molybdate-azole, nitrite-molybdate-azole, and polysilicate-azole. Program selection depends on operating temperature and makeup water quality.

### Temperature-Based Program Selection

High temperature systems above 350°F require soft, de-aerated water with sulfite-caustic treatment. Medium temperature systems (250-350°F) also use sulfite-caustic with soft, de-aerated makeup. Lower temperature systems below 250°F can use makeup water with less than 100 ppm total hardness and have multiple program options.

Chilled water systems (40-55°F), chilled brine systems (30-50°F), and chilled glycol systems (20-35°F) all accept makeup water below 250 ppm total hardness and can use nitrite-azole, molybdate-azole, or combination programs.

### Sulfite-Caustic Programs

Sulfite-caustic programs handle water temperatures above 250°F with total hardness below 10 ppm and M-alkalinity under 200 ppm. Sulfite levels run 10-50 ppm with pH maintained at 9-10. Daily testing ensures sulfite levels stay adequate for oxygen scavenging.

### Nitrite-Azole Programs

Nitrite-azole programs work below 250°F with total hardness under 10 ppm and combined chloride plus sulfate below 800 ppm. M-alkalinity stays below 200 ppm. Nitrite levels run 600-1000 ppm with azole at 10 ppm plus 2 ppm for each ppm of iron present. pH operates between 8.5-9.5 with monthly testing.

### Molybdate-Azole Programs

Molybdate-azole programs operate under the same water quality limits as nitrite-azole but use 125 ppm molybdate (as MO4) instead of nitrite. Azole dosing follows the same 10 ppm plus 2 ppm per ppm iron rule. pH and testing frequency match nitrite-azole programs.

### Combination Nitrite-Molybdate-Azole Programs

Combination programs use 50-75 ppm molybdate and 300-400 ppm nitrite with the same azole dosing strategy. These programs require careful chloride-sulfate balance: the sum of chloride and sulfate must stay below the sum of nitrite and molybdate minus 100.

**What it measures:** The relationship between aggressive anions and protective inhibitors in closed loop systems

Formula:
```
(Cl + SO4) < ((NO2 + MO4) - 100)
```

**Example:** A system with 200 ppm chloride and 150 ppm sulfate (350 ppm total) needs nitrite plus molybdate levels above 450 ppm to maintain the protective balance.

### Polysilicate-Azole Programs

Polysilicate-azole programs excel when aluminum components are present. They operate below 250°F with total hardness under 10 ppm. Polysilicate levels run 80-100 ppm (as SiO2) with standard azole dosing. pH operates lower at 7.5-8.9 compared to other programs.

## Microbiological Control

Closed loop systems maintain strict microbiological limits: total aerobes below 1000 CFU, sulfate-reducing bacteria at non-detect levels, and slime formers under 10,000 CFU.

Isothiazolone and glutaraldehyde serve as the most common microbiocides. Oxidizing biocides must be avoided because they interfere with corrosion inhibitors. Chlorine dioxide oxidizes nitrite to nitrate, reducing inhibitor effectiveness, while oxidizers degrade both isothiazolone and glutaraldehyde.

**What it measures:** The chemical reaction that destroys nitrite corrosion inhibitors

Formula:
```
2ClO2 + NO2⁻ + H2O → 2ClO2⁻ + NO3⁻ + 2H⁺
```

**Example:** Adding 2 ppm chlorine dioxide to a system with 800 ppm nitrite converts the protective nitrite to ineffective nitrate, requiring complete inhibitor replacement.

## Key Takeaways

Test polymer dispersant levels weekly in open cooling towers and maintain 5-30 ppm to prevent scaling. Monitor closed loop nitrite levels monthly and keep them between 600-1000 ppm for systems below 250°F. Switch to non-oxidizing biocides like isothiazolone in closed loops to preserve corrosion inhibitor effectiveness.
