# What is a good RFQ for an outside lab for testing open cooling tower and closed chilled water systems?

## Executive Summary

A comprehensive RFQ for industrial water testing labs should specify 25+ water chemistry analytes (including metals, phosphate speciation, and corrosion inhibitors), biological testing capabilities including Legionella, and structured data deliverables with defined turnaround times.

## Overview

Industrial water systems generate complex chemistry that requires specialized laboratory expertise most facilities don't maintain in-house. A single cooling tower sample might need analysis for 30+ parameters ranging from basic ions to sophisticated azole inhibitors, each with different preservation requirements and holding times. The difference between a good RFQ and a problematic one often comes down to whether you've specified the exact phosphate fractionation methods and particle size bins you actually need.

## Essential RFQ Structure

The RFQ should open with clear identification of your organization, submission requirements, and due dates. Specify that responses must arrive as single PDF files via email with defined contact information and submission deadlines.

State your purpose directly: you need analytical testing for open recirculating cooling towers, closed chilled water systems, and related industrial water matrices. This testing supports corrosion control, scale management, fouling prevention, and biological risk management.

## Critical Laboratory Qualifications

Require ISO/IEC 17025 accreditation or equivalent state certification covering the proposed analytical methods. The laboratory must demonstrate specific experience with cooling tower and closed-loop water matrices, not just drinking water or wastewater.

Biological testing capability proves essential, particularly for Legionella analysis using ISO 11731 or equivalent culture methods. Many labs claim biological testing but lack the specialized media and incubation protocols industrial water systems require.

Data management capability separates professional operations from basic labs. Require electronic data deliverables in CSV or Excel format with structured analyte-level data, not just PDF reports.

## Required Water Chemistry Analytes

**Metals and Elements:** Aluminum, barium, boron, cadmium, calcium, chromium, copper, iron, lead, lithium, magnesium, manganese, molybdenum, nickel, potassium, silicon, sodium, strontium, and vanadium. Specify whether you need both total and dissolved results for each metal.

**Anions:** Chloride, nitrite, bromide, nitrate, and sulfate. Also request ion equivalents expressed as CaCO₃ for major anions to support water balance calculations.

**Alkalinity System:** Total alkalinity, P alkalinity, bicarbonate, and carbonate all expressed as CaCO₃. These parameters control pH buffering and scale formation potential.

**Phosphate Speciation:** This represents one of the most technically demanding requirements. Request inorganic phosphate, orthophosphate, organic phosphate, polyphosphate, and total phosphate. Require the lab to describe their digestion and hydrolysis methods plus known interferences from other water treatment chemicals.

**What it measures:** Phosphate speciation reveals how much corrosion inhibitor remains active versus tied up in scale or biological films.

Formula:
```
Total Phosphate = Orthophosphate + Organic Phosphate + Polyphosphate
```

**Example:** A cooling tower dosed with 15 mg/L phosphonate might show 12 mg/L total phosphate but only 3 mg/L orthophosphate, indicating most inhibitor has complexed with calcium or been consumed by bacteria.

**Azole Inhibitors:** Benzotriazole, tolyltriazole, and halogen-resistant azoles require specialized extraction methods. These copper corrosion inhibitors degrade rapidly in chlorinated systems, making preservation and holding times critical.

**General Parameters:** Conductivity, pH, turbidity, and total suspended solids provide basic water quality context.

## Biological Testing Requirements

**Routine Indicators:** Total viable plate count, pigmented bacteria, mucoid bacteria, total coliforms, and E. coli establish baseline biological activity.

**Target Organisms:** Pseudomonas species, sulfate-reducing bacteria, iron bacteria, and denitrifying bacteria indicate specific corrosion and fouling risks.

**Fungal Analysis:** Mold and yeast testing becomes important in systems with organic inhibitors or contamination sources.

**Legionella Testing:** Specify culture methods following ISO 11731 with results in CFU/L. Define control bands: target non-detect, lower control limit of 1 CFU/L, and upper control limit above 100 CFU/L. Include both Legionella pneumophila serogroup 1 and serogroups 2-15.

## Optional Particle Size Analysis

Particle size distribution reveals fouling mechanisms that turbidity measurements miss. Acceptable methods include laser diffraction, optical particle counting, or electrical sensing zone analysis.

Require reporting in defined size bins: <1 μm, 1-5 μm, 5-10 μm, 10-50 μm, 50-100 μm, 100-200 μm, and >200 μm. Request D10, D50, and D90 values plus total particle concentration.

The lab should acknowledge limitations including sensitivity to air bubbles, oil droplets, and biological debris that can skew particle counts.

## Sample Handling and Logistics

Require the lab to provide appropriate sampling containers, preservatives, and chain-of-custody documentation. Many analytical failures trace back to improper sample collection rather than laboratory errors.

The lab must specify bottle types, preservative requirements, filtration needs, temperature control, and maximum holding times for each analyte group. Azoles might require amber glass bottles with acid preservation, while biological samples need sterile containers and rapid processing.

## Quality Assurance Requirements

Request batch QC summaries as minimum reporting with full QC packages available on demand. The lab should describe corrective action procedures for QC failures and participation in proficiency testing programs.

Require reporting of QC acceptance criteria, data qualifiers, and any subcontracted analyses. Many labs subcontract specialized tests like Legionella or azoles without clearly disclosing this arrangement.

## Reporting and Data Delivery

Reports must include sample identification, analytical methods, reporting limits, receipt timestamps, analysis dates, and holding time compliance. Electronic data deliverables should provide structured, analyte-level data suitable for trending and statistical analysis.

Define standard and expedited turnaround times for each analytical group. Routine chemistry might require 5-7 business days while Legionella culture needs 10-14 days due to incubation requirements.

## Key Takeaway

Issue your RFQ with a two-week response deadline, require monthly proficiency testing documentation, and set a maximum 7-day turnaround for routine chemistry with 14 days for Legionella culture. Consider pre-qualifying 2-3 laboratories to ensure backup capacity during peak sampling periods or quality issues.
