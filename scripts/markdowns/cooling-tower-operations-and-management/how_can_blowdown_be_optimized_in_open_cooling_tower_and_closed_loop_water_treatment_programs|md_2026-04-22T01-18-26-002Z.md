# How can blowdown rate be optimized in open cooling tower and closed loop water systems?

## Executive Summary

Optimize blowdown by running cooling towers at the highest stable cycles of concentration the system can tolerate, and minimize drain-and-refill events in closed loops by controlling makeup water and using filtration instead of dumping.

## Overview

A cooling tower running at 6 cycles instead of 3 cuts blowdown volume in half, but most facilities never reach their system's true potential because they haven't identified what actually limits their cycles. Meanwhile, closed loop systems that should need minimal makeup often waste thousands of gallons through unnecessary drain-and-refill cycles triggered by contamination that filtration could have prevented.

## Open Cooling Towers: Maximizing Cycles of Concentration

### Establish the True Limiting Cycle

Set maximum allowable cycles by identifying which parameter hits its limit first as cycles rise. The most common limiting factors include:

- **CaCO₃ scaling** from calcium hardness, alkalinity, pH, and temperature/heat flux
- **Silica and magnesium-silicate precipitation**
- **Chloride/sulfate levels** that cause corrosion and affect stainless steel or galvanized components
- **Phosphate/zinc program deposition risk**
- **Suspended solids and fouling** from dirty basins

Calculate maximum cycles per constituent using: allowable concentration ÷ makeup concentration, then operate below the lowest result.

### Use Conductivity Control Correctly

Deploy automatic blowdown via conductivity controller that bleeds only when the setpoint is exceeded. Measure makeup conductivity range seasonally and set tower conductivity setpoint accordingly.

Validate conductivity-derived cycles monthly with a conservative tracer like chloride. Makeup variability and chemistry changes can make conductivity readings misleading for cycle calculations.

### Fix False Blowdown Losses First

Before pushing cycles upward, eliminate uncontrolled losses that waste water and destabilize treatment:

- **Leaking blowdown valves** that create continuous bleed
- **Drift and overflow** losses
- **Basin leaks**

These issues distort required blowdown calculations and prevent stable control.

### Improve Solids Control for Higher Cycles

Higher cycles increase precipitation and deposition tendency. Avoid being forced to lower cycles by:

- Adding or optimizing **side-stream filtration**
- Tightening **basin cleaning and housekeeping**
- Maintaining **dispersant performance**

This approach often determines whether a tower runs at 3-4 cycles versus 6+ cycles.

### Quantify Performance with Metering

Monitor these parameters to optimize measurably:

- Makeup volume
- Blowdown volume  
- Conductivity (makeup and tower)

Optimized performance shows stable conductivity around setpoint, stable pH and inhibitor residuals, no upward trend in approach temperature or turbidity, and lowest blowdown volume that keeps the system clean and controlled.

## Closed Loop Systems: Minimizing Drain-and-Refill

### Target Minimal Makeup Requirements

A properly functioning closed loop should require less than 5% of system volume per year in makeup water. Continuous routine blowdown is not normal - draining typically occurs only for contamination, poor startup cleaning, major repairs, or chemistry problems.

### Reduce Makeup Water as Primary Strategy

Frequent makeup imports oxygen, salts, and microbes that drive corrosion and deposits, eventually forcing dump-and-fill cycles. Significant losses occur through leaks or expansion tank overflow.

Meter makeup water and set alarms above expected baseline. Find and fix leaks, auto-fill valve issues, and open expansion tank overflows.

### Control Chemistry by Inhibitor Residuals

Avoid using conductivity as a proxy for inhibitor concentration in closed loops. Conductivity drifts for reasons unrelated to inhibitor strength.

Maintain inhibitor at target ppm by direct measurement rather than conductivity readings. This prevents unnecessary dumps caused by control or sampling issues.

### Deploy Filtration Instead of Dumping

When suspended solids like magnetite or construction debris contaminate the system, use side-stream filtration or magnetic separation rather than large-volume drains. Implement low-point flushing strategies to reduce solids inventory.

When drain-and-refill becomes necessary due to contamination or poor startup, prefer partial drain with targeted cleaning and flushing over full system dumps. Re-passivate and reset chemistry properly afterward to prevent recurrence.

## Key Takeaways

Monitor makeup and blowdown volumes monthly with flow meters to track optimization progress. For cooling towers, target stable operation at maximum safe cycles - typically 4-6 cycles for most water chemistries. For closed loops, investigate any makeup exceeding 5% of system volume annually and address through leak repair and filtration before resorting to system drains.
