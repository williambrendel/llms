# What is the impact of under blowdown in open cooling tower and closed loop water systems?

## Executive Summary

Under-blowdown creates rapid scaling and fouling in open cooling towers while allowing corrosion byproducts to accumulate in closed-loop systems, leading to performance loss and higher maintenance costs.

## Overview

Under-blowdown might sound like a minor operational adjustment, but it triggers a cascade effect that can cripple cooling systems within weeks. In open towers, cycles can climb so fast that calcium and silica literally precipitate out of solution, while closed loops develop iron sludge that turns clear water into a rusty soup that clogs everything downstream.

## Open Cooling Towers: When Less Blowdown Means More Problems

Under-blowdown in open cooling towers means bleeding less water than needed to control dissolved solids and cycles. This forces salts and impurities to accumulate rapidly.

### Scaling and Fouling Take Over

The highest impact comes from rapid increases in scaling and fouling risk. Higher cycles drive concentrations of calcium, alkalinity, silica, phosphate, and suspended solids toward precipitation and deposit formation. These deposits reduce heat transfer and flow capacity, typically resulting in rising condenser approach temperature, higher energy use, and loss of cooling capacity.

### Corrosion Accelerates Through Multiple Pathways

Corrosion risk increases through both high salinity and deposit formation. Higher dissolved salts raise conductivity, increasing corrosion current and galvanic effects. Deposits from high cycles create under-deposit corrosion conditions and drive pitting, even when bulk corrosion rates appear moderate.

### Biological Control Becomes Unstable

Higher suspended solids and deposits provide attachment sites and biofilm habitat, increasing disinfectant demand and reducing biocide penetration. Biological control becomes both unstable and more expensive to maintain.

### Systems Lose Control and Enter Runaway Conditions

Conductivity continues climbing while pH and inhibitor chemistry drift out of the intended range. Controllers become ineffective when probes foul or scale, causing the system to swing into frequent upsets.

### Early Warning Signs in Towers

Watch for tower conductivity rising above setpoint and cycles (chloride ratio) trending upward. Increasing turbidity, total suspended solids, total iron, and ATP/HPC rebound signal trouble ahead. Rising approach temperature, exchanger pressure drop, and nozzle or strainer plugging confirm the cascade has begun.

## Closed-Loop Systems: When No Blowdown Becomes a Problem

Closed loops normally should not require routine blowdown. Under-blowdown in these systems usually means failing to perform needed corrective draining or flushing when contaminants accumulate, or not purging low points and dead legs when required.

### Corrosion Byproducts Keep Building Up

Persistent solids like magnetite and iron sludge continue fouling coils, valves, and strainers while creating under-deposit corrosion environments. Turbidity, cloudiness, and elevated metals indicate active corrosion and demand immediate evaluation and prevention measures.

### Localized Corrosion and MIC Risk Increases

When deposits and low-oxygen zones exist, biology can persist and drive microbiologically influenced corrosion (MIC), even when bulk water tests look acceptable. Slow response allows deposits to mature, making later cleaning more aggressive and disruptive.

### Performance Loss and Maintenance Burden Rises

Systems experience more frequent strainer cleaning, control valve issues, coil and heat exchanger pressure drop increases, and complaints of poor heat transfer. Eventually, the system may require major cleaning and re-passivation instead of controlled partial cleanup.

### Recognizing Under-Blowdown in Closed Loops

Under-blowdown looks like rising iron and turbidity with magnetite formation, but only topping off chemicals without removing solids. Low points never get flushed, dead legs remain stagnant, and commissioning debris continues circulating because it was never fully removed.

## Urgency and the Cost of Slow Action

### Open Towers Demand Immediate Response

High urgency applies to towers because under-blowdown drives a fast cascade: higher cycles lead to precipitation and deposits, which create biofilm habitat, which increases chemical demand, which promotes corrosion under deposits, which generates more solids, which creates worse deposits.

### Closed Loops Require Prompt Intervention

Moderate to high urgency applies to closed loops. When solids and iron trend upward, slow action increases the probability of needing larger, more disruptive cleaning later while increasing leak and pitting risk under deposits.

## Key Takeaway

Monitor conductivity and cycles daily in open towers, maintaining blowdown rates that keep cycles within design limits (typically 3-6 cycles). For closed loops, test iron and turbidity monthly and perform corrective flushing when iron exceeds 2-3 ppm or turbidity rises above baseline. Address under-blowdown conditions within days for towers and weeks for closed loops to prevent cascade failures that require major system cleaning and downtime.
