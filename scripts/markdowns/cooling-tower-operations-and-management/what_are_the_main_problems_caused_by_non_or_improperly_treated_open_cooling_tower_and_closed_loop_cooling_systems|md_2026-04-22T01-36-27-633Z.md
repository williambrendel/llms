# What are the main problems caused by improper treatment in open tower and closed loop cooling systems?

## Executive Summary

Improper treatment in open cooling towers primarily causes scale formation, corrosion, biological growth, and plugging that reduce heat transfer and create health risks. Closed loop systems mainly suffer from oxygen-driven corrosion that generates iron deposits, leading to plugging and equipment failures.

## Overview

A single untreated cooling tower can lose 30% of its heat transfer capacity within months, while closed loops accumulate enough iron sludge to plug entire heat exchanger sections. Both system types follow predictable failure cascades, but the mechanisms differ dramatically because open towers concentrate minerals through evaporation while closed loops battle oxygen infiltration and metal corrosion.

## Open Cooling Tower Systems

### Scale and Deposit Formation

Open towers concentrate dissolved minerals through evaporation, and without proper cycles control and inhibitors, these minerals precipitate as scale on fill, heat exchangers, and piping. EPA and GSA documentation confirms that evaporation concentrates minerals, accelerating scale formation without adequate blowdown and treatment.

Scale formation creates immediate operational problems:
- Rising condenser approach temperatures and head pressure
- Plugged spray nozzles and scaled fill media  
- Increased kW/ton energy consumption and capacity loss

### Corrosion and Metal Loss

Open towers maintain continuous oxygen contact and chemical fluctuations. Without corrosion inhibitors and deposit control, corrosion rates accelerate and equipment failures follow. Scale, sediment, and biofilm create under-deposit corrosion conditions that attack metal surfaces locally. CDC guidelines explicitly connect scale, corrosion, and sediment controls as essential cooling tower operations.

Corrosion manifests through:
- Iron concentration spikes and rust sludge accumulation
- Pitting corrosion at welds and heat-affected zones, plus basin deterioration
- Shortened equipment life and heat exchanger tube leaks

### Biological Growth and Biofilm

Biofilm forms on wetted surfaces without effective biocide control and maintenance. CDC emphasizes that scale, sediment, and biofilm control with regular cleaning prevents Legionella growth and spread.

Biological problems include:
- Loss of disinfectant residual at system endpoints
- Slime, algae growth, odors, and foaming
- Heat transfer reduction and microbiologically influenced corrosion
- Elevated Legionella risk, creating health compliance issues

### Plugging and Hydraulic Problems

Towers feeding plate-and-frame heat exchangers face rapid plugging when solids and biological control fails. HEI documentation notes that plugging results from bulk material buildup in plate channels, causing pressure drop increases that filtration and proper design can mitigate.

Plugging creates:
- Pressure drop increases across plate heat exchangers
- Economizer capacity loss, forcing chiller operation
- Frequent cleaning requirements and equipment tear-downs

## Closed Loop Cooling Systems

### Oxygen-Driven Corrosion

Closed loops should remain oxygen-limited after startup, but treatment failures or makeup water addition accelerates corrosion. NIH research shows dissolved oxygen drives closed loop corrosion through aerated water, pressure imbalances, or non-barrier piping. Chemaqua technical data confirms that corrosion and corrosion byproduct deposition exceed mineral scaling as closed loop problems.

Oxygen corrosion produces:
- Rising iron concentrations and black magnetite sludge formation
- Plugged strainers, coils, and plate exchangers
- Pump seal wear, valve sticking, and system noise or cavitation
- Persistent "dirty system" conditions despite acceptable chemistry at sample points

### Deposition and Under-Deposit Corrosion

Iron oxides and magnetite circulate and settle, creating differential aeration cells that drive localized metal attack.

Deposition effects include:
- Heat exchanger performance loss and system imbalance
- Repeated plugging after flow pattern changes
- Increased maintenance requirements and premature component replacement

### Microbiological Growth

Closed loops present lower biological risk than open towers, but stagnant zones or glycol degradation enable microbial growth and biofilm formation that worsen corrosion and deposition. Chemaqua guidelines require excellent control of corrosion, deposition, and microbiological fouling in closed systems.

Microbial problems cause:
- Slime accumulation in low-flow areas and strainers  
- Increased corrosion, fouling, and odors in glycol systems

### Mineral Scale Formation

High-temperature closed loop applications or repeated makeup water addition can cause scaling on heat transfer surfaces, particularly where hardness and alkalinity accumulate.

## The Damage Cascade

Both system types follow predictable failure patterns. Loss of chemical control and solids control allows deposits and biofilm formation, often invisible initially. Heat transfer drops, worsening temperatures and approach conditions. Higher temperatures and deposits accelerate scaling and corrosion. Sludge and plugging increase, leading to equipment failures and system downtime.

## Key Takeaways

Open tower operators should monitor conductivity weekly to maintain 3-6 concentration cycles, test Legionella monthly, and install 25-50 micron side-stream filtration for plate heat exchanger protection. Closed loop systems require quarterly iron testing with targets below 1.0 ppm, annual system cleaning when iron exceeds 5.0 ppm, and oxygen scavenger treatment during any makeup water additions.
