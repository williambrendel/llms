# How can water consumption be reduced in open cooling tower and closed loop water systems?

## Executive Summary

Water consumption in cooling towers drops primarily by maximizing cycles of concentration and eliminating drift and leaks, while closed loop systems require finding and fixing makeup water losses from seals, overflows, and expansion tank issues.

## Overview

A single cooling tower running at 3 cycles instead of 6 cycles wastes roughly double the water it should through excessive blowdown. Most facilities focus on the wrong metrics or tolerate "small" leaks that compound into major losses. The real opportunity lies in the control precision and maintenance details that operators often overlook.

## Open Cooling Towers: Reducing Water Consumption

Cooling towers lose water through evaporation, blowdown (bleed), drift, and leaks/overflows. Reducing consumption means minimizing blowdown, drift, and leaks while maintaining control, with maximizing cycles of concentration as the primary lever to cut blowdown.

Blowdown controls dissolved solids buildup. You reduce blowdown by running at the highest stable cycles allowed by scaling, corrosion, and biological constraints.

**Determine the limiting constituent** first - often silica, CaCO₃, chlorides, or phosphate program limits. Set a conductivity setpoint and validate true cycles periodically with a more conservative tracer like chloride, especially when makeup water quality varies.

**Tighten conductivity controller performance** to reduce wasted blowdown. Calibrate and clean conductivity probes and sample lines regularly. Confirm the blowdown valve seats properly and doesn't leak through. Consider dual-probe cycles control using both makeup and tower conductivity when makeup conductivity varies significantly.

**Reduce drift and uncontrolled losses** by maintaining or upgrading drift eliminators, correcting tower water level control, and fixing basin and valve leaks.

**Improve solids control to allow higher cycles** through side-stream filtration and basin cleaning. This reduces suspended solids and biofilm seeding. Cleaner systems typically tolerate higher cycles with lower deposition risk, letting you reduce blowdown without losing control.

**Consider blowdown reuse or recovery** where permitted and economical. Reuse blowdown for non-potable applications like toilets, irrigation, or other process needs, or install blowdown recovery technologies.

## Closed Loop Water Systems: Reducing Water Consumption

A properly functioning closed loop requires very little makeup water. Recurring makeup usually signals leaks, overflows, or design and control issues.

Closed loops suffer significant water loss through leaks in mechanical seals or overflow from expansion tanks, requiring makeup that introduces impurities and oxygen.

**Measure and minimize makeup water** as the primary lever. Install or monitor an existing makeup meter and trend gallons per day or percentage of system volume per month. Find and eliminate loss sources including pump seal leaks, valve packing leaks, coil leaks, open or atmospheric expansion tank overflow, relief valve discharge, and automatic fill valve drift.

**Address open expansion tanks**, often the hidden driver of both water loss and oxygen ingress. Convert to sealed diaphragm or bladder expansion vessels or pressurization units where feasible. If conversion isn't possible, reduce aeration and mixing in the tank and improve air separation and degassing to reduce corrosion-driven solids that force system dumps.

**Avoid unnecessary dumps and drains by stabilizing water quality**. Control inhibitors by measured residual (such as nitrite ppm) rather than conductivity. Use side-stream filtration or magnetic separation if magnetite dominates to keep solids low, reducing the perceived need for partial drains.

**Reset systems that started dirty**. New loops with poor cleaning and passivation often require repeated flushing events that waste significant water. A proper one-time reset involving cleaning, flushing, passivation, and stabilization often reduces long-term water use by eliminating recurring corrective drains.

## Taking Action

Address cooling tower control, valve, probe, and drift issues immediately - slow correction wastes large volumes continuously through excess blowdown and leaks. For closed loops, fix makeup loss sources quickly since delays perpetuate both water waste and oxygen ingress, driving corrosion solids and future maintenance flushing. Monitor makeup water consumption weekly, target cycles of concentration based on your limiting water quality parameter, and establish monthly calibration schedules for conductivity probes and control valves.
