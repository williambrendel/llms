# How Do You Switch a Cooling Tower from Soft to Hard Makeup Water Without Causing Scale or Corrosion?

## Executive Summary

Switching from softened to hard makeup water requires lowering cycles initially, adding scale inhibitors, and monitoring daily for 48 hours to prevent calcium carbonate scaling while maintaining corrosion protection.

## Overview

Most cooling tower operators discover this transition challenge when water treatment costs spike or softening equipment fails. The switch eliminates sodium while introducing calcium and magnesium hardness that can form scale at just 3-4 cycles of concentration—half the cycles many towers run on soft water. Missing this chemistry shift has shut down heat exchangers in under a week.

## The Critical Chemistry Change

Ion-exchange softened water removes calcium and magnesium ions, replacing them with sodium. Hard makeup water reverses this completely. The tower now concentrates calcium carbonate and calcium sulfate instead of benign sodium salts.

This change affects every aspect of water chemistry. Calcium carbonate solubility drops rapidly as pH rises above 8.0 and cycles increase. Where softened makeup might safely reach 6-8 cycles, hard makeup often requires starting at 3-4 cycles until the chemical program adjusts.

## Scale Risk Management

**What it measures:** The relationship between calcium hardness, alkalinity, and scaling potential

Formula:
```
Langelier Saturation Index = pH - pHs
where pHs = (9.3 + A + B) - (C + D)
A = (log10[TDS] - 1)/10
B = -13.12 × log10(°C + 273) + 34.55
C = log10[Ca2+ as CaCO3] - 0.4
D = log10[alkalinity as CaCO3]
```

**Example:** At 85°F with 300 mg/L calcium hardness and 150 mg/L alkalinity at pH 8.2, LSI = +0.8, indicating moderate scaling potential requiring inhibitor addition.

Phosphonate scale inhibitors and polymer dispersants become essential. Phosphonates chelate calcium ions, while polymers disperse crystal formation. Feed rates typically increase to 15-25 mg/L active ingredient for hard makeup versus 5-10 mg/L for soft makeup.

Sulfuric acid addition controls pH between 7.8-8.2 to reduce carbonate scaling. Sulfuric acid avoids adding chloride ions that concentrate and accelerate corrosion, unlike hydrochloric acid.

## Corrosion Protection Adjustments

Softened water removes the natural calcium carbonate film protection that hard water provides. This transition requires maintaining corrosion inhibitors throughout the switch.

Azole inhibitors protect copper alloys like brass fittings and heat exchanger tubes. These form protective films on metal surfaces. Molybdate or phosphate inhibitors protect steel components.

The loss of incidental calcium carbonate protection means corrosion rates can spike during transition if inhibitor levels drop.

## Transition Protocol

Start conservatively with lower cycles targets. If the tower ran 6 cycles on soft water, begin at 3-4 cycles with hard water. Monitor conductivity setpoints daily and adjust blowdown accordingly.

Increase scale inhibitor feed rates by 50-100% initially. Add acid feed to maintain pH at 7.8-8.2. Continue existing corrosion inhibitor programs without interruption.

Test recirculating water pH and conductivity daily for the first week. Test makeup water conductivity daily to calculate actual cycles. Monitor heat exchanger performance through approach temperature and differential pressure.

## Performance Monitoring

Heat exchanger approach temperature increases signal scaling problems. Differential pressure increases across heat exchangers confirm deposit formation. These indicators appear before visible scale formation.

Trending hardness and alkalinity levels in recirculating water at constant cycles reveals scaling tendency. Rising levels at the same concentration factor indicate precipitation and deposit formation.

## Actionable Implementation

Monitor conductivity and pH daily for the first 48 hours, then weekly thereafter. Maintain calcium carbonate LSI below +0.5 through acid addition and cycles control. Add phosphonate scale inhibitors at 20 mg/L minimum during transition, then optimize based on weekly testing results.
