# What are the differences between open tower and closed loop cooling systems?

## Executive Summary

Open cooling towers expose water to air for evaporative cooling, while closed loop systems transfer heat through sealed heat exchangers without air contact. This fundamental difference drives completely different water treatment challenges and biological risks.

## Overview

A single design choice—whether to expose cooling water to air—creates two entirely different worlds of water management. Open towers concentrate dissolved solids up to 10 times through evaporation, while closed loops can maintain stable water chemistry for months. The contamination sources, corrosion mechanisms, and even public health risks diverge so dramatically that the treatment approaches share almost nothing in common.

## Contact with Air and Its Consequences

Open cooling towers maintain constant air contact as their primary cooling mechanism. Water cascades through fill media while fans pull air across the flow, creating evaporation that removes heat. This exposure brings high dissolved oxygen, strips CO₂, and generates aerosol drift that carries water droplets into the surrounding environment.

Closed loop systems seal the water circuit completely. Heat transfers through tube walls in heat exchangers, keeping the cooling water isolated from atmospheric contact. After startup, these systems should become oxygen-limited with minimal gas exchange occurring only through minor leaks or maintenance activities.

This air contact difference makes open towers inherently more challenging for corrosion and biological control, while closed loops function primarily as corrosion-control systems that rely on oxygen exclusion combined with chemical inhibitors.

## Water Loss and Concentration Effects

Evaporation in open towers removes pure water while leaving dissolved solids behind. This concentrates minerals, requiring continuous blowdown to control cycles of concentration. Without blowdown control, calcium carbonate, calcium sulfate, and silica can precipitate rapidly on heat transfer surfaces.

Closed loop systems experience no evaporation concentration. Water chemistry should remain stable for extended periods unless makeup water enters through leaks or system additions. Scale formation becomes uncommon unless poor-quality fill water was used or frequent makeup introduces hardness minerals.

The concentration effect makes scale control a major ongoing requirement for open towers, while closed loops rarely encounter scaling issues under normal operation.

## Contamination Sources

Open towers face external contamination from airborne dirt, organic matter, and process leaks. Sunlight exposure can promote algae growth, while atmospheric dust and pollen provide nutrients for biological activity. Industrial environments may introduce specific contaminants through air currents.

Closed loop contamination comes primarily from internal sources: construction debris left during installation, corrosion products from system metallurgy, or contamination introduced through makeup water or system leaks. The sealed environment eliminates most external contamination pathways.

## Biological Risk Profiles

Open cooling towers present high biological growth potential and significant Legionella risk. The combination of warm water temperatures, air contact, and aerosol generation creates ideal conditions for pathogen amplification and transmission. Aerosols can travel considerable distances, creating public health exposure pathways.

**What it measures:** Legionella risk assessment considers water temperature, aerosol generation, and exposure pathways.

Formula:
```
Risk = Temperature factor × Aerosol generation × Exposure time × Population density
```

**Example:** A 500-ton cooling tower operating at 95°F with drift eliminators achieving 0.005% drift loss still generates approximately 150 gallons per day of aerosol containing potentially amplified Legionella bacteria.

Closed loop systems carry much lower aerosol and public health risks. Microbial issues can occur if oxygen enters the system or in glycol-containing systems, but these typically don't create Legionella aerosol pathways that threaten public health.

## Corrosion Mechanisms

Open towers maintain constant oxygen presence, making corrosion control dependent on inhibitor films and deposit control. The high dissolved oxygen levels, typically 6-8 ppm, drive continuous electrochemical corrosion processes that require active chemical inhibition.

Closed loop corrosion is dominated by oxygen ingress through leaks, makeup additions, or improper venting procedures. Inadequate passivation during startup or insufficient inhibitor levels can accelerate metal loss. Iron pickup rates above 0.2 ppm per month indicate active corrosion requiring immediate attention.

## Scaling Drivers

Open towers face primary scaling risk from concentration effects plus hot-surface precipitation. Carbonate, phosphate, sulfate, and silica scaling can all occur depending on water chemistry and cycles of concentration. Heat exchanger surfaces operating above 140°F become particularly susceptible to calcium carbonate precipitation.

Closed loops typically maintain low scale risk due to stable water chemistry. Localized scaling can occur on hot surfaces if fill water contains high hardness or if system pH rises above 9.5, but these conditions are usually avoidable through proper water selection and pH control.

## Treatment Approaches

| **Feature** | **Open Cooling Tower** | **Closed Loop** |
|-------------|------------------------|-----------------|
| Air contact | Yes | No (should be minimal) |
| Evaporation | Yes | No |
| Dissolved solids | Concentrate (needs blowdown) | Stable (if tight) |
| Scale risk | High/ongoing | Low (unless makeup) |
| Corrosion driver | Oxygen + deposits | Oxygen ingress + poor passivation |
| Biological risk | High (Legionella pathway) | Lower (no aerosol device) |
| Treatment priority | Scale + bio + corrosion | Corrosion + oxygen control |
| Makeup expected | Continuous | Minimal after startup |

Open tower treatment requires scale and corrosion inhibitors (phosphonates, phosphates, polymers), both oxidizing and non-oxidizing biocides, blowdown control based on conductivity monitoring, and filtration for solids control. Treatment programs must address all three major issues simultaneously.

Closed loop treatment focuses on corrosion inhibitors selected for system metallurgy (nitrite/molybdate/phosphate combinations), pH and alkalinity control, oxygen exclusion through tight system design, and dirt separation to remove magnetite particles.

## Monitoring Requirements

Open tower monitoring centers on conductivity for cycles control, pH and alkalinity for scale prevention, hardness and inhibitor residuals for treatment effectiveness, oxidizer residuals for biological control, microbiological indicators for pathogen risk, and deposit/fouling KPIs for heat transfer efficiency.

Closed loop monitoring emphasizes makeup rate (should approach zero), inhibitor residual levels, pH stability, iron and copper pickup rates, conductivity stability over time, solids loading from magnetite formation, and air binding that indicates oxygen ingress.

## Key Takeaway

Choose your system design based on heat rejection requirements and acceptable maintenance complexity. Open towers require daily conductivity monitoring, weekly biocide residual testing, and monthly Legionella risk assessments. Closed loops need monthly inhibitor residual checks, quarterly iron pickup analysis, and annual system integrity testing to verify oxygen exclusion effectiveness.
