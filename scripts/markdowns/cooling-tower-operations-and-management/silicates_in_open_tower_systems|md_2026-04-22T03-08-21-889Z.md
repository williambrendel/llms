# Can Silicates Effectively Control Corrosion in Open Cooling Tower Systems?

## Executive Summary

Silicates can control corrosion in open cooling tower systems for mild steel and galvanized surfaces, but they provide weaker protection than zinc or phosphonate-based treatments and work best in soft water with strict phosphorus discharge limits.

## Overview

Silicate films dissolve under stress far more easily than you'd expect from their glassy appearance. While a 50 ppm silicate dose might cut corrosion rates in half for mild steel, the same system could see 5-10 times better protection from a zinc-phosphonate program. This performance gap explains why silicates occupy a specific niche rather than dominating cooling water treatment.

## What Are Silicates in Water Treatment?

Water treatment programs typically add sodium silicate (Na₂SiO₃) as an anodic corrosion inhibitor. The silicate promotes formation of a passivating film of silica (SiO₂) on metal surfaces. This glassy layer acts as a barrier between the metal and the corrosive environment.

Silicate also provides mild alkaline buffering, helping maintain pH in the 8-9 range where most metals resist corrosion. This buffering becomes valuable when systems face acidic influences from CO₂ absorption or biological activity.

## How Silicates Protect Against Corrosion

The silicate corrosion control mechanism centers on anodic protection. When silicate contacts a metal surface in alkaline conditions, it forms a thin silica film that reduces metal ion dissolution and slows the anodic corrosion reaction.

This film proves particularly effective at inhibiting localized pitting on mild steel and galvanized steel surfaces. The protection works best when the metal surface starts clean and the silicate can establish an uninterrupted coating.

The pH buffering effect supports this protection by maintaining conditions where the silica film remains stable and metal passivation occurs naturally.

## Application Parameters and Dosing

| Parameter | Typical Range |
| --- | --- |
| Target dose | 20–150 ppm as SiO₂ |
| pH range | 7.5–9.0 preferred |
| Best for | Mild steel, galvanized steel |
| Not great for | Copper or mixed metallurgy systems |
| Application style | Constant feed with makeup water |

Operators feed silicate continuously with makeup water to maintain consistent protection levels. The wide dosing range reflects different system demands - higher TDS or temperature conditions require doses toward the upper end of the range.

## Limitations and Performance Trade-offs

Silicate films lack the robustness of phosphate/zinc/PBTC films. Under high temperature or high cycles of concentration, silicate protection degrades faster than alternative chemistries. The corrosion protection consistently measures lower than zinc or phosphonate programs under demanding conditions.

Precipitation presents a significant operational risk. At high pH or in hard water, silicates precipitate as calcium silicate scale. This scaling removes the protective chemical from solution while creating fouling problems that reduce heat transfer efficiency.

Silicates provide no protection for copper or brass components. The chemistry that forms protective films on steel surfaces simply doesn't work on copper alloys. Systems with mixed metallurgy require additional or alternative corrosion inhibitors.

Water clarity can suffer with silicate programs. Silicate precipitation or polymerization can cause turbidity and cloudiness that affects system appearance and may indicate loss of corrosion protection.

## Optimal Applications for Silicate Programs

Silicates work best when environmental regulations limit phosphorus discharge. Many municipal systems face strict P-discharge limits that make traditional phosphate programs impossible. Silicate programs provide a phosphate-free and zinc-free alternative that meets environmental requirements.

Mild steel-dominant systems represent the ideal metallurgy for silicate protection. HVAC cooling systems, particularly those operating at lower temperatures and cycles of concentration, often see adequate protection from silicate programs.

Soft to moderate hardness waters minimize precipitation risks while allowing effective film formation. Systems using reclaimed water sometimes benefit from silicate programs when other chemistries prove incompatible with water quality variations.

Galvanized towers during startup phases use silicates specifically to prevent white rust formation. The alkaline conditions and film-forming properties help protect new galvanized surfaces during the critical initial operating period.

## Practical Implementation Considerations

Silicate programs require more careful water quality monitoring than robust alternatives. Operators must track pH, hardness, and cycles of concentration more closely to prevent precipitation while maintaining protection levels.

Biostability offers one advantage over phosphate programs. Silicates don't provide nutrients for bacterial growth the way phosphates can, potentially reducing biocide requirements in some systems.

The weaker protection means silicate programs work best in less demanding applications - lower temperature, lower cycles, or systems where moderate corrosion rates are acceptable given other constraints.

## Key Takeaway

Choose silicate corrosion control when phosphorus discharge limits eliminate phosphate options and your system operates with mild steel metallurgy in soft water. Monitor silica levels weekly, maintain pH below 8.5 to prevent calcium silicate precipitation, and consider hybrid programs with molybdate or nitrite for enhanced protection in demanding applications.
