# How do seasonal changes affect treatment of open tower and closed loop cooling systems?

## Executive Summary

Seasonal changes create distinct treatment challenges for cooling systems: open towers face peak difficulty during summer heat and shoulder seasons due to accelerated concentration and biofilm growth, while closed loops struggle most during seasonal transitions when makeup water introduces contaminants and disturbs settled deposits.

## Overview

A cooling tower that runs flawlessly in March can become a maintenance nightmare by July, then present entirely different problems when winter economizer mode kicks in. Summer heat loads can triple evaporation rates in open systems, concentrating dissolved solids so rapidly that scale inhibitors can't keep pace. Meanwhile, closed loops that remain chemically stable for months can lose control in a matter of days during seasonal changeovers when oxygen-laden makeup water floods the system and mobilizes accumulated deposits.

## Open Cooling Tower Systems

### Summer Operations Under High Heat Load

Higher heat rejection during peak summer increases evaporation rates and makeup water demand. Dissolved solids concentrate faster unless operators increase blowdown proportionally. This acceleration affects every aspect of water treatment.

Scale formation becomes significantly harder to control as mineral concentrations rise and hot surface temperatures create ideal precipitation conditions. Calcium carbonate and calcium phosphate deposits form rapidly on heat exchanger surfaces when concentration ratios climb above design limits.

Corrosion intensifies through multiple mechanisms. Chlorides and sulfates concentrate alongside other dissolved solids, creating aggressive conditions for metal surfaces. Under-deposit corrosion accelerates where scale and biofilm create oxygen concentration cells and trap corrosive species against metal surfaces.

Biological fouling presents the greatest challenge during summer operation. Higher water temperatures, increased nutrient loading from makeup water, and abundant surface area in cooling tower fill create ideal conditions for biofilm development. Biofouling drives many cascading problems including fouling, microbiologically influenced corrosion, flow restrictions, and health concerns from Legionella growth.

Treatment programs require tightening during summer peaks. Conductivity control becomes more critical as concentration happens faster. Blowdown strategies need adjustment to maintain target concentration ratios. Inhibitor residual monitoring increases in importance as higher temperatures can destabilize some chemical treatments. Solids control through filtration and basin cleaning becomes essential to prevent deposit accumulation.

### Shoulder Season Challenges

Variable loads during spring and fall create unique operational problems. Part-load operation and cycling between active and idle cells creates dead zones where water stagnates and solids settle. Control systems for chemical feed and blowdown can "hunt" with frequent on/off cycling, leading to poor chemical residual control.

Biology control becomes most difficult during shoulder seasons despite moderate temperatures. Intermittent wetting and low shear conditions in cycling equipment favor biofilm persistence. Established biofilms can survive dry periods and rapidly recolonize when water flow resumes.

Localized deposition increases in low-flow zones during variable operation. Scale and corrosion products accumulate in areas with poor circulation, creating nucleation sites for further deposit growth when full load resumes.

Inspection and cleaning frequency must increase during shoulder seasons. Basin solids removal becomes critical before deposits migrate to heat exchangers. Side-stream filtration systems prove their value by continuously removing suspended solids that would otherwise settle in dead zones.

### Winter Operations and Shutdown

Low heat loads during winter reduce evaporation rates, which may slow concentration of dissolved solids. However, reduced flow rates and poor water distribution can increase deposition and biofilm formation in protected areas of the cooling tower.

Seasonal shutdown requires careful layup procedures. The main treatment requirement shifts from active water conditioning to preservation during downtime. Disinfection prevents biological growth in stagnant water, while corrosion inhibitors protect metal surfaces during extended idle periods.

## Closed Loop Systems

### Seasonal Transitions Present Greatest Risk

Closed loops should not concentrate through evaporation under normal operation. Seasonal changes matter primarily through startup and shutdown transitions and makeup events that introduce contaminants.

Changeover between cooling and heating seasons creates the highest risk periods. Makeup water additions from system leaks, valve maintenance, or air venting introduce dissolved oxygen and dilute inhibitor concentrations. Fresh oxygen accelerates corrosion reactions that may have been dormant in the oxygen-depleted closed loop environment.

Low-flow conditions during changeover allow magnetite and other suspended solids to settle in system low points. These deposits can later mobilize and cause fouling problems in heat exchangers or control valves.

Treatment focus during transitions centers on maintaining inhibitor residuals and pH control. Minimizing makeup water additions through leak repair becomes critical. Cleaning strainers and dirt separators after seasonal changeover removes accumulated solids before they can cause problems.

## Waterside Economizer Systems

### Plate-and-Frame Heat Exchanger Complications

Many economizer systems use plate-and-frame heat exchangers to transfer heat between cooling tower water and chilled water loops while keeping the fluids separate. These systems face unique seasonal challenges.

During economizer season (typically cool weather), system performance depends entirely on the plate-and-frame heat exchanger's ability to transfer heat efficiently. The narrow channels in these heat exchangers become the limiting factor for several problems.

Tower-side fouling and plugging can rapidly disable economizer operation. PHE channels are narrow compared to shell-and-tube heat exchangers, making them sensitive to particulate matter. Plugging shows up as rapid pressure drop increases across the heat exchanger and loss of heat transfer capacity. Proper straining and filtration upstream of the PHE becomes essential.

Material compatibility limits at the PHE can force changes in biocide strategy. Some manufacturers recommend maximum free available chlorine concentrations around 0.3 mg/L at heat exchanger inlets to limit corrosion risk in certain designs. Chloride, pH, and temperature limits help avoid crevice corrosion in the narrow channels and gasket areas.

Approach temperature requirements become more stringent during economizer operation. Control systems typically use combined tower and heat exchanger approach temperatures as enable/disable logic for economizer mode. As approach temperatures narrow in winter conditions, small amounts of fouling have outsized impacts on system capacity.

### Fall Transition Into Economizer Mode

Switching into economizer mode exposes the clean PHE to cooling tower water that may carry summer deposits and biofilm. The first weeks of economizer operation often show pressure drop increases as loosened solids migrate through the system.

Effective strainers and side-stream filtration must be online before changeover. Basin cleaning to remove accumulated sludge prevents feeding settled solids directly to the sensitive heat exchanger. Confirming compatibility between tower biocide programs and PHE metallurgy requirements prevents corrosion problems during the transition.

### Winter Economizer Operations

Lower heat loads during winter can reduce evaporation and slow concentration in the cooling tower, but tower water quality can still deteriorate from biological growth and solids accumulation. Small amounts of biofilm or particulate loading can quickly reduce PHE performance because economizer systems need tight approach temperatures to deliver required chilled water setpoints.

Solids control becomes the highest priority during economizer season. Keeping channels clean prevents pressure drop increases and maintains heat transfer efficiency. Biological control must continue without exceeding PHE material constraints, which creates challenges when vendor chlorine limits are restrictive.

### Spring Transition Out of Economizer Mode

If the PHE has accumulated fouling during winter operation, chillers may pick up load unexpectedly or run inefficiently during the spring transition. Disturbed deposits can circulate through the system during mode changes.

PHE inspection and cleaning based on pressure drop and approach temperature trends becomes necessary. Re-establishing tower control setpoints for higher summer concentration rates prepares the system for increased heat loads.

## Treatment Burden by System Type

Open towers without economizers face their hardest treatment challenges during peak summer and shoulder seasons. Variable operation and higher biofouling risk during these periods require the most intensive treatment programs.

Closed loops encounter their greatest difficulties during seasonal maintenance and changeover periods when oxygen ingress and solids mobilization can rapidly upset stable water chemistry.

Towers with plate-and-frame economizers face their most demanding treatment requirements during economizer season if solids and biofouling control is inadequate. The sensitivity of PHE performance and tight approach temperature requirements make these systems less forgiving of water quality problems.

## Key Takeaways

Successful seasonal treatment requires adjusting monitoring frequency and control strategies to match changing conditions. Open towers need daily conductivity monitoring and weekly inhibitor residual testing during summer peaks, with monthly basin cleaning to remove accumulated solids. Closed loops require inhibitor residual verification and makeup water minimization during each seasonal transition, with strainer cleaning within two weeks of changeover. Systems with plate-and-frame economizers benefit from continuous side-stream filtration and should maintain tower-side free chlorine below manufacturer limits (typically 0.3 mg/L) while ensuring basin solids removal before each economizer season startup.
