# How Can You Reduce Energy Waste from Poor Cooling Tower Approach to Wet Bulb Temperature?

## Executive Summary

Ten specific maintenance and operational actions can prevent approach temperature increases in cooling towers, with proper nozzle cleaning, fill media maintenance, and fan optimization delivering the most immediate efficiency gains.

## Overview

A cooling tower running just 2°F above its optimal approach temperature can waste 15-20% more energy than necessary. Most facilities unknowingly operate with degraded approach temperatures because the problems develop gradually—clogged nozzles here, scaled fill media there, fans running at suboptimal speeds. The cumulative effect turns what should be an efficient heat rejection system into an energy drain.

## Primary Mechanical Actions

### Clean or Replace Nozzles

Clogged or misaligned nozzles disrupt water distribution across the fill media. This creates hot spots and prevents proper film formation over the fill surface. Poor water distribution means some areas receive too much water while others run dry, destroying the uniform heat transfer that cooling towers depend on.

### Clean Fill Media

Scale, biofilm, and debris accumulate on fill media over time, reducing the available heat transfer area and preventing proper air-water contact. Clean fill media maximizes the surface area where evaporation occurs—the fundamental mechanism that removes heat from your system.

### Verify Fan Speed and Pitch

Fans running under speed or with improper blade pitch reduce airflow through the tower, cutting evaporative cooling effectiveness. Each cooling tower has an optimal airflow rate. Too little air means insufficient evaporation; too much wastes fan energy without proportional cooling benefit.

## System-Level Optimizations

### Check for Media Damage or Bypass

Cracked, broken, or bypassed fill creates short-circuiting paths where water flows through without adequate air contact. This reduces residence time and heat transfer efficiency. Water should spend enough time in contact with air to reach thermal equilibrium.

### Improve Water Treatment

Scale, biofilm, and corrosion degrade heat transfer in both the fill media and condenser bundle. Proper water treatment prevents these deposits from forming and removes existing buildup that insulates surfaces from heat transfer.

### Balance Sump Level and Flow Rate

Insufficient sump levels cause air entrainment, pump cavitation, or dry spots in the fill. Excessive flow rates can overflow the fill media, reducing contact time. The sump level must provide adequate suction head while maintaining proper water distribution across all fill sections.

## Multi-Cell Operations

### Ensure Even Flow Across All Cells

Uneven flow distribution through multiple tower cells creates overload conditions on some cells and underload on others, worsening the average approach temperature. Each cell should handle its designed portion of the total heat load.

### Correct Fan Rotation and Belt Tension

Reversed fan rotation or slipping belts move less air than designed. Fans must rotate in the correct direction at the specified speed to generate the airflow patterns that optimize air-water contact throughout the fill.

### Run Multiple Cells Instead of Overloading One

Operating fewer cells than necessary forces the active cells past their design airflow-to-water loading ratio. This degrades performance more than running additional cells at their optimal loading points.

## Advanced Control Systems

Smart controls that ramp fans and pumps based on wet bulb temperature maintain optimal approach without energy overrun. Variable frequency drives (VFDs) allow precise matching of fan speed to actual cooling demand rather than running at full speed regardless of conditions.

## Key Takeaway

Monitor approach temperature weekly and target staying within 2°F of design conditions. When approach temperatures rise above this threshold, start with nozzle inspection and fill media cleaning—these two actions resolve 60-70% of approach temperature problems. For comprehensive optimization, implement wet-bulb tracking controls with VFDs to automatically maintain optimal approach temperatures while minimizing fan energy consumption.
