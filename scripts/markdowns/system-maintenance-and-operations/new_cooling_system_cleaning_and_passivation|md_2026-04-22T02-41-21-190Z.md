# How Do You Properly Start Up and Maintain Passivation Films in New Cooling Tower Systems?

## Executive Summary

New cooling tower systems require specialized cleaning and passivation procedures that take 5-21 days to form protective films, with initial chemical dosages 2-4 times higher than maintenance levels. Galvanized steel systems demand pH control below 8.5 to prevent destructive white rust formation.

## Overview

A brand-new cooling tower system contains fabrication residues—oils, dirt, and rust—that will destroy equipment if left untreated. The most critical insight: galvanized steel, commonly used in these systems, forms protective films only under controlled conditions, but conventional high-pH cleaning chemicals create fragile white rust that accelerates corrosion rather than preventing it.

## Initial System Preparation and Cleaning

New cooling tower systems arrive contaminated with fabrication debris that must be removed before any protective films can form. The pre-operational cleaning process removes dirt, oils, greasy films, and rust from metal surfaces while preparing them for corrosion control.

Systems containing galvanized steel require special attention during this phase. pH levels must stay below 8.5 throughout cleaning and passivation to prevent white rust formation. White rust appears when galvanized surfaces encounter high-pH conditions—it looks protective but actually accelerates corrosion because it lacks the dense, adherent structure of proper zinc films.

The hydrostatic testing phase begins system preparation. After inspecting and removing debris, operators fill the system with treated water containing orthophosphate or hexametaphosphate at 40-60 ppm (as PO4) and tolyltriazole at 10-20 ppm. This initial treatment begins the passivation process even during testing.

## Pre-Operational Cleaning Procedure

The cleaning sequence follows a precise protocol that ensures complete contaminant removal. After passing hydrostatic tests, operators immediately drain and flush debris, then circulate a specialized cleaning formula for 24 hours. The system gets drained again, strainers cleaned, and fresh makeup water circulated for 30 minutes.

Water quality verification confirms cleaning completion. System water samples must match makeup water supply for clarity and suspended solids, with conductivity within 10% and pH within 0.3 units of the 7.5-8.5 target range. Operators repeat the cleaning cycle until water meets these specifications.

**What it measures:** Chemical cleaning formulation for removing fabrication contaminants while initiating passivation

Formula:
```
Orthophosphate (or hexametaphosphate): 60 ppm as PO4
Polyacrylate: 20 ppm active
Tolyltriazole: 10 ppm active  
Sodium gluconate: 50 ppm
Pluronic L-61: 400 ppm active
Phosphate scale inhibitor: 50 ppm
```

**Example:** A 10,000-gallon cooling system requires 2.25 pounds of orthophosphate, 0.75 pounds of polyacrylate, and 15 pounds of surfactant during the 24-hour cleaning cycle.

## Passivation Film Formation Requirements

Different corrosion inhibitors create protective films at varying rates and require specific dosing protocols. The formation time ranges from 5-6 days for fast-acting inhibitors to 10-12 days for slower but more durable films.

| **Selected Corrosion Inhibitor** | **Initial Dosage (ppm)** | **Maintenance Dosage (ppm)** | **Film-Formation Time (Days)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cathodic Inhibitor** |  |  |  |
| Polyphosphate | 40-60 as PO4 | 10-20 as PO4 | 5-6 |
| Zinc salts | 10-20 as Zn | 3-5 as Zn | 5-6 |
| Polysilicate | 40-60 as SiO2 | 10-20 as SiO2 | 10-12 |
| Molybdate | 40-60 as Mo | 5-20 as Mo | 10-12 |
| **Anodic Inhibitor** |  |  |  |
| Orthophosphate | 40-60 as PO4 | 15-20 as PO4 | 5-6 |
| Orthosilicate | 40-60 as SiO2 | 10-15 as SiO2 | 10-12 |
| **Copper Corrosion Inhibitor** |  |  |  |
| Tolyltriazole | 10-20 as TTA | 1-2 as TTA | 5-6 |

Galvanized steel passivation occurs through exposure to highly aerated water combined with phosphate or polysilicate treatment. This creates a zinc corrosion inhibition layer—a natural, dense, adherent protective film that prevents further corrosion.

## Required and Supplemental Chemical Technologies

Primary chemical technologies provide the foundation for film formation. Polyphosphate, orthophosphate, and tolyltriazole each require 14-21 days at specific dosages within the 6.5-7.9 pH range.

| **Inhibitor** | **Dosage** | **pH** | **Film Formation** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Polyphosphate | 40-60 ppm as PO4 | 6.5-7.9 | 14-21 days |
| Orthophosphate | 20-30 ppm as PO4 | 6.5-7.9 | 14-21 days |
| Tolytriazole | 10-20 ppm as TTA | 6.5-7.9 | 14-21 days |

Supplemental technologies enhance protection but require different pH conditions. Zinc and polysilicate operate in distinct pH ranges, with polysilicate and molybdate requiring higher pH levels than zinc treatments.

| **Inhibitor** | **Dosage** | **pH** | **Film Formation** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Zinc | 10-20 ppm as Zn | 6.5-7.5 | 14-21 days |
| Polysilicate | 40-50 ppm as SiO2 | 7.5-8.5 | 14-21 days |
| Molybdate | 40-60 ppm as Mo | 7.5-8.5 | 14-21 days |

## System Layup and Long-Term Storage

Systems requiring extended shutdown need wet layup procedures that maintain protection during idle periods. Physical cleaning removes all suspended materials before applying corrosion inhibitors at 4-5 times normal maintenance dosages.

Biocide application becomes critical during layup. Non-oxidizing biocides with long half-lives—glutaraldehyde or isothiazolone—prevent biological growth that could compromise protective films. EPA limits govern maximum biocide concentrations during storage.

## Implementation Strategy

Start passivation immediately after hydrostatic testing—never allow untreated water in the system. Monitor pH continuously during the first week, maintaining levels between 7.5-8.5 for galvanized systems. Test water quality every 48 hours during the initial 14-day formation period, then weekly until films fully develop. Reduce chemical dosages to maintenance levels only after confirming film integrity through corrosion rate measurements below 2 mils per year.
