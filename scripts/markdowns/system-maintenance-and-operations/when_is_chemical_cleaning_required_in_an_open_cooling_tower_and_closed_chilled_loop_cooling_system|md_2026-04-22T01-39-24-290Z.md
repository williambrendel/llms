# When is chemical cleaning required in operation of open cooling tower and closed loop cooling systems?

## Executive Summary

Chemical cleaning becomes necessary when deposits, corrosion products, or biological growth measurably reduce heat transfer performance or threaten equipment integrity, with open cooling towers requiring intervention at >3-5 ppm iron levels and closed loops at >1 ppm iron levels.

## Overview

Scale as thin as 0.025 inches can slash heat transfer efficiency by 15-20%, while biofilm just 0.1 mm thick cuts performance by over 10%. Open cooling towers face constant contamination from airborne particles and evaporation, making them more vulnerable than sealed closed-loop systems that should theoretically stay clean with proper treatment.

## Open Cooling Tower Systems

### Scale Formation Triggers

Chemical cleaning becomes essential when approach temperature exceeds design parameters by 2-3°F or more. Condenser pressure increases alongside visible calcium carbonate deposits signal scale buildup. When the Langelier Saturation Index exceeds +0.5 with confirmed deposition, immediate action prevents further efficiency loss.

**What it measures:** The relationship between scale thickness and heat transfer loss

Formula:
```
Efficiency Loss (%) ≈ 15-20% per 0.025 in (0.6 mm) scale thickness
```

**Example:** A chiller with 0.025 inches of scale loses 15-20% heat transfer efficiency, increasing energy consumption from 0.6 kW/ton to 0.72 kW/ton.

### Heat Transfer Efficiency Decline

Chiller energy consumption increases when fouling factors exceed design allowances. Heat exchanger temperature differentials rise abnormally, typically triggering intervention when fouling factors surpass 0.002 hr·ft²·°F/Btu, though this varies by equipment type.

### Corrosion Product Accumulation

Iron concentrations above 3-5 ppm in tower water indicate active corrosion requiring cleaning intervention. Red or black sludge accumulates in basins while differential pressure increases across heat exchangers signal flow restriction from corrosion debris.

### Biological Fouling

Visible slime layers, elevated ATP levels, and bacteria counts exceeding 10⁴-10⁵ CFU/mL demand immediate cleaning. Legionella detection triggers emergency cleaning protocols regardless of other parameters.

### Extended Shutdown Recovery

Systems idle for more than 30-60 days without proper layup protection require chemical cleaning before restart. New construction systems need pre-operational cleaning to remove mill scale, welding slag, oil, grease, and construction debris.

## Closed Loop Cooling Systems

### Iron and Corrosion Management

Closed loops should maintain iron levels below 0.5 ppm during normal operation. Concentrations exceeding 1 ppm indicate corrosion requiring cleaning intervention. Magnetite sludge in strainers, pump seal wear, and flow restrictions confirm the need for chemical cleaning.

### Pressure and Flow Monitoring

Differential pressure increases across heat exchangers, coils, and plate-and-frame units signal fouling. Uneven coil temperatures, hot and cold spots, and increased energy consumption indicate heat transfer loss requiring cleaning.

### Microbial Control in Glycol Systems

Bacteria counts above 10³ CFU/mL in glycol systems trigger cleaning requirements. Glycol degradation and pH drift outside the 8.0-10.0 range for steel systems indicate microbial contamination requiring immediate attention.

### Contamination Events

Air ingress, makeup water leaks, freeze events, and glycol breakdown contaminate closed systems, necessitating complete chemical cleaning and system restoration.

## Decision Criteria and Cleaning Methods

| Parameter | Open Tower Trigger | Closed Loop Trigger |
|-----------|-------------------|---------------------|
| Iron | >3-5 ppm | >1 ppm |
| Approach Temp | >2-3°F above design | Any unexplained increase |
| Differential Pressure | >10-15% rise | >10% rise |
| Visible Scale | Any confirmed | Any confirmed |
| Bacteria Count | >10⁴ CFU/mL | >10³ CFU/mL |
| Biofilm | Visible or ATP elevated | Visible |

Different contaminants require specific cleaning approaches. Scale removal uses inhibited hydrochloric, sulfamic, or citric acid cleaning. Iron oxide removal employs EDTA-based chelant cleaning. Biofilm elimination combines oxidizing agents with dispersants. Oil and grease removal requires alkaline degreasing, while new systems need pre-operational flushing plus passivation.

## Key Takeaways

Monitor iron levels monthly in closed loops (trigger: >1 ppm) and weekly in open towers (trigger: >3-5 ppm), measure approach temperatures daily with cleaning required when exceeding design by 2-3°F, and consider chelant-based cleaning for iron removal or inhibited acid cleaning for scale when fouling factors exceed 0.002 hr·ft²·°F/Btu.
