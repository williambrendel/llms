# How often should systems be inspected in open cooling tower and closed chilled water systems?

## Executive Summary

Open cooling towers require daily mechanical and thermal checks, while closed chilled water systems need weekly mechanical inspections. Open towers demand more frequent attention because they face constant contamination from air exposure, biological growth, and mechanical stress from fans and pumps.

## Overview

A single failed pump seal in an open cooling tower can cascade into corrosion problems within days, yet most facilities inspect these systems far less frequently than their complexity demands. Closed loop systems, protected from environmental contamination, follow a more relaxed schedule but still require systematic monitoring to prevent oxygen ingress and fouling.

## Daily Operations for Open Cooling Towers

Open cooling towers operate as the most vulnerable component in most HVAC systems. Daily inspections focus on mechanical integrity and thermal performance because problems compound rapidly in these open-air environments.

Operators should check pump seals and flanges every day, looking for active leaks, salt deposits, or staining around connections. Pump casings and suction/discharge piping require visual inspection for seepage or corrosion evidence. These mechanical checks prevent oxygen introduction that accelerates system-wide corrosion.

Fan systems demand daily attention through auditory and visual assessment. Irregular fan noise—grinding, rattling, cyclical thumping, or tonal changes—signals bearing wear or imbalance. Visible fan oscillation or vibration transmitted to the tower structure indicates mounting problems or blade damage. Gearbox or motor abnormal noise, including whine, growl, or sudden pitch changes, warns of lubrication failure or mechanical wear.

Daily thermal performance monitoring tracks tower delta-T (hot water in versus cold water out), approach to wet bulb temperature, and supply water temperature against set point. Reduced delta-T indicates flow loss, pump seal failure, or bypassing. Rising approach temperatures signal fan degradation, airflow restriction, or fouled fill media.

## Weekly Water Treatment and Performance Reviews

Water treatment service inspections occur weekly in open cooling towers. These visits include chemistry testing, biocide feed verification, controller and sensor checks, and correlation of chemistry excursions with mechanical findings. Weekly frequency prevents biological growth that can establish within 5-7 days under favorable conditions.

**What it measures:** The relationship between thermal efficiency loss and mechanical degradation over time.

Formula:
```
Tower Efficiency = (T_hot_in - T_cold_out) / (T_hot_in - T_wet_bulb) × 100%
```

**Example:** A tower receiving 95°F water should deliver 85°F water when the wet bulb temperature is 78°F. Efficiency = (95-85)/(95-78) × 100% = 59%. Declining efficiency below design values indicates fouling or mechanical problems.

## Monthly and Quarterly Assessments

Distribution system inspections occur monthly for visual checks and quarterly for hands-on assessment. Monthly reviews identify plugged or missing nozzles, uneven wetting of fill media, and evidence of water channeling through the tower. Quarterly inspections verify distribution deck levelness and measure flow uniformity.

Fill media, drift eliminators, basin condition, and structural components require monthly visual inspection and quarterly detailed review. Fill fouling or collapse reduces heat transfer efficiency. Basin sludge depth indicates filtration effectiveness and blowdown adequacy. Side-stream filter condition affects overall water quality maintenance.

## Closed Loop System Inspection Protocols

Closed chilled water systems follow a less intensive schedule due to environmental protection from the closed loop design. Weekly mechanical inspections focus on pump seals, valve packing, flange connections, and air elimination equipment. Any leakage introduces oxygen that drives corrosion throughout the system.

Pump and motor noise assessment occurs weekly, listening for bearing wear or alignment problems. Vibration at pumps and piping connections indicates mounting issues or system imbalance. Expansion tank level and strainer differential pressure provide operational status indicators.

Monthly thermal performance checks track loop delta-T between supply and return temperatures, set-point compliance, and coil performance complaints that indicate fouling or air binding. Water treatment inspections occur monthly for standard systems or quarterly for proven stable systems, testing pH, inhibitor residuals, conductivity trends, and metals transport indicators.

## Risk-Based and Comprehensive Reviews

Microbiological inspections in open cooling towers occur weekly to monthly, with immediate inspection required after chemical feed loss, high bacterial counts, or system outages. Biological growth accelerates rapidly in warm, nutrient-rich tower environments.

Annual comprehensive audits review full mechanical condition, long-term thermal performance trends, distribution and fill condition, and program governance documentation. These audits identify gradual degradation not apparent in routine inspections.

Closed loop systems require comprehensive audits every 1-2 years due to their more stable operating environment and reduced contamination risk.

## Key Takeaways

Implement daily visual and auditory inspections for open cooling towers, focusing on pump seals, fan noise, and thermal performance trending through building automation systems. Establish 85% thermal efficiency as a threshold for investigating mechanical or fouling problems. Consider automated monitoring systems that track approach temperature, delta-T, and vibration continuously, alerting operators when parameters exceed acceptable ranges rather than relying solely on manual daily rounds.
