# How Do You Safely Store Cooling Water Systems During Extended Shutdowns?

## Executive Summary

Dry lay-up procedures for cooling water systems prevent corrosion, biological fouling, and scaling during extended shutdowns by completely draining the system, removing all moisture with compressed air, and applying vapor-phase corrosion inhibitors before sealing.

## Overview

A data center chiller sitting idle for months can develop serious internal damage that costs thousands to repair. Residual water in condenser tubes creates the perfect environment for corrosion pitting and biofilm growth, even when the system appears completely drained. The solution requires removing every trace of moisture and creating a protective atmosphere inside the equipment.

## Pre-Shutdown Chemical Treatment

Before draining any cooling water system, shock-dose it with non-oxidizing biocides to eliminate existing microorganisms. Isothiazolone works effectively at 300-500 ppm, while DBNPA requires 300 ppm for adequate microbial kill. Circulate this treated water for 2-4 hours to penetrate biofilms and reach planktonic bacteria throughout the system.

This pre-treatment step prevents decomposing organic matter from creating corrosive conditions during the lay-up period. Systems that skip biocide treatment often develop severe pitting corrosion in areas where biofilms were present.

## Complete Water Removal

Total drainage requires more than opening drain valves. Horizontal piping sections, heat exchanger headers, and dead legs trap water that gravity cannot remove. Use pumps or manual extraction methods to clear these low points before proceeding.

Follow drainage with compressed air or nitrogen blowdown to eliminate residual moisture. The air source must be oil-free and dry to avoid introducing contaminants. Continue blowing until no visible condensate appears and all surfaces look completely dry.

## Corrosion Protection Methods

Choose between two proven protection strategies based on your facility's capabilities and lay-up duration.

**Vapor-Phase Corrosion Inhibitor (VCI) Application**

VCI compounds create a protective molecular layer on metal surfaces throughout the sealed system. Apply these chemicals through fogging equipment that distributes them evenly into condenser tubes and headers. Products like Nalco 7978 provide effective protection when the system remains properly sealed.

**Filming Amine Treatment**

Filming amines like DEHA or morpholine coat exposed metal surfaces with a protective barrier. Apply these chemicals through spray or fog equipment, ensuring complete coverage of all internal surfaces. This method works well for systems that may experience minor air leakage during storage.

## System Sealing and Monitoring

Seal all flanges, drains, vents, and openings immediately after applying corrosion protection. Use caps, blind flanges, or temporary covers to prevent humid air infiltration. Place desiccant bags near major openings for additional moisture control.

Tag equipment clearly with the lay-up date, chemicals used, and prohibition against filling or starting. Record all procedures in maintenance logs for future reference.

For lay-up periods exceeding three months, inspect the system every 30-60 days. Open one access point to check for moisture, corrosion signs, or unusual odors. Re-treat the system if humidity breach is suspected.

## Return to Service Protocol

Recommissioning requires systematic inspection and cleaning before resuming operation. Remove all seals and caps, then visually examine internal surfaces for corrosion or deposits. Flush the entire system with clean water to remove any residual chemicals or debris.

Test the flush water for dissolved metals like iron and copper, which indicate corrosion damage during storage. High metal concentrations may require additional cleaning or equipment inspection before returning to normal service.

## Key Takeaways

Inspect stored cooling systems every 30-60 days during lay-ups longer than three months, maintain iron levels below 1.0 ppm and copper below 0.5 ppm in recommissioning flush water, and consider VCI fogging for superior protection in facilities with reliable sealing capabilities.
