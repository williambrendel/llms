# How can I tell if my cooling tower and closed loop programs are working?

## Executive Summary

Cooling towers work when cycles of concentration stay stable within 5-10% of target while maintaining clean basins and consistent disinfectant residuals. Closed loops work when metal ion trends flatten after initial cleanup and makeup water requirements remain minimal.

## Overview

A properly functioning water treatment program saves facilities thousands in energy costs and prevents catastrophic equipment failures, yet 60% of cooling systems operate below optimal efficiency due to poor monitoring. The difference between "working" and "failing" often shows up in subtle chemistry trends weeks before visible problems appear. Most facility managers wait for scale buildup or biofilm formation, but the real indicators hide in conductivity ratios and metal ion concentrations.

## Cooling Tower Program Performance

### Control the Concentration Mechanism

Track makeup conductivity versus recirculating/blowdown conductivity to calculate cycles of concentration (CoC). Most systems use this simple formula:

**What it measures:** How many times water concentrates dissolved solids before blowdown removes them

Formula:
```
CoC = Blowdown (tower) conductivity ÷ Makeup conductivity
```

**Example:** If makeup water measures 400 µS/cm and blowdown measures 2,000 µS/cm, your system runs at 5 cycles of concentration.

Working systems maintain CoC stable within 5-10% of target without constant manual adjustments. Blowdown control responds predictably to load changes, and scaling/corrosion risk indicators stay within established limits.

### Optimize Concentration Without Damage

Push your system to the highest sustainable CoC without creating scale or corrosion problems. Inspect fill and heat exchangers regularly for new scale formation. Monitor pH, alkalinity, and hardness to confirm they align with your target CoC.

Stable corrosion indicators prove the system operates efficiently. Rising pH or hardness beyond expected concentration levels signals potential scaling, while declining alkalinity suggests acid dosing problems or biological activity.

### Biological Control Indicators

Monitor disinfectant residual continuously when possible. Effective biological control requires managing scale, corrosion, and sediment alongside disinfection, since biofilms thrive on surface deposits.

Working systems show disinfectant residual and pH consistently in range with low variability (±1-2%). Visual inspections reveal minimal slime/biofilm, low sediment accumulation, and clean basin/fill surfaces.

Legionella testing results require interpretation within your complete water management program context, not as isolated data points.

### Physical Inspection Validation

Regular inspections for scale, corrosion products, biofilm, and sediment must align with chemistry data. Working systems demonstrate good cleanliness, absence of chronic deposition, and no recurring fouling events.

Operational outcomes improve or remain stable: heat transfer approach/ΔT, condenser pressure, energy consumption, unplanned cleanings, plugged strainers/nozzles, and drift eliminator fouling all indicate program effectiveness.

## Closed Loop Program Performance

### Metal Ion and Contamination Monitoring

Closed loops fail differently than open towers. Track iron, copper, and zinc concentrations alongside chloride, sulfate, and TDS trends to detect treatment deficiencies, contamination, or active corrosion.

**What it measures:** Ongoing corrosion activity and contamination sources in closed systems

**Corrosion coupons** provide the standard method for quantifying corrosion rates over time. **Dissolved metals** (Fe, Cu, Zn) compared to makeup water identify corrosion sources and activity levels.

**Example:** If system iron rises from 0.2 mg/L to 1.8 mg/L over three months while makeup contains 0.1 mg/L iron, active corrosion contributes 1.7 mg/L—indicating treatment failure.

Working systems show coupon rates low and stable relative to metallurgy-specific targets. Iron and copper trends flatten or decline after initial cleanup and stabilization periods.

### Chemistry Stability

Monitor chloride, sulfate, and TDS to detect corrosive contaminant buildup or excessive makeup additions. pH stability helps identify contamination sources—steam leaks in hot water systems create characteristic pH signatures.

Working systems require minimal makeup volume while maintaining stable pH and inhibitor residuals (nitrite/molybdate/azoles/phosphate-based programs). Glycol concentrations remain constant when present.

### Solids and Microbiology Control

Effective filtration prevents side-stream filter pressure spikes and maintains low turbidity after initial cleanup. Working systems avoid recurring coil or heat exchanger plugging.

Closed loops generally show less biological activity than towers, but monitor low-flow and stagnant points for slime/biofilm evidence (Pseudomonas) and MIC indicators (sulfate-reducing bacteria).

## Performance Scorecards

### Weekly/Monthly Cooling Tower Review

Working towers maintain CoC on target and stable, disinfectant residual stable with clean basin/fill, and show no new scale/corrosion deposition with fewer required cleanings.

### Monthly/Quarterly Closed Loop Review

Working closed loops demonstrate low/stable coupon corrosion rates, stable metals (Fe/Cu/Zn) and stable chloride/sulfate/TDS, plus minimal makeup requirements with stable inhibitor residual and pH.

### Failure Indicators

Towers fail when disinfectant residual varies significantly, slime/sediment recurs, cleaning frequency increases, approach temperature rises, chronic scaling develops, or CoC control drifts.

Closed loops fail when iron/copper concentrations rise, chloride/sulfate/TDS increase (indicating makeup intrusion), solids persist, or coupon rates fail to improve after cleanup procedures.

## Key Takeaways

Monitor conductivity ratios weekly for towers and metal ion trends monthly for closed loops to catch problems before equipment damage occurs. Target tower CoC within ±10% of design and closed loop iron concentrations below 2 mg/L above makeup levels. Consider automated monitoring systems for critical applications, side-stream filtration for closed loops with recurring solids issues, and quarterly corrosion coupon analysis for accurate long-term trending.
