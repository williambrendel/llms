# How do lay up processes prevent damage in open cooling tower and closed loop cooling systems?

## Executive Summary

Layup processes prevent damage by controlling the three main failure drivers during downtime: microbial growth (especially Legionella in open towers), oxygen-driven corrosion, and deposition from settling solids that cause under-deposit corrosion.

## Overview

A cooling system sitting idle without proper layup can transform from a functioning heat exchanger into a biological incubator and corrosion accelerator within just days. Open cooling towers face the added risk of becoming Legionella amplification sites, while closed loops can generate massive amounts of magnetite sludge from oxygen corrosion in a matter of weeks. The right layup approach depends entirely on whether you're dealing with an open system exposed to air or a closed loop designed to exclude oxygen.

## Open Cooling Towers

### What damage occurs without layup

Open cooling towers face multiple simultaneous threats during downtime. **Legionella amplification** occurs in stagnant warm water and existing biofilm, creating serious health risks. **Biofilm thickening** reduces heat transfer efficiency and causes plugging throughout the system. **Corrosion** from oxygenated stagnant water leads to basin pitting and steel attack. **Sludge settling** creates conditions for under-deposit corrosion and odor problems. When the system restarts, **severe fouling** occurs as iron deposits and biofilm mobilize throughout the system.

### Wet Layup (preferred for short outages)

Wet layup works best when downtime typically spans days to a few weeks and you can maintain water in the system. This approach prevents damage by keeping **biocide residual** present to suppress bacteria and biofilm growth, maintaining **corrosion inhibitor films** on metal surfaces, and allowing **periodic circulation** to prevent stagnation and settling.

The process starts with **cleaning gross debris and sludge** from the basin and strainers. **Pre-disinfection** follows as a critical step for Legionella risk management: maintain free chlorine at **5–10 mg/L** for **≥6 hours** at pH 7.0–7.6 while circulating. **Drain and refill** if water shows high solids or contamination. After refilling, establish a **maintenance residual** of free chlorine at **0.5–1.0 mg/L** (or bromine equivalent). **Disable fans** when possible to reduce aerosol drift. **Circulate water** periodically—daily or several times per week—to avoid dead zones.

Regular monitoring checks oxidizer residual, pH/conductivity, and visual signs of slime formation. The critical failure mode: without maintaining oxidizer residual and circulation, the tower becomes a **biological incubator** within days.

### Dry Layup (preferred for long outages)

Dry layup suits downtime periods **longer than 30 days**, especially when sites cannot maintain chemical control. This method prevents damage by eliminating the water environment needed for Legionella growth, stopping deposition and sludge settling, and reducing corrosion when surfaces are thoroughly dried and protected from humidity.

The process begins with **cleaning and disinfecting** using the same pre-disinfection protocol as wet layup. **Drain completely** from basin, sumps, low points, and piping pockets. **Remove remaining sludge** through vacuum or hand removal. **Dry thoroughly** using drain-down, airflow, and time—the goal is eliminating all standing water.

**Protect metal surfaces** with light protective coating on bare steel or vapor phase corrosion inhibitor (VCI) where site practices allow. **Cover openings** to prevent rain and humid air exchange.

The critical point: "dry layup" that remains actually wet creates worse conditions than wet layup. Residual wet sludge drives **under-deposit corrosion** and concentrated microbiological activity.

## Closed Loop Cooling Systems

Closed loops—including chilled water, hot water, and closed condenser loops—are designed to operate with low oxygen levels. Layup focuses on **preventing oxygen entry and preserving inhibitor chemistry**.

### What damage occurs without layup

Without proper layup, closed loops experience **flash rusting** after drain and refill cycles. **Magnetite sludge formation** results from oxygen corrosion, leading to **plugging** of coils, heat exchangers, and strainers. **Pump seal wear** and bearing stress increase from circulating solids. **Microbial growth in glycol** can occur if systems are neglected, though this is less common.

### Wet Layup (preferred)

Wet layup keeps the loop full and chemically protected. A full, sealed system minimizes oxygen ingress while inhibitors maintain protective films on steel surfaces. Periodic circulation prevents settling and stratification.

Keep the system **full** and avoid partial draining. Maintain inhibitor targets for typical carbon steel nitrite programs: **sodium nitrite at 600–1,200 ppm as NO₂** and **pH between 8.5–10.0**. Eliminate air by venting high points and ensuring air separators function properly. Circulate periodically—weekly is common practice—to keep chemistry uniform.

Quarterly monitoring checks nitrite (or other inhibitor levels), pH, conductivity, and iron content. The goal for iron is commonly **less than 0.5 ppm** after system stabilization.

The key failure mode: draining or allowing air entry raises dissolved oxygen levels, accelerating corrosion rapidly. The loop can generate large amounts of magnetite within weeks.

### Dry Layup (only when necessary)

Dry layup applies when freeze protection is impossible and the system requires draining, or for long-term mothballing. When executed correctly, removing water eliminates aqueous corrosion and prevents freeze burst damage.

**Pre-clean** and stabilize chemistry before draining when possible to reduce remaining sludge. **Drain completely** from all low points, coils, and heat exchanger headers. **Blow down and dry** using dry compressed air or nitrogen purge to drive out moisture. **Protect internals** with nitrogen blanket where feasible and VCI products designed for hydronic storage. **Cap or close openings** to exclude humid air.

Partial draining creates the worst-case scenario: wet oxygen-rich surfaces cause severe corrosion at the air-water interface.

## System-Specific Layup Strategy

| **System** | **Biggest downtime risk** | **Layup goal** | **Best default** |
|------------|---------------------------|----------------|------------------|
| Open tower | Legionella + biofilm + corrosion | Control biology or remove water | Wet for short, Dry for long |
| Closed loop | Oxygen corrosion + sludge | Keep oxygen out, preserve inhibitors | Wet (keep full) |

## Key Takeaways

Disinfect open towers before shutdown and before restart if idle for extended periods. Keep closed loops full and inhibited rather than draining. Never allow stagnation without maintaining residual biocide in open systems or inhibitor in closed systems. Partial drains create the fastest corrosion damage in both system types. Document all layup procedures including date, chemistry levels, residuals, circulation frequency, and inspection results for future reference and regulatory compliance.
