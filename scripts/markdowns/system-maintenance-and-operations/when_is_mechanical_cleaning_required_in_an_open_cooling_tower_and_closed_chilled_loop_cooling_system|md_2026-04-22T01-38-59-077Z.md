# When is mechanical cleaning required in operation of open cooling tower and closed loop cooling systems?

## Executive Summary

Open cooling towers require bi-annual mechanical cleaning minimum per ASHRAE 188 compliance, while closed loop systems need cleaning only when performance degrades or contamination occurs. Performance triggers like sustained approach temperature increases over 1-2°F above baseline or visible sludge accumulation over ½ inch depth demand immediate mechanical intervention.

## Overview

A cooling tower running perfectly for months can suddenly lose 15% capacity in weeks once biofilm takes hold of the fill media. The difference between open and closed cooling systems isn't just atmospheric exposure—it's the fundamental cleaning philosophy that separates regulatory compliance from performance-based maintenance.

## Open Cooling Tower Systems

Open cooling towers face continuous atmospheric contamination that accumulates faster than chemical treatment can control. These systems collect dirt, airborne debris, biological growth, biofilm, scale, corrosion products, and basin sediment through normal operation.

### Regulatory Compliance Requirements

ASHRAE 188 and OSHA guidance establish non-negotiable cleaning frequencies for Legionellosis risk management. Systems require bi-annual cleaning and disinfection as the recommended standard, with annual cleaning as the absolute minimum. Each cleaning cycle must include basin sludge removal, fill inspection and cleaning, distribution deck and nozzle inspection, plus disinfection before returning to service. These requirements apply regardless of apparent system performance.

### Performance-Based Triggers

Several measurable conditions trigger immediate cleaning needs. A sustained approach temperature increase exceeding 1-2°F above the clean baseline, verified under comparable wet bulb and load conditions, indicates fouled fill, scaling, or biofilm formation. Rising chiller condenser approach temperatures, when tower airflow and flow rates remain adequate, suggest fouling within the tower or condenser tubes. Reduced tower temperature differential at similar loads often stems from distribution problems or fill fouling.

### Physical and Visual Indicators

Basin conditions provide clear cleaning triggers. Sediment depth reaching ½ inch represents the screening threshold for action, especially when sludge accumulates near pump suction areas. Visible biofilm or scale formation on fill or structural components, scaling or brittle fill material, and uneven water distribution patterns all indicate that chemistry alone cannot restore hardened deposits—mechanical cleaning becomes necessary.

Persistent operational problems also signal cleaning needs. Strainers clogging frequently and elevated suspended solids despite active control programs indicate systematic fouling beyond chemical treatment capabilities.

### Biological Triggers

Microbiological conditions often drive emergency cleaning schedules. Chronic high heterotrophic plate counts, Legionella detection events, and increasing oxidant demand all suggest established biofilm that requires physical removal before chemical treatment regains effectiveness.

## Closed Loop Cooling Systems

Closed loop systems avoid atmospheric debris but accumulate different contaminants: corrosion byproducts like magnetite, construction debris, scale from makeup events, and biofilm when oxygen ingress occurs. These systems typically require lower cleaning frequency than open towers and operate on condition-based maintenance schedules.

### Performance Triggers

Differential pressure increases across coils, strainers, or heat exchangers indicate fouling accumulation. Frequent strainer cleaning requirements and low temperature differential syndrome—where return temperature differential decreases and coil capacity complaints increase—signal the need for mechanical intervention.

### Contamination Indicators

Iron levels consistently exceeding 1-2 mg/L serve as screening indicators for corrosion product accumulation. Visible magnetite in strainers and elevated turbidity measurements confirm the need for system cleaning. Major leak events, oxygen ingress situations, significant makeup additions, system draining and refilling, or glycol contamination may require comprehensive flushing and mechanical cleaning.

### Scheduled Maintenance

Unlike open systems, closed loops generally do not require annual mechanical cleaning unless specific triggers occur. However, flushing becomes recommended after construction, major repairs, or when heavy corrosion products accumulate in the system.

## System Comparison

| **Condition** | **Open Cooling Tower** | **Closed Loop** |
|---------------|------------------------|-----------------|
| Annual regulatory cleaning | **Minimum required** | Not typically required |
| Recommended cleaning frequency | **Bi-annual** | Condition-based |
| Performance degradation | Clean | Clean |
| Visible sludge/deposits | Clean | Flush |
| Chronic bio issues | Clean & disinfect | Investigate |
| Major contamination event | Clean | Flush & rebalance |

## Key Takeaway

Schedule bi-annual mechanical cleaning for open cooling towers regardless of performance, and monitor closed loop systems monthly for iron levels above 2 mg/L or differential pressure increases exceeding 10% of baseline. When deposits, sludge, scale, or biofilm exceed chemical treatment capabilities, mechanical cleaning restores the baseline conditions that chemistry maintains between interventions.
