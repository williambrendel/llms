# How do process leaks negatively impact a treatment program?

## Executive Summary

Process leaks harm treatment programs by changing water chemistry faster than control systems can correct, introducing contaminants that defeat inhibitors and biocides, and increasing corrosion, scale, fouling, and biological risk.

## Overview

A single process leak can destroy months of careful water treatment optimization within days. In open cooling towers, even small leaks concentrate into major chemical loads as water evaporates, while closed loops lose their fundamental stability the moment any leak allows oxygen ingress or inhibitor loss.

## Open Cooling Tower Systems

### Why leaks are especially damaging in open towers

Open towers evaporate water, so non-volatile contaminants from a leak concentrate with cycles of concentration. Even a "small" leak can quickly become a large chemical load.

### Core negative impacts

#### Increased Scale and Deposition

**Leak sources:** high-hardness water, process brines, caustic/acid streams, cement wash, CIP wastes.

**Mechanisms:**
- Higher calcium, alkalinity, silica, phosphate drive higher scaling indices and precipitation
- Increased TSS/turbidity creates under-deposit scale and fouling

**Operational symptoms:**
- Rising condenser approach temperature
- Increased head pressure / kW/ton
- Fill scaling and nozzle plugging

#### Corrosion Acceleration

**Leak sources:** acids, chlorides, sulfates, oxidants, oxidizing cleaners, ammonia/amine streams.

**Mechanisms:**
- Chlorides increase pitting risk (carbon steel, stainless depending on grade)
- Low pH increases general corrosion
- High oxidant demand causes intermittent loss of residual, leading to microbiological upsets and MIC (microbiologically influenced corrosion)
- Some contaminants strip inhibitor films

**Operational symptoms:**
- Iron spikes
- Rapid basin sludge formation
- High coupon corrosion / pitting rates
- Leaks at welds/heat-affected zones

#### Biological Control Failure

**Leak sources:** sugars, glycols, organics, food process streams.

**Mechanisms:**
- Adds biological food (BOD/COD), causing bacteria populations to explode
- Biocide demand rises, causing residuals to collapse
- Biofilm thickens, protecting bacteria from biocides and increasing aerosol risk

**Operational symptoms:**
- Loss of oxidizer residual at distal points
- Rising ATP/HPC
- Slime, odor, foaming
- Higher risk of Legionella amplification

#### Foaming and Drift Problems

**Leak sources:** surfactants, soaps, detergents, oils.

**Mechanisms:**
- Surfactants lower surface tension, creating foam
- Foam increases drift carryover and chemical loss
- Can cause low-level alarms, slip hazards, overflow

**Operational symptoms:**
- Persistent foam despite antifoam use
- Drift eliminator wetting/entrainment
- Chemical overfeed chasing losses

#### Treatment Chemistry Instability and Cost

Conductivity controllers try to respond with blowdown. Blowdown increases makeup, which increases chemical feed. The program becomes "chasing" contamination rather than controlling corrosion, scale, and biofilm.

**Practical rule:** In open towers, a process leak often shows up first as unexplained conductivity rise, then biocide residual instability, then performance loss.

## Closed Loop Cooling Systems

Closed loops should be stable, low oxygen, low solids. Leaks break that stability.

### External water leaks out (system losing water)

#### Oxygen ingress
As water leaves, air enters (or is pulled in at high points). Dissolved oxygen drives corrosion, creating magnetite sludge.

#### Inhibitor dilution
Auto-fill adds fresh water with no inhibitor, lowering nitrite/molybdate/phosphate. pH can drift.

#### Solids introduction
Makeup water brings hardness, chlorides, silica, iron, leading to deposits and corrosion.

**Operational symptoms:**
- Rising iron (often >0.5–1 ppm and trending up)
- Black magnetite in strainers/dirt separators
- Air binding / frequent venting
- Increasing ΔP across coils/plate HX
- Pump seal wear

### Internal cross-leak (process fluid into closed loop or vice versa)

**Examples:**
- Plate heat exchanger leak
- Coil leak
- Condenser tube leak between process and closed loop

**Impacts depend on what enters:**

**Glycol/process organics enter:**
- Fuels bacteria, increases biofilm risk
- Can destabilize pH

**Chlorides/brines enter:**
- Raises conductivity, pitting risk
- Can attack stainless components depending on grade

**Acids/caustics enter:**
- pH swings cause rapid corrosion or precipitation

**Oil enters:**
- Coats surfaces; inhibits passivation; creates under-deposit corrosion zones

**Operational symptoms:**
- Sudden conductivity change in a system that should be stable
- Inhibitor consumption spikes
- pH excursion
- Unusual odor/film
- Rapid fouling of strainers

**Practical rule:** In closed loops, any need for frequent makeup is a red flag; the loop should require little to none after commissioning.

## Treatment Program Consequences (Both Systems)

### Loss of Control Margin

Your setpoints are designed for normal loads. Leaks add unplanned loads that exceed:
- Oxidizer demand capacity
- Dispersant capacity
- Inhibitor film stability
- Blowdown capacity (open towers)

### Increased Under-Deposit Corrosion

Leaks often increase solids and organics, creating sludge and deposits. Under deposits:
- Create differential aeration cells
- Concentrate chlorides
- Promote MIC

### Monitoring Becomes Misleading

Routine tests can look "fine" while fouling grows because:
- Deposits form on surfaces, not in bulk water
- Biofilm consumes residual locally
- Intermittent leaks cause cyclic upsets

Trending and equipment KPIs become essential.

## Most Useful Early Indicators of a Leak

### Open towers
- Conductivity rising faster than normal (or unstable)
- Blowdown running more than expected
- Unexplained increase in chemical feed (biocide/inhibitor)
- Persistent foam
- ATP/HPC rising despite normal feed
- Increasing approach temperature

### Closed loops
- Any frequent makeup water usage
- Conductivity trending upward
- Inhibitor residual trending downward
- New air binding
- Rising iron / magnetite accumulation
- Increasing ΔP across strainers/HX

## What Treatment Programs Must Change When Leaks Exist

### Open towers (temporary stabilization only)
- Increase blowdown to control contaminants (until corrected)
- Increase biocide frequency/dose to maintain residual
- Increase dispersant/polymer to manage solids
- Add side-stream filtration if solids/organics elevated
- Monitor daily instead of weekly

### Closed loops (temporary stabilization only)
- Stop leak / isolate exchanger; otherwise chemistry cannot remain stable
- Restore inhibitor levels and pH after repairs
- Flush strainers; remove magnetite
- Consider cleaning/passivation if iron spiked

## Key Takeaway

Chemistry adjustments can reduce damage temporarily, but they rarely "solve" an ongoing leak. Fix the leak first—monitor conductivity trends daily, maintain biocide residuals above 0.5 ppm free chlorine in open towers, and investigate any makeup water usage in closed loops immediately.
