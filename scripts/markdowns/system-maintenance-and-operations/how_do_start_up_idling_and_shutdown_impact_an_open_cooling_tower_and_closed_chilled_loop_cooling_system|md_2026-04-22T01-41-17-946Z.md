# How do start up, shut down, and idling practices affect successful operations of open cooling tower and closed loop cooling systems?

## Executive Summary

Improper startup, shutdown, and idling practices can increase corrosion rates from 1–2 mpy to 5–10 mpy while reducing equipment life by 30–50%. Startup controls corrosion through passivation, shutdown controls biology through disinfection, and idling prevents stagnation through circulation management.

## Overview

The first 72 hours of startup create more corrosion damage than months of steady operation. During this critical window, fresh metal surfaces oxidize rapidly without protective films, while unstable chemistry fluctuations trigger scaling before inhibitors stabilize. Meanwhile, improper shutdown transforms cooling systems into bacterial amplification chambers that compromise both equipment and public health.

## Startup Practices

### Open Cooling Towers – Startup

Startup represents the highest corrosion-risk period for cooling towers. Fresh metal surfaces lack protective films, creating ideal conditions for rapid oxidation when exposed to oxygen and warm water. The chemistry remains unstable as alkalinity, hardness, and pH fluctuate wildly. This instability creates scaling risks before control systems stabilize.

The first 7–14 days provide a critical window for bacterial colonization and biofilm establishment. Construction debris, rust, and dirt mobilize throughout the system during initial operation.

**Pre-cleaning and passivation remove oils and debris before operation begins.** Nitrite or high-alkalinity passivation prevents flash rust formation. Without proper passivation, flash rust occurs within 24–72 hours of startup.

**Controlled chemistry ramp-up maintains specific parameters during the first 7–14 days:**

- pH: 8.5–9.0
- LSI: +0.2 to +0.5  
- Orthophosphate: 5–10 ppm
- Free chlorine: 0.5–1.0 ppm continuous or intermittent

Iron monitoring becomes critical during startup. Daily testing targets iron levels below 2 ppm by week two. Monitoring frequency increases from weekly to daily for pH, conductivity, iron testing, and biocide residual checks.

Gradual thermal loading prevents scaling before inhibitors stabilize. Immediate full thermal load increases scaling risk significantly.

Poor startup practices create persistent high iron levels (3–10 ppm), under-deposit corrosion, early tube pitting, and premature fill fouling.

### Closed Loop Systems – Startup

Closed loops depend heavily on correct initial chemistry establishment. Dissolved oxygen trapped during filling, residual mill scale and oil, improper inhibitor concentration, and glycol interference with passivation create major startup challenges.

**Oxygen control requires 24–48 hours of continuous circulation after filling.** High points need venting and air pockets require removal. Oxygen drives the initial corrosion spike that damages system components.

**Nitrite passivation uses 1,000 ppm sodium nitrite at pH 9.0–9.5 for 48 hours circulation.** Operators reduce nitrite to operating levels after passivation completes. Iron monitoring targets levels below 0.5 ppm after stabilization through daily testing for the first week.

Glycol addition timing matters significantly. Systems require passivation first, then glycol addition, because glycol slows oxide formation and interferes with passivation.

Poor closed-loop startup creates magnetite sludge formation, pump seal wear, plate heat exchanger plugging, and persistent corrosion above 3–5 mpy. Closed loops can remain unstable for months when startup procedures fail.

## Shutdown Practices

### Open Cooling Towers – Shutdown

Shutdown creates stagnant warm water conditions with loss of biocide residual, oxygen saturation, and debris accumulation. Two shutdown options address these challenges effectively.

**Wet layup works best for downtime under 30 days.** The process involves tower cleaning, shock disinfection with 5–10 ppm chlorine for 6 hours, basin sediment draining, and refilling. Maintenance requires 1–2 ppm free chlorine OR non-oxidizing biocide weekly with periodic circulation.

**Dry layup handles long-term shutdowns.** Complete cleaning and disinfection precede complete draining, sludge removal, and full surface drying. Vapor phase inhibitors protect metal when required. Dry systems eliminate Legionella growth risk entirely.

Shutdown failures lead to Legionella amplification, heavy biofilm development, severe restart corrosion, and basin pitting.

### Closed Loop Shutdown

Closed loops face less biological risk but remain corrosion-sensitive. Oxygen ingress during draining, inhibitor dilution, pH drift, and glycol degradation create shutdown challenges.

**Wet layup maintains filled systems with nitrite above 600 ppm and pH between 8.5–10.** Quarterly checking and oxygen entry minimization preserve system integrity.

**Drained systems require complete drying with desiccant, nitrogen blanket application, or vapor phase inhibitor addition.** Partial draining creates the worst-case scenario for corrosion.

Shutdown failures produce flash rusting, high restart iron levels, pump damage, and heat exchanger fouling.

## Idling Conditions

### Open Cooling Towers – Idling

Low-load operation creates specific problems often overlooked by operators. Reduced heat loads increase concentration cycles and scaling risk. Low flow promotes biofilm development while low oxidizer demand creates overfeed risks. Dead legs form in low-circulation areas.

Required adjustments include increased blowdown to prevent scaling, maintained oxidizer residuals, weekly pump exercise, fill inspection for slime, and avoiding complete stagnation beyond 72 hours.

### Closed Loop – Idling

Low circulation creates stratification while oxygen accumulates in high points. Sediment settles and bacteria grow in glycol systems during extended idling.

Adjustments include weekly pump operation, quarterly inhibitor testing, nitrite maintenance above 600 ppm, and keeping systems completely full.

## Risk Comparison and Impacts

| **Phase** | **Open Tower Risk** | **Closed Loop Risk** |
|-----------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Startup | High corrosion + biofilm | High corrosion spike |
| Shutdown | Legionella amplification | Oxygen corrosion |
| Idling | Slime + scale | Sludge formation |

Improper transitions increase biofilm thickness beyond 0.1 mm, causing 10–20% heat loss and 5–15% energy penalties on chillers.

## Key Takeaways

Monitor iron levels daily during the first week of startup, maintain nitrite above 600 ppm in closed loops, and circulate pumps weekly during idling periods. Never start systems without passivation, never shut down open towers without disinfection, and never allow partial draining of closed loops. Transition periods determine long-term system life more than steady-state operation, making proper chemistry adjustment, circulation management, and oxygen control critical in each operational phase.
