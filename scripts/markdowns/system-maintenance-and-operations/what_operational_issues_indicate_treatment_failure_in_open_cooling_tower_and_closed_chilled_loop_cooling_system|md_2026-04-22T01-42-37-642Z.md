# What operational issues indicate failure of a treatment program?

## Executive Summary

Treatment program failures show up as measurable system symptoms: rising approach temperatures, unstable water chemistry, elevated iron levels, and loss of biocide residuals. Open cooling towers typically fail through scale buildup and biofilm formation, while closed loops fail primarily from corrosion and magnetite sludge accumulation.

## Overview

A single lab result rarely tells the full story of treatment program failure. The most reliable indicators emerge from trended changes over weeks or months. Open cooling towers can lose 2-3°F of approach temperature performance before operators notice comfort issues, while closed loops can accumulate magnetite sludge for months before plugging becomes severe enough to trigger maintenance calls.

## Heat Transfer and Capacity Indicators

These symptoms usually point to scale, biofilm, or fouling in open cooling tower systems. Rising condenser approach temperature serves as the most sensitive early warning signal. A sustained increase of 2-3°F over baseline strongly indicates fouling problems developing in the heat exchanger.

Chillers running harder to maintain the same cooling load show up as higher kW/ton consumption and elevated condenser head pressure. Operators often notice increasing pressure drop across condensers or heat exchangers as deposition restricts flow passages.

Scale formation from hardness or phosphate control failure commonly causes these symptoms. Biofilm growth from biocide program failure creates similar heat transfer losses. Suspended solids control problems from inadequate filtration or blowdown issues also contribute to fouling.

## Water Quality and Chemical Control Indicators

Unstable conductivity or readings consistently above setpoint signal fundamental control problems. Blowdown valve failures, controller malfunctions, sensor fouling, or valve positioning issues disrupt the water balance that prevents scale formation.

Cycles of concentration drifting upward create dangerous conditions. Monitoring chloride levels or conductivity ratios reveals when systems concentrate beyond safe operating limits. Scale risk rises sharply as cycles increase beyond design parameters.

pH excursions outside target ranges drive either scale formation or corrosion. High pH promotes calcium carbonate scale precipitation, while low pH accelerates metal corrosion throughout the system.

Inhibitor residuals disappearing or fluctuating wildly indicate feed system problems. Phosphonate, phosphate, or polymer levels should remain stable when programs function correctly. Excessive chemical consumption without performance improvement suggests contamination, wrong feed points, or underlying fouling creating additional chemical demand.

## Corrosion Indicators

Iron concentrations persistently above 3-5 mg/L in recirculating water indicate active corrosion or deposit sloughing. Visible rust particles confirm metal loss occurring somewhere in the system.

Corrosion coupon rates provide quantitative measurement of metal loss. Steel corrosion rates exceeding 2-3 mils per year in open recirculating systems typically exceed acceptable limits, though specific targets vary by site requirements.

Rapid pitting development on coupons warns of localized corrosion that can cause sudden equipment failures. Rising turbidity with red or brown solids creates operational problems including basin sludge accumulation, plugged strainers, and fouled fill or spray nozzles.

## Biological Control Indicators

Loss of oxidizer residual at distant monitoring points shows biocide distribution problems. Chlorine or bromine levels should remain detectable throughout the system to prevent microbial growth.

Heterotrophic plate count bacteria exceeding 10,000-100,000 CFU/mL and trending upward indicates biological control failure. ATP measurements rising above baseline provide faster feedback than traditional culture methods.

Visible slime, algae growth, or offensive odors signal advanced biological fouling. Microbiological upsets frequently occurring after weekends or low-load periods suggest inadequate biocide residuals during stagnant conditions.

Legionella detection requires immediate corrective action regardless of concentration. Action thresholds depend on site-specific policies, but any detection should trigger program review and remediation.

## Closed Loop System Failures

Closed loops should maintain stable conditions indefinitely. Visible problems usually indicate significant treatment failures requiring immediate attention.

Iron concentrations rising above 0.5-1 mg/L and remaining elevated suggest oxygen ingress or inadequate inhibitor protection. Black magnetite sludge accumulating in strainers, air separators, or dirt separators confirms ongoing corrosion.

Pressure drop increases across coil circuits, plate-and-frame heat exchangers, or filtration equipment indicate particle accumulation from corrosion products. Frequent pump seal failures or valve sticking problems result from abrasive solids circulation.

Heat transfer problems manifest as coils failing to meet heating or cooling loads, uneven coil temperatures from partial plugging, declining chiller efficiency, or unexpected flow imbalances in hydronic distribution systems.

Chemistry control failures show up as pH drift outside target ranges, typically 8.5-10 for steel systems. Nitrite, molybdate, or phosphate residuals dropping below specification levels compromise corrosion protection. Conductivity increases suggest ongoing makeup water entry from system leaks.

## Critical Failure Thresholds

Specific operational changes should trigger immediate program evaluation. For open cooling towers, approach temperature increases exceeding 2-3°F from baseline, unstable conductivity control combined with rising cycles of concentration, iron levels above 3-5 ppm with increasing basin sludge, loss of oxidizer residual at monitoring points, or recurrent slime growth with rising ATP or bacteria counts all indicate program failure.

Closed loop systems require investigation when iron rises above normal baseline levels and stays elevated, magnetite sludge accumulates with rising pressure drops, pH or inhibitor residuals fall out of control with ongoing makeup water entry, new recurring plugging affects plate heat exchangers or coils, or frequent air binding suggests oxygen ingress.

## Common Root Causes

Open tower failures often trace to fouled or miscalibrated controllers and sensors, stuck blowdown valves, chemical feed pump failures or incorrect feed points, makeup water quality changes, poor filtration, or intermittent operation creating weekend stagnation.

Closed loop problems typically stem from makeup water leaks or active auto-fill systems, malfunctioning expansion tanks, air separator problems requiring frequent venting, inadequate initial passivation, or incorrect inhibitor dosages with incompatible glycol chemistry.

## Key Takeaways

Monitor approach temperatures weekly and investigate any sustained 2-3°F increase immediately. Test iron levels monthly, maintaining below 3-5 mg/L in open towers and 0.5-1 mg/L in closed loops. Verify oxidizer residuals daily at the farthest monitoring point from feed injection, replacing biocide feed systems that cannot maintain detectable residuals throughout the distribution.
