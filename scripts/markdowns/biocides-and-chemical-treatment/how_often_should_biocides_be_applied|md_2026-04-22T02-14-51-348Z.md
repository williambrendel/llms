# How often should biocides be applied?

## Executive Summary

There is no single universal interval that applies to every system. Biocide application frequency should be set by the water management program based on system type, operating schedule, environmental load, and measured control parameters like disinfectant residual and bacteria counts.

## Overview

A cooling tower running continuously in hot, humid conditions might need biocide every few hours, while a closed heating loop might go months between treatments. The difference comes down to microbial growth pressure - open systems face constant recontamination from air and makeup water, while closed systems operate in controlled environments. Public health guidance emphasizes that operation and maintenance frequency depends on tower design, cooling load, and environmental conditions.

## Set frequency by system type and risk

**Open recirculating cooling towers** represent the highest routine need for biocide application. These systems face constant exposure to airborne contamination and warm temperatures that accelerate microbial growth.

For oxidizing biocide approaches, continuous or near-continuous application during operation is typical. The goal is applying often enough to maintain a measurable disinfectant residual at the control point. Continuous feed is common when feasible.

Non-oxidizing biocide approaches typically use intermittent slug applications. These are applied on a repeating interval, commonly multiple times per week, to control organisms less susceptible to steady oxidant residual and to address biofilm risk. The interval gets adjusted based on monitoring results and system demand.

**Closed loop heating and cooling systems** have lower routine needs. Biocide is usually not fed on a fixed frequent schedule. Instead, apply as needed for commissioning, after major maintenance, after contamination events, or when monitoring indicates biological activity. Routine control is often achieved primarily through system integrity with low oxygen entry, filtration, and maintaining appropriate chemical conditions.

**Potable building water disinfection** follows site-specific requirements that are often regulated. Biocide application frequency is typically driven by utility residual and regulatory requirements. Building-added disinfection, when used, is generally event-driven for remediation or controlled to maintain a defined residual per the site plan and applicable rules.

## Choose the feed strategy first, then set the interval

**Constant feed** means continuous dosing while the system operates. Application is effectively constant, with adjustment based on measured residual and demand. This provides more stable suppression and less rebound between treatments. However, it requires reliable feed and monitoring equipment and can increase total chemical use in high-demand systems.

**Slug feed** involves intermittent dosing on a repeating interval plus event-based dosing. A common starting pattern in cooling towers is multiple times per week for non-oxidizers, with the exact interval set by trend data and label directions. This approach delivers rapid knockdown and proves useful after excursions and for deposit disruption when coordinated with dispersants. The downside is that concentration drops between doses, making regrowth possible if intervals are too long for system conditions.

## Use monitoring and control limits to decide frequency

Water management programs must establish control limits and response actions. Define what "in control" means through specific parameters: disinfectant residual at the control location, deposit and biofilm indicators, and bacteria trend indicators.

Adjust biocide frequency based on performance. Increase frequency when residual is not maintained, bacteria trends rise, or deposits increase. Decrease frequency only after sustained control is demonstrated.

Cooling tower operational factors that drive frequency include design, cooling load, and environmental conditions, which change the needed frequency of operation and maintenance activities. Sediment, biofilm, temperature, water age, and disinfectant residual are key growth drivers.

## Practical starting-point schedules for cooling towers

These are common starting points, not universal requirements. Always follow product label directions and your site water management plan.

**Oxidizing biocide programs** apply continuously or very frequently during tower operation. The objective is maintaining target disinfectant residual consistently.

**Non-oxidizing biocide programs** apply by slug dosing on a repeating cycle, commonly two to three times per week. The objective is periodic high exposure to supplement oxidant control and help manage biofilm.

**After events**, apply additional dosing following startup after shutdown, loss of disinfectant residual, high bacteria indicators or visible slime, and heavy process contamination or high solids loading.

## Key takeaway

Monitor disinfectant residual daily in operating cooling towers, maintain residual above 0.5 ppm free chlorine equivalent, and switch to twice-weekly non-oxidizing biocide applications when oxidant alone cannot control biofilm indicators.
