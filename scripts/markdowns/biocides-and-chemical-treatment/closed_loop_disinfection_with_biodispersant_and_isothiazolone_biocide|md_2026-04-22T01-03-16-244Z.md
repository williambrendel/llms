# How do you control biofilm in closed-loop systems with high pH and dissolved metals?

## Executive Summary

A two-step approach using biodispersant pre-treatment followed by isothiazolinone shock dosing can effectively control biofilm in closed-loop systems operating at pH 9 with high dissolved metals. The biodispersant strips protective biofilm while the non-oxidizing biocide delivers the kill, with both products requiring dose corrections for metal demand and alkaline conditions.

## Overview

Closed-loop systems running at pH 9 with elevated iron and copper create a perfect storm for biofilm growth. The alkaline conditions reduce biocide effectiveness by 30-40% while dissolved metals consume active ingredients before they reach target organisms. A biodispersant pre-soak followed by extended-contact isothiazolinone dosing breaks this cycle by first exposing protected microbial colonies, then delivering a sustained kill pulse that accounts for chemical demand.

## The Two-Product Strategy

**Biodispersants** target the protective matrix that shields microbial colonies from biocides. These specialized chemicals break down the polysaccharide and protein structures that bind biofilm to pipe surfaces. Without this pre-treatment, biocides often fail because they never reach the organisms hiding beneath the protective layer.

**Isothiazolinone blends** (CMIT/MIT) serve as the primary kill chemistry. Unlike oxidizing biocides that react instantly with metals, isothiazolinones penetrate biofilm and provide sustained antimicrobial activity. A typical industrial formulation contains 1.11% CMIT and 0.39% MIT, delivering 0.127 pounds of active ingredients per gallon.

## Initial Clean and Kill Protocol

**Step A: Biodispersant Strip**

The pre-soak targets 1.5 gallons of biodispersant per 10,000 gallons of system volume. Circulate for 4-8 hours at design flow, purging low-flow stubs when possible. This aggressive film detachment reduces the organic demand on the subsequent biocide dose.

**Step B: Isothiazolinone Shock**

Label shock ranges for fouled closed-loop systems call for 8 gallons per 10,000 gallons of system volume. Maintain circulation for 6-8 hours minimum. The extended contact time compensates for reduced efficacy at pH 9.

## Chemical Demand Corrections

**pH Impact**

CMIT/MIT efficacy decreases as pH approaches 9. Choose the upper half of the label shock range and extend contact time when operating near pH 9.

**Metals Demand**

Dissolved iron and copper catalytically deactivate isothiazolinones. While labels don't publish stoichiometric deactivation factors, field experience suggests adding 2-3 ppm active ingredient per 1 ppm dissolved iron and 3-5 ppm active per 0.1 ppm dissolved copper.

**What it measures:** The additional biocide needed to overcome metal interference

Formula:
```
Dose correction = Base dose + (2-3 × Fe ppm) + (30-50 × Cu ppm)
```

**Example:** A system with 1 ppm iron and 0.1 ppm copper requires 2-3 ppm extra active for iron plus 3-5 ppm for copper, effectively increasing the base dose by 30-40%.

## Maintenance Program

**Biodispersant Pulse**

Apply 0.5 gallons per 10,000 gallons weekly for 4-8 hours of circulation. Time this pulse 24-48 hours before the biocide treatment to maximize penetration.

**Isothiazolinone Maintenance**

Label maintenance ranges from 4.5-28 fluid ounces per 1,000 gallons weekly. At pH 9 with chronic metals, run the upper-mid range (20-28 fl oz/1,000 gal) until iron and copper are controlled, then reduce dosing based on monitoring data.

## Mechanical Support Systems

**Side-Stream Filtration**

Install filtration for 5-10% of system flow using 5-10 micron nominal elements. Run for 1-2 complete turnovers during and after the first two treatment pulses. This removes iron fines and bio-solids so the biocide contacts biology rather than rust particles.

**Dead-Leg Management**

Temporarily open normally closed branches during treatment pulses. Stagnant areas harbor biofilm and reduce overall treatment effectiveness.

**System Integrity**

Air separators, proper expansion tank charge, and elimination of vacuum makeup prevent oxygen ingress that drives corrosion and provides electron acceptors for microbial growth.

## Monitoring and Targets

**Critical Parameters**

Test total plate count, Pseudomonas, total and dissolved iron, and copper before and after each treatment. During pulses, verify flow throughout the system and log chemical consumption to prevent phantom dosing.

**Acceptance Criteria**

Target steady-state levels of TPC and Pseudomonas below 100 CFU/mL, iron under 0.3 ppm, and copper below 0.05 ppm. Exceeding these metals targets indicates ongoing corrosion that will consume biocide and support microbial growth.

## Field Testing Considerations

No colorimetric field test exists for isothiazolinones in high-metals water. Use the Lovibond/Kathon field kit for approximate dosing checks, but run a system blank and subtract background interference. Verify critical treatments with laboratory LC-UV analysis when iron and copper demand is high.

## Key Takeaways

Test total plate counts and dissolved metals weekly during the first month, then monthly once targets are achieved. Maintain iron below 0.3 ppm and copper under 0.05 ppm to prevent chronic biocide loss. Consider passivation with alkaline cleaner if iron continues rising above 1 ppm despite treatment, as this indicates active corrosion that no biocide program can overcome.
