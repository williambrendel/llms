# How often should biocides be applied to open cooling tower and closed loop systems?

## Executive Summary

Open cooling towers require continuous or daily-to-weekly biocide applications due to constant biological pressure, while closed loops need only quarterly-to-annual treatments when properly maintained.

## Overview

A cooling tower pulling air through millions of gallons of warm water faces the same biological challenge as a stagnant pond—except worse, because it never stops inoculating itself with airborne microbes. Meanwhile, a truly closed loop system operates more like a sealed medical IV bag, where contamination events are rare and controllable. The difference in biocide frequency between these systems can span from daily dosing to annual maintenance.

## Open Recirculating Cooling Towers

Open cooling towers face the highest biological pressure of any industrial water system. The combination of warm water, continuous air contact, and nutrient availability creates ideal conditions for microbial growth.

### Oxidizing Biocides

**Continuous feed with automated control** represents the most effective approach for oxidizing biocides like chlorine, bromine, chlorine dioxide, or monochloramine. This method maintains a stable disinfectant residual that responds immediately to biological challenges.

When continuous feed isn't feasible, **slug or intermittent oxidizer applications** occur **daily to several times per week**. Tower systems experience continuous re-seeding from airborne microbes and fluctuating oxidant demand from organic loads.

The final frequency depends on residual stability and biological indicators rather than a fixed schedule. Systems monitor disinfectant residual stability, ORP/ppm trends, BART/HPC results, visible slime formation, filtration performance, and Legionella indicators to determine optimal dosing intervals.

### Non-Oxidizing Biocides

Non-oxidizing biocides like glutaraldehyde, isothiazolin, DBNPA, and THPS typically require **1-2 applications per week** as shock treatments. Programs often alternate or rotate different non-oxidizing biocides to improve control when biofilm pressure increases.

Some exceptionally clean and stable systems operate with **biweekly dosing**, while others require **2-3 treatments per week** during warm seasons, high-load periods, or after system upsets.

### Offline Cleaning and Disinfection

Beyond routine biocide feeding, cooling towers require **offline cleaning and disinfection at least annually** as part of comprehensive water management programs. This intensive treatment addresses accumulated biofilms and deposits that routine biocide applications cannot penetrate.

## Closed-Loop Systems

Closed-loop cooling and heating systems operate under fundamentally different biological conditions. When properly maintained with minimal makeup water, low oxygen ingress, and effective filtration, these systems experience dramatically lower biological pressure.

### Startup and Commissioning

New or reopened closed loops receive biocide treatment during initial cleaning, conditioning, and stabilization. The frequency during this phase depends on the specific cleaning protocol and corrosion inhibitor program being implemented.

### Routine Operation

Well-maintained closed loops typically require only **infrequent "as-needed" biocide dosing** based on monitoring results. Many stable programs settle into **quarterly to semi-annual** treatments.

Systems requiring more frequent dosing usually indicate underlying problems: excessive makeup water addition, oxygen ingress, glycol degradation, dead legs, or stagnation zones.

### Event-Driven Treatment

Closed loops require immediate biocide treatment after **significant makeup water addition**, **system leaks or repairs**, **extended stagnation periods**, or **detected microbial spikes**. These events introduce contamination that the system's normal biological controls cannot handle.

### Monitoring and Control

Regular bacterial counting and other key performance indicators drive dosing decisions in closed loops. This monitoring-based approach prevents both under-treatment and unnecessary chemical addition.

## Key Takeaway

If a "closed" loop system needs monthly biocide treatment, the real problem lies with **makeup water ingress**, **oxygen infiltration**, **dead legs**, **deposit accumulation**, or **nutrient sources**—fix these root causes before increasing biocide frequency. Monitor bacterial counts weekly in cooling towers and monthly in closed loops, maintaining oxidizing biocide residuals of 1-3 ppm free chlorine equivalent in towers, and consider rotating between glutaraldehyde and isothiazolin-based non-oxidizers for enhanced biofilm control.
