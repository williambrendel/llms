# What is Hypobromous Acid and How Does It Work in Cooling Towers?

## Executive Summary

Hypobromous acid (HOBr) is a fast-acting oxidizing biocide that excels in alkaline cooling tower systems where traditional chlorine loses effectiveness, working optimally at pH 7.5–9.0 while being less corrosive and producing fewer harmful byproducts.

## Overview

Most cooling towers operate at pH levels above 8, where chlorine becomes nearly useless as a biocide. Hypobromous acid solves this problem by maintaining its killing power even at pH 9, making it particularly valuable for hot water systems where biofilm control matters most. The compound can eliminate dangerous bacteria like *Legionella* in conditions where chlorine would fail completely.

## Chemical Properties and Mechanism

Hypobromous acid shares structural similarities with hypochlorous acid (HOCl) but demonstrates superior performance characteristics in cooling water applications. The uncharged molecular form penetrates microbial cells effectively, where it oxidizes critical cellular components including enzymes, DNA, and cell membranes.

This oxidation process destroys cells rapidly, with particular effectiveness against biofilms that protect bacteria in cooling systems. The compound maintains this activity across a broader pH range than chlorine-based alternatives, making it especially valuable in systems that naturally drift toward alkaline conditions.

## Production Methods

**What it measures:** The chemical conversion of sodium bromide and bleach into active biocide

Formula:
```
NaBr + NaOCl + H₂O → HOBr + NaCl + NaOH
```

**Example:** When 42% sodium bromide solution mixes with 12% sodium hypochlorite (standard bleach), the reaction occurs instantly in tower water at pH 7–9, producing hypobromous acid ready for immediate biocidal action.

Larger cooling systems typically use this NaBr plus bleach activation approach because it costs less and handles more safely than bromine tablets. Smaller systems often rely on pre-stabilized products sold at 7% or 10% activity levels.

## Application Guidelines

The dosing strategy depends on system size, contamination levels, and operational preferences. Continuous feeding maintains a steady residual that prevents microbial growth, while slug dosing delivers concentrated treatment that penetrates established biofilms.

| Purpose | Dosage of HOBr | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Continuous Feed | 0.5–2.0 ppm as Br₂ | Maintains residual |
| Slug Dose | 3–10 ppm as Br₂ | 1–2× per day, ~30–60 min |
| NaBr Feed Rate | ~10–50 ppm as product | Depends on system size & demand |

Systems using the NaBr activation method typically feed sodium bromide at 10–50 ppm as product, with the exact rate determined by system volume and biocidal demand.

## Target Organisms

Hypobromous acid demonstrates broad-spectrum antimicrobial activity against the primary threats in cooling water systems. It kills bacteria including *Legionella*, which poses serious health risks in cooling tower aerosols. The compound also eliminates algae that can clog heat exchangers and provide nutrients for bacterial growth.

Fungi and established biofilms respond particularly well to hypobromous acid treatment. Biofilms create protective matrices that shield bacteria from many biocides, but HOBr's penetrating ability overcomes this resistance effectively.

## System Compatibility and Advantages

The compound works well with most scale and corrosion inhibitors used in cooling water treatment programs. This compatibility simplifies chemical management and reduces the risk of adverse interactions that could compromise system performance.

Hypobromous acid produces fewer chlorinated organic compounds compared to free chlorine, reducing environmental concerns and regulatory complications. Its lower corrosivity toward metals extends equipment life while maintaining effective biocidal performance.

## Key Takeaways

Monitor hypobromous acid residuals daily using DPD colorimetric tests, maintaining 0.5–2.0 ppm for continuous programs or achieving 3–10 ppm during slug doses. Systems operating above pH 8.5 should consider switching from chlorine to hypobromous acid for reliable *Legionella* control. Implement automated NaBr/bleach feed systems for facilities using more than 50 gallons of biocide monthly to reduce costs and improve safety.
