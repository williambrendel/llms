# How Do You Disinfect Cooling Towers for Legionella Without Shutting Down the System?

## Executive Summary

Online disinfection using stabilized bromine and biodispersant eliminates Legionella from operating cooling towers in 8-12 hours, achieving nondetect levels (<0.1 CFU/mL) without system shutdown.

## Overview

Most cooling tower Legionella disinfection requires shutting down the entire system—but that's not always possible when buildings need continuous cooling. The stabilized bromine approach maintains 25-50 ppm free bromine for 6 hours while the system keeps running, reaching the same kill rates as offline methods. The secret lies in the chemical sequencing: biodispersant first to crack open protective biofilms, then sustained bromine contact to penetrate where Legionella colonies actually hide.

## Target Pathogen and System Requirements

This protocol specifically targets Legionella pneumophila in open, recirculating cooling towers. The system must maintain normal thermal load during treatment to ensure proper chemical circulation and contact time. Nalco Water's stabilized bromine chemistry provides the foundation, combining hypobromous acid (HOBr) as the primary killing agent with polymeric biodispersants for biofilm penetration.

The key chemical players each serve distinct roles. Stabilized bromine delivers HOBr, the active biocide that penetrates cell walls and destroys Legionella. Polymeric biodispersant loosens and removes protective biofilm matrices where bacteria embed and multiply. Sodium hypochlorite acts as the activator, oxidizing sodium bromide to form HOBr directly in the system.

## Pre-Treatment Preparation

Successful disinfection starts 24 hours before chemical application. Operators must notify all stakeholders—operations, EH&S, and building management—since the process affects water quality and discharge. The system needs normal thermal load to maintain proper circulation patterns that distribute chemicals evenly throughout the tower.

Reducing blowdown rates temporarily extends chemical contact time by preventing premature dilution. Baseline measurements establish the starting point: system volume for accurate dosing calculations, pH levels (ideal range 7.0-8.3), existing free and total bromine residuals, and microbial counts including Legionella antigen tests reading 0.1 CFU baseline levels.

## Biofilm Conditioning Phase

The first 90-120 minutes focus entirely on biofilm disruption. Biodispersant doses at 100-200 ppm, fed directly to the tower basin or recirculation line suction. This step cannot be rushed—the polymeric dispersant needs time to penetrate biofilm matrices and expose embedded Legionella colonies that standard biocides cannot reach.

During this conditioning phase, no bromine enters the system. The biodispersant works mechanically, breaking apart the protective slime layers that bacteria use as shields. Operators monitor circulation to ensure even distribution throughout the cooling loop.

## Bromine Shock Treatment

After biofilm conditioning, stabilized bromine application begins with a target of 25-50 ppm free HOBr maintained for at least 6 hours. Free bromine measurement uses standard DPD chlorine test kits with bromine correction factors, adjusted for system pH levels.

pH control becomes critical during this phase. System pH must stay below 8.5 to maximize biocide effectiveness—higher pH levels convert active HOBr to less effective hypobromite ions. Hourly monitoring catches any drops below 25 ppm, triggering immediate re-dosing to maintain lethal concentrations.

The 6-hour contact time reflects the biology of Legionella destruction. These bacteria resist many disinfectants, but sustained bromine exposure at 25+ ppm overwhelms their cellular defenses and achieves the logarithmic kill rates needed for nondetect results.

## System Normalization

Post-treatment flushing removes residual chemicals and dead biomass. Operators flush until tower conductivity matches makeup water conductivity, indicating complete turnover. A follow-up biodispersant slug at 25-50 ppm helps remove mobilized biofilm debris loosened during the main treatment.

Normal operations resume with standard blowdown and makeup rates restored. The cooling tower returns to its regular treatment program: scale inhibitors, corrosion inhibitors, and maintenance biocide dosing at routine levels.

## Verification and Monitoring

Testing at 24 and 48 hours confirms treatment effectiveness. Both laboratory culture methods and rapid field tests measure Legionella levels against pre-treatment baselines. Success means Legionella nondetect (<0.1 CFU/mL) and total bacterial counts below 10⁴ CFU/mL.

## Safety and Regulatory Requirements

Chemical handling requires full PPE: gloves, goggles, face shield, and chemical apron. Mechanical ventilation becomes essential when adding concentrated bleach or dispersants to prevent vapor exposure. Spill kits and emergency wash stations must be positioned nearby before chemical application begins.

Discharge compliance varies by location—many jurisdictions limit bromine residuals in cooling tower blowdown. Operators notify local authorities when biocide concentrations exceed permitted discharge levels, coordinating timing with wastewater treatment facilities if necessary.

## Key Success Factors

Online disinfection works when operators control four critical variables: chemical sequencing (biodispersant before bromine), pH management (below 8.5 throughout treatment), accurate contact time (full 6 hours at target concentrations), and consistent monitoring (hourly bromine checks with immediate correction). Skip any of these steps, and Legionella populations survive to recolonize the system within weeks.
