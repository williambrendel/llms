# How do you know if your cooling tower or closed-loop system is over or under-treated?

## Executive Summary

Undertreatment shows up as rising iron levels, biological growth, and heat transfer loss, while overtreatment causes accelerated corrosion from excess oxidizers, filter plugging from polymer overdose, and unnecessary chemical costs that can worsen the problems you're trying to solve.

## Overview

A cooling system that looks clean on the surface can still be failing catastrophically underground. Iron concentrations climbing from 0.5 to 2.0 ppm over three months signal corrosion damage that costs far more than the chemicals you're trying to save. The tricky part: both too little and too much treatment create similar symptoms, making proper diagnosis critical before you adjust anything.

## Open Cooling Tower Warning Signs

### Undertreatment Symptoms

**Biological Control Breakdown**
Rising ATP, HPC, or dip slide trends with frequent "rebounds" after biocide dosing indicate resistant biofilm formation. You'll see visible slime, algae, or detect musty odors around fouled fill media. Clogged strainers and nozzles follow quickly. The system shows increasing differential pressure across heat exchangers and filters while losing approach temperature control—direct evidence of heat transfer loss.

Your oxidant demand climbs while you struggle to maintain residual levels at both the basin and remote monitoring points throughout the system.

**Scale and Deposition Issues**
Approach temperatures rise as flow rates drop and fan/pump energy consumption increases. White or tan hard deposits accumulate on corrosion coupons and heat exchanger surfaces. Calcium hardness and alkalinity measurements show frequent excursions or cycles of concentration climb too high.

Turbidity and total suspended solids increase even without changes to filtration schedules or basin cleaning frequency.

**Corrosion Acceleration**
Iron trends climb steadily, with copper following if present in the system metallurgy. Water takes on a rusty appearance or leaves staining. Corrosion coupons show rates above target thresholds while pinhole leaks multiply, especially in low-flow areas or spots with under-deposit corrosion.

Pitting increases on coupons even when uniform corrosion rates in mils per year appear acceptable.

### Overtreatment Problems

**Oxidizer and Biocide Excess**
Persistent high oxidizer residuals beyond required levels accelerate corrosion on mild steel and galvanized components. Elastomers and protective coatings degrade faster while strong oxidant odors off-gas from the system.

Repeated low pH events from acid feed overshoot or rapid pH swings from poor control system response create additional stress.

**Chemical Program Imbalances**
Excess inhibitor feed increases total suspended solids and turbidity, causing deposition and filter plugging. This problem hits phosphate and zinc programs particularly hard when control systems malfunction.

Excess dispersant can temporarily mobilize existing deposits, causing spikes in turbidity and filter loading. Short-term increases are expected and normal, but persistent problems indicate overdosing.

The operational impact compounds: unnecessary chemical costs, higher corrosion risk especially on galvanized components and zinc-based metallurgy, plus deposit and solids issues that paradoxically create conditions for worse biofouling.

## Closed-Loop System Indicators

### Undertreatment in CHW/HW Systems

**Corrosion and Oxygen Problems**
Iron and copper concentrations rise while black sludge and magnetite accumulate, plugging strainers frequently. Nitrite-based programs show nitrite depletion while other inhibitor chemistries display unstable residual concentrations.

Pitting risk increases from microbiologically influenced corrosion and under-deposit attack, even when bulk water analysis appears clean.

Evidence of makeup water addition reveals the system isn't truly closed: frequent pressure drops, recurring venting requirements, and persistent air handling issues.

**Biological and Heat Transfer Loss**
Bio slime accumulates in system low points alongside black deposits and foul odors. Bacterial counts spike repeatedly after biocide treatment cycles.

BART tests and anaerobe indicators turn positive for iron-reducing bacteria (IRB) and sulfate-reducing bacteria (SRB), especially when iron is already present.

Coil fouling reduces temperature differentials while pump head and energy consumption rise from deposit formation throughout the system.

The operational risk escalates quickly: microbiologically influenced corrosion and under-deposit pitting increase as deposits mature and become difficult to remove without aggressive cleaning procedures and extended downtime.

### Overtreatment in Closed Systems

**Inhibitor Imbalances**
Nitrite concentrations too high increase chemical costs while potentially supporting certain bacterial populations under specific conditions. Total dissolved solids rise when systems use indirect control methods like conductivity monitoring rather than direct ppm measurement.

**Polymer and Biocide Excess**
Overfeeding polymers and other solids-control chemicals causes filter and strainer fouling plus deposition in low-velocity system areas.

Too frequent or high-dose biocide application in truly closed loops can damage seals and elastomers depending on chemistry selection. Some biocides disrupt passivation films on metal surfaces.

Frequent biocide use often indicates you're treating symptoms while the root cause—makeup water addition and oxygen ingress—continues uncorrected.

## Taking Action

Monitor iron and copper trends monthly rather than waiting for visible problems—concentrations doubling over 90 days demand immediate investigation. Test biological indicators like ATP or HPC weekly during summer months when conditions favor growth.

For open towers, maintain oxidizer residuals between 0.5-1.0 ppm free chlorine equivalent while keeping pH between 7.8-8.2 for optimal inhibitor performance. Check approach temperature weekly as your primary heat transfer indicator.

For closed loops, verify makeup water addition stays below 2% annually and maintain inhibitor residuals within manufacturer specifications—typically 1,500-3,000 ppm nitrite or equivalent for other chemistries. Address any oxygen ingress sources immediately rather than increasing biocide frequency.
