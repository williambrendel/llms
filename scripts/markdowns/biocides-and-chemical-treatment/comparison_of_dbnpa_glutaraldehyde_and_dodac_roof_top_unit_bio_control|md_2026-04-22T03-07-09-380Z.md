# Which biocide works best for rooftop cooling units: DBNPA, glutaraldehyde, or DODAC?

## Executive Summary

Glutaraldehyde delivers the most effective Legionella control for rooftop cooling units when you can guarantee hours of contact time, while DBNPA provides rapid knockdown but requires pairing with residual treatments, and DODAC performs poorly against Legionella with problematic foaming.

## Overview

Rooftop cooling units create a perfect storm for biocide selection. These systems cycle between wet and dry periods, leaving gaps where bacteria can rebound. A single rooftop unit can spray mist containing 10,000+ CFU/mL of Legionella across building air intakes if the wrong biocide fails during a dry cycle.

## Mechanism Differences Drive Performance

Each biocide attacks microorganisms through fundamentally different pathways. DBNPA launches rapid electrophilic attacks on cell enzymes and proteins, delivering fast kills but hydrolyzing quickly in water. Glutaraldehyde cross-links proteins and penetrates biofilms when given adequate contact time. DODAC disrupts cell membranes through cationic surfactant action while creating strong surface adsorption.

These mechanisms directly determine how each performs in wet/dry intermittent operation. DBNPA excels at rapid knockdown when systems wet up but provides very short residual protection through dry periods. Glutaraldehyde works effectively in intermittent operation if you secure hours of contact during wet cycles, with better persistence than DBNPA. DODAC shows some residual film on surfaces but delivers weak performance in high-load, Gram-negative-dominant systems while creating problematic foam on rooftops.

## Legionella Control Effectiveness

**What it measures:** Comparative kill rates against Legionella bacteria in planktonic and biofilm states

Formula:
```
Effectiveness = (Initial CFU - Final CFU) / Initial CFU × 100%
Contact time and dose-dependent for all biocides
```

**Example:** DBNPA at 5 ppm achieves 99.9% planktonic Legionella kill in 30 minutes, but counts rebound to 10,000+ CFU/mL within 48 hours without residual protection.

DBNPA delivers high rapid planktonic kill but limited biofilm impact. Counts often rebound without a residual or film-penetrating partner. Glutaraldehyde provides high effectiveness against both planktonic and biofilm Legionella when dosed correctly with proper contact time. DODAC shows low effectiveness against Legionella since quats generally perform weakly against Gram-negative bacteria and biofilm.

## Dosing and Chemical Requirements

| Parameter | DBNPA (pucks) | Glutaraldehyde | DODAC (Quat) |
|-----------|---------------|----------------|--------------|
| **Typical dosing (active)** | Slug: ~2–10 ppm for 2–4 h (pucks dissolve when wet) | Slug: ~100–300 ppm for 4–6 h; continuous: ~25–100 ppm where used | Slug/continuous: ~10–50 ppm (application-specific) |
| **Effective pH window** | ~5.0–8.5 (efficacy drops quickly above ~8.5) | ~5.5–9.0 (best near neutral to mildly alkaline) | ~6.0–10 (cationic; inactivated by anionic load) |
| **Residual / carryover** | Minimal (hours) | Moderate (often 1–3 days, condition-dependent) | Apparent residual on surfaces, but limited true biocidal value against Legionella |

DBNPA gets consumed by nucleophiles like sulfides and amines, creating short active life. Glutaraldehyde can react with amines and gets neutralized by sulfite. Under-dosing risks "fixing" biofilm rather than killing it. DODAC becomes inactivated by anionic dispersants and soaps, high organic load, and can foul with some polymers.

## Safety and Environmental Hazards

Handling hazards vary dramatically between products. DBNPA causes irritation and corrosion to eyes and skin, with dust exposure when handling pucks. Glutaraldehyde presents significant risks as a strong irritant and respiratory sensitizer with pungent vapor requiring tight PPE and ventilation. DODAC creates moderate skin and eye irritation with slippery surfaces but lower volatility than glutaraldehyde.

Mist and drift present the greatest rooftop-specific risks. DBNPA shows low to moderate mist hazard due to low volatility and quick hydrolysis, reducing persistence in aerosols while remaining irritant if inhaled near the source. Glutaraldehyde vapor and mist can sensitize airways, making any drift bypass through poor eliminators or damaged media a real worker exposure risk. DODAC binds to droplets and persists, causing respiratory and eye irritation downwind while foaming increases aerosol formation.

Environmental persistence differs significantly. DBNPA degrades to bromide and other byproducts with short persistence. Glutaraldehyde biodegrades over time but remains acutely toxic at discharge when concentrated. DODAC persists on solids with aquatic toxicity requiring strict discharge controls.

## Performance in Rooftop Applications

Foaming tendency creates operational problems specific to rooftop units. DBNPA and glutaraldehyde both show low foaming, while DODAC demonstrates moderate to high foaming that can elevate drift and mist formation.

The wet/dry cycling of rooftop units exposes each biocide's core limitations. DBNPA works great for rapid knockdown when wet but provides very short residual protection, meaning rebound becomes likely unless paired with a residual program. Glutaraldehyde performs moderately to highly if you secure hours of contact during wet cycles with better persistence than DBNPA. DODAC shows low to moderate effectiveness with some residual film on surfaces, but weak performance in high-load systems creates foam risk problematic for rooftops.

## Implementation Strategy

Glutaraldehyde emerges as the most effective non-oxidizing choice for Legionella control in rooftop units when you can enforce hours of contact and maintain tight drift control with proper PPE. However, it carries the highest worker safety risk if mist escapes containment.

DBNPA serves best as a rapid knockdown adjunct after wetting events but requires pairing with a residual and film-penetrating biocide to prevent rebound. The mist hazard remains relatively lower due to short persistence, but drift control remains essential.

DODAC plays a limited role and cannot serve as primary Legionella control. Its weakness against Legionella combined with foam elevation and environmental persistence restricts use to niche supplemental applications where foam and inactivation can be managed.

Successful rooftop biocide programs require monitoring CFU levels weekly during active seasons, maintaining contact times of 4-6 hours minimum for glutaraldehyde applications, and implementing engineering controls to prevent mist drift beyond 50 feet from units.
