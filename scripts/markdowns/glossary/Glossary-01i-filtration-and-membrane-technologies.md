# Filtration & Membrane Technologies — Q&A

**Summary**
Covers physical separation methods from granular media filtration through high-pressure membrane processes. Defines the full filtration spectrum — media, cartridge, bag, and membrane filtration — and the four membrane tiers (microfiltration, ultrafiltration, nanofiltration, reverse osmosis), plus forward osmosis. Explains what each technology removes and where it fits in a treatment train.

**Scope:** This glossary is organized for app seeding and user education. Definitions are intentionally short and practical. Legionella-prevention entries align with widely used building-water-management principles, and chemical entries use common active-ingredient names rather than brand names.

---

**Q:** What is the difference between microfiltration, ultrafiltration, nanofiltration, and reverse osmosis?

**A:** These are membrane filtration technologies of increasing tightness: microfiltration removes larger solids and bacteria; ultrafiltration also removes fine colloids and many viruses; nanofiltration removes many divalent ions; reverse osmosis removes nearly all dissolved salts.

---

**Q:** What is reverse osmosis?

**A:** A high-pressure membrane separation process that forces water through a semi-permeable membrane, removing most dissolved salts and many contaminants while concentrating the rejected substances as brine.

---

**Q:** What is filtration in water treatment?

**A:** The removal of suspended particles by passing water through a porous medium — the broad category that includes media filters, cartridge filters, bag filters, and membrane systems of all types.

---

**Q:** What is media filtration?

**A:** Filtration through a bed of granular material such as sand, anthracite, or garnet — removes suspended solids by physical straining and adsorption onto media surfaces. Widely used as a primary solids-removal step in conventional treatment.

---

**Q:** What is cartridge filtration?

**A:** Filtration through a replaceable cylindrical element — commonly used for final polishing, pre-treatment before membranes, or point-of-use protection. Cartridges are replaced rather than backwashed when they reach capacity.

---

**Q:** What is bag filtration?

**A:** Filtration through a fabric bag element that captures suspended solids — typically used for coarser solids removal and higher flow rates than cartridge filters, and commonly employed as pre-treatment or in industrial process water.

---

**Q:** What is membrane filtration?

**A:** The broad category of filtration using semi-permeable membranes to separate contaminants from water — ranges from microfiltration at the coarse end to reverse osmosis at the fine end, each removing progressively smaller particles and ions.

---

**Q:** What is forward osmosis, and how does it differ from reverse osmosis?

**A:** Forward osmosis uses the natural osmotic pressure difference between two solutions to draw water across a membrane — no high hydraulic pressure is required. It is used in some niche applications such as emergency water purification and concentration of dilute streams, but reverse osmosis is far more common for desalination.
