# Swamp Coolers and Direct Evaporative Cooling — Q&A

**Summary**
Covers direct and indirect evaporative cooling equipment used primarily in dry climates for residential, commercial, and spot-cooling applications. Defines cooler types and configurations, pad media types and their failure modes, and the water management practices — bleed-off, float valve operation, and seasonal startup and shutdown — that govern mineral control and biological safety. Emphasizes the connection between climate conditions (wet-bulb temperature, humidity) and cooling effectiveness.

**Scope:** This glossary is organized for app seeding and user education. Definitions are intentionally short and practical. Legionella-prevention entries align with widely used building-water-management principles, and chemical entries use common active-ingredient names rather than brand names.

---

**Q:** What is a swamp cooler?

**A:** A common term for a direct evaporative air cooler — a device that cools air by evaporating water into it.

---

**Q:** How does a direct evaporative cooler work?

**A:** Warm dry air is pulled through a wetted pad or media, water evaporates into the air, and the resulting air is cooler and more humid than the inlet air.

---

**Q:** What is the difference between direct and indirect evaporative cooling?

**A:** A direct evaporative cooler adds moisture directly to the supply air; an indirect evaporative cooler uses evaporation on one side of a heat exchanger so the supply air is cooled without gaining moisture.

---

**Q:** What is a two-stage evaporative cooler?

**A:** A unit that combines an indirect stage (no moisture added) followed by a direct stage, achieving greater cooling than either stage alone.

---

**Q:** When is evaporative cooling most effective?

**A:** In dry climates with low outdoor humidity — low wet-bulb temperatures allow greater evaporation and more cooling effect.

---

**Q:** What is saturation efficiency?

**A:** How closely the leaving air temperature approaches the theoretical evaporative cooling limit (the entering air wet-bulb temperature) — higher efficiency means better performance.

---

**Q:** What is a cooling pad?

**A:** A wetted porous medium through which air passes for evaporative cooling — the pad provides surface area for water-to-air contact.

---

**Q:** What is the difference between an aspen pad and a rigid media pad?

**A:** An aspen pad uses traditional wood fiber and is lower cost but less durable; a rigid media pad uses structured synthetic or cellulose channels that provide more consistent airflow and last longer.

---

**Q:** What is pad scaling?

**A:** Mineral deposits that accumulate on the cooling pad as water evaporates and leaves dissolved solids behind — reduces airflow and wetting uniformity.

---

**Q:** Why does pad scaling worsen over time?

**A:** As water evaporates, dissolved minerals concentrate and eventually precipitate onto pad surfaces. Without regular bleed-off, mineral buildup accelerates with each cycle.

---

**Q:** What is bleed-off in an evaporative cooler?

**A:** Controlled discharge of recirculating cooler water to limit the concentration of dissolved minerals in the reservoir — prevents excessive pad scaling and mineral carryover.

---

**Q:** Why is bleed-off important for evaporative cooler water quality?

**A:** Without bleed-off, minerals concentrate rapidly as evaporation removes pure water, leading to scale deposits on pads and potential mineral carryover into the conditioned space.

---

**Q:** What is mineral carryover?

**A:** Fine droplets or mineral particles carried from the wet pad section into the airstream and delivered to the conditioned space — can deposit on surfaces and equipment.

---

**Q:** What is face velocity?

**A:** The speed of air moving through the pad surface — too high a face velocity can carry water droplets into the airstream; too low reduces cooling effectiveness.

---

**Q:** What is seasonal startup for an evaporative cooler?

**A:** The steps needed to prepare the cooler for operation at the start of the warm season — including inspecting pads, cleaning the reservoir, checking the pump and float valve, and flushing the system before occupying the space.

---

**Q:** What is seasonal shutdown for an evaporative cooler?

**A:** Cleaning, draining, and preserving the cooler at the end of the cooling season — including removing and inspecting pads, draining the reservoir, and protecting the unit from weather damage.

---

**Q:** Why is seasonal shutdown important for Legionella risk management?

**A:** Stagnant water left in the reservoir over winter provides conditions for biofilm development, so thorough draining and cleaning before shutdown and before restart are both important.

---

**Q:** What is a float valve in a swamp cooler?

**A:** A mechanically actuated valve that maintains water level in the reservoir by opening to admit makeup water when the level drops and closing when the correct level is restored.

---

**Q:** What is a down-discharge cooler?

**A:** A roof-mounted evaporative cooler that supplies air vertically downward into ductwork or the space below.

---

**Q:** What is pad fouling?

**A:** Accumulation of dirt, biological slime, or mineral deposits on the pad that reduces airflow, wetting, and cooling effectiveness.

---

**Q:** When is indirect evaporative cooling preferred over direct?

**A:** When supply air humidity must be controlled — hospitals, data centers, and some manufacturing environments cannot tolerate moisture addition, making indirect cooling (which cools without adding humidity) the appropriate choice.

---

**Q:** Why does saturation efficiency matter when comparing evaporative coolers?

**A:** Because it determines how closely the cooler approaches its theoretical maximum cooling potential — a unit with 80% efficiency leaves significant cooling capacity on the table compared to one achieving 90%, especially in hot dry climates where the wet-bulb depression is large.

---

**Q:** What is the difference between a down-discharge and side-discharge evaporative cooler?

**A:** A down-discharge unit supplies cooled air vertically downward through the roof into ductwork below; a side-discharge unit supplies air horizontally and is typically used where vertical duct penetrations are not practical.

---

**Q:** How is pad fouling prevented in an evaporative cooler?

**A:** Through regular bleed-off to limit mineral concentration, periodic pad inspection and replacement, seasonal draining and cleaning, and in some cases pre-filtering the makeup water to reduce incoming solids and hardness.
