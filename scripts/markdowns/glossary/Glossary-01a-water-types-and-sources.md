# Water Types & Sources — Q&A

**Summary**
Covers classification of water by origin, intended use, and treatment state. Defines the full spectrum of water types from raw source water through potable, process, and utility water, as well as specialized categories — reclaimed, reuse, greywater, blackwater, stormwater, brackish, seawater, produced water, condensate, and distillate. Establishes the vocabulary for describing what kind of water is being handled before any treatment discussion begins.

**Scope:** This glossary is organized for app seeding and user education. Definitions are intentionally short and practical. Legionella-prevention entries align with widely used building-water-management principles, and chemical entries use common active-ingredient names rather than brand names.

---

**Q:** What is water treatment?

**A:** The controlled conditioning of water so it is safer or more useful for a specific purpose.

---

**Q:** What is raw water?

**A:** Untreated source water entering a plant, building, or process before any conditioning steps.

---

**Q:** What is potable water?

**A:** Water that meets quality standards for human consumption, including drinking and food preparation.

---

**Q:** What is the difference between potable, process, and utility water?

**A:** Potable water is intended for human consumption; process water is used directly in manufacturing or industrial operations; utility water supports operations like cooling, heating, or fire protection without direct product contact.

---

**Q:** What is feedwater?

**A:** Water entering a piece of equipment such as a boiler, cooling system, or treatment skid — the water that the equipment will condition or use.

---

**Q:** What is makeup water?

**A:** Fresh water added to a recirculating system to replace losses from evaporation, bleed, drift, or leaks.

---

**Q:** Why is makeup water important to track in recirculating systems?

**A:** Because every volume of makeup adds dissolved minerals that concentrate over time, directly driving scale, corrosion, and biological risk.

---

**Q:** What is reclaimed water?

**A:** Municipal or industrial wastewater that has been treated to a level suitable for beneficial reuse such as irrigation or cooling.

---

**Q:** What is the difference between greywater and blackwater?

**A:** Greywater is lightly contaminated wastewater from sinks, showers, and laundry; blackwater contains toilet waste or heavy organic contamination and requires more intensive treatment.

---

**Q:** What is produced water?

**A:** Water brought to the surface during oil and gas production, typically containing dissolved salts, hydrocarbons, and other contaminants.

---

**Q:** What is condensate in a water system context?

**A:** Liquid water formed when vapor cools and changes phase — for example, steam condensing in a heat exchanger or water vapor condensing in ductwork.

---

**Q:** Why is condensate valuable to recover in steam systems?

**A:** Condensate is already treated, hot, and nearly mineral-free — recovering it as feedwater reduces makeup water consumption, lowers chemical costs, and cuts the energy needed to heat cold makeup water.

---

**Q:** What is reuse water?

**A:** Water that has been used once and then treated or redirected for use again — distinct from reclaimed water in that it may be reused within the same facility or process without full municipal treatment.

---

**Q:** What is stormwater?

**A:** Rain or runoff collected from roofs, pavement, or land surfaces — often contaminated with sediment, nutrients, metals, and organic compounds depending on the catchment area.

---

**Q:** What is brackish water?

**A:** Water with dissolved salt content higher than freshwater but lower than seawater — typically 1,000 to 10,000 mg/L TDS. Common in estuaries, coastal aquifers, and some inland groundwaters.

---

**Q:** What is seawater in a water treatment context?

**A:** Ocean water with high dissolved salt content — typically around 35,000 mg/L TDS — requiring energy-intensive desalination such as reverse osmosis to produce usable freshwater.

---

**Q:** What is distillate?

**A:** Condensed purified water produced by distillation — essentially mineral-free because evaporation leaves dissolved salts behind and only pure vapor is collected and condensed.
