# Ion Exchange, Softening & Adsorption — Q&A

**Summary**
Covers chemical removal of dissolved ions and organic contaminants through exchange resins and carbon media. Defines ion exchange and its cation and anion variants, water softening, lime softening, electrodeionization, mixed bed deionization, activated carbon adsorption, and the difference between granular and powdered carbon. Also covers resin regeneration and the operational importance of softening before boiler and cooling system use.

**Scope:** This glossary is organized for app seeding and user education. Definitions are intentionally short and practical. Legionella-prevention entries align with widely used building-water-management principles, and chemical entries use common active-ingredient names rather than brand names.

---

**Q:** What is ion exchange?

**A:** A process where dissolved ions in water are removed by reversible exchange with ions bound to a resin bead — commonly used for softening and deionization.

---

**Q:** What is the difference between cation exchange and anion exchange?

**A:** Cation exchange removes positively charged ions such as calcium, magnesium, or sodium; anion exchange removes negatively charged ions such as chloride, sulfate, or silica in some configurations.

---

**Q:** What is water softening?

**A:** The reduction of hardness minerals — primarily calcium and magnesium — typically by ion exchange or lime precipitation, to prevent scale formation.

---

**Q:** What is lime softening?

**A:** A hardness-reduction process where lime and sometimes soda ash are added to water, precipitating calcium and magnesium as solids that can be settled and removed.

---

**Q:** What is activated carbon adsorption?

**A:** A treatment process that removes chlorine, organics, taste, odor, and some other compounds by adsorbing them onto the surface of carbon media.

---

**Q:** What is the difference between granular activated carbon (GAC) and powdered activated carbon (PAC)?

**A:** GAC is a loose-bed media used in fixed filter vessels for ongoing adsorption; PAC is finely divided carbon dosed directly into water for temporary or emergency adsorption treatment.

---

**Q:** How is an ion exchange resin regenerated?

**A:** By flushing the exhausted resin with a concentrated regenerant solution — sodium chloride brine for softening resins, acid for cation deionization resins, and caustic for anion deionization resins — which displaces the captured ions and restores exchange capacity.

---

**Q:** Why is water softening important before using water in boilers or cooling systems?

**A:** Because calcium and magnesium scale on heat-transfer surfaces acts as insulation, dramatically reducing efficiency and potentially causing overheating damage — softening prevents this by removing the scale-forming ions before they enter the system.

---

**Q:** What is electrodeionization (EDI)?

**A:** A continuous process that removes dissolved ions using a combination of ion exchange resin, ion-selective membranes, and an electric current — it regenerates the resin continuously with electricity rather than chemicals, making it well suited for producing high-purity water without chemical regeneration waste.

---

**Q:** What is mixed bed deionization?

**A:** A water purification method using a single vessel containing both cation and anion exchange resins mixed together — ion exchange reactions occur simultaneously, producing very high purity water with extremely low conductivity. Used as a final polishing step in high-purity water production.
