# Gas Transfer & Chemical Adjustment — Q&A

**Summary**
Covers processes that transfer dissolved gases out of water or chemically adjust water composition. Defines aeration, air stripping, degasification, and decarbonation for gas removal, and neutralization, chemical precipitation, crystallization, evaporation, and distillation for chemical adjustment and concentration. These processes are often used as pre-treatment steps or to manage the final concentration and composition of treatment streams.

**Scope:** This glossary is organized for app seeding and user education. Definitions are intentionally short and practical. Legionella-prevention entries align with widely used building-water-management principles, and chemical entries use common active-ingredient names rather than brand names.

---

**Q:** What is aeration?

**A:** The process of contacting water with air to strip volatile gases or to increase dissolved oxygen content.

---

**Q:** When is aeration used as a treatment step?

**A:** When dissolved gases such as carbon dioxide or hydrogen sulfide need to be stripped, or when water needs more dissolved oxygen — commonly used for groundwater treatment before distribution or further processing.

---

**Q:** What is air stripping?

**A:** A process that transfers volatile contaminants from water into air by maximizing water-to-air contact — used to remove volatile organic compounds, hydrogen sulfide, carbon dioxide, and other dissolved gases from groundwater and process water.

---

**Q:** When is air stripping used?

**A:** When contaminated groundwater contains volatile organics such as solvents, or when dissolved gases such as hydrogen sulfide or carbon dioxide need to be removed before further treatment or distribution — it is a cost-effective non-chemical removal method for volatile compounds.

---

**Q:** What is degasification?

**A:** The removal of dissolved gases from water — a broader term that includes aeration, air stripping, vacuum degasification, and membrane contactors, all aimed at reducing dissolved gas concentrations.

---

**Q:** What is decarbonation?

**A:** The specific removal of dissolved carbon dioxide from water — typically performed by aeration or packed tower stripping after lime softening or other processes that generate CO₂. Reduces corrosivity and raises pH.

---

**Q:** What is neutralization in water treatment?

**A:** Chemical adjustment of pH toward a target range by adding acid or base — used to correct process streams, treat acid or alkaline waste, and prepare water for downstream treatment steps that require a specific pH window.

---

**Q:** What is chemical precipitation?

**A:** The deliberate formation of insoluble solid compounds through chemical reaction so they can be separated from water by settling or filtration — used to remove metals, hardness, phosphate, and other dissolved substances that cannot be removed by filtration alone.

---

**Q:** What is crystallization in water treatment?

**A:** The controlled formation and growth of solid crystals from a supersaturated solution — used intentionally in processes like pellet softening and seed crystal reactors to precipitate scale-forming minerals in a vessel rather than on equipment surfaces.

---

**Q:** What is evaporation in water treatment?

**A:** The removal of water as vapor, concentrating the remaining dissolved substances in the liquid — used in zero liquid discharge systems and brine concentration processes where the goal is to reduce liquid volume and recover water or solids.

---

**Q:** What is distillation in water treatment?

**A:** Purification by evaporating water and then condensing the vapor — the condensate is highly purified because dissolved salts and most contaminants are left behind in the liquid phase. Used for high-purity water production and in some desalination systems.
