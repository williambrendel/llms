# Organic & Biological Constituents — Q&A

**Summary**
Covers the organic compounds and biological entities found in water systems that drive treatment decisions around disinfection, fouling, and corrosion. Defines organic matter, natural organic matter, microorganisms, biofilm, and slime — focusing on why biological growth in water systems is more resistant and dangerous than isolated organisms, and how slime and biofilm connect to operational and health risks.

**Scope:** This glossary is organized for app seeding and user education. Definitions are intentionally short and practical. Legionella-prevention entries align with widely used building-water-management principles, and chemical entries use common active-ingredient names rather than brand names.

---

**Q:** What is biofilm?

**A:** A structured layer of microorganisms encased in a protective slime matrix, attached to a wet surface — biofilm protects bacteria from disinfectants and promotes corrosion and fouling.

---

**Q:** Why is biofilm more difficult to control than free-floating bacteria?

**A:** Because the slime matrix physically shields organisms from disinfectants and can harbor pathogenic bacteria like Legionella.

---

**Q:** What is organic matter in water?

**A:** Natural or synthetic carbon-based compounds dissolved or suspended in water — includes humic substances, algal byproducts, industrial compounds, and microbial metabolites. Organic matter drives chlorine demand, promotes biological growth, and can form disinfection byproducts.

---

**Q:** What is natural organic matter (NOM), and why does it matter in treatment?

**A:** Naturally occurring dissolved organics from decayed plants and soils — it causes color and taste issues, exerts chlorine demand, and reacts with disinfectants to form potentially harmful disinfection byproducts such as trihalomethanes.

---

**Q:** What is a microorganism in water treatment?

**A:** Any microscopic living organism — bacteria, fungi, protozoa, algae, or viruses — that can be present in water systems. Treatment programs aim to control populations that cause disease, fouling, or corrosion.

---

**Q:** What is slime in a water system?

**A:** Soft, sticky biological or chemical deposits on wet surfaces — slime is often a visible indicator of biofilm development and can foul heat-transfer surfaces, restrict flow, and harbor pathogens including Legionella.
