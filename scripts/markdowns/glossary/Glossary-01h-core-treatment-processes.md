# Core Treatment Processes — Q&A

**Summary**
Covers the fundamental treatment operations used to remove contaminants and make water safer or more useful. Defines disinfection, sanitization, oxidation, reduction, coagulation, flocculation, sedimentation, and clarification — the building-block steps that underpin nearly all conventional water treatment trains from drinking water to industrial systems.

**Scope:** This glossary is organized for app seeding and user education. Definitions are intentionally short and practical. Legionella-prevention entries align with widely used building-water-management principles, and chemical entries use common active-ingredient names rather than brand names.

---

**Q:** What is the difference between disinfection and sanitization?

**A:** Disinfection inactivates or destroys disease-causing organisms; sanitization reduces microorganisms to a safer level but does not necessarily achieve complete destruction.

---

**Q:** What is coagulation?

**A:** Chemical destabilization of fine suspended particles so they can aggregate and be removed more easily from water.

---

**Q:** What is the difference between coagulation and flocculation?

**A:** Coagulation is the chemical destabilization step; flocculation is the gentle mixing step that follows, encouraging small destabilized particles to collide and form larger settleable flocs.

---

**Q:** What is clarification?

**A:** The overall process of removing suspended solids from water to produce clearer water, typically combining coagulation, flocculation, and sedimentation.

---

**Q:** What is oxidation as a treatment process?

**A:** A chemical reaction involving electron loss that is used in water treatment to destroy contaminants, convert soluble metals to filterable forms, break down organics, or inactivate microorganisms — chlorine, ozone, permanganate, and hydrogen peroxide are common oxidants.

---

**Q:** What is reduction in water chemistry?

**A:** A chemical reaction involving electron gain — used in treatment to convert oxidized contaminants to less harmful or more removable forms, such as reducing hexavalent chromium to trivalent chromium before precipitation.

---

**Q:** What is sedimentation?

**A:** The removal of suspended solids from water by allowing gravity to settle them to the bottom of a basin — typically follows coagulation and flocculation, and precedes filtration in conventional treatment trains.
