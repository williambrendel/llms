# Cooling Towers and Evaporative Cooling Systems — Q&A

**Summary**
Covers the design, operation, and water management of open recirculating cooling systems and evaporative cooling equipment. Defines tower types and configurations, key mechanical components (fill, drift eliminators, fans, basins, nozzles), and the core operating concepts that govern water chemistry — cycles of concentration, bleed, makeup, and conductivity control. Also addresses operational risks including fouling, scaling, dead legs, and Legionella vulnerability, as well as seasonal procedures such as layup and passivation startup.

**Scope:** This glossary is organized for app seeding and user education. Definitions are intentionally short and practical. Legionella-prevention entries align with widely used building-water-management principles, and chemical entries use common active-ingredient names rather than brand names.

---

**Q:** What is a cooling tower?

**A:** A heat rejection device that cools water mainly by evaporating a small portion of it into moving air.

---

**Q:** How does an open recirculating cooling system work?

**A:** Process heat is transferred to recirculating water, which is then pumped to an open tower where it is cooled by evaporation and air contact before returning to the process.

---

**Q:** What is the difference between a closed circuit cooler and an open recirculating cooling system?

**A:** In a closed circuit cooler, the process fluid stays inside a coil and never contacts the atmosphere — spray water and air cool the coil exterior. In an open system, the recirculating water is directly exposed to air in the tower.

---

**Q:** What is an evaporative condenser?

**A:** A heat rejection device that condenses a refrigerant inside a coil while water evaporates and air flows over the coil exterior — commonly used in ammonia refrigeration systems.

---

**Q:** What is the difference between a crossflow and counterflow cooling tower?

**A:** In a crossflow tower, air moves horizontally across the falling water; in a counterflow tower, air moves upward against the downward-flowing water. Counterflow designs are typically more thermally efficient.

---

**Q:** What is the difference between a forced draft and induced draft cooling tower?

**A:** A forced draft tower has fans at the air inlet pushing air in; an induced draft tower has fans at the air outlet pulling air through.

---

**Q:** What is a multi-cell cooling tower?

**A:** A tower with two or more independent cells, each with its own fill section and usually its own fan, allowing partial operation and more flexible capacity control.

---

**Q:** What is the cooling tower basin?

**A:** The collection sump at the base of the tower where cooled water collects before being pumped back to the process.

---

**Q:** What is the purpose of a side-stream filter on a cooling tower?

**A:** To continuously clean a portion of recirculating tower water by removing suspended solids, helping control fouling, biological growth, and deposit-related corrosion.

---

**Q:** What is fill in a cooling tower?

**A:** Structured material inside the tower that increases the contact area between water and air, maximizing heat transfer and evaporation.

---

**Q:** What is the difference between film fill and splash fill?

**A:** Film fill spreads water in thin sheets over closely spaced surfaces; splash fill repeatedly breaks water into droplets over bars or blocks. Film fill offers more surface area but is more prone to fouling in dirty or scaling water.

---

**Q:** What is a drift eliminator?

**A:** A component that removes entrained water droplets from the air stream leaving the tower, reducing water loss and preventing chemical-laden droplets from entering the environment.

---

**Q:** What is drift, and why does it matter?

**A:** Drift is small liquid droplets carried out of the tower with the discharge air. It represents water loss and, more importantly, a potential pathway for biological aerosols including Legionella.

---

**Q:** What is the difference between drift loss and evaporation loss?

**A:** Evaporation loss is water intentionally converted to vapor during the cooling process; drift loss is unintended carryover of liquid droplets with the leaving air.

---

**Q:** What is bleed in a cooling tower system?

**A:** The intentional discharge of a portion of recirculating water to prevent dissolved solids from concentrating too high as evaporation removes pure water.

---

**Q:** What is the difference between bleed and blowdown?

**A:** They are the same thing — bleed and blowdown are interchangeable terms for the intentional controlled discharge of recirculating water to manage dissolved solids concentration.

---

**Q:** What are cycles of concentration?

**A:** The ratio showing how much dissolved minerals have concentrated in recirculating water compared with makeup water — for example, 4 cycles means the recirculating water has four times the mineral content of the makeup.

---

**Q:** Why do cycles of concentration matter?

**A:** Higher cycles mean less water and chemical waste, but also greater scaling, corrosion, and biological risk — managing cycles is a core balancing act in cooling tower operation.

---

**Q:** What is a conductivity controller?

**A:** An instrument that automatically triggers bleed when recirculating water conductivity rises above a set point, maintaining dissolved solids within acceptable limits.

---

**Q:** What is the cooling tower approach?

**A:** The temperature difference between the cold water leaving the basin and the entering air wet-bulb temperature — a smaller approach means the tower is performing closer to its theoretical limit.

---

**Q:** What is the cooling tower range?

**A:** The temperature drop of the water as it passes through the tower — the difference between hot water entering and cold water leaving.

---

**Q:** What is wet-bulb temperature?

**A:** The lowest air temperature achievable by evaporative cooling at the current humidity level — the theoretical performance limit for evaporative cooling devices.

---

**Q:** Why is wet-bulb temperature important for cooling tower sizing?

**A:** Because a cooling tower can never cool water below the entering air wet-bulb temperature — it determines the minimum achievable cold water temperature.

---

**Q:** What is a makeup valve?

**A:** A valve that adds fresh water to the basin to maintain the correct water level as losses occur from evaporation, drift, and bleed.

---

**Q:** What is a dead leg in a cooling tower system?

**A:** A section of piping with little or no flow, which promotes stagnation, loss of disinfectant residual, and biological growth — a recognized Legionella risk location.

---

**Q:** What is an equalizer line?

**A:** A pipe connecting the basins of adjacent tower cells so water levels stay similar during operation.

---

**Q:** What is a variable frequency drive (VFD) on a cooling tower fan?

**A:** An electrical control that changes fan motor speed to match the actual cooling load, saving energy during lower-demand periods.

---

**Q:** What is tower layup?

**A:** The preservation steps taken when a cooling system will be idle for an extended time — including draining, cleaning, and often applying a biocide or corrosion inhibitor to protect inactive surfaces.

---

**Q:** Why is layup important for Legionella risk management?

**A:** Stagnant, untreated water during shutdown can allow biofilm to establish, meaning the system may harbor significant biological contamination when it restarts.

---

**Q:** What is passivation startup?

**A:** A carefully controlled startup treatment that forms a protective film on new or freshly cleaned metal surfaces before full chemical treatment begins.

---

**Q:** What is fill fouling?

**A:** Deposit buildup specifically on the fill surfaces of a cooling tower — caused by scale, biological slime, or particulate — which reduces airflow, water distribution, and heat transfer.

---

**Q:** What is channeling in a cooling tower?

**A:** Uneven water or air distribution that causes some sections of fill to do most of the work while others are bypassed, reducing effective cooling.

---

**Q:** What is tower recirculation of discharge air?

**A:** Re-entry of warm, saturated discharge air back into the tower air inlet — it raises the effective wet-bulb temperature and reduces cooling efficiency.

---

**Q:** What is a basin heater?

**A:** A heater used to prevent basin water from freezing in cold weather, protecting pumps and piping from ice damage.

---

**Q:** What is plume abatement?

**A:** Design features intended to reduce the visible cloud of condensed vapor leaving the cooling tower — often required in urban locations or near airports.

---

**Q:** What is condenser water?

**A:** Water used to remove heat from a refrigeration or HVAC condenser — it is typically cooled in a cooling tower and recirculated continuously.

---

**Q:** What is basin cleaning, and when should it be done?

**A:** The physical removal of solids, sludge, and biological material from the tower basin — should be done at least annually and whenever visual inspection shows significant accumulation.

---

**Q:** What is a suction screen?

**A:** A coarse screen protecting the pump suction inlet from large debris that could damage pump impellers or clog nozzles.

---

**Q:** What is a basin sweep?

**A:** A piping arrangement that circulates water through the basin to keep solids suspended rather than settling, reducing the rate of sediment accumulation.

---

**Q:** What is the difference between a natural draft and a mechanical draft cooling tower?

**A:** A natural draft tower relies on buoyancy and the stack effect of a tall hyperbolic shell to move air through without fans — common in large power plants; a mechanical draft tower uses fans to move air and is far more common in commercial and industrial buildings.

---

**Q:** How does a variable frequency drive save energy on a cooling tower?

**A:** By slowing the fan motor during cooler weather or lower load conditions — fan power drops roughly with the cube of speed, so even modest speed reductions produce significant energy savings.

---

**Q:** When is passivation startup performed on a cooling system?

**A:** When a system has new metal surfaces, after acid cleaning, or after any event that strips the existing protective film — it is performed before normal treatment chemicals are introduced at full operating concentrations.

---

**Q:** Why does cooling tower approach matter operationally?

**A:** A rising approach — with no change in load or weather — signals that the tower is losing efficiency, often due to fill fouling, nozzle clogging, or air distribution problems that need attention.

---

**Q:** Why is channeling a problem in a cooling tower?

**A:** Because water and air bypass large portions of the fill, reducing effective cooling surface and tower capacity — the system appears to be operating but is delivering less cooling than expected.

---

**Q:** Why does tower fouling matter beyond heat transfer?

**A:** Fouled surfaces — especially biological slime — create sheltered environments where Legionella and other organisms can grow, protected from both disinfectants and physical cleaning. Fouling is therefore both an efficiency and a safety issue.
