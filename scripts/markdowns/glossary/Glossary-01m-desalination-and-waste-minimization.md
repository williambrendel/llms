# Desalination & Waste Minimization — Q&A

**Summary**
Covers strategies for removing dissolved salts from water and minimizing liquid waste discharge. Defines desalination, zero liquid discharge, and brine — the three concepts that frame how treatment systems handle the concentrated waste streams produced when dissolved substances are removed from water. Relevant to water-scarce operations, tight discharge permits, and facilities seeking to close the loop on water use.

**Scope:** This glossary is organized for app seeding and user education. Definitions are intentionally short and practical. Legionella-prevention entries align with widely used building-water-management principles, and chemical entries use common active-ingredient names rather than brand names.

---

**Q:** What is desalination?

**A:** The removal of dissolved salts from saline or brackish water to produce freshwater suitable for use.

---

**Q:** What is zero liquid discharge (ZLD)?

**A:** A treatment strategy designed to eliminate liquid waste discharge entirely by recovering usable water and converting remaining contaminants to solid waste.

---

**Q:** What is brine in water treatment?

**A:** The concentrated salt solution produced as a reject stream by treatment processes such as reverse osmosis or as a byproduct of system concentration.

---

**Q:** Why would a facility pursue zero liquid discharge?

**A:** To eliminate regulated liquid discharge, comply with strict environmental permits, or recover water in water-scarce regions — ZLD is costly and energy-intensive but avoids discharge fees and regulatory exposure.
