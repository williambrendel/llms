# Nereus Sentinel: Detection and Predictive Intelligence

## Executive Summary

Nereus Sentinel continuously monitors water chemistry, cooling performance, and facility telemetry to detect operational drift, predict emerging risks, and translate every deviation into concrete financial impact before it becomes downtime, asset damage, or compliance exposure.

## Overview

Most facilities don't fail because data is missing. They fail because critical operational changes go undetected until systems are already outside safe or efficient operating conditions. A single out-of-range antiscale reading can project to over 1.4 million gallons of excess water and sewer consumption annually and over 1.5 million kWh of excess electrical use. Sentinel surfaces those costs the moment they begin to accumulate, not when they show up on a year-end invoice that no one traces back to its true source.

## Continuous Operational Vigilance

Sentinel analyzes live system behavior across water and energy infrastructure in real time, identifying patterns that traditional threshold alarms often miss. By combining statistical anomaly detection with thermodynamic, hydraulic, and chemistry-based system models, Sentinel understands not only when a system changes — but whether the change is operationally meaningful, physically abnormal, or economically inefficient.

The platform monitors live data from automated probes, meters, and water treatment controllers alongside manual test entries, tracking operational metrics including temperature, flow, and approach to wet bulb (WB) across cooling towers, closed loops, and humidifiers. The result is a unified picture of each system without requiring operators to pull data from multiple sources.

## Early Detection of System Drift

Most failures begin as small deviations. Sentinel continuously evaluates operating conditions against expected system behavior to surface hidden drift in scale formation, corrosion potential, biological activity, chemical feed performance, heat-transfer efficiency, water consumption, sensor reliability, and energy utilization. Operators gain early visibility into instability long before conventional alarms would trigger.

## Physics and Chemistry-Aware Intelligence

Unlike generic monitoring platforms, Sentinel is purpose-built for industrial water and energy systems. The platform applies domain-specific intelligence rooted in water chemistry equilibrium, heat-transfer dynamics, flow and pressure relationships, conductivity and concentration trends, thermodynamic behavior, energy efficiency modeling, and system-specific operational baselines. This enables Sentinel to distinguish between normal operational variability and truly abnormal system behavior — the difference between "a number changed" and "this matters."

## Comprehensive Data Capture

Sentinel replaces manual spreadsheets and paper records with a streamlined digital interface that feeds every test entry directly into the analytics engine. Built-in pop-ups provide test directions, reagent ordering prompts, and contextual guidance at each step, making accurate data collection accessible to operators without deep technical expertise.

The range of supported tests is comprehensive. Phase 1 covers core chemical parameters: pH, conductivity, calcium (Ca), magnesium (Mg), barium (Ba), sodium (Na), iron (Fe), zinc (Zn), chloride (Cl), sulfate (SO4), M alkalinity, silica (Si), phosphorus (P), nitrate (NO3), and fluorescence. Phase 2 extends into advanced chemistry and biological monitoring, including orthophosphate, phosphonate, molybdate (MoO4), nitrite (NO2), and free chlorine, along with a full suite of BART biological tests — SLYM, HAB, SRB, APB, DN, and ALGE — as well as dip slide testing.

## Process Capability and Forward Projection

Every parameter is tracked against a defined lower control limit (LCL) and upper control limit (UCL), and Sentinel continuously calculates process capability — the statistical measure of how consistently a system performs within those limits.

The target benchmark for healthy system performance is a total defect rate below 5%. When a parameter's defect rate climbs above that threshold, Sentinel quantifies the risk in concrete, financial terms rather than abstract alerts. For example, if tower conductivity runs below its lower control limit 23.7% of the time and above its upper control limit 13.7% of the time, the platform reports a combined defect rate of 37.48% and maps those deviations to their specific consequences: the below-LCL condition drives up water and sewer spend beyond budget, while the above-UCL condition signals expected scale accumulation that will increase energy use and elevate *Legionella* risk.

Sentinel also produces forward projections by analyzing historical trends and water chemistry patterns. Analytical views include histograms, trend charts, trends with forward projection, variable correlation analysis, and process capability reports against stated control ranges — letting facility teams see where performance is heading before a problem fully develops.

## Intelligent Alarming with Financial Impact

When a condition goes out of range, Sentinel generates a detailed alarm that functions as a full decision-support briefing, not a simple status flag. Each alarm communicates the current reading alongside the target value and both control limits; exactly how long the condition has been active and when it was first acknowledged; whether the condition poses an immediate risk of system damage; and a complete projection of the annual financial impact if left unresolved.

The financial projection breaks down across three dimensions: excess water consumption in gallons per year and its associated annual spend; excess sewer discharge in gallons per year and its associated annual spend; and excess electrical consumption in kilowatt-hours per year and its associated annual spend.

## Surfacing Forgotten Money

One of Sentinel's defining purposes is identifying and recovering forgotten money — the financial losses that accumulate quietly from unresolved water treatment issues and are almost never attributed to their true cause in standard facility accounting.

These losses fall into three categories. The first is electricity: scale deposits on heat exchange surfaces act as insulation, reducing thermal transfer efficiency and forcing systems to consume more energy to achieve the same cooling output. Even modest scale accumulation drives meaningful increases in electrical spend over time. The second is water and sewer: when systems operate outside their optimal cycles of concentration, they discharge more water than necessary through blowdown, wasting treated water, increasing sewer fees, and adding chemical costs to re-treat replacement water. The third is operations and maintenance: running equipment in suboptimal chemistry conditions accelerates wear, drives up the frequency of reactive repairs, shortens equipment lifespan, and increases the risk of unplanned downtime.

Sentinel automatically tracks all cost avoidances across each of these categories, logging every instance where an alarm was resolved before it accumulated to its projected annual impact. Facility teams get a running, year-end total of the value delivered by disciplined water and energy management — the data needed to demonstrate ROI clearly and concretely.

## Key Takeaways

Sentinel runs against a 5% defect-rate benchmark across every monitored parameter; anything above that threshold is quantified in annual water, sewer, and kWh impact rather than left as an abstract alert. Shifting from reactive maintenance to data-driven preventive maintenance is one of the most durable financial benefits the platform enables, and Sentinel's cost-avoidance tracking provides the year-end ROI evidence to back that case to facility leadership.
