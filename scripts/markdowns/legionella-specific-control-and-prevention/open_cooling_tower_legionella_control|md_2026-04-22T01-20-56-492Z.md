# How Do You Control Legionella in Open Cooling Tower Systems?

## Executive Summary

Control Legionella in cooling towers by maintaining effective disinfectant residuals throughout the system, controlling biofilm and scale buildup, and operating under a written water management program with defined monitoring points and corrective actions.

## Overview

A single cooling tower can circulate thousands of gallons per minute, creating ideal conditions for Legionella amplification if not properly managed. The CDC specifically requires automated disinfectant monitoring for these systems because manual control fails too often. Most control breakdowns happen not from lack of treatment, but from losing residual effectiveness where it matters most.

## Prevention Through System Control

### Maintain Effective Disinfectant Residual

The CDC specifically notes that disinfectant residual should be monitored and adjusted by an automated system for cooling towers. This means maintaining residual throughout the entire system, not just at the feed point. Manual dosing creates gaps where organisms can multiply between treatments.

### Control Biofilm, Sediment, Scale, and Corrosion

The CDC highlights scale, corrosion, and sediment control plus system cleaning as critical for cooling tower operations and Legionnaires' disease prevention. These deposits create protected niches where biocides cannot penetrate effectively, allowing Legionella to survive even when bulk water shows adequate residual.

### Operate Under Written Water Management Program

A water management program (WMP) defines control limits, monitoring points, and corrective actions. This ensures consistent application rather than occasional treatment. The program should specify what to measure, where to measure it, acceptable ranges, and exactly what to do when limits are exceeded.

## Testing Strategy: Local Screening Plus Off-Site Lab Confirmation

### Local On-Site Testing

**Environmental Antigen Tests (Water Tests)**

These rapid immunoassay or lateral-flow tests work on water samples, typically detecting cell-surface antigens. Some tests target only *Legionella pneumophila* serogroup 1. One manufacturer produces a lateral-flow immunochromatographic water test specifically for *L. pneumophila* sg1.

Important limitation: if rapid antigen water tests focus only on specific types like sg1, a negative result does not prove the system is controlled against all Legionella species.

**On-Site PCR/qPCR**

These methods provide rapid and sensitive detection and quantification of Legionella DNA. ISO has developed a technical specification specifically addressing on-site qPCR methods for this purpose.

Key limitation: qPCR measures genetic material and does not directly equal viable CFU unless your program accounts for the difference between DNA detection and living organisms.

### Off-Site Laboratory Testing

**Culture (CFU) Reference Method**

Culture remains the reference approach for enumeration. ISO 11731 specifies culture methods for isolating Legionella and estimating numbers in water and related matrices including biofilm and sediment.

**Lab qPCR**

Laboratory qPCR can provide faster trending and speciation targets, with interpretation aligned to your WMP and method detection limits.

**Clarification on Antigen Testing**

The antigen testing described for towers refers to environmental water tests, not the urinary antigen test used for human diagnosis.

## Top Three Failure Modes

### Loss of Disinfectant Control Where It Matters

Residual is not maintained at the worst-case points due to poor mixing, injection problems, underfeed, or high demand from organics and solids. This allows regrowth between treatments. The CDC emphasizes residual control specifically for towers because these systems have complex hydraulics that create dead zones.

### Biofilm, Sediment, and Scale Not Controlled

Inadequate cleaning, poor solids control, basin sludge, fouled fill, and scaling or corrosion products create protected niches where biocide penetration is weak. The CDC calls out scale, corrosion, and sediment control plus cleaning as critical because organisms hide in these deposits.

### Monitoring, Testing, and Response Failures

Over-reliance on a single method creates false reassurance. Examples include using only rapid antigen water tests that focus on sg1, or only qPCR without a framework for interpreting viability. Poor sampling locations, inadequate technique, infrequent testing, and results not tied to predefined corrective actions all contribute to control breakdown.

## Key Takeaway

Test cooling tower water weekly using both on-site screening and monthly laboratory culture, maintain automated disinfectant residual above 1.0 ppm free chlorine equivalent throughout the system, and clean towers quarterly to remove biofilm and scale that protect Legionella from treatment.
