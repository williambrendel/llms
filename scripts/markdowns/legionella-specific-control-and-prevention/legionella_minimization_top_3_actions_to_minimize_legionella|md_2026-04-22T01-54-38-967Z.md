# How often should I test and maintain different water systems for Legionella prevention?

## Executive Summary

Testing and maintenance frequencies for Legionella prevention vary dramatically by system type, with humidifiers requiring daily attention while closed-loop systems need monitoring only every two weeks under normal conditions.

## Overview

A cooling tower demands continuous disinfectant monitoring, but your office humidifier needs emptying every single day. The maintenance burden between different water systems can differ by orders of magnitude, yet building operators often apply one-size-fits-all schedules that either waste resources or miss critical risks.

## Open Cooling Tower Systems

Open cooling towers face the highest maintenance burden due to their constant exposure to environmental contamination and optimal conditions for bacterial growth. 

**Maintain disinfectant residual** represents the most critical ongoing task, typically requiring continuous monitoring via automated control systems with field verification according to the Water Management Program (WMP). This continuous attention reflects the system's high risk profile and rapid potential for contamination.

**Clean and disinfect prior to commissioning, before startup, when idling, and after shutdown** follows an event-based schedule rather than fixed intervals. These transition periods create vulnerability windows where stagnant water and disrupted treatment can allow rapid bacterial proliferation.

**Offline disinfection and cleaning at least annually** provides the deep maintenance that daily operations cannot achieve, addressing biofilm buildup and system components that remain contaminated despite routine treatment.

## Swamp Coolers and Evaporative Coolers

Swamp coolers require intensive weekly maintenance despite their simpler design compared to cooling towers.

**Drain reservoir weekly** represents the minimum manufacturer recommendation, with some systems requiring more frequent drainage based on water quality and environmental conditions. This frequent draining prevents the stagnant water conditions that promote bacterial growth.

**Inspect and clean reservoir for dirt and scale weekly** coincides with the drainage schedule, allowing operators to address contamination sources during the same maintenance window. The "clean as needed" approach during weekly drains provides flexibility while maintaining regular oversight.

**Disinfect before placing back in service** occurs on an event-based schedule tied to startup and restart operations, similar to cooling tower protocols but typically less complex due to simpler system design.

## Humidifiers

Humidifiers demand the most frequent routine maintenance of any building water system, requiring daily attention to prevent Legionella growth in their warm, stagnant water reservoirs.

**Empty and clean tanks daily** prevents the overnight stagnation that can allow rapid bacterial multiplication in the warm, nutrient-rich environment typical of humidifier reservoirs.

**Empty, wipe dry, and refill daily** ensures complete water turnover and removes the biofilm precursors that can establish on wet surfaces during idle periods.

**Clean every third day** for portable units provides the deeper cleaning that daily emptying cannot achieve, addressing mineral buildup and biofilm formation on internal components.

## Decorative Fountains

Decorative fountains follow the most variable maintenance schedules, with frequencies dependent on specific design characteristics and environmental conditions rather than standardized intervals.

**Routine cleaning and operation per manufacturer** varies significantly based on system complexity, water volume, filtration capacity, and environmental exposure. The Water Management Program must establish specific frequencies based on these site-specific factors.

**Follow authority having jurisdiction requirements** introduces additional variability, as local health departments and building codes may impose different standards based on public access, indoor versus outdoor installation, and perceived risk levels.

**Regular maintenance to reduce exposure risk** provides the overarching principle when specific guidance is unavailable, requiring risk assessment based on system design and environmental conditions.

## Closed-Loop Chilled Water Systems

Closed-loop systems require the least frequent monitoring due to their isolation from environmental contamination sources, though they still need regular oversight to detect system breaches or treatment failures.

**Routine water quality monitoring every two weeks** represents typical frequency during normal operation under specified circumstances, according to CIBSE training materials. This interval reflects the system's lower contamination risk while maintaining adequate surveillance for early problem detection.

**Post-commission and early-life testing every 1-3 months** addresses the higher risk period immediately after system startup, when construction debris, inadequate flushing, or treatment program establishment can create temporary vulnerabilities.

**Maintain and verify treatment control** follows frequencies established by the specific closed-loop treatment program rather than universal Legionella prevention schedules, as chemical treatment effectiveness varies with system design, water chemistry, and operational patterns.

## Potable Hot and Cold Building Water

Building potable water systems require routine maintenance focused on preventing stagnation in low-flow areas while maintaining the overall system's protective chlorine residual.

**Flush low-flow piping runs and dead legs at least weekly** prevents stagnation in system areas where normal water demand does not provide adequate turnover. These areas can lose disinfectant residual and accumulate sediment that supports bacterial growth.

**Flush infrequently used fixtures regularly as needed** maintains control limits in areas where sporadic use cannot ensure adequate water turnover. The "as needed" frequency depends on monitoring results and system-specific stagnation risks.

**Clean and maintain components regularly** encompasses thermostatic mixing valves, aerators, showerheads, hoses, filters, and storage tanks with frequencies established by the Water Management Program based on component-specific risk factors and manufacturer recommendations.

## Key Takeaways

Match your monitoring frequency to system risk levels: test closed-loop systems every two weeks, drain swamp coolers weekly, and empty humidifiers daily. Establish event-based cleaning protocols for system startups, shutdowns, and commissioning periods when standard schedules cannot address transition risks. Consider automated monitoring for high-risk systems like cooling towers where continuous disinfectant residual measurement can replace manual daily testing.
