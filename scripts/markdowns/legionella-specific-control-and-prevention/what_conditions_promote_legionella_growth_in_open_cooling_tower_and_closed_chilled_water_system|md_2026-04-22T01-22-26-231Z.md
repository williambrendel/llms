# What conditions promote Legionella growth in open cooling tower and closed chilled water systems?

## Executive Summary

Legionella thrives when water temperatures reach 20-45°C (68-113°F), flow stagnates, biofilms accumulate, and disinfectant residuals weaken. Open cooling towers face the highest risk due to warm recirculating water and aerosol generation, while closed chilled water systems become vulnerable when they behave "less closed" through contamination pathways or warm stagnant zones.

## Overview

A single cooling tower can generate 10,000 times more airborne Legionella than a shower when contaminated. The bacteria doesn't just survive in building water systems—it multiplies rapidly under specific conditions that building operators encounter daily. What makes some systems breeding grounds while others remain relatively safe comes down to how four critical factors align.

## Core Growth Drivers Across Both Systems

Legionella multiplication accelerates when these conditions converge in any water system:

**Temperature sits in the multiplication zone.** The bacteria multiplies mainly between 20-45°C (68-113°F), with optimal growth commonly occurring around 25-45°C (77-113°F). This range overlaps with many normal operating conditions in building systems.

**Water stagnates and turnover drops.** Low flow creates the perfect storm—biofilms build up, disinfectant residuals decay, and temperatures drift into the growth range. High "water age" essentially gives Legionella time and space to establish itself.

**Biofilm, scale, and sediment provide food and shelter.** These deposits don't just harbor the bacteria—they actively support biofilm development and shield organisms from disinfectants. Corrosion products add another layer of protection and nutrients.

**Disinfectant residuals become inadequate.** When effective biocide levels drop, especially in open systems, one of the primary barriers to growth disappears.

**Microbial ecology supports amplification.** Legionella multiplies within amoebae and protozoa that live in biofilms, creating a protected environment for rapid reproduction.

## Open Cooling Towers: Highest-Risk Conditions

Cooling towers create especially favorable conditions when multiple factors align:

**Warm recirculating water operates in the danger zone.** Many cooling towers routinely circulate water at temperatures that fall directly into Legionella's preferred growth range.

**Sediment and biofilm accumulate in key areas.** Basins, fill media, drift eliminators, and low-flow zones become collection points for the organic matter that supports biofilm development.

**Biocide control becomes weak or inconsistent.** Poor chemical feed systems, inconsistent dosing, lack of automated control, or high organic demand can leave systems vulnerable.

**Scale and corrosion increase surface complexity.** Rough surfaces and deposits create more opportunities for biofilm attachment and make disinfection less effective.

**Water age increases during low-load periods.** Side streams, offline cells, or seasonal operation can create stagnant zones where conditions deteriorate.

**Aerosol generation amplifies transmission risk.** When contamination occurs, cooling towers can disperse Legionella-laden droplets over wide areas through drift.

## Closed Chilled Water Systems: Lower but Not Zero Risk

True closed loops generally present less favorable conditions due to limited nutrients and colder operating temperatures. However, risk increases when these systems behave "less closed":

**Any section drifts into the 20-45°C range.** This happens during swing seasons, in warm mechanical rooms, or with heat recovery configurations that push water temperatures higher than typical chilled water operation.

**Stagnant sections develop.** Idle branches, bypasses, coils with minimal flow, capped equipment, or seasonal air handlers create zones where water sits without adequate turnover.

**Open-to-atmosphere points create contamination pathways.** Open or poorly protected expansion tanks, frequent makeup water additions, chronic leaks, or maintenance practices can introduce nutrients and organisms. These entry points align with the basic risk equation: nutrients plus growth-range temperatures plus stored water equals potential problems.

**Biofilm and sludge accumulate from poor water quality control.** Corrosion products, sludge, and scale deposits provide both nutrients and habitat for bacterial growth.

**Biocide strategies remain inadequate where needed.** Closed loops often focus primarily on corrosion and scale control. When microbial control gets neglected in warmer or stagnant areas, risk climbs.

## Key Takeaway

Monitor water temperatures monthly in all system zones, maintain disinfectant residuals above 0.5 mg/L free chlorine (or equivalent), and implement automated biocide control systems for cooling towers. Address stagnation by ensuring minimum flow rates of 3 feet per second in cooling tower basins and quarterly flushing of low-flow branches in closed systems.
