# If a Legionella culture comes back "not detected," can I be confident no viable Legionella are present?

## Executive Summary

No — culture can miss viable but non-culturable (VBNC) Legionella, which are cells that remain biologically intact or metabolically active but won't grow on standard media. Standard PCR and most antigen tests can detect signals from VBNC cells but cannot prove viability, which is why culture remains the U.S. regulatory and outbreak-investigation reference method despite its blind spot.

## Overview

Environmental stress and disinfection — the very interventions that should protect a water system — can push Legionella into the VBNC state, where the cells stay infective to amoebae and capable of resuscitating under favorable conditions, yet return "not detected" on a culture plate. That gap is why CDC positions quantitative PCR as a tool for screening and prioritizing samples for culture rather than a replacement: a genome unit and a colony-forming unit are not interchangeable values, and action thresholds in U.S. guidance are still written in colony-forming units per milliliter.

## What VBNC Legionella means

Legionella in the viable but non-culturable state are cells that remain alive — or at least biologically intact and metabolically active — but will not grow into colonies under the standard culture method. The common abbreviation is VBNC. You may also encounter VNC, viable but non-cultivable, viable but not culturable, or ABNC for active but non-culturable.

The practical concern is straightforward: a culture result of "not detected" does not prove that all viable Legionella risk is absent. Evidence shows that environmental stress and disinfection can push Legionella into the VBNC state, and VBNC Legionella can retain traits of viability — including amoeba infectivity and possible resuscitation under favorable conditions. That creates a potential blind spot in water safety programs.

## Whether PCR and antigen tests detect VBNC Legionella

The short answer is that most non-culture methods can detect signals from VBNC organisms, but signal detection is not the same as proving viability. The table below maps each method to what it can and cannot tell you.

| Method | Detects VBNC? | Distinguishes live from dead? | Main limitation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Culture** | No | Yes, only cells that grow | Misses VBNC by definition |
| **Standard PCR / quantitative PCR** | Usually yes | No | Detects DNA from culturable, VBNC, dead, and sometimes extracellular DNA |
| **Viability PCR / PMA or EMA quantitative PCR** | Intended to, yes | Partly | Better live/dead discrimination, but method-dependent and not universally standardized for regulatory decisions |
| **Environmental antigen / lateral-flow antigen tests** | Often yes | No | Detects antigen, not viability; often qualitative and may be limited by species or serogroup |
| **Antigen test** | Yes | No | Detects Legionella pneumophila serogroup 1 or 1-15 depending on test |

ISO's quantitative PCR method reports **genome units** and explicitly states that quantitative PCR methods do not give information about the physiological state of the Legionella.

For antigen testing, the answer depends on the test. A common environmental lateral-flow manual states that a positive result indicates Legionella pneumophila serogroup 1 or 1-15 above the detection limit, but the test does not differentiate between viable and non-viable organisms and will detect viable but non-culturable bacteria. Independent review literature notes that field-use lateral-flow devices generally provide presence/absence information and that potable water testing can be problematic.

## Whether PCR and antigen tests distinguish live from dead cells

**Standard PCR: no.** It detects target DNA. That DNA can come from living culturable cells, VBNC cells, dead cells, or free DNA remaining after cells break apart. ISO also notes that chemicals, suspended matter, and accompanying flora can interfere with quantitative PCR sensitivity and detection limits.

**Viability PCR: partly.** Viability PCR commonly uses PMA or EMA dyes to suppress DNA amplification from membrane-damaged dead cells. It helps, but it isn't perfect. Studies show that results depend on sample conditions, dye exposure protocol, cell load, and matrix effects, and PMA quantitative PCR may still overestimate viable cells when many dead cells are present.

**Antigen testing: no.** Antigen tests detect antigen, not growth or viability. A positive result can mean viable cells, VBNC cells, or non-viable antigen-containing material, depending on the product, water matrix, and detection limit.

## Why culture is still the U.S. reference method

Culture is not perfect. It is slow, variable, and misses VBNC cells. ISO itself notes that not all Legionella species are culturable, so culture methods do not recover every Legionella present.

Culture remains the main reference method because it answers a different regulatory and epidemiological question: can Legionella be recovered as a living isolate under the defined method? Several specific reasons hold it in place:

**Public health source confirmation needs isolates.** CDC states that molecular comparison of clinical and environmental isolates helps confirm outbreak sources. Culture provides those isolates. Antigen and standard PCR do not.

**CDC positions PCR as a screening tool, not a full replacement.** CDC says environmental samples collected during public health investigations may be tested by culture and PCR, but if PCR is used, CDC recommends using it primarily to screen and prioritize large numbers of samples for culture.

**CDC ELITE proficiency is culture-based.** CDC's Environmental Legionella Isolation Techniques Evaluation Program documents laboratories that can isolate Legionella from simulated environmental samples by culture. The program does not allow PCR or other non-traditional methods for its proficiency panels.

**Action thresholds are historically built around colony-forming units.** CDC routine testing guidance uses colony-forming units per milliliter for response levels and notes that if results are expressed in other units, users should consult the lab or test manufacturer for interpretation.

**Genome units, antigen presence, and colony-forming units are not interchangeable.** A review of testing methods notes there is no agreement on comparing genome units to colony-forming units and action limits. The same review notes that lateral-flow tests may only provide presence/absence information and may be limited by serogroup.

**Some laws explicitly require culture.** For example, New York cooling tower rules refer to Legionella culture analyses and require them to be performed by an approved laboratory.

## Interpreting mixed results

A **culture-negative, PCR-positive** result can mean any of the following: VBNC Legionella are present; dead Legionella DNA is present; Legionella are present but suppressed by competing flora or sample treatment; the count is near the detection limit; or PCR contamination or inhibition issues affected the result.

A **culture-negative, antigen-positive** result can mean: VBNC Legionella are present; non-viable Legionella antigen is present; the test target is present above its detection limit but viability is not proven; or the result is limited to a particular species or serogroup, depending on the test.

## Consulting language

"VBNC Legionella are viable or biologically active cells that do not grow by standard culture. Culture remains the regulatory and outbreak-investigation reference method because it provides colony-forming-unit results and recoverable isolates for confirmation and source matching. Standard PCR and antigen tests can detect signals from VBNC organisms, but they do not by themselves prove viability or infectivity. Viability PCR improves live/dead interpretation but still requires method-specific validation and does not yet replace culture in most United States public health and regulatory frameworks."

## Glossary

| Term | Definition | Synonyms / related terms |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Viable but non-culturable** | A bacterial condition in which cells do not grow on standard culture media but may remain biologically active or capable of resuscitation. | VBNC; VNC; viable but not culturable; viable but non-cultivable; non-culturable viable state |
| **Active but non-culturable** | A related term emphasizing measurable biological activity rather than growth by culture. | ABNC; metabolically active but non-culturable |
| **Culturable** | Able to grow into detectable colonies under the specific culture method used. | Recoverable by culture; colony-forming |
| **Non-culturable** | Not able to grow into colonies under the test conditions, even if cells or cell material may be present. | Not recoverable by culture; culture-negative |
| **Culture method** | A laboratory method that attempts to grow Legionella colonies from a water sample. | Plate culture; spread plate culture; ISO 11731 method; culture-based testing |
| **Colony-forming unit** | A culture result unit estimating the number of organisms capable of forming colonies under the method conditions. | CFU; colony count; culturable count |
| **Polymerase chain reaction** | A molecular method that detects target DNA sequences. It can detect DNA from live, VBNC, dead, or extracellular material unless paired with additional viability controls. | PCR; molecular testing; DNA amplification |
| **Quantitative polymerase chain reaction** | A polymerase chain reaction method that estimates the amount of target DNA and reports results such as genome units. | qPCR; real-time PCR |
| **Genome unit** | A molecular result unit reflecting detected target genetic material, not necessarily viable or culturable organisms. | GU; genomic unit; DNA target count |
| **Viability polymerase chain reaction** | A modified molecular approach intended to reduce detection of DNA from dead cells, often using dyes that penetrate damaged cell membranes. | PMA-qPCR; EMA-qPCR; live-dead PCR |
| **PMA** | Propidium monoazide, a dye used before polymerase chain reaction to help suppress signal from membrane-damaged dead cells. | PMA dye; viability dye |
| **EMA** | Ethidium monoazide, another dye used for live-dead discrimination before polymerase chain reaction. | EMA dye; viability dye |
| **Antigen test** | A test that detects target Legionella antigen rather than growth or DNA. It does not by itself prove viability. | Lateral-flow test; immunoassay; field antigen test |
| **Urinary antigen test** | A clinical test used on patient urine, mainly for Legionella pneumophila serogroup 1 infection. It is not a water sample viability test. | UAT; clinical antigen test |
| **Lateral-flow assay** | A rapid field test format that produces a visual result for a target antigen. | LFA; rapid antigen strip; field test |
| **Live-dead differentiation** | The ability to distinguish viable cells from dead cells. Standard culture, polymerase chain reaction, and antigen testing answer different parts of this question and are not interchangeable. | Viability discrimination; viability assessment |
| **Resuscitation** | Return of VBNC bacteria to a culturable state under favorable conditions. | Recovery; return to culturability |
| **Amoeba co-culture** | A method using amoebae to help evaluate whether Legionella can infect host cells or resuscitate. | Amoeba assay; host-cell co-culture |
| **False negative culture** | A culture result that reports no detected Legionella even though Legionella cells or relevant biological material may be present. | Culture-negative but positive by molecular or antigen test |
| **Isolate** | A recovered organism grown in culture that can be further characterized or compared with clinical isolates during outbreak investigations. | Cultured isolate; environmental isolate |
| **Source confirmation** | Public health process of linking a patient case to an environmental source, often supported by comparison of clinical and environmental isolates. | Source attribution; outbreak source matching |

## Takeaway

Treat any culture-negative result on a recently disinfected or stressed system as provisional, not conclusive — pair routine culture with quantitative PCR at the same sampling points and frequency, and use PCR positivity above the lab's reporting threshold as the trigger to resample by culture or escalate to viability PCR (PMA-qPCR). For regulatory and outbreak work, keep culture as the primary method so isolates are available for clinical-environmental matching, and read action thresholds in colony-forming units per milliliter rather than converting from genome units.
