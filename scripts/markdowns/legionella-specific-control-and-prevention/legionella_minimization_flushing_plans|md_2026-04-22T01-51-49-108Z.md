# How are flushing plans set up and implemented for building water systems?

## Executive Summary

Flushing plans provide written operating procedures to reduce water age, restore disinfectant levels, and re-establish temperature control across building water systems. These plans require system-specific approaches: potable systems use sequential outlet flushing with temperature and disinfectant endpoints, cooling towers rely on circulation and blowdown rather than traditional flushing, and closed-loop systems focus on maintaining circulation during low-load periods.

## Overview

A single week of building closure can transform fresh water into a breeding ground for harmful bacteria. The CDC and EPA have responded to widespread stagnation issues by emphasizing flushing as a core intervention, but the devil lies in the details of implementation. Most building operators discover that effective flushing requires far more than simply "running the taps" — it demands a systematic approach that varies dramatically by system type.

## Potable Hot and Cold Water Systems

### Plan Design Process

**Defining objectives and triggers** forms the foundation of any effective flushing plan. Triggers commonly include low occupancy periods, post-construction activities, system shutdowns, or monitoring results showing loss of control. The EPA explicitly warns that low or no use creates stagnant conditions that favor Legionella growth, particularly when hot water cools and disinfectant levels decline.

**Mapping zones and selecting representative outlets** requires grouping areas by floors, wings, or risers, then identifying distal outlets like showers and faucets in each zone. Key system monitoring points include incoming water lines, storage tanks, heater outlets, and hot water return lines. The CDC's building water system guidance emphasizes understanding system conditions and checking parameters during both recovery and routine operations.

**Establishing flushing methods and endpoints** typically focuses on four measurable criteria: stable cold-water temperature maintenance, hot-water temperature recovery at outlets, disinfectant residual presence where applicable, and clarity improvement when sediment is suspected. The EPA recommends combining flushing with parameter verification as part of comprehensive water quality restoration.

**Setting risk-based frequency** accounts for building population vulnerability and stagnation likelihood. Higher-risk facilities require more frequent flushing protocols. The CDC's potable water toolkit emphasizes layered controls and ongoing verification rather than single interventions.

### Implementation Operations

**Sequential flushing prevents re-contamination** through a systematic approach: flush mains and risers first to move fresh water through the building, then branch lines, followed by distal outlets at the farthest points, and finally low-use areas with documented completion.

**Safety controls** prevent scalding during hot-water flushing, manage aerosol generation from showers with appropriate precautions, and follow established site safety procedures.

**Documentation and verification** requires recording outlet identification, start and end times, temperatures, disinfectant measurements when applicable, and any system abnormalities. Regular log reviews provide verification following the CDC water management toolkit approach.

## Open Cooling Towers

Cooling towers require different approaches than traditional fixture flushing. The functional equivalent involves maintaining circulation and turnover, implementing bleed or blowdown procedures, and conducting cleaning and disinfection protocols, particularly during startup, shutdown, and low-load periods.

### Plan Development

**Identifying stagnation-prone locations** includes basin dead zones, low-flow piping sections, offline heat exchangers, standby cells, and idle pumps. **Defining turnover actions** establishes written requirements for minimum circulation through pump operation, bleed and blowdown targets to control concentration and remove contaminants, and biocide feed and monitoring during circulation.

**Establishing layup and startup procedures** for planned shutdowns or seasonal idle periods requires documented protocols for cleaning, disinfecting, circulating, and verifying system conditions before returning to service. The CDC module emphasizes operation, maintenance, and remediation planning as integral control components.

### Implementation Strategy

**Operational equivalents to flushing** focus on maintaining water movement to avoid dead zones, sustaining chemical control and monitoring programs, and conducting routine physical cleaning to remove deposits that consume disinfectant and harbor bacteria.

## Closed-Loop Chilled Water Systems

Truly closed chilled water loops present lower Legionella exposure risks than potable systems or cooling towers because they typically lack aerosol exposure pathways. The CDC distinguishes between open and closed-circuit designs based on atmospheric exposure and aerosol release potential.

### Plan Structure

Closed-loop plans emphasize **maintaining circulation** to avoid long-term stagnant isolated branches, **implementing side-stream filtration** or dirt separation where installed, **establishing water treatment programs** to control corrosion and microbiological growth, and **developing controlled fill and makeup procedures** to maintain system integrity.

### Operational Implementation

Implementation involves periodic pump operation during low-load seasons when system design permits, **establishing drain and fill event procedures** for flushing and cleaning during maintenance when loops are opened, and **documenting operating status** and treatment checks.

## Essential Plan Components

Comprehensive flushing plans contain **system scope and zone definitions**, **trigger conditions** specifying when to flush, circulate, or implement layup-startup procedures, **step-by-step procedures** detailing sequence, endpoints, and precautions, **acceptance criteria** including temperature stabilization and disinfectant presence requirements, **risk-based frequency tables**, and **documentation forms** with review protocols for verification and trend analysis.

## Key Takeaways

Implement system-specific flushing protocols immediately after any closure exceeding 72 hours. Monitor disinfectant residuals weekly at distal outlets, maintaining chlorine levels above 0.2 mg/L in potable systems. Consider automated blowdown controllers for cooling towers to maintain cycles of concentration below 6.0, and document all flushing activities with temperature and time stamps for regulatory compliance and system optimization.
