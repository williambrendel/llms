# How Do You Communicate During Legionella Events Without Causing Panic or Legal Issues?

## Executive Summary

Effective Legionella crisis communication requires pre-written templates, clear action levels (normal, elevated risk, critical), and immediate coordination with health authorities while sticking to facts about what's known, who's at risk, and what's being done.

## Overview

A single positive Legionella test can trigger media attention, tenant evacuations, and regulatory scrutiny within hours. Most facility managers discover too late that their crisis communication plan consists of a phone tree and good intentions. The difference between a manageable incident and a reputation-destroying crisis often comes down to having the right message ready before you need it.

## Communication Principles for Every Scenario

These principles apply whether you're dealing with open cooling towers, closed cooling systems, or building water systems.

### Essential Pre-Planning Elements

Prepare three critical assets before any incident occurs. **Pre-written templates** cover each escalation level with placeholder fields for dates, systems, and specific actions. **Distribution lists** organize contacts into four categories: local operations staff, corporate incident team members, tenant contacts, and authority having jurisdiction (AHJ) contacts. A one-page **"key messages" sheet** maintains consistency across all communications and prevents speculation.

Assign two dedicated roles during any incident: one incident communications lead and one technical lead. This separation keeps messages consistent across different audiences and prevents technical details from overwhelming public communications.

Coordinate early and often with the authority having jurisdiction and key stakeholders. Never speculate about contamination sources until confirmation through proper investigation channels.

### Standard Terminology Framework

Use consistent, agreed-upon definitions across all communications. A **positive test** means a water sample testing positive for either Legionella serogroup 1 or Legionella serogroups 2-15.

In public health surveillance and outbreak investigations, a **case** refers to a person or event meeting pre-specified case definition criteria. These definitions typically combine clinical criteria (signs and symptoms), laboratory criteria (test evidence), and sometimes epidemiologic linkage (exposure connection). Surveillance systems classify cases as **confirmed** (meets confirmatory lab evidence and defined epidemiologic link criteria), **probable** (meets clinical criteria plus supportive lab or epidemiologic evidence), or **suspect** (used in some jurisdictions for early signals, with variable inclusion in official counts).

### Universal Message Structure

Build every external notice around five core elements: what's known, who's at risk, what's being done, what people should do, and who to contact.

For suspected illness or outbreaks tied to cooling towers, CDC's cooling tower module emphasizes that the public health AHJ determines whether illnesses constitute an outbreak association. Remediation and testing should proceed in conjunction with public health authorities.

## Three-Level Action Framework

### Level 1: Normal Operations

**Normal operations** means all control limits are met during routine monitoring. 

**Local staff** receive updates through routine logs and weekly or monthly meetings. The status message template includes: "All Legionella minimization control limits met for [system(s)] during [dates]," work completed including key preventive maintenance and cleaning schedule adherence, exceptions listed as "None" or specific items with due dates, and reminders about escalation triggers for Level 2 and 3 events.

**Corporate staff** receive monthly dashboard or KPI emails covering systems under management, monitoring compliance rates, any exceptions, and upcoming high-risk periods like seasonal startup or low occupancy transitions.

No external communications are required during Level 1 operations. **Building customers** typically receive no communications unless organizational policy requires routine transparency updates. **Health officials** receive no routine notifications.

### Level 2: Elevated Risk or Deviation

**Elevated risk** situations involve one or more missed control limits, abnormal trends, equipment upsets, or events increasing risk. These include positive test results exceeding Level 1 thresholds but remaining below Level 3 triggers, stagnation events, loss of disinfectant feed, tower idle or startup issues, or construction tie-ins.

**Local staff** receive immediate work orders plus same-day huddles. Messages specify what happened: "At [time/date], [parameter/equipment] was out of limit: [value vs limit]." Immediate actions include: "Implemented [corrective actions]. Increased monitoring to [frequency]." Any restrictions are clearly stated, such as "Pause non-essential aerosol-generating maintenance near tower until stabilized." Documentation requirements specify: "Record in WMP corrective action log; attach readings/photos/service report."

**Corporate staff** receive same-day incident notifications plus daily updates until resolution. Messages include summary information, program-defined risk assessment, actions taken, expected stabilization criteria, and sampling plans without promising specific results. Decision points are clearly defined: "If not restored by [time] or if repeat deviation occurs → Level 3."

**Building customers** receive communications only if deviations affect service or require access for flushing or shutdown procedures. Messages state: "We are performing preventive maintenance/corrective actions on [system]. There is no confirmed illness or exposure associated with the building at this time." Include what customers may notice, such as temperature fluctuations or brief water interruptions, plus any required customer actions like "Avoid using [specific device] until [time]."

**Health officials** receive optional pre-notification if jurisdiction or policy expects it, or if deviation severity warrants early contact. Messages include: "We had a loss-of-control event in [system]. Corrective actions underway; no associated cases reported; will notify if test/case information changes."

### Level 3: Critical or Imminent Risk

**Critical situations** involve positive test results exceeding Level 2 thresholds, sustained loss of control, major system failures, confirmed aerosol exposure risk in open cooling systems, and may require shutdown or restricted use.

**Local staff** receive immediate phone or text notifications plus incident command activation. Messages state: "Level 3 declared for [system] due to [reason]. Execute emergency SOP: [shutdown/restrictions], [disinfection/cleaning], [sampling plan if used]." Role assignments include safety steps with proper PPE and controls for aerosol exposure in open cooling systems. "All actions logged in real time."

**Corporate staff** receive immediate notifications to EHS, legal, and leadership teams, plus scheduled updates at designated times like 9am and 4pm. Messages include clear decision statements: "Operations restricted/shutdown of [system] as precaution." External communications posture designates spokesperson, approves customer notice language, and establishes media handling procedures.

**Building customers** receive same-day notices if any restrictions, shutdowns, or potential exposure concerns exist. Messages stick to facts: "We identified a condition that can increase Legionella growth risk in [system]. As a precaution, we have [restricted use/shut down] and initiated [corrective actions]." Customer actions are usually "none," unless specific fixtures or devices require restriction. Provide a point of contact for questions.

**Health officials** receive notifications when Level 3 events involve aerosol-generating systems like cooling towers, large public settings, or when required by local rules or policy. Messages include facts only and request guidance on recommended notifications or testing approaches.

## Event-Driven Communication Scenarios

### Positive Legionella Test Results

The key rule for environmental testing: never equate a positive environmental result with human illness. Communicate what actions you're taking next. CDC emphasizes coordinated testing and remediation approaches.

**Local staff** receive immediate notification: "Positive Legionella result received for [system/location], sample date [date], method [culture/qPCR] if known." Response initiation follows WMP protocols: "[online remedial / online disinfection / offline cleaning & disinfection / emergency offline], increased monitoring, resampling plan." "Preserve chain-of-custody and lab report in WMP records."

**Corporate staff** receive same-day updates including result details plus risk context covering system type, aerosol potential, and occupancy vulnerability. Response steps are documented as started, external notification requirements are clarified based on jurisdiction-specific rules, and messaging remains unified without speculation on contamination sources.

**Building customers** receive notifications if open cooling towers are involved, public concern is likely, or if required by regulations. Template messages state: "Routine/targeted testing detected Legionella in [system]. This does not mean anyone has been exposed or is ill." "We have initiated corrective actions including [describe plainly], and are working with qualified water treatment professionals." "If you have pneumonia symptoms, seek medical care; questions → [contact]." Use CDC-aligned symptom and call-to-action language per organizational policy.

**Health officials** receive reports with timing dependent on jurisdiction requirements, particularly for cooling towers. "Report positive/elevated result and actions taken; request guidance on public notification language and any additional sampling." In New York State with cooling towers, notify local health departments and public as required for elevated levels.

### Single Illness Event

When one suspected or confirmed Legionnaires' disease case has potential facility linkage, the primary goal involves coordinating with public health immediately, preserving records, and avoiding linkage claims unless AHJ confirms.

**Local staff** receive notification: "Potential case reported/identified. Do not share personal health details beyond need-to-know." "Secure WMP logs, trend data, and recent maintenance records for [30–90 days]." "Implement precautionary controls (per WMP) and prepare for AHJ-directed sampling."

**Corporate staff** initiate incident communications plans, notify legal and risk management teams, assign spokesperson roles, draft holding statements for customers and media, and prepare data rooms with schematics, recent logs, test results, and contractor reports.

**Building customers** receive communications only if AHJ advises or if setting suggests significant concern. Template messages state: "A case of Legionnaires' disease has been reported in a person who has spent time at/near [building] during [date range]. At this time, the source has not been confirmed." "We are coordinating with [local health department] and implementing precautionary control measures." Include symptoms and when to seek care, who faces higher risk, and contact information for health departments.

**Health officials** receive immediate calls or emails: "We have been notified of a potential case with possible exposure at [site]. Provide system inventory and WMP; request investigation guidance." "We will follow AHJ direction on sampling and remediation."

### Outbreak or Cluster Events

When two or more cases are linked by place and time per AHJ determination, use CDC's outbreak communication guidance emphasizing consistent terminology, frequent updates, clear statements of "what we know / don't know," plus specific calls to action and contact points.

**Local staff** receive immediate notifications plus daily briefings: "Outbreak/cluster investigation underway with AHJ." "Stop/limit operation of suspected aerosol sources if directed (e.g., cooling tower shutdown) and execute emergency cleaning/disinfection steps per SOP." "Maintain a strict communications protocol: all external questions routed to spokesperson."

**Corporate staff** implement incident command with situation report cadence twice daily including cases and updates as provided by AHJ, operational status, remediation progress, and customer communications status. Media handling plans include Q&A sheets aligned with AHJ messaging.

**Building customers** receive rapid notification plus regular updates. Template notices specify what's known: "Public health is investigating a Legionnaires' disease cluster/outbreak associated with [area/building] during [date range]." What's unknown: "The source is under investigation." Actions taken: "We have [shut down/treated/cleaned] [system(s)] and are working with [AHJ] to prevent additional illnesses." Include information about who faces higher risk, symptoms, when to seek care, where to get more information, and health department contact details.

**Health officials** receive continuous coordination including requested logs, access, and sampling support. Follow AHJ direction on public communications and control measures.

## Implementation Strategy

Successful Legionella crisis communication depends on preparation, not improvisation. Create your templates now, test your distribution lists quarterly, and train your designated communicators before any incident occurs. The goal is never to hide information, but to provide accurate, helpful guidance that protects public health while maintaining stakeholder confidence in your water management program.
