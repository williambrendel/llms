# What are acceptable Legionella "action levels" (CFU/L) and what actions correspond to each level?

## Executive Summary

There is no single universally "acceptable" Legionella CFU/L action level, as the CDC states "There is no known safe level or type of Legionella." Action thresholds vary by system type, building use, and jurisdiction, with widely used frameworks providing specific numeric thresholds and corresponding response actions.

## Overview

A single drop of water from a "well-controlled" cooling tower can legally contain up to 9,000 Legionella bacteria, while the same concentration in hospital drinking water triggers immediate remediation. Different water systems face vastly different risk tolerances and regulatory frameworks, creating a patchwork of action levels that can differ by orders of magnitude depending on whether you're managing a healthcare facility's potable water or an office building's cooling tower.

## CDC Performance Indicators for Potable Water Systems

The CDC uses colony-forming unit thresholds as performance indicators rather than safety limits, linking bacterial counts to program management intensity. These categories help facility managers understand when their control programs need adjustment.

**What it measures:** System control effectiveness based on bacterial enumeration

**Potable Water Thresholds:**

| CDC category | CDC threshold (CFU/mL) | Equivalent (CFU/L) | CDC-linked action intent |
|--------------|------------------------|-------------------|-------------------------|
| Well controlled | 0 to 0.9 | 0 to 900 | Continue the program when <1 CFU/mL |
| Poorly controlled | 1.0–9.9 | 1,000–9,900 | Implement suggested response activities (review conditions/controls, corrective actions, follow-up testing) |
| Uncontrolled | ≥10 | ≥10,000 | Implement suggested response activities, typically more urgent/expansive; consider extent/trend/species too |

The CDC's "suggested response activities" include environmental assessment, review of control limits for temperature/residual/biocide, evaluation of water age and biofilm/scale/sediment conditions, implementation of corrective actions, and resampling to validate control effectiveness.

## CDC Performance Indicators for Cooling Tower Systems

Cooling towers operate under different risk parameters than potable water systems, reflected in their higher acceptable bacterial loads before triggering response protocols.

**Cooling Tower Thresholds:**

| CDC category | CDC threshold (CFU/mL) | Equivalent (CFU/L) | CDC-linked action intent |
|--------------|------------------------|-------------------|-------------------------|
| Well controlled | 0 to 9 | 0 to 9,000 | Continue the program when <10 CFU/mL |
| Poorly controlled | 10–99 | 10,000–99,000 | Implement suggested response activities |
| Uncontrolled | ≥100 | ≥100,000 | Implement suggested response activities; consider trend/extent/species |

## UK HSE Action Levels for Evaporative Cooling Systems

The UK Health and Safety Executive provides explicit action levels with defined response protocols for evaporative cooling systems, offering more prescriptive guidance than CDC performance indicators.

| Legionella result (CFU/L) | HSE comment / action |
|---------------------------|---------------------|
| Not detected or up to 100 | Focus on maintaining controls; keep general aerobic count under control (no special action solely from this result) |
| >100 to 1,000 | Reassess control program; ensure treatment is operating; adjust biocide if needed; resample to verify and confirm remedial actions effective |
| >1,000 or persistent low-level results | Immediate action: resample; precautionary shot-dose or increase continuous biocide; reassess entire control program; resample again within 48 hours; if persistent, review risk assessment and further remedial actions |

## Healthcare Facility Potable Water Action Levels

Healthcare premises with susceptible patients operate under the most stringent protocols, as established by Public Health England guidance for hot and cold water systems.

| Legionella result (CFU/L) | Recommended actions (PHE) |
|---------------------------|---------------------------|
| Not detected or up to 100 | Any detection should be investigated; resample as needed per monitoring strategy/risk assessment |
| >100 to 1,000 | If minority positive → resample; if repeated, review controls/risk assessment. If majority positive → immediate review; consider system colonized; consider disinfection |
| >1,000 | Immediate review of controls/risk assessment; consider disinfection; retest a few days after disinfection and frequently thereafter until satisfactory control |

## Jurisdictional Reporting Requirements

Local regulations add another layer of compliance obligations. New York City requires cooling tower operators to upload results of ≥1,000 CFU/mL to the city portal within 24 hours, demonstrating how jurisdictional requirements can impose specific reporting triggers regardless of federal guidance.

## Key Takeaways

Use CDC performance indicators to interpret routine monitoring results and adjust program intensity accordingly. Apply HSE or PHE action level frameworks when you need specific numeric triggers with defined response protocols. Always verify local regulations, particularly for cooling towers, as jurisdictional requirements may impose additional reporting thresholds, testing frequencies, or remediation timelines beyond federal guidance.
