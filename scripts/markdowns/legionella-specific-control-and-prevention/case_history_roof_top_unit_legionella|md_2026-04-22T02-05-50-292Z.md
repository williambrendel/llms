# Why Do Rooftop Cooling Units Keep Testing Positive for Legionella Despite Biocide Treatment?

## Executive Summary

Rooftop cooling units continue testing positive for Legionella because biocide treatments lack proper residual verification and biofilm disruption mechanisms. Without confirming actual biocide concentrations or breaking apart protective biofilms, even multiple decontamination attempts will fail to eliminate embedded Legionella colonies.

## Overview

A data center's rooftop cooling units failed Legionella testing despite DBNPA biocide treatment, and subsequent isothiazolone decontamination failed in 9 out of the treated units. The root cause reveals a fundamental gap in water treatment verification: no one actually confirmed whether the biocides reached effective concentrations or contact times. Meanwhile, Legionella colonies remained protected inside biofilms that were never disrupted.

## The Technical Failures Behind Treatment Ineffectiveness

### Biocide Application Without Verification

The DBNPA pucks were applied 30 days before testing, but no residual testing confirmed whether the biocide reached effective concentrations. DBNPA degrades rapidly in warm water systems, especially those with organic loading from cooling tower operations. Without real-time or laboratory confirmation, there's no evidence the systems were ever properly dosed.

The situation worsened during remediation with isothiazolone. This non-oxidizing biocide requires precise concentration control and verified contact time to be effective. The target dose ranges from 150-350 ppm as product, maintained for 2-4 hours. Yet no residual testing verified whether this concentration was achieved or maintained throughout the required contact period.

### Invalid Testing Methods

Free chlorine testing was suggested to verify DBNPA dosage—a scientifically invalid approach. DBNPA is not an oxidizer and does not yield free halogen residuals. Using chlorine tests to justify DBNPA feed not only provides inaccurate data but creates false confidence in system protection.

### Missing Biofilm Control

No biodispersant was applied despite the systems operating in wet-dry cycles that promote biofilm formation on fill media, coil surfaces, and sump walls. Biofilms create protective matrices where Legionella colonies can survive even direct biocide contact. Isothiazolone particularly requires biofilm disruption to access embedded bacteria. Without dispersant treatment, the biocides essentially treated only the bulk water while leaving the actual contamination source untouched.

## Required Improvements for Effective Legionella Control

### Mandatory Residual Testing Protocols

Every biocide application must include verification testing:

| Biocide | Proper Residual Test | Testing Frequency |
|---------|---------------------|-------------------|
| DBNPA | Colorimetric or HPLC via laboratory; some field kits available | Weekly or biweekly during active cooling |
| Isothiazolone | Active ingredient field testing or laboratory verification | Before, during, and after each disinfection event |

Testing must occur before, during, and after application to verify that concentration and contact time goals were met.

### Biofilm Disruption Strategy

Effective biofilm control requires systematic dispersant application:

| Action | Recommendation |
|--------|---------------|
| Bio-dispersant dosing | Feed 300-500 ppm slug 1-2 times per month during wet operation, preceding biocide treatment by 30-60 minutes |
| Mechanical contact | Wet all surfaces for minimum 2-4 hours during treatment through automated or manual initiation |
| Verification | Use BART testing to verify biofilm disruption; monitor turbidity changes as indirect confirmation |

## Liability and Compliance Consequences

### Documentation Gaps Create Legal Exposure

The Legionella Minimization Plan requires 95% of biological testing to show non-detect at 1 cfu. Without residual testing documentation:

- No evidence exists that biocides ever reached effective concentrations
- Disinfection treatments lack technical validity without dosage and contact time records  
- Re-colonization events cannot be analyzed or explained due to missing baseline data
- Third-party auditors, insurance carriers, or legal investigators will flag these omissions

### Responsibility Under the Minimization Plan

Those named as "responsible" in the LMP face potential civil liability, breach of duty claims, or insurance denial if illness or regulatory action occurs. The combination of positive test results, failed remediation, absent residual verification, missing dispersant protocols, and invalid testing methods constitutes both technical and procedural failures.

## Actionable Path Forward

Implement residual testing for all biocide applications within the next treatment cycle. Add biodispersant protocols at 400 ppm concentration, applied 45 minutes before biocide treatment. Establish weekly DBNPA residual monitoring during cooling season and document all concentration and contact time data for each isothiazolone disinfection event. These changes will provide the verification framework needed to achieve the 95% non-detect standard and demonstrate due diligence in Legionella control.
