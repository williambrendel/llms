# What biocides work best for controlling Legionella in closed-loop water systems?

## Executive Summary

Non-oxidizing biocides—particularly isothiazolinones, glutaraldehyde, DBNPA, and THPS—provide the most effective microbial control in closed-loop systems by preventing biofilm formation that supports Legionella persistence.

## Overview

Closed-loop systems present a paradox: they carry low Legionella exposure risk since they don't normally create aerosols, yet they can harbor persistent microbial growth if water stagnates or gets contaminated during maintenance. The key insight is that effective Legionella control in these systems isn't about direct killing—it's about eliminating the biofilm environments where Legionella thrives.

## Understanding Closed-Loop Legionella Risk

Closed loops like chilled water and heating/cooling circuits typically pose low Legionella exposure risk because they don't aerosolize water during normal operation. However, these systems can still develop biofouling and slime that supports microbial persistence under specific conditions:

- Water stagnation in system dead legs or during shutdowns
- Contamination during fill operations or maintenance activities  
- Poor water treatment allowing biofilm establishment

The primary concern becomes preventing biofilm formation rather than direct Legionella disinfection, since biofilms provide the protective environment Legionella needs to survive and multiply.

## Non-Oxidizing Biocides for Closed Systems

Non-oxidizing biocides dominate closed-loop treatment because they provide sustained microbial control without the corrosion concerns of continuous oxidizer exposure.

**Isothiazolinones** represent the most commonly deployed option for closed-loop microbial control. These products specifically target biofilm-forming bacteria and maintain effectiveness in the non-aerated environment typical of closed systems.

**Glutaraldehyde** offers fast-acting control against biofilm-forming bacteria in water systems. Its rapid action makes it particularly useful for addressing established contamination or as part of system startup procedures.

**DBNPA** (2,2-dibromo-3-nitrilopropionamide) provides another fast-acting non-oxidizing option frequently referenced alongside glutaraldehyde and isothiazolinone for industrial water systems.

**THPS** (tetrakis(hydroxymethyl)phosphonium sulfate) rounds out the commonly monitored non-oxidizer options for industrial open and closed systems, offering broad-spectrum microbial control.

## Oxidizing Approaches and Shock Treatment

While continuous oxidizing treatment is less common in closed loops, oxidizing disinfection events serve specific purposes. Some programs implement oxidizing "shock" treatments when severe contamination occurs or after major maintenance that opens the system to atmospheric exposure. These systems then return to normal closed-loop treatment protocols.

This approach recognizes that closed systems occasionally need aggressive intervention while avoiding the ongoing corrosion risks of continuous oxidizer exposure.

## Implementation and Monitoring

Effective closed-loop biocide programs focus on practical application rather than complex chemistry rotation. Select one primary non-oxidizer—typically isothiazolinone or glutaraldehyde—and apply as slug doses according to your water treatment plan. Only rotate chemistries if you have documented evidence of poor control or resistant biofilms, preferably with vendor or consultant guidance.

Biofilm control drives the entire strategy. Closed-loop guidance consistently emphasizes preventing biofouling as the cornerstone of overall closed-system treatment, since biofilm elimination removes the environment Legionella requires.

Field test kits exist specifically for monitoring non-oxidizer levels, including DBNPA, glutaraldehyde, isothiazolinone, and THPS. These kits confirm the biocide maintains its intended concentration throughout the system.

Product selection must account for regulatory approval in your jurisdiction and compatibility with system metals and elastomers. Additionally, manage discharge requirements if you blow down or dump the loop, as these requirements vary by site and don't follow universal rules.

## Key Takeaways

Monitor biocide levels monthly using field test kits to maintain 10-50 ppm active ingredient depending on your selected chemistry. When biofilm control becomes inadequate, conduct oxidizing shock treatment followed by system flushing before returning to non-oxidizer protocols. Consider upgrading to automated slug-dose systems for consistent biocide delivery in large closed-loop installations.
