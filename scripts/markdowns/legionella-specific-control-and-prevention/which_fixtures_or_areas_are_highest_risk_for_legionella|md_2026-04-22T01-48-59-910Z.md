# Which building water systems pose the highest Legionella risk?

## Executive Summary

Cooling towers, decorative fountains, and humidifiers pose the highest Legionella risk in buildings, with cooling towers scoring 95 out of 100 on relative risk scales due to their combination of aerosol generation and warm recirculating water.

## Overview

A single cooling tower can expose hundreds of people to Legionella bacteria through airborne droplets that travel far beyond the building itself. While most building owners focus on obvious water sources like drinking fountains, the real danger comes from systems that both amplify bacteria in warm, stagnant conditions and spray that contaminated water into the air people breathe.

## Understanding Legionella Risk Factors

Legionella bacteria become dangerous when five conditions align in building water systems. Maintenance quality determines how well systems prevent bacterial growth through regular cleaning and disinfection. Water temperature creates the perfect breeding ground when systems operate between 68°F and 113°F (20°C to 45°C). Stagnation and water age allow bacteria to multiply in dead-end pipes and unused fixtures where fresh water never reaches. Disinfectant residual levels drop over time, especially in remote parts of large buildings, leaving water vulnerable to contamination. Biofilm and scale buildup provide protected environments where Legionella can thrive even when disinfectants are present.

The risk score reflects how likely each system is to both amplify Legionella bacteria and expose people through inhalation of contaminated aerosols. Systems that create fine water droplets while recirculating warm water consistently rank as the most dangerous sources in outbreak investigations.

## High-Risk Systems: The Aerosol Generators

**Cooling Towers and Evaporative Equipment**

Cooling towers earn the highest risk score of 95 because they combine every dangerous factor into one system. These rooftop units continuously spray warm water through the air to cool buildings, creating massive amounts of aerosols that can travel over a mile from the source. The recirculating water stays in the ideal temperature range for Legionella growth, and the constant evaporation concentrates nutrients that feed bacterial colonies.

**Decorative Water Features**

Decorative fountains and water features score 85 on the risk scale because, as the CDC states, "all decorative fountains produce aerosols." Unlike cooling towers hidden on rooftops, fountains sit directly in occupied spaces where people walk through the spray zones daily. The warm, recirculating water in these systems often lacks adequate disinfection, creating perfect conditions for bacterial amplification.

**Humidification Systems**

Humidifiers that aerosolize water score 80, particularly those that spray fine mists directly into air handling systems. The WHO and HSE specifically identify these systems as linked outbreak sources because they distribute contaminated droplets throughout entire buildings via the ventilation system.

## Moderate-Risk Systems: Point-of-Use Exposures

**Showers and Shower Heads**

Shower use scores 75 while the fixtures themselves score 70, reflecting their dual risk profile. Showers create intense aerosol exposure in confined spaces, and shower heads develop biofilm reservoirs that release bacteria bursts when first turned on. The combination of warm water and direct inhalation makes showers a repeatedly cited exposure route in Legionella cases.

**Evaporative Coolers**

Portable and installed swamp coolers score 65, with risk varying significantly by design and maintenance practices. The HSE treats evaporative cooling as a primary risk category because these systems can generate aerosols while operating in the temperature ranges where Legionella thrives.

## Infrastructure Risks: The Hidden Amplifiers

**Building Water Systems**

Hot water systems score 60 while cold water systems score 45, reflecting how temperature affects bacterial growth. Large, complex plumbing creates multiple zones where water temperature drifts into dangerous ranges and disinfectant levels decay over time. Hot water systems pose higher risks because they operate closer to optimal Legionella temperatures.

**Storage and Stagnation Points**

Storage tanks and dead-leg sections both score 55 because they create the stagnation conditions that drive Legionella amplification. Stored water allows sediment and biofilm accumulation while temperature control becomes difficult in large tanks. Dead-end pipes and low-flow sections eliminate the water movement that prevents bacterial buildup and disinfectant decay.

## Lower-Risk Systems: Limited Exposure Pathways

**Ice Machines and Drinking Fountains**

Ice makers score 35 and drinking fountains score 25 because they primarily involve ingestion rather than inhalation. However, the WHO notes that infection can still occur through aspiration of contaminated water or ice, particularly in vulnerable patients who may accidentally inhale while drinking.

**Closed HVAC Loops**

Heating loops score 20 and chilled loops score 15 because properly maintained closed systems have limited exposure pathways. Risk increases significantly when these systems aren't truly closed, develop makeup water issues, or connect to components that create aerosols elsewhere in the building.

**Toilets and Urinals**

These fixtures score only 5 because Legionella guidance rarely identifies them as transmission sources. While they contain water, they don't typically create the aerosol conditions necessary for respiratory exposure.

## Key Takeaways

Focus Legionella prevention efforts on aerosol-generating systems first, particularly cooling towers and decorative fountains that combine warm water recirculation with airborne exposure. Monitor water temperatures monthly in all systems, maintaining cold water below 68°F and hot water above 140°F at points of use. Consider replacing decorative fountains with dry features and humidifiers with steam-based alternatives that don't aerosolize standing water.
