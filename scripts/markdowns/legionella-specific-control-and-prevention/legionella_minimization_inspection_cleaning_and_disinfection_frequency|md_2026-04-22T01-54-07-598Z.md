# How Often Should I Clean and Maintain Water Systems to Prevent Legionella?

## Executive Summary

Open cooling towers require cleaning and disinfection at least annually plus before startup and after shutdown, while humidifiers need daily tank emptying and cleaning, and potable water systems require weekly flushing of dead legs and low-flow runs.

## Overview

A single cooling tower can generate over 100,000 gallons of water droplets per hour, making cleaning frequency the difference between safe operation and a potential Legionella outbreak. The CDC has established minimum cleaning intervals for different water systems, but the actual frequency depends on your specific equipment, water quality, and operating conditions.

## Cooling Towers and Evaporative Equipment

**Open cooling towers** follow a four-tier maintenance schedule. Clean and disinfect per manufacturer specifications prior to commissioning, before startup, when idling, and after shutdown. The CDC requires offline disinfection and cleaning at least annually. During operation, maintain continuous routine inspection and chemistry control without deep cleaning every time. Trigger full cleaning and disinfection before startup, after shutdown, or after any loss-of-control event.

**Swamp coolers and indirect evaporative coolers** require a maintenance schedule tied to startup, in-season operation, shutdown, and any loss-of-control situations. Loss-of-control includes standing water, visible slime, odor, heavy scale, or pan drainage issues. Use manufacturer instructions as the minimum baseline and adjust frequency based on local conditions.

## Indoor Water Features and Humidification

**Humidifiers** demand the most frequent attention. The CDC states: "Empty and clean tanks on humidifiers daily." The EPA adds recommendations for portable units: daily empty, wipe, dry, and refill, plus cleaning every third day. For commercial installations, drain and empty tanks daily as the CDC minimum, with scheduled deeper cleaning per manufacturer specifications or more often if mineral scale appears or water is hard.

**Decorative fountains** require cleaning and disinfection as a regular part of operation and maintenance, but frequency depends on fountain design and environmental conditions. Establish a routine (weekly, biweekly, or monthly) based on aerosol generation, water temperature, visible biofilm, filter performance, make-up water quality, visitor proximity, and manufacturer instructions.

## Building Water Systems

**Closed-loop chilled water systems** use the water treatment program to set routine inspection schedules, periodic side-stream filtration and strainer cleaning, plus clean and flush actions after opening the loop for drain, refill, or major maintenance. Increase attention if the system has open interfaces or aerosol pathways.

**Potable hot and cold building water systems** follow CDC recommendations for flushing low-flow piping runs and dead legs at least weekly, plus flushing infrequently used fixtures regularly as needed to maintain control limits. Clean and maintain components like thermostatic mixing valves, aerators, showerheads, hoses, filters, and storage tanks regularly with frequency set by the water management program.

## Key Takeaways

Implement weekly flushing of dead legs in potable systems, daily humidifier tank cleaning, and annual cooling tower disinfection as your minimum baseline. Monitor for loss-of-control indicators like visible biofilm, standing water, or temperature excursions that trigger immediate cleaning regardless of schedule. Document all maintenance activities and adjust frequencies based on water quality testing results and equipment performance.
