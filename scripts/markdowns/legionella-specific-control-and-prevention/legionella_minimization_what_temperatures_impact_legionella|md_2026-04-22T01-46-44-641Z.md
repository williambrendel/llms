# What Temperature Range Promotes Legionella Growth and How Can We Control It?

## Executive Summary

Legionella bacteria thrive in water temperatures between 77–113°F (25–45°C), with thermal control requiring cold water below 68°F (20°C) and hot water storage above 140°F (60°C).

## Overview

A seemingly comfortable water temperature that feels pleasant to touch creates the perfect breeding ground for one of the most dangerous waterborne pathogens. Legionella bacteria can multiply rapidly in the same temperature range where many building water systems naturally operate, turning routine plumbing into a potential health hazard. The difference between safe and dangerous water systems often comes down to just a few degrees.

## The Legionella Growth Zone

Legionella bacteria find their sweet spot in water temperatures between 77–113°F (25–45°C). This range represents the optimal conditions where these pathogens multiply most rapidly, transforming from a manageable presence into a serious threat.

The bacteria don't simply shut down outside this range. Growth can occur at temperatures as low as 68°F (20°C), though multiplication slows considerably at these cooler temperatures. This extended growth range means that even moderately cool water systems can harbor developing Legionella populations given enough time.

Legionella multiply where water temperatures reach 20–45°C when other enabling conditions exist simultaneously. These conditions include the presence of biofilm, available nutrients, and water stagnation that allows bacteria to establish and maintain colonies.

## Thermal Control Strategies

### Cold Water Management

Effective Legionella control starts with keeping cold water systems genuinely cold. Cold water should remain below 77°F (25°C) according to CDC guidance, as this temperature marks the lower boundary of the optimal growth range.

Many control programs set an even more conservative target, aiming to keep cold water below 68°F (20°C) where feasible. This lower threshold provides additional protection by moving water temperatures outside even the extended growth range where slow multiplication can occur.

### Hot Water Requirements

Hot water systems require aggressive temperature management to inhibit and kill Legionella bacteria. Storage tanks should maintain water above 140°F (60°C), a temperature that creates hostile conditions for bacterial survival.

Circulating hot water systems must maintain temperatures above 120°F (49°C) throughout the distribution network. This ensures that even the furthest points in the system receive water hot enough to prevent bacterial establishment.

### Distribution Performance Standards

Real-world effectiveness depends on delivery performance at actual use points. Hot water should reach at least 122°F (50°C) at outlets within one minute of opening the tap. Healthcare settings often require even higher delivery temperatures of 131°F (55°C) to account for the increased vulnerability of patients.

This rapid delivery requirement prevents the formation of lukewarm zones in distribution lines where Legionella could establish during the time it takes for hot water to travel from storage to use points.

## Practical Implementation

Temperature control works best as part of a comprehensive water management approach. Monitor cold water temperatures at the furthest points from chillers or incoming supply lines, as these locations often show the highest temperatures in cold water systems.

For hot water systems, measure temperatures at both storage tanks and the most distant fixtures. Install thermostatic mixing valves carefully to prevent creating intermediate temperature zones in the distribution system.

Document temperature readings weekly at minimum, with daily monitoring during high-risk periods or in healthcare facilities. Investigate any readings that approach the 68–77°F (20–25°C) transition zone, as these indicate systems moving toward conditions that support bacterial growth.
