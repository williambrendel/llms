# How does side stream filtration help in open cooling tower and closed loop water systems?

## Executive Summary

Side stream filtration continuously filters 10% of system flow to remove suspended solids, improving biological control stability in cooling towers and reducing corrosion byproduct fouling in closed loops.

## Overview

A small dedicated pump filtering just one-tenth of your system's water can solve problems that plague entire facilities. Side stream filtration captures the particles that seed biofilms, foul heat exchangers, and create under-deposit corrosion conditions. The approach works differently in open towers versus closed loops, but both systems gain equipment protection and more stable chemical control.

## What Side-Stream Filtration Is

A dedicated pump creates a continuous loop that filters a **portion** of system flow and returns it to the basin or header. Most installations filter around 10% of the total recirculation flow. This design removes **suspended solids** — dirt, silt, corrosion products, and precipitated solids — without forcing the entire system flow through a filter that could plug and interrupt operations.

## Open Cooling Towers: How Side-Stream Filtration Helps

### Reduces Sediment and Biofilm Drivers, Improving Bio Control Stability

Suspended solids and sediment create habitat and protection for biofilm formation. Removing these particles reduces biological pressure and lowers disinfectant demand throughout the system. The CDC specifically recommends **considering filtration to reduce suspended solids** as part of comprehensive cooling tower control measures.

### Reduces Deposition, Scale Seeding, and Heat-Transfer Fouling

Particles act as nucleation sites for scale formation and become incorporated into deposits that foul condensers and fill media. These deposits reduce heat transfer efficiency and drive approach temperatures upward. Filtration removes the debris and particles that would otherwise settle and accumulate in critical heat transfer surfaces.

### Enables Higher Stable Cycles, Reducing Blowdown and Makeup

Lower suspended solids levels allow towers to operate at higher concentration cycles without experiencing fouling problems. This operational improvement reduces both blowdown volumes and makeup water consumption, delivering direct cost savings.

### Protects Equipment and Control Instrumentation

Cleaner circulating water prevents plugging and fouling of **nozzles, strainers, and fill media**. Clean water also keeps **sample lines, probes, and control valves** functioning reliably, which stabilizes automated treatment control systems.

### Practical Targets for Tower Applications

Deploy filtration when you observe rising turbidity or total suspended solids, basin sludge accumulation, recurring heat exchanger fouling, increasing approach temperatures, unstable biological control, or heavy environmental dust loading affecting system performance.

## Closed-Loop Systems: How Side-Stream Filtration Helps

Closed loops operate differently from cooling towers because they don't concentrate solids through evaporation. The dominant solids in these systems are typically **corrosion byproducts like magnetite and iron oxides**, plus construction debris in new or poorly cleaned installations.

### Removes Corrosion Byproducts That Foul Coils and Drive Under-Deposit Corrosion

Filtration reduces the inventory of circulating solids that settle in low-flow areas and create under-deposit conditions. These settled areas become sites where localized corrosion and microbiologically influenced corrosion (MIC) risks increase significantly. NIH guidance emphasizes that closed loops should maintain stability, noting that water losses, makeup additions, and contamination drive most problems. Filtration provides practical control over solids after addressing these root causes.

### Speeds Recovery After Poor Startup Cleaning and During Corrective Cleaning

New systems with inadequate cleaning and passivation generate substantial debris and corrosion products during initial operation. Side-stream filtration captures this washout material, reducing repeated strainer plugging and shortening the time needed to reach a stable, clean baseline condition.

### Improves Reliability of Monitoring and Chemical Control

Cleaner water reduces fouling at sample points and supports stable inhibitor residual control. This benefit becomes especially important when systems are actively transporting iron corrosion products that can interfere with chemical monitoring and treatment effectiveness.

### Important Limitation for Closed Loops

Side-stream filtration does **not** stop the source of solids generation. Systems with atmospheric expansion tanks, chronic makeup requirements, or oxygen ingress will continue generating magnetite corrosion products. Filtration helps manage the symptoms, but you must fix the underlying oxygen and makeup drivers to stop continued corrosion product formation.

## Implementation Guidance

### Sizing and Placement

Filter a **side stream** rather than the full system flow to avoid risking interruption of main cooling operations if a filter becomes loaded. Full-stream filtration remains less common due to higher costs and plugging risks that could shut down critical systems.

### Choosing Filtration Type Based on Particle Characteristics

**For towers:** Target sediment and organic dust using screen filters, media filters, or centrifugal separators as appropriate for the specific particle types and sizes present.

**For closed loops:** When solids consist largely of ferrous materials like magnetite, consider **magnetic separation** as part of the filtration strategy. Magnetic separators often prove highly effective for iron-based corrosion products. Base the final selection on actual solids characterization rather than assumptions.

## Key Takeaway

Install side stream filtration when you see rising turbidity, recurring fouling, or unstable chemical control. Target 10% of recirculation flow, and characterize your solids first — magnetic separation works exceptionally well for closed loop magnetite, while towers typically need conventional filtration for mixed environmental debris.
