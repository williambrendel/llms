# What types of filtration systems are used in open cooling tower and closed loop water systems?

## Executive Summary

Open cooling towers primarily use centrifugal separators, screen filters, disc filters, and sand filters to remove suspended solids, while closed-loop systems rely on magnetic separators for magnetite control, strainers for debris, and side-stream filters for fine particles.

## Overview

A single cooling tower can cycle through millions of gallons per day, collecting everything from airborne dust to corrosion byproducts that would otherwise accumulate and destroy heat transfer efficiency. Closed-loop systems face a different challenge—they generate their own contamination internally through corrosion, creating magnetic iron oxide particles that standard filters miss entirely.

## Open Cooling Tower Filtration Systems

Open cooling towers face constant contamination from environmental debris, requiring robust filtration to maintain heat transfer efficiency and prevent basin sludge buildup.

### Centrifugal Separators (Hydrocyclones)

Centrifugal separators excel at removing heavier, larger inorganic particles like sand and grit. These units typically capture particles in the 40–75 micron range, though performance varies by specific unit design. They offer low maintenance operation with no filter media to replace, making them cost-effective for first-stage treatment.

However, centrifugal separators perform poorly on light or organic particles. Most installations require additional "polishing" filtration downstream when very clean water is needed.

### Automatic Screen Filters

Screen filters provide automated backwash capability for general suspended solids removal. Modern units can capture particles down to approximately 10 microns, depending on screen specification. Their compact design and continuous operation make them popular for installations requiring minimal operator intervention.

The main drawbacks include performance dependency on particle shape and screen integrity, plus water loss during backwash cycles.

### Disc Filters

Disc filters occupy a similar automation niche as screen filters, offering fine solids capture with self-cleaning capability. They appear alongside screens in DOE/FEMP "core four" filtration recommendations for cooling towers.

Like screen filters, disc filters require backwash water and regular maintenance to maintain performance.

### Media Filters (Sand/Multi-Media)

Sand and multi-media filters provide the broadest solids removal spectrum, effectively capturing both heavy inorganics and lighter organic particles. These systems can materially reduce basin sludge accumulation and heat exchanger fouling.

DOE/FEMP guidance includes media filtration as a primary option for cooling tower applications. The tradeoffs include higher backwash water demand, larger footprint requirements, and the need for properly designed backwash systems.

### Microfiltration Polishing

Bag and cartridge filters serve as polishing stages when very low suspended solids levels are required. These systems often follow primary filtration, such as sand filtration followed by 1-micron bag filtration.

While microfiltration provides excellent fine particle capture, using it as the sole filtration method on dirty towers creates high consumable costs and frequent plugging issues.

## Closed-Loop System Filtration

Closed-loop systems generate contamination internally through corrosion processes, requiring specialized filtration approaches focused on magnetite and startup debris removal.

### Strainers (Y-Strainers/Basket Strainers)

Basic strainers protect equipment during commissioning and catch large debris throughout operation. Y-strainers and basket strainers offer simple, low-cost protection but require manual cleaning, which can be frequent in heavily contaminated systems.

These units provide coarse filtration only and do not address fine particle contamination.

### Cyclonic/Dirt Separators

Low-pressure drop separators remove heavier particulate matter through cyclonic action. These "dirt separators" provide continuous separation with minimal maintenance requirements.

They work well for bulk solids removal but cannot provide fine polishing by themselves.

### Magnetic Dirt Separators

Magnetic separators target ferrous particles and magnetite—the primary contaminants in closed-loop systems. Modern designs include easy blowdown cleaning systems for removing captured magnetic debris.

These units prove highly effective on magnetite but cannot remove non-ferrous particles unless combined with additional filtration media.

### Side-Stream Filter Vessels

Side-stream bag and cartridge systems provide fine filtration for non-magnetic debris and post-chemical-cleaning polishing. These systems offer flexible micron rating selection and excellent fine particle capture.

Manufacturers often package magnetic separation with side-stream filtration in integrated units. The main limitation involves consumable costs and plugging when applied to heavy solids loading without pre-separation.

### Air/Dirt Separators

Combination units remove both entrained air and dirt particles. Air removal prevents corrosion acceleration, while dirt removal prevents deposit formation. Some manufacturers integrate magnetic capture capability into air/dirt separator designs.

## Selection Guidelines

For cooling towers facing heavy grit and dust, start with centrifugal separation followed by screen, disc, or media polishing. Applications requiring fine solids removal with automation benefit from automatic screen or disc filters. When the cleanest possible water is needed or basin sludge reduction is critical, media filtration provides the best performance, sometimes with polishing bag or cartridge stages.

For closed-loop systems, magnetic separators deliver the highest return on investment when magnetite contamination is present. Strainers handle startup debris effectively, often combined with temporary high-capture bag or cartridge filtration. Persistent non-magnetic particles require side-stream bag or cartridge systems downstream of magnetic capture.

## Key Takeaways

Install magnetic separators first in closed-loop systems showing black sludge or magnetite—they typically provide immediate energy savings through improved heat transfer. For cooling towers, monitor suspended solids weekly and consider media filtration when levels consistently exceed 50 mg/L, as this threshold often indicates insufficient filtration capacity for optimal heat transfer performance.
