# What happens if I switch from potable water to partially softened reclaim water in an open cooling tower?

## Executive Summary

Switching to reclaimed water will reduce your cooling tower cycles from 6-9 down to approximately 3 cycles due to higher chloride and sulfate concentrations. The 95% softened reclaim blend offers no improvement over straight reclaimed water and actually increases corrosion risk due to near-zero hardness.

## Overview

Most facilities expect reclaimed water to deliver similar performance to potable water, but the reality hits when chloride levels jump 2.6 times higher. This Ashburn, Virginia case study reveals that a seemingly attractive 95% softened blend still carries the full corrosive load of the original reclaimed water while stripping away protective hardness minerals.

## Key Chemistry Differences

The makeup water analysis reveals the core challenge. Reclaimed water delivers 82 mg/L chloride compared to potable's 32 mg/L, while sulfate jumps from 55 mg/L to 130 mg/L. The 95% softened blend maintains these same aggressive anion levels but drops calcium hardness from 170 mg/L down to just 8.5 mg/L.

| **Parameter** | **Potable** | **Reclaimed** | **95% Softened + 5% Reclaim** |
|---------------|-------------|---------------|-------------------------------|
| Chloride (mg/L) | 32 | 82 | 82 |
| Sulfate (mg/L) | 55 | 130 | 130 |
| Alkalinity (mg/L as CaCO₃) | 74 | 140 | 140 |
| Ca Hardness (mg/L as CaCO₃) | 140 | 170 | 8.5 |
| Conductivity (µS/cm) | 480 | 860 | 860 |

Conductivity nearly doubles with reclaimed water, signaling higher dissolved solids that will concentrate rapidly in the cooling system.

## Corrosion Risk Assessment

The corrosion indices tell a clear story at 105°F operating temperature. The Larson-Skold ratio jumps from 1.38 for potable water to 1.79 for both reclaimed options. Values above 1.2 indicate high corrosion potential relative to the water's alkalinity buffering capacity.

| **Index** | **Potable** | **Reclaimed** | **95/5 Blend** |
|-----------|-------------|---------------|----------------|
| LSI | -0.37 | +0.40 | -0.90 |
| Ryznar | 8.00 | 6.90 | 9.50 |
| Puckorius | 7.98 | 6.90 | 9.51 |
| Larson–Skold | 1.38 | 1.79 | 1.79 |

The softened blend shows the worst corrosion profile with a Ryznar index of 9.50, indicating aggressive conditions that will attack carbon steel systems without robust inhibitor programs.

## Cycles of Concentration Limits

Sulfate constraints hit first with both reclaimed options. Using a conservative 400 mg/L recirculation limit, potable water achieves approximately 7 cycles while reclaimed water tops out at 3 cycles.

Chloride limits paint an even starker picture:

| **Recirc Cl Limit** | **Potable** | **Reclaimed / Blend** |
|---------------------|-------------|----------------------|
| 250 mg/L | 7.8 | 3.0 |
| 300 mg/L | 9.4 | 3.7 |
| 500 mg/L | 15.6 | 6.1 |

Combined chloride plus sulfate limits, following BAC-style guidance, constrain reclaimed systems even further:

| **Combined Limit** | **Potable** | **Reclaimed / Blend** |
|-------------------|-------------|----------------------|
| 500 mg/L | 5.8 | 2.4 |
| 650 mg/L | 7.5 | 3.1 |

Conservative operation with reclaimed water will typically limit you to 2.5-3.5 cycles regardless of the softening treatment.

## Metallurgy Considerations

Carbon steel and copper alloy systems face the greatest constraints with reclaimed water. Expect chloride and sulfate to control your operating envelope at approximately 3 cycles, requiring aggressive inhibitor programs and continuous corrosion monitoring.

304 stainless steel components approach their chloride tolerance limits around 200-300 mg/L in recirculation, which reclaimed water hits at just 3 cycles. Temperature control and deposit prevention become critical.

316 stainless steel offers more flexibility, tolerating 500-1000 mg/L chloride in typical screening scenarios. This metallurgy could potentially support 5-6+ cycles, though sulfate and combined anion limits may still constrain operation.

## Operational Reality Check

Potable water systems typically operate at 6-9+ cycles under conservative screening limits, with lower corrosion burden and manageable water chemistry. The predictable mineral profile makes treatment straightforward.

Reclaimed water systems face sulfate and chloride constraints that kick in immediately, creating higher corrosion and pitting risks. Biological nutrient loading typically increases, demanding tighter monitoring and enhanced solids control programs.

The 95% softened reclaim blend delivers the worst of both worlds - the same aggressive anion profile as straight reclaimed water, but with increased corrosion tendency due to the loss of protective hardness minerals.

## Economic and Operational Tradeoffs

Your total cost of operations depends on three key factors: water pricing and availability, sewer pricing, and water use efficiency optics. Lower cycles mean higher makeup water consumption and increased blowdown volumes, potentially offsetting any cost savings from using reclaimed water.

The operational burden increases significantly with enhanced monitoring requirements, more aggressive chemical treatment programs, and potential metallurgy upgrades to handle the corrosive environment.

## Key Takeaways

Test your specific reclaimed water source monthly for chloride and sulfate levels, as these will determine your maximum achievable cycles. Plan for conservative operation at 3 cycles maximum without metallurgy upgrades. Consider 316 stainless steel for critical components if you need to push beyond 3 cycles while maintaining system reliability.
