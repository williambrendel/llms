# How do I remove biofouling from my cooling system (open and closed)?

## Executive Summary

Biofouling removal requires a combined approach: (1) physically remove deposits, (2) chemically detach and kill remaining organisms, and (3) prevent rapid regrowth by correcting root causes (solids, hydraulics, nutrients, and inadequate biocide delivery). Open recirculating systems need more aggressive physical cleaning, solids control, and ongoing oxidizing and non-oxidizing biocide programs because they are continuously seeded from air and make up water.

## Overview

Closed loops usually require disinfection primarily after contamination events, poor chemistry control, oxygen ingress, or commissioning and major maintenance. Biofouling there is often tied to corrosion products, dirt ingress, and low-flow zones rather than continuous external seeding. The key difference between open and closed systems determines your cleaning strategy and prevention approach.

## Confirm it is biofouling and define its extent (before removal)

### Identify where it is (map the system)

**Open system high-probability locations:**
- Cooling tower basin corners, sumps, strainers, distribution deck, spray nozzles, fill edges, drift eliminators
- Condenser-water piping low points, dead legs, idle branches
- Heat exchanger inlet zones and low-velocity channels

**Closed loop high-probability locations:**
- Low-flow branches, idle coils, bypasses, and dead legs
- Strainers, dirt separators, low points, and air separators
- Heat exchangers (water-side inlet zones), expansion tank connections, and high points with poor venting

### Confirm biofouling (do not rely only on bulk water)

**Visual and tactile inspection:**
Look for slime, gelatinous films, stringy growth, musty odor, dark or green films.

**Surface sampling:**
Swab known surface area and test biological activity (adenosine triphosphate) or culture as needed.

**Operational indicators:**
Rising heat exchanger approach temperature, rising differential pressure, unstable disinfectant residual.

## Remove biofouling in open recirculating cooling systems (cooling tower + condenser loop)

### Make the system safe and controllable before cleaning

**Control aerosols and exposure:**
- Restrict access around the tower
- Use protective equipment per product label and safety data sheet (minimum: chemical gloves, splash goggles, protective clothing; add face shield and respiratory protection if mist or vapor exposure is plausible)

**Stabilize hydraulics for cleaning:**
- Confirm pumps and flow paths are correct and all intended valves are open
- Identify and isolate dead legs if possible
- Confirm blowdown control does not flush chemical during planned contact time

### Physical removal (this is the highest-yield step)

**Cooling tower basin and sump:**
- Remove debris mechanically (screens, vacuum, skimming)
- Drain and clean basin surfaces (corners, seams, under strainers)
- Clean and disinfect basin strainers and suction screens

**Distribution system and fill:**
- Remove and clean plugged spray nozzles and distribution troughs
- Inspect fill for matting and plugging; clean or replace if heavily colonized
- Clean drift eliminators and louvers where slime accumulates

**Condenser-water piping components:**
- Open and clean Y-strainers and baskets
- Flush low points and dead ends at high velocity where feasible

### Chemical detachment (disperse and lift the remaining biofilm)

**Apply a biodispersant and deposit conditioner:**
- Use a dispersant compatible with your biocide program and metallurgy
- Circulate long enough to detach biofilm and suspend solids
- Run side stream filtration (if installed) during dispersant circulation to remove lifted material

**Remove the detached material:**
- Increase blowdown temporarily to export suspended solids (verify discharge constraints)
- Clean strainers repeatedly during this stage (expect rapid loading)

### Chemical kill (after detachment, not instead of detachment)

**Oxidizing biocide "shock" or high-dose event:**
- Apply per label and site plan to achieve the required concentration and contact time
- Verify residual at the worst-circulated location, not only at the chemical feed point

**Non-oxidizing biocide slug (if biofilm is persistent):**
- Apply as a follow-on slug per label (common when biofilm is mature)
- Ensure contact time is achievable given blowdown and system turnover

### Verification and stabilization (prevent rebound)

**Verify cleaning effectiveness:**
- Visual inspection of basin and strainers after 24–72 hours of operation
- Repeat surface adenosine triphosphate swabs at the same location
- Confirm heat exchanger approach and differential pressure return toward baseline

**Prevent rapid regrowth:**
- Maintain stable oxidizing residual (or equivalent primary control) during operation
- Use non-oxidizing biocide rotation as needed for biofilm control
- Add or optimize side stream filtration to reduce sediment habitat
- Correct hydraulics: eliminate dead legs and improve circulation in low-flow zones

## Remove biofouling in closed cooling or heating loops (closed circuit)

### Confirm whether biofouling is the primary problem

Closed loops often have mixed fouling. Biofilm plus corrosion products (iron oxide) is common when oxygen ingress occurs. Slime complaints may actually be iron bacteria plus corrosion transport.

### Mechanical and hydraulic preparation

**Improve venting and remove trapped air:**
- Confirm air separators work and vents are open
- Eliminate chronic makeup addition (it continuously adds oxygen and nutrients)

**Remove solids before disinfection:**
- Clean strainers and dirt separators
- Install or operate side stream filtration for several days before chemical cleaning if solids are high

### Chemical cleaning and disinfection sequence (closed loop best practice)

**Step 1: Circulate a compatible cleaner and dispersant**
- Use a closed-loop cleaner formulated for organic and biofilm deposits if biofilm is confirmed
- Circulate at temperature and flow recommended by the product for the required duration
- Filter continuously and clean strainers frequently

**Step 2: Flush thoroughly**
- Remove suspended material until flush water is clear and stable
- Flush until key indicators stabilize (turbidity, iron transport)

**Step 3: Disinfect with an appropriate biocide (shock dose)**
- Apply a biocide suitable for closed loops and compatible with metallurgy and seals
- Hold for the required contact time (closed loops can usually hold contact time well if isolated)

**Step 4: Final flush (if required by product) and re-passivate/protect**
- Add corrosion inhibitor package for closed loops (film-forming inhibitor and pH control as applicable)
- Confirm target pH and inhibitor residual

### Verification and prevention in closed loops

**Verify removal:**
- Trend differential pressure and heat transfer approach across coils/heat exchangers
- Repeat surface checks where accessible (strainer slime, coupon surfaces if used)
- Biological indicators should trend down and remain stable

**Prevent recurrence:**
- Eliminate oxygen ingress (fix leaks, maintain expansion tank integrity)
- Maintain stable chemistry (pH, inhibitor residual) and avoid frequent makeup
- Maintain filtration and periodic solids blowdown from separators

## Decision triggers for escalation (when routine steps are not enough)

### If biofouling returns within days to weeks

Root cause is likely not removed:
- Uncontrolled sediment loading (no filtration, dirty basin)
- Poor circulation and dead legs reseeding the system
- Oxidizer demand too high (organics, deposits) collapsing residual
- Inadequate contact time due to high blowdown during slug events

### If heat transfer and pressure drop do not improve after cleaning

Fouling may be mixed or hard scale dominates. Perform deposit analysis and adjust scale control and cleaning approach.

## Key Takeaways

Focus on physical removal first—it delivers the highest yield in deposit elimination. For open systems, maintain continuous oxidizing residual and implement side stream filtration to prevent recolonization. For closed loops, eliminate oxygen ingress and maintain stable inhibitor chemistry. If biofouling returns within 2-4 weeks, the root cause remains unaddressed: check circulation patterns, filtration capacity, and chemical contact time at the worst-case locations.
