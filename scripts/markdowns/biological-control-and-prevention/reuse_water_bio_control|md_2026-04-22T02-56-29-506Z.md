# How do you control biological growth in cooling towers using reclaimed water?

## Executive Summary

Cooling towers using reclaimed water require a multi-layered biocide program combining oxidizing agents (3-7 ppm hypobromous acid), biodispersants, and non-oxidizing biocides, with weekly microbial testing to maintain total coliforms below 100 CFU/100 mL and prevent pathogen growth.

## Overview

Data centers cooling with reclaimed water face a biological control challenge that municipal water users never encounter. Tertiary-treated reclaimed water carries nutrient loads and microbial populations that can explode into biofilm colonies within days, potentially harboring pathogens like *E. coli* in the very systems meant to keep critical infrastructure cool.

## Chemical Treatment Program

The foundation of reclaimed water microbial control rests on three coordinated chemical interventions, each targeting different aspects of biological growth.

### Oxidizing Biocide (Primary Disinfectant)

The primary weapon against microbial growth uses hypobromous acid generated on-site by combining sodium bromide with sodium hypochlorite. This approach delivers 3-7 ppm free halogen as slug doses rather than continuous feed.

**What it measures:** The oxidizing power available to kill vegetative bacteria and viruses on contact.

Formula:
```
NaBr + NaOCl → HOBr + NaCl
```

**Example:** A 500-ton cooling tower receiving a 5 ppm slug dose maintains 1.5 ppm free halogen residual at the far end of the system two hours after injection, indicating sufficient oxidant distribution throughout the entire loop.

Summer operations demand this oxidizing treatment three times weekly, while cooler months require only twice-weekly application. Each treatment must maintain contact for at least two hours, with target residuals of 1.0-2.0 ppm free halogen measured at the system's furthest point from injection.

### Biodispersant

Biodispersants break apart established biofilm matrices that protect bacteria from oxidizing biocides. These non-ionic or amphoteric compounds dose at 20-50 ppm, applied 30 minutes before every oxidizer treatment to maximize penetration.

The timing proves critical—dispersants need sufficient contact time to disrupt biofilm structure before oxidizers arrive to kill the newly exposed microorganisms underneath.

### Non-Oxidizing Biocide

Glutaraldehyde, DBNPA, or isothiazolone compounds provide penetrative kill capability that oxidizers cannot achieve alone. Applied weekly at 100-300 ppm active ingredient, these biocides maintain residual protection between oxidizer slug doses.

Unlike oxidizing biocides that react quickly and dissipate, non-oxidizing biocides persist longer in the system, providing sustained antimicrobial activity even in biofilm-protected environments.

## Monitoring and Testing Requirements

Effective biological control demands frequent testing across multiple parameters, each revealing different aspects of microbial activity.

| **Test** | **Frequency** | **Target / Limit** |
|----------|---------------|-------------------|
| Free halogen residual | 3×/week after slug dose | ≥1.0 ppm (far point) |
| Total viable plate count (TVPC) | Weekly | <10⁴ CFU/mL |
| Total coliforms (MPN) | Weekly | <100 CFU/100 mL |
| *E. coli* (if detected coliforms) | Weekly or confirmatory | Non-detect |
| ORP (if used) | Continuous | >650 mV (with calibration) |
| ATP (optional) | Weekly | <10 pg/mL |
| Turbidity | Weekly | <5 NTU |

Total viable plate counts reveal overall bacterial populations, while coliform testing specifically targets enteric bacteria that indicate fecal contamination. The 100 CFU/100 mL coliform limit represents the threshold where pathogen risk becomes unacceptable for cooling tower operations.

Sampling locations matter as much as testing frequency. Dead zones in basin corners, behind drift eliminators, and in low-flow areas often harbor the highest microbial concentrations, making these critical monitoring points.

## Corrective Actions and System Response

When microbial populations exceed control limits, rapid response prevents system-wide contamination that could take weeks to remediate.

| **Condition** | **Corrective Action** |
|---------------|----------------------|
| TVPC > 10⁴ CFU/mL | Increase biocide dose and frequency; inspect for biofilm |
| Total coliforms > 100 CFU/100 mL | Increase oxidizer frequency and review dispersant program |
| *E. coli* detected | Immediate escalation: shock dose + root cause investigation |
| Biofilm observed | Immediate dispersant + oxidizer pairing; flush low-flow zones |

*E. coli* detection triggers immediate escalation protocols because this pathogen indicates recent fecal contamination and potential presence of other dangerous microorganisms. Shock dosing with oxidizers while investigating the contamination source prevents further proliferation.

Visual biofilm formation signals that the chemical program has failed to prevent surface colonization. These situations require aggressive dispersant treatment followed immediately by oxidizer application to penetrate and kill the established microbial communities.

## Preventive Maintenance

Physical cleaning complements chemical treatment by removing the organic matter that feeds microbial growth and provides biofilm attachment sites.

| **Task** | **Frequency** |
|----------|---------------|
| Basin vacuum and sludge removal | Quarterly |
| Offline chemical clean of fill pack | Semi-annually |
| Filter media changeout | Per pressure drop or quarterly minimum |

Quarterly basin cleaning removes settled organic matter that creates anaerobic zones where sulfate-reducing bacteria can establish. Semi-annual fill pack cleaning eliminates biofilm accumulation in the high-surface-area heat transfer media where most biological growth occurs.

## Key Takeaways

Successful biological control in reclaimed water cooling systems requires testing total coliforms weekly with results below 100 CFU/100 mL, applying oxidizing biocides at 3-7 ppm at least twice weekly, and maintaining detailed dosing logs to demonstrate regulatory compliance and optimize treatment effectiveness.
