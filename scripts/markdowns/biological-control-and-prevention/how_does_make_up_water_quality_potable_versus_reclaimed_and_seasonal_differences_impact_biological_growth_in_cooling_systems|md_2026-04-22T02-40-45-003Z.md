# How does make up water quality (potable versus reclaimed) and seasonal differences impact biological growth in cooling systems?

## Executive Summary

Make up water quality changes biological growth risk by altering microbial seed, nutrient loading, and chemical demand in cooling systems. Reclaimed water typically introduces more nutrients and microorganisms than potable water, increasing biofouling pressure and requiring strengthened control strategies.

## Overview

Reclaimed water can consume 2-3 times more disinfectant than potable water before achieving the same residual levels. This higher chemical demand creates under-treated zones where biofilms persist, especially during peak summer temperatures when biological activity accelerates. The combination of nutrient-rich makeup water and seasonal temperature swings creates a shifting target for microbial control.

## How Make Up Water Quality Changes Biological Growth Conditions

### Microbial Seed and Nutrient Input

Potable makeup water generally delivers lower nutrient and microbial loading than reclaimed sources, but still contains microorganisms and organics that support growth once concentrated and warmed in the tower. The controlled treatment process removes most pathogens and reduces organic carbon levels.

Reclaimed makeup water introduces a fundamentally different challenge. Reuse of wastewater for cooling adds nutrients and contributes large amounts of microorganisms to the cooling system, increasing the environment for microbial growth. Recycled water commonly contains higher nutrient levels that sustain bacterial growth, and increasing cycles of concentration can intensify biofilm risk.

### Increased Chemical Demand and Reduced Control Margin

Higher organic and nutrient loading increases disinfectant or biocide demand significantly. More oxidizer or biocide gets consumed before an effective residual remains at surfaces, which increases the risk of under-treated zones and biofilm persistence, especially in low-flow areas.

### Higher Suspended Solids and Sediment Potential

Solids increase sediment and biofilm habitat throughout the system. Sediment and biofilm are identified as key factors affecting Legionella growth in cooling towers; makeup water that drives more sediment increases growth pressure and creates protected environments where disinfectants cannot penetrate effectively.

## Seasonal Differences That Increase or Decrease Biological Growth Risk

### Temperature and Warm-Season Operation

Higher ambient temperatures commonly increase biological growth pressure across all system components. Temperature is one of the key factors affecting Legionella growth in cooling towers, with growth rates doubling for every 10°C increase within the optimal range.

Warm weather can increase biohazard considerations when reclaimed water is used. Reclaimed makeup water evaluations note the importance of assessing biological hazards, including in hot weather conditions when pathogen survival and multiplication accelerate.

### Sunlight Effects

More sunlight increases algae and photosynthetic growth potential in exposed tower sections. Algae and similar growth increase organic loading and can reinforce biofilm formation, with the highest concentrations occurring in sunlit tower zones where light penetration supports photosynthesis.

### Water Age Changes with Load and Operating Pattern

Lower load or intermittent operation increases water age and stagnation risk. Water age is a key factor affecting Legionella growth in cooling towers, as longer residence times allow more opportunity for biofilm establishment and pathogen multiplication in low-velocity zones.

### Disinfectant Residual Control Challenges During Transitions

Startup, shoulder seasons, and variable load can destabilize residual control systems. Disinfectant residual is a key factor affecting Legionella growth; variability increases risk of excursions if control strategies are not adjusted for changing conditions and chemical demand.

## Practical Implications for Detection and Control

### Risk-Based Monitoring Focus

Trending the four key growth drivers provides the most effective early warning system:

- **Sediment and biofilm** accumulation in low-flow areas
- **Temperature** patterns and thermal stratification  
- **Water age** during variable load conditions
- **Disinfectant residual** consistency and distribution

### Program Upgrades for Reclaimed Water Use

Pretreatment and solids control become critical when switching to reclaimed sources. Increased filtration or side stream filtration reduces sediment habitat, though specific requirements depend on source water characteristics and system design.

More robust microbial control strategies must account for higher baseline demand. This includes adjusting disinfectant or biocide dosing rates and increasing verification frequency to maintain effective residuals throughout the system, particularly during peak biological activity periods.

## Key Takeaways

Monitor disinfectant residuals daily when using reclaimed water, maintaining minimum levels 50% higher than potable water requirements. Install side-stream filtration to reduce sediment accumulation, and increase biocide dosing by 25-40% during summer months when water temperatures exceed 25°C. Consider UV pretreatment for reclaimed sources with high organic carbon content above 10 mg/L.
