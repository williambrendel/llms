# How does the cause and control of E. coli differ from Total Viable Plate Count in towers and other aerosol forming units?

## Executive Summary

E. coli indicates fecal contamination requiring immediate source investigation and potential shutdown, while Total Viable Plate Count reflects general biofouling managed through routine chemistry and cleaning protocols.

## Overview

When E. coli appears in a cooling tower, you're likely dealing with bird droppings or a cross-connection problem. When Total Viable Plate Count spikes, your biocide program probably needs attention. The difference matters because cooling towers aerosolize water beyond the site, turning what might be a routine water quality issue into a potential public health concern.

## What the Results Mean in Aerosol-Forming Systems

**E. coli** serves as a fecal indicator organism. Its presence strongly suggests recent fecal contamination and the possible presence of other pathogens associated with fecal material.

**Total Viable Plate Count (TVC/HPC)** provides a non-specific estimate of live, culturable heterotrophs. Operators use it to track overall water quality and biological condition, detecting changes and trends over time.

In aerosol-forming units such as cooling towers, the concern amplifies because towers aerosolize water, which can spread microorganisms beyond the site.

## How Causes Differ

### Excessive E. coli in Cooling Towers

Excessive E. coli most likely indicates a contamination pathway — a "sanitary breach" — not normal tower biofouling. Common sources include:

**Fecal loading from birds and rodents** through droppings entering basins, sumps, and drift eliminator areas represents the most frequent cause.

**Cross-connection, backflow, or contamination of makeup water** occurs through temporary hoses, improper air gaps, and failed backflow prevention devices.

**Contaminated alternative water sources** such as stormwater, recycled water, or surface water used as makeup without adequate treatment introduce fecal indicators.

**Maintenance and construction contamination** happens when basins remain open during work or debris enters the system.

Because E. coli serves specifically as a fecal contamination indicator, a positive result should be treated as evidence of source intrusion until proven otherwise. In towers, E. coli is not a typical routine control metric the way HPC is — it usually signals something abnormal about the source or exposure pathway.

### Excessive TVC/HPC in Cooling Towers

Excessive TVC/HPC usually indicates system-wide biological growth conditions:

- Loss of oxidant control and poor residual distribution
- High nutrients and organics combined with warm conditions  
- Poor solids control, sediment accumulation, biofilm formation, and inadequate cleaning
- Stagnant zones and intermittent operation

HPC explicitly indicates general water quality and can flag water quality problems when it rises significantly.

## How Control Strategies Differ

### E. coli Control: Treat as Sanitary Incident

The primary objective focuses on eliminating the contamination source and pathway while reducing immediate aerosol risk.

**Immediate operational risk control** requires considering restriction of the aerosolizing device operation until controls are restored, especially if public exposure is plausible.

**Find and correct the contamination pathway** through systematic investigation:

- Inspect for wildlife access at screens, covers, drift eliminators, and basin access points; remove standing debris and implement exclusion measures
- Verify makeup water protection by checking backflow preventer status, air gaps, hose bib connections, temporary makeup arrangements, and recent plumbing changes

**Sanitary cleaning and disinfection response** includes:

- Mechanical cleaning of basins and sumps
- Disinfection consistent with tower emergency cleaning guidance
- Resampling to confirm resolution at incoming makeup, basin, downstream points, and any suspect locations
- Documentation of all corrective actions

**Escalation and notification** depends on jurisdiction and context. Coordinate with the Authority Having Jurisdiction if potential exposure exists, illness concerns arise, or local rules require notification.

The key difference from HPC control: prioritize source control and pathway correction first, not just "increase biocide."

### TVC/HPC Control: Treat as Biofouling

The primary objective centers on restoring routine control of growth drivers through process optimization.

**Verify oxidant feed and control** by confirming residual achievement at representative points throughout the system.

**Improve solids and deposit control** through blowdown optimization, sidestream filtration performance, strainer maintenance, and basin cleanliness. Towers require strong scale, corrosion, and sediment control paired with regular cleaning practices.

**Optimize biocide programs** using oxidizing and non-oxidizing rotation as specified in treatment plans, combined with dispersants and removal of loosened solids.

**Address stagnation and dead zones** while improving intermittent operation practices.

The key difference from E. coli control: HPC management is primarily process control through chemistry, cleaning, and hydraulics — not an intrusion investigation.

## Practical Interpretation for Operators

**High E. coli signals** "fecal contamination pathway likely" — investigate intrusion sources, wildlife access, and makeup protection; consider shutdown or restriction; clean and disinfect; resample to verify correction.

**High HPC/TVC indicates** "general biofouling" — restore residual control, improve deposits and solids management, implement routine cleaning, and trend HPC over time to verify sustained improvement.

## Key Takeaway

Monitor E. coli monthly in aerosol-forming systems, investigate any detection above 1 CFU/100mL as a sanitary breach, and prioritize source elimination over chemical treatment. For HPC, maintain levels below 10,000 CFU/mL through consistent oxidant residuals and regular cleaning, treating spikes as biofouling rather than contamination events.
