# How should systems be treated after a biological excursion or upset?

## Executive Summary

After a biological excursion, treat the system in two phases: rapid stabilization to restore effective control immediately, and root-cause remediation to remove reservoirs and fix why control was lost. A single exceedance may require confirmation sampling plus short-term corrections; repeated or severe exceedances require full remediation including physical cleaning, enhanced solids removal, verified biocide exposure, and increased monitoring until control is stable.

## Overview

A cooling tower that tests positive for Legionella at 10,000 CFU/mL can drop to undetectable levels in 72 hours with the right response—or it can climb to 100,000 CFU/mL and spread throughout the building if you guess wrong about what caused the problem. The difference lies in understanding that biological excursions have two phases: the immediate crisis that needs rapid stabilization, and the underlying reservoir that needs systematic elimination.

## Immediate Response (Same Shift; First 0–4 Hours)

### Confirm the Excursion is Real and Classify Severity

Start by verifying the result itself. Confirm sample location, time, container, preservation, and chain-of-custody requirements. A contaminated sample container can create a false positive that triggers unnecessary emergency responses.

Classify what you're dealing with: single small exceedance versus major spike, single location versus multiple locations, and indicator excursion versus pathogen-specific result. A single borderline result at one location suggests a different response than simultaneous high counts at multiple sampling points.

### Protect People and Assets if Risk is High

For aerosol-generating systems with high-risk results, restrict access and reduce exposure opportunity near the source until control is restored. When chemical controls will be intensified, confirm safety controls including personal protective equipment, ventilation, eyewash, spill kit, and chemical segregation.

### Capture System State Data Before Changing Anything

Record the current operating snapshot before making changes: water temperatures, conductivity and pH, blowdown rate and makeup rate, disinfectant or biocide feed status and measured residual at the control point, pump status, flow indication, and valve positions—especially bypasses and three-way valves. This data helps identify what failed and prevents repeating the same mistake.

## Rapid Stabilization Actions (First 4–24 Hours)

### Restore Disinfectant or Biocide Control Immediately

For oxidizing programs, confirm feed equipment is functioning by checking pump stroke, suction, injection quill, and control signal. Measure oxidizing residual at the chemical feed point, the worst-circulated or most remote point, and a known "first-foul" location like basin corners, strainer outlets, or heat exchanger inlets.

When residual is zero or unstable, treat it as a demand or delivery failure rather than assuming dose alone will solve it. Increase feed per the site plan and product directions, then re-check residual at the limiting location.

For non-oxidizing programs, apply a corrective slug dose per product directions and site plan when indicated. Ensure contact time is achievable by avoiding heavy blowdown during the slug window.

### Stop the Common "Silent Causes" of Excursions

Restore circulation by eliminating unintended bypassing, opening any partially closed isolation valves left after maintenance, and confirming pumps are delivering expected differential pressure or flow.

Address sudden oxidizer demand spikes by looking for process leaks introducing organics, heavy solids disturbance from recent mechanical cleaning or basin sludge mixing, and sudden makeup water quality shifts from seasonal changes or source changes.

### Begin Solids and Deposit Export

Clean and re-clean strainers and screens, recording how quickly they foul again as an indicator of system loading. Increase solids removal by operating side stream filtration if available and temporarily increasing blowdown to export suspended material when safe and permitted.

## Escalation Logic (Single Exceedance Versus Persistent Exceedance)

### Single Exceedance (One-Time)

Treat single exceedances as "corrective action with confirmation." Re-sample promptly at the same location, confirm stable residual and stable key control parameters for at least 24–72 hours. If the re-test returns to normal and controls are stable, return to routine monitoring frequency.

### Persistent or Severe Exceedance (Repeated or Multi-Location)

Treat persistent or severe exceedances as "remediation required." Assume a reservoir exists in biofilm, sediment, dead legs, low-flow zones, or fouled heat exchanger surfaces. Proceed to mechanical cleaning plus chemical detachment plus verified kill plus export plus validation.

## Mechanical Cleaning and Deposit Removal

### Open Recirculating Systems (Cooling Tower and Condenser-Water)

For basin and sump areas, remove debris and sludge by vacuuming, scooping, or drain-and-clean if heavy. Clean basin corners, seams, and areas under suction screens where deposits accumulate.

For distribution and surfaces, clean spray nozzles and distribution troughs. Inspect fill for matting and clean or replace if heavily fouled.

For piping and heat exchangers, clean strainers repeatedly until loading normalizes. If differential pressure rises across a heat exchanger, isolate and clean it.

### Closed Loops

Remove solids first by cleaning strainers and dirt separators, then operate filtration continuously during cleanup. Correct oxygen entry by fixing leaks and chronic makeup, and restore proper expansion tank and venting function.

## Chemical Detachment and Kill Sequence

### Detachment Step (Break the Biofilm Matrix)

Apply a biodispersant or surfactant compatible with the program. Circulate long enough to loosen deposits, then remove loosened material concurrently via filtration and strainer cleaning.

### Kill Step (After Detachment, Not Instead of Detachment)

For oxidizing biocide high-dose events, achieve a measurable residual at the limiting location and hold for the required contact time per product and plan.

For non-oxidizing biocide slugs commonly used for persistent biofilm, apply after oxidizer when indicated and control blowdown to preserve contact time.

### Export Step (Flush Out What You Detached and Killed)

Flush and filter until suspended solids stabilize. Continue side stream filtration for multiple turnover cycles and resume normal blowdown only after water clears and residual control is stable.

## Verification and Validation

### Verification (Did We Do What We Said We Would Do?)

Record actions and results including chemical dose, timing, and measured residual at multiple points. Document valve lineups, pump status, filtration run time, and cleaning performed with areas addressed.

### Validation (Did It Work?)

Increase monitoring frequency temporarily with daily residual and key control parameters until stable. Repeat biological testing at the same locations as the excursion samples plus at least one "worst-case" location with low flow, remote access, or first-foul conditions.

Confirm performance recovery by verifying that heat exchanger approach temperatures normalize and differential pressure trends stabilize or improve.

## Root-Cause Prevention Changes

### Fix Delivery and Hydraulics

Eliminate dead legs or add controlled flush points. Confirm minimum flow paths in all operating modes including summer, winter economizer, and partial load conditions.

### Improve Solids Control

Add or optimize side stream filtration and increase basin cleaning frequency if solids loading is high.

### Reduce Nutrient and Demand Sources

Stop process leaks and organic intrusion. Review makeup water changes and seasonal impacts that increase biological growth potential.

### Tighten Control Limits and Response Steps

Define an alert level and an action level with clear escalation criteria. Specify what happens after one exceedance versus repeated exceedances to prevent both over-reaction and under-reaction.

## Key Takeaway

Implement a two-tier response system: single exceedances get 24-72 hour confirmation monitoring with basic corrective actions, while repeated or multi-location exceedances trigger full remediation with mechanical cleaning, verified biocide contact time at limiting locations, and validation testing until three consecutive samples show control restoration. Monitor disinfectant residual daily at the worst-circulated location until biological results return to normal range for at least one week.
