# How Do Coliforms Enter Cooling Towers When Using Clean Makeup Water?

## Executive Summary

Coliforms routinely appear in cooling towers fed with potable water through airborne contamination, biofilm reservoirs, and system component infiltration—not just fecal contamination. When reclaimed water serves as makeup, coliform presence becomes constant and requires intensified biocide programs, weekly monitoring, and mandatory biodispersant treatment.

## Overview

A cooling tower using city water can still test positive for coliforms every month, even with zero sewage intrusion. The tower's open design makes it a collection point for environmental coliforms from soil dust, plant debris, and bird droppings that multiply rapidly in warm, nutrient-rich conditions. With reclaimed water makeup, baseline coliform counts jump from near-zero to thousands of colony-forming units per 100 mL, transforming occasional contamination into a continuous management challenge.

## How Coliforms Enter Under Normal Conditions

**Airborne Intrusion (Most Common)**

Cooling towers operate as open atmospheric systems, efficiently collecting airborne particles including soil dust rich in environmental coliforms, plant debris and pollen, bird droppings containing both fecal and environmental coliforms, and insects and spiders that decompose in basins or drift eliminators. Once in the water, coliforms exploit the warm, nutrient-laden environment to multiply rapidly, especially without continuous biocide control.

**Biofilm Reservoirs**

Coliforms persist in biofilms formed by other organisms like _Pseudomonas_ or algae. These protective films promote slow, chronic low-level coliform growth, resist eradication even with slug disinfection, and create false assurance of "clean" systems based solely on residual oxidant readings.

**Makeup Water Fluctuations**

Even potable water carries low-level coliforms within EPA limits—less than 5% positive samples per month for total coliforms in treated drinking water. Fluctuations in chlorine residual, construction disturbance, or biofilm release in distribution systems can allow transient seeding of towers. Dead legs or underused makeup lines also become biofilm reservoirs.

**System Components as Reservoirs**

Contaminants enter through unsealed makeup tank floats, overflows, or vents; fouled filtration systems that discharge microbial loads during backwash; and poorly maintained strainers, drift eliminators, or overflow basins.

**Cross-contamination by Personnel or Tools**

Maintenance tools, hoses, or sample bottles rinsed with contaminated water introduce coliforms, as does foot traffic through bird-fouled rooftops entering water contact zones. Occasional manual dumping of condensate drains or wetted vacuum lines also contributes.

**Total Coliform vs. Fecal Coliform**

Most positive total coliform results involve non-fecal species like _Enterobacter_ or _Citrobacter_, which are ubiquitous in soil, plants, and water. Fecal coliforms like _E. coli_ rarely appear in potable-fed towers and indicate true sanitary breaches such as cross-connections or bird nesting on fill.

## Best Practices to Minimize Intrusion

| **Practice** | **Purpose** |
|--------------|-------------|
| Seal vented makeup tanks | Prevent airborne dust/debris ingress |
| Regular biocide + biodispersant | Suppress biofilm that shelters coliforms |
| Backflush filters and clean strainers | Remove trapped biomass before release |
| Inspect for organic debris on rooftop | Remove leaf litter, bird droppings |
| Test total coliforms monthly | Use as early indicator of contamination |
| Distinguish total vs. fecal coliform | To determine if sanitary source is involved |

## Coliforms in Reclaimed Water Systems

When reclaimed water serves as makeup source in data center cooling towers, coliform introduction, persistence, and control dynamics shift significantly.

| **Factor** | **Potable Water Makeup** | **Reclaimed Water Makeup** |
|------------|--------------------------|----------------------------|
| **Baseline coliform count** | Near-zero (post-disinfection) | Detectable to high (~10²–10⁴ CFU/100 mL common) |
| **Nutrient content** | Very low | High in TOC, ammonia, phosphorus |
| **Background oxidant demand** | Low | High—HOBr demand spikes immediately |
| **Biofilm pressure** | Low-moderate | High—rapid growth of protective slime layers |
| **Algae potential** | Moderate (seasonal) | High, even in off-season (due to nutrients) |
| **Dispersant need** | Situational | **Critical**—to prevent fouling and release trapped biomass |
| **Pathogen risk** | Minimal | Elevated—coliforms may include **fecal indicators** or **opportunistic pathogens** like _Pseudomonas_ or _Legionella_ |

## Key Changes in Risk Profile with Reclaimed Water

**Coliforms Are No Longer Incidental**

In reclaimed water systems, coliforms are always present in makeup and continuously reseed the system. Even chlorinated reclaimed water can seed coliforms if residual dissipates before reaching the tower basin.

**Biocide Dose Must Increase**

Reclaimed water contains higher oxidant demand from chloramines, TOC, and nitrite that rapidly consume HOBr or chlorine. Slug dosing at 5–7 ppm HOBr may be entirely consumed before achieving full disinfection, particularly in warm weather. Routine addition of non-oxidizing biocides becomes mandatory for residual control.

**Dispersant Is No Longer Optional**

Nutrients in reclaimed water lead to rapid biofilm development, especially in shaded, low-flow areas. Dispersants prevent early colonization of slime-forming bacteria, expose coliforms embedded in protective matrices, and keep debris and biomass in suspension for filter removal.

**Routine Monitoring Must Intensify**

Monthly coliform testing proves inadequate; weekly or biweekly testing during warm seasons becomes necessary. Test for total coliforms, _E. coli_, TVPC, and biofilm indicators using ATP or bio-luminescence swabs. Set internal action limits far tighter than municipal standards: TVPC ≤10⁴ CFU/mL, total coliforms <100 CFU/100 mL, and _E. coli_ non-detect.

## Control Strategy for Reuse Water Systems

| **Control Element** | **Minimum Requirement** |
|---------------------|-------------------------|
| **HOBr Slug Dosing** | 5–7 ppm, ≥2×/week (more in summer) |
| **Biodispersant** | 20–50 ppm, before or with oxidizer |
| **Non-Oxidizing Biocide** | Weekly or continuous low-level feed |
| **Filtration** | Sub-50 micron side-stream filter; replace media more frequently |
| **Monitoring** | Weekly: coliforms, TVPC, oxidant residual, biofilm indicators |
| **System Maintenance** | Bi-annual offline cleaning + quarterly basin vacuuming |

## Standard Operating Plan: Microbial Control

**System Overview**

| **Item** | **Specification** |
|----------|-------------------|
| System Type | Open recirculating cooling tower |
| Application | Data center cooling |
| Makeup Source | Tertiary-treated reclaimed water |
| Capacity | [Insert tonnage] |
| Cycles of Concentration | [Insert value] |

**Chemical Treatment Program**

**Oxidizing Biocide (Primary Disinfectant)**

| **Item** | **Specification** |
|----------|-------------------|
| Type | Hypobromous acid (HOBr, generated in situ via NaBr + NaOCl) |
| Dose | 5–7 ppm (free halogen, slug) |
| Frequency | Minimum 3× per week in summer, 2× per week otherwise |
| Contact Time | ≥2 hours per event |
| Target Residual (Post-Injection) | 1.0–2.0 ppm free halogen at far end of system |

**Biodispersant**

| **Item** | **Specification** |
|----------|-------------------|
| Type | Non-ionic or amphoteric dispersant, compatible with metals |
| Dose | 20–50 ppm |
| Frequency | Applied 30 minutes prior to every oxidizer dose |
| Function | Biofilm breakup and surfactant for oxidant penetration |

**Non-Oxidizing Biocide**

| **Item** | **Specification** |
|----------|-------------------|
| Type | Glutaraldehyde, DBNPA, or isothiazolone |
| Dose | Per manufacturer, typically 100–300 ppm active slug |
| Frequency | Once weekly or every 3–4 days in summer |
| Function | Penetrative kill; residual protection between oxidizer slugs |

**Monitoring Program**

| **Test** | **Frequency** | **Target / Limit** |
|----------|---------------|-------------------|
| Free halogen residual | 3×/week after slug dose | ≥1.0 ppm (far point) |
| Total viable plate count (TVPC) | Weekly | <10⁴ CFU/mL |
| Total coliforms (MPN) | Weekly | <100 CFU/100 mL |
| _E. coli_ (if detected coliforms) | Weekly or confirmatory | Non-detect |
| ORP (if used) | Continuous | >650 mV (with calibration) |
| ATP (optional) | Weekly | <10 pg/mL |
| Turbidity | Weekly | <5 NTU |
| Oxidant demand (field test) | Monthly | Track baseline |

**Inspection Points:** Drift eliminators, basin corners, spray headers and fill. Sample from dead zones, not just main basin.

**Corrective Action Triggers**

| **Condition** | **Corrective Action** |
|---------------|----------------------|
| TVPC > 10⁴ CFU/mL | Increase biocide dose and frequency; inspect for biofilm |
| Total coliforms > 100 CFU/100 mL | Increase oxidizer frequency and review dispersant program |
| E. coli detected | Immediate escalation: shock dose + root cause investigation |
| Biofilm observed | Immediate dispersant + oxidizer pairing; flush low-flow zones |
| ORP < 600 mV post-sanitization | Verify feed system, check demand load |
| Turbidity spikes | Clean filters; inspect for organic ingress |

**Preventive Maintenance**

| **Task** | **Frequency** |
|----------|---------------|
| Basin vacuum and sludge removal | Quarterly |
| Offline chemical clean of fill pack | Semi-annually |
| Filter media changeout | Per pressure drop or quarterly minimum |
| Dispersant compatibility test | Annually or with chemical change |
| Cross-connection backflow testing | Annually (per plumbing code) |

**Emergency Response Plan**

**Trigger:** Positive detection of _E. coli_ or Legionella

**Actions:** Isolate tower and notify operations management. Apply shock treatment with HOBr at 10 ppm for ≥6 hours, dispersant at 50 ppm, followed by non-oxidizer slug post-flush. Retest water 24 hours post-treatment. Conduct root cause analysis examining bird droppings, drift recirculation, and source water. Update SOPs if systemic vulnerabilities are identified.

## Key Takeaways

For potable water systems, test total coliforms monthly and maintain biocide residuals above 1.0 ppm free halogen at system endpoints. With reclaimed water makeup, implement weekly microbiological monitoring, apply biodispersant before every oxidizer dose at 20-50 ppm, and use side-stream filtration below 50 microns with quarterly media replacement to manage the continuous coliform load effectively.
