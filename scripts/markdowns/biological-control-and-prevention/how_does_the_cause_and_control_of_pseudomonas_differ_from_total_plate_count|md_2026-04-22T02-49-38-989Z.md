# How does the cause and control of Pseudomonas differ from Total Viable Plate Count in towers and other aerosol forming units?

## Executive Summary

Pseudomonas indicates specific biofilm reservoirs and contamination pathways requiring targeted cleaning and source elimination, while Total Viable Plate Count signals general biological control failures that respond to system-wide disinfectant and deposit management.

## Overview

A cooling tower with modest Total Viable Plate Count can still harbor dangerous Pseudomonas colonies in protected biofilm zones. These two microbial indicators tell completely different stories about what's wrong with your water system and demand opposite response strategies — one calls for broader process adjustments, the other for surgical strikes on hidden reservoirs.

## What each result means in aerosolizing water systems

**Total Viable Plate Count (TVC/HPC)** measures non-specific counts of live, culturable heterotrophs including bacteria, yeasts, and molds. This serves mainly as a trend indicator of general biological condition and changes in water quality.

**Pseudomonas** (typically P. aeruginosa when specified) represents a specific opportunistic waterborne pathogen that readily colonizes wet surfaces and biofilms. Water treatment professionals recognize this as a problem organism in water systems serving vulnerable populations.

In cooling towers and evaporative systems, both indicators carry added concern because these systems aerosolize water, increasing potential exposure beyond the equipment footprint.

## How the causes differ

**High TVC/HPC** signals general "biofouling and loss of microbiological control" caused by system-wide growth drivers:

- Inadequate oxidant control or poor residual distribution
- High nutrients and organics
- Suspended solids and deposits (sediment, scale, corrosion products)  
- Stagnation and poor turnover

Cooling towers require controlling scale, corrosion, sediment, and cleaning while maintaining disinfectant residual to prevent microbial growth.

**Excess Pseudomonas** indicates specific "biofilm niche, selection, or contamination source" problems:

- **Persistent biofilm reservoirs** in protected slime zones (basin sludge, fouled fill, strainers, low-flow legs, sumps)
- **Intermittent or ineffective disinfectant exposure** that selects for biofilm-adapted organisms  
- **Contaminated inputs** from makeup water quality issues, temporary hoses, backflow vulnerabilities, or ingress from debris and wildlife contamination

## How control differs

**If TVC/HPC is excessive**, restore general biological control:

1. Verify disinfectant feed, control, and measurement accuracy
2. Ensure residual control reaches representative points, not just one location
3. Improve solids and deposit control through blowdown, filtration performance, strainer maintenance, and basin cleanliness — deposits protect microbial growth
4. Address hydraulics and operations that increase water age, including cell isolation practices, intermittent operation, and dead zones

Use HPC/TVC as a trend verification metric after implementing these changes.

**If Pseudomonas is excessive** (or detected where it should be absent), eradicate reservoirs and eliminate the enabling niche:

1. **Treat as a biofilm problem first**: Inspect and physically clean high-risk areas including basin, sumps, strainers, and fill, then remove loosened material via filtration and blowdown to prevent re-seeding

2. **Confirm the pathway**: Test and verify makeup water protections including air gaps, backflow prevention, and temporary hoses. Inspect for ingress points from debris and wildlife, then correct exclusion measures

3. **Escalate risk controls** for healthcare-adjacent aerosolizing equipment and high-risk occupants. Water system professionals treat Pseudomonas as a significant organism in high-risk settings, applying tighter response thresholds than for generic HPC

4. **Resample strategically** to prove removal of the reservoir by testing upstream makeup, basin/sump, and a representative distribution point

## Practical interpretation for field operations

**High TVC/HPC with no Pseudomonas** indicates mainly general biofouling requiring process control adjustments. **Pseudomonas present or excessive, even with modest TVC,** signals biofilm reservoirs or pathway contamination requiring targeted inspection, cleaning, and source control rather than simply increased oxidant levels.
