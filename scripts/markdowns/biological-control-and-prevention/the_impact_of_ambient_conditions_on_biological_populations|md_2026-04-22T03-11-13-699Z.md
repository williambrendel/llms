# How Do Ambient Conditions Impact Biological Growth in Cooling Towers?

## Executive Summary

Ambient conditions drive dramatic seasonal swings in cooling tower biological populations, with very high growth in summer months (July-September) and very low populations in winter (December-January). Slime-forming species follow the same pattern but peak even higher in summer.

## Overview

A cooling tower's biological population can swing from "very low" to "very high" based purely on weather — even when you change nothing else about your treatment program. San Jose cooling towers see biological populations jump nearly tenfold from winter lows to summer peaks, creating a moving target that can mask whether your recent program changes actually worked.

## Seasonal Temperature and Light Patterns

San Jose experiences predictable ambient cycles that directly influence cooling tower conditions. Temperatures reach their coolest point from December through January, then climb to peak warmth during July through September. This ambient-driven temperature trend carries directly into tower water temperatures.

Sunlight follows a complementary pattern, peaking during late spring and summer months before dropping to winter lows. These two factors — temperature and solar radiation — create the foundation for biological activity in open cooling systems.

Both higher water temperature and sunlight generally increase biological growth pressure in open cooling towers. The pattern shows slime-forming species respond even more aggressively to favorable conditions, reaching "very high" levels from June through August.

## Program Evaluation Challenges

Ambient conditions create a significant challenge when evaluating biological control program changes. Populations in December naturally run lower than those in October simply due to weather conditions. When you implement a program change — such as switching to slug feed at a proper dose — two variables change simultaneously: your treatment approach and the ambient conditions.

This overlap can mask the true effectiveness of program modifications. A successful treatment change implemented in fall might appear less effective than it actually is, while a mediocre change implemented in winter might seem more successful due to naturally declining biological pressure.

## Actionable Recommendations

Consider ambient conditions as a critical factor when evaluating any biological control program changes. Adjust biological control measures to match seasonal ambient conditions rather than maintaining static treatment levels year-round. Monitor biological populations monthly to establish baseline seasonal patterns for your specific location, and compare program performance against these ambient-adjusted expectations rather than absolute population numbers.
