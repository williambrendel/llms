# What is Biofilm and why is it a problem in cooling towers and closed loops?

## Executive Summary

Biofilm creates multiple cascading problems in water systems: it harbors dangerous pathogens like Legionella, blocks disinfectants from working effectively, reduces heat transfer efficiency, and accelerates corrosion through localized chemical attacks on metal surfaces.

## Overview

The CDC identifies biofilm as one of the key factors affecting Legionella growth in cooling towers, but that's just the beginning of the problem. This slimy matrix of microorganisms acts like a fortress that protects harmful bacteria while simultaneously eating away at your equipment's efficiency and structural integrity.

## Understanding Biofilm Formation

Biofilm forms when microorganisms attach to surfaces and grow within a self-produced protective matrix made of extracellular polymers. This matrix doesn't just house bacteria—it actively traps particles and debris, creating an increasingly complex and problematic deposit over time.

## Biofilm Problems in Cooling Towers

### Legionella Risk

The CDC identifies sediment and biofilm as key factors in their "STAR" framework that affect Legionella growth in cooling towers. Cooling-water biofilm literature specifically documents the association between biofilm and Legionella pneumophila risk, with biofilm acting as both a habitat and reservoir for these dangerous pathogens.

### Disinfection Resistance

The biofilm matrix interferes with disinfectants, creating a protective barrier that allows embedded organisms to survive treatment and rebound afterward. This makes routine water treatment significantly less effective and requires more aggressive approaches.

### Heat Transfer Loss and Fouling

Biofilm represents a form of biofouling deposit that accumulates on heat-transfer surfaces. These deposits reduce system efficiency, drive higher energy consumption, and create operational problems that compound over time.

### Microbiologically Influenced Corrosion

Biofilm creates localized microenvironments at metal surfaces that actively promote microbiologically influenced corrosion (MIC). This leads to under-deposit corrosion that can cause serious structural damage to system components.

## Biofilm Problems in Closed Loops

Closed loops typically experience less biological loading than open cooling towers, but biofilm still creates significant problems in these systems.

### Corrosion Acceleration

The NIH's closed-loop corrosion bulletin explicitly calls out microbial growth assessment and identifies MIC as a major concern in closed-loop hot and chilled water systems. The localized chemical conditions under biofilm deposits accelerate metal degradation.

### Flow Restrictions and Plugging

Biofilm traps corrosion by-products and particulates, forming under-deposit conditions that contribute to system plugging and localized attack. This problem becomes especially severe in low-flow areas and dead legs where circulation cannot naturally clear deposits.

## Key Takeaway

Treat biofilm as a multiplier problem that requires proactive management. Monitor biofilm formation monthly in cooling towers and quarterly in closed loops, maintain biocide residuals above 0.5 ppm free chlorine equivalent, and consider advanced monitoring technologies like ATP testing to detect biological activity before visible biofilm develops.
