# How do drift and aerosols relate to biological risks?

## Executive Summary

Drift and aerosols create biological risks by transporting microorganisms from water systems into breathable air. Cooling towers present the highest concern because contaminated water droplets can carry pathogens like Legionella to people downwind through inhalation.

## Overview

A single cooling tower can release millions of water droplets per hour into the surrounding air. When these droplets contain harmful microorganisms, they transform a localized water quality problem into an airborne health risk that can affect people hundreds of feet away. The combination of warm water, biofilm growth, and mechanical droplet generation makes cooling towers particularly effective at both amplifying pathogens and delivering them through the air.

## Definitions and relationship to exposure

### Drift

Drift consists of liquid water droplets physically carried out of a cooling tower by exhaust air. These droplets matter biologically because they contain whatever microorganisms are present in the circulating water, including pathogens.

### Aerosols

Aerosols are suspended airborne droplets small enough to remain in air and be inhaled. They provide the pathway for inhalation exposure, creating what specialists call bioaerosol exposure when the droplets contain biological material.

## How drift and aerosols increase biological risk

### They create an airborne exposure pathway

Droplets act as vehicles that transport microorganisms from the water system to people and the environment. Waterborne organisms become airborne, bypassing traditional water contact barriers.

### They extend the area of concern beyond the equipment

Drift can travel beyond the tower footprint through downwind transport. Risk depends on droplet size, wind conditions, and plume behavior, potentially affecting areas far from the original source.

### They bypass typical water-only barriers

People face exposure without ingesting water. Inhalation becomes the key pathway for Legionella disease risk in cooling tower scenarios, making respiratory exposure the primary concern rather than direct water contact.

## What conditions increase the likelihood that drift or aerosols are hazardous

### High microbial amplification in the system

Several factors raise organism levels in the source water. Biofilm and sediment create reservoirs where pathogens multiply. Warm water temperature ranges provide ideal growth conditions. Inadequate disinfectant or biocide residual combined with poor circulation allows unchecked microbial growth.

### Increased droplet escape (higher drift rate)

Mechanical and operational problems drive higher drift rates. Damaged, missing, or poorly installed drift eliminators fail to capture droplets before they escape. Poor water distribution causes splashing and droplet carryover. High fan speed or airflow conditions increase mechanical entrainment of water droplets.

### High proximity and exposure opportunity

Risk increases when receptors are close to the tower. Outdoor air intakes, walkways, loading docks, and occupied areas near the discharge plume create direct exposure opportunities for building occupants and workers.

## Practical indicators that drift-related biological risk is increasing

### Visible mist or wetting beyond the tower

Physical observation provides immediate feedback. Persistent wetting or spotting on nearby surfaces indicates excessive drift rates that could carry contaminants beyond the intended area.

### Rising biological indicators in the system

Trend-based monitoring offers early warning. Increasing bacterial indicators and recurring biofilm signs increase the probability that escaping droplets contain higher organism loads.

## Control actions

### Control the source: reduce microorganisms in circulating water

Maintain effective disinfectant or biocide control by ensuring adequate concentration, mixing, and contact time. Control sediment and biofilm through cleaning, filtration, and deposit removal to eliminate microbial reservoirs.

### Control the pathway: reduce droplet emission

Inspect and maintain drift eliminators, repairing or replacing damaged components. Optimize water distribution to reduce splashing and droplet carryover. Manage airflow and mechanical conditions by maintaining fans, louvers, and fill to limit entrainment.

### Reduce exposure opportunity

Manage siting and air intake placement by keeping outdoor air intakes away from tower drift plumes where feasible. Consider prevailing wind patterns and plume modeling when planning facility layouts.

## Key Takeaway

Effective biological risk management requires monitoring both water quality and drift rates weekly during operation. Maintain disinfectant residuals above 1.0 ppm free chlorine (or equivalent biocide levels), inspect drift eliminators monthly, and keep outdoor air intakes at least 25 feet from cooling tower discharge plumes where possible.
