# How Does the Cause and Control of Diatoms and Amoebas Differ from Total Viable Plate Count in Cooling Towers?

## Executive Summary

Diatoms, amoebas, and total viable plate count (TVC) represent three distinct microbial problems in cooling towers with fundamentally different causes and control strategies. TVC indicates general bacterial control issues requiring disinfectant management, diatoms are photosynthetic organisms driven by light and silica availability, and free-living amoebas thrive in biofilm habitats where they can harbor dangerous pathogens like Legionella.

## Overview

A cooling tower operator might see elevated microbial counts and assume one treatment approach will solve all problems. This assumption can prove costly and dangerous. Free-living amoebas can increase Legionella persistence by up to 1000-fold compared to bacteria living freely in water, while diatoms may flourish even when bacterial counts remain normal.

## What Each Result Represents

**Total Viable Count (TVC) and Heterotrophic Plate Count (HPC)** provide a general estimate of live, culturable heterotrophic bacteria in bulk water. A significant increase indicates a general water-quality or microbial control problem across the system.

**Diatoms** are photosynthetic microalgae distinguished by their silica cell walls called frustules. Many routine HPC and TVC methods do not enumerate them because they require different culture conditions. In cooling systems, diatoms generally are not a major driver of cooling-system problems compared with other algae and biofouling organisms.

**Free-living amoebae (FLA)** are protozoa that live in water and biofilms, acting as hosts and reservoirs for bacteria including Legionella. These single-celled organisms provide protection that can increase bacterial persistence and tolerance to stress and biocides.

## How Causes Differ

**High TVC/HPC** stems from system-wide issues: insufficient disinfectant exposure, poor solids control, deposits and biofilm accumulation, and stagnation or excessive water age. Scale, corrosion, and sediment control combined with maintaining adequate disinfectant residual are critical factors in cooling towers.

**Excess diatoms** develop under specific conditions that favor photosynthetic growth. Light exposure on sunlit wetted surfaces drives diatom proliferation. These organisms also require nutrients plus silica availability for building their frustules. Surface attachment zones like louvers, decks, and basin edges provide ideal colonization sites, similar to other algae species.

**Excess amoebae** thrive in biofilm and sediment habitats where they graze and live within biofilm microenvironments. Warm, stagnant, or low-flow niches create ideal conditions for amoeba growth. A coexisting bacterial food supply sustains amoeba populations. In aerosolizing systems, FLA matter because they can support persistence and amplification of pathogens, notably Legionella.

## Control Strategies for Each Organism Type

**Controlling excessive TVC/HPC** requires restoring overall microbial control throughout the system. Operators must verify disinfectant feed and control systems while ensuring representative residual control. Improving sediment, biofilm, and deposit control through cleaning, solids removal, and filtration or blowdown becomes essential. Reducing water age and stagnation through operational changes and addressing low-flow zones completes the approach.

**Managing excessive diatoms** focuses on removing algae-enabling conditions, especially light exposure, and preventing re-attachment. Light control through covers, shading, or reducing sun penetration often provides higher leverage than changing bacterial biocide programs. This follows general algae control principles since diatoms are photosynthetic organisms.

Physical removal combined with solids management requires cleaning affected surfaces and ensuring loosened biomass gets removed through filtration or blowdown. Without proper removal, the biomass re-seeds the system. Operators should not use diatom results as a proxy for bacterial control since diatoms may be present while HPC remains normal, and vice versa.

**Controlling excessive amoebae** demands eliminating biofilm and sediment habitats while tightening controls that reduce both protozoa and the bacteria they host. Biofilm and sediment removal takes priority, along with ongoing deposit control, because amoebae live in these specific niches.

Maintaining stable disinfectant control and regular cleaning becomes critical because amoebae can shelter bacteria. Controlling the habitat reduces both organism types simultaneously. Operators should treat amoebae findings as a "hidden reservoir" signal, indicating the need for targeted inspection and cleaning of low-flow and sediment areas even when bulk HPC does not appear alarming.

## Key Takeaways

High TVC/HPC indicates broad loss of microbial control and represents a process control issue requiring system-wide disinfectant and cleaning improvements. High diatoms primarily signal a light, surface growth, and housekeeping/solids issue that demands targeted algae control rather than general bacterial treatment. High amoebae reveal a biofilm/sediment niche issue with added concern for Legionella persistence, requiring focused habitat removal and enhanced cleaning protocols in low-flow areas.
