# How do algae affect operations in open cooling tower and closed loop chilled systems?

## Executive Summary

Algae severely compromise cooling tower performance by reducing heat transfer efficiency, plugging distribution systems, and increasing corrosion and biocide demand, while closed chilled-water systems typically remain algae-free unless contaminated through light exposure or makeup water ingress.

## Overview

A single algae bloom can consume enough biocides to destabilize an entire cooling tower's microbial control program. Most facility managers expect algae problems in their cooling towers, but few realize that finding algae in a "closed" chilled-water system signals a fundamental breach in system integrity. The distinction matters because algae need light to survive, making their presence a diagnostic tool for system health.

## Algae Impact on Open Cooling Tower Operations

### Heat Transfer Loss and Energy Penalties

Algae contribute to biofouling and deposit formation on heat-exchange surfaces and tower internals, which reduces heat transfer efficiency and can degrade cooling performance. These biological deposits act as insulating barriers that force cooling systems to work harder and consume more energy to achieve the same cooling effect.

### Flow Distribution and Plugging Issues

Algae grow in sunlit areas, often on tower decks and basins in crossflow designs, then slough off into circulating water. This adds suspended solids that foul and plug spray nozzles, strainers, and distribution paths, reducing effective wetting of fill and overall tower performance. When distribution becomes uneven, hot spots develop that further compromise system efficiency.

### Corrosion Acceleration

Algal deposits act as under-deposit corrosion initiation points, including localized pitting, by creating differential aeration and trapping moisture and solids. The organic matter creates micro-environments where oxygen levels vary dramatically across metal surfaces, setting up the electrochemical conditions that drive accelerated corrosion.

### Biocide Demand and Control Complications

Algae add biomass and organic load that consume oxidants and biocides, making microbial control less stable and supporting thicker biofilms and slime formation. The CDC emphasizes that biofilm and sediment enable microbial growth and complicate control in cooling towers, creating a cascade effect where algae problems multiply into broader microbiological challenges.

### Legionella Risk Amplification

Algae contribute to the broader biofilm and sediment ecology that supports microbial amplification in towers. CDC guidance highlights sediment and biofilm as key factors affecting Legionella growth, positioning algae as an indirect but significant contributor to this serious health risk.

### Maintenance and Operational Impact

Algae mats impede flow and create operational nuisance conditions. Industrial guidance notes potential damage concerns for certain tower materials and structures when mats persist, leading to increased maintenance frequency and costs.

## Closed Chilled-Water System Considerations

In a true closed loop, algae are generally not expected because algae require light to grow. Reputable industrial water-quality guidance explicitly notes you "do not find algae in closed circuits" for this reason.

### Diagnostic Value of Algae Presence

If algae are observed in a "closed" chilled-water system, it usually indicates abnormal conditions:

- **Open or vented expansion tank** or another light-exposed reservoir
- **Frequent makeup water** requirements or contamination ingress  
- **Poorly protected storage or side-stream components**

### Operational Consequences in Compromised Systems

When algae do appear in supposedly closed systems, they still contribute to strainer plugging, deposits, and under-deposit corrosion. However, their presence serves more as a diagnostic indicator that the loop is not truly closed or is being contaminated from external sources.

## Key Takeaways

Monitor cooling towers weekly for visible algae growth, especially in sunlit areas, and maintain oxidant residuals above 1.0 ppm free chlorine to prevent establishment. If algae appear in closed chilled-water systems, immediately inspect expansion tanks and makeup water connections for light exposure or contamination sources, then consider UV sterilization or closed-loop biocides as alternatives to traditional chemical treatment.
