# How Does the Cause and Control of Excessive Sulfate Reducing Bacteria Differ from Total Viable Plate Count in Cooling Towers?

## Executive Summary

Sulfate-reducing bacteria (SRB) and total viable plate count (TVC/HPC) require fundamentally different control strategies because they indicate distinct problems: TVC/HPC signals overall biological activity requiring system-wide disinfection improvements, while SRB indicates anaerobic deposit problems that drive microbiologically influenced corrosion and require physical cleaning plus habitat elimination.

## Overview

A cooling tower can pass its general bacterial counts yet still harbor aggressive corrosion in dead legs and under deposits. SRB thrive in oxygen-depleted microzones beneath biofilms and sludge where standard disinfectants cannot reach, producing sulfides that accelerate metal deterioration even when bulk water appears well-controlled.

## What Each Result Means in Cooling Systems

**Total viable plate count (TVC/HPC)** provides a broad, non-specific estimate of live, culturable heterotrophs. Operators use this measurement to indicate general water quality and biological condition while detecting trends. A significant increase signals a water quality problem developing system-wide.

**Sulfate-reducing bacteria (SRB)** represents a specific functional group that grows in low-oxygen anaerobic micro-environments, typically under slime, biofilm, or sludge deposits. These bacteria strongly associate with microbiologically influenced corrosion (MIC) through sulfide production.

The key difference: High TVC/HPC means overall biological activity has increased. High SRB means anaerobic niches and deposit problems exist that can drive MIC, even when bulk-water counts remain normal.

## How the Causes Differ

**Excessive TVC/HPC stems from system-wide drivers** that enable general biofouling and loss of microbiological control. These include ineffective disinfectant exposure and residual control, high nutrient and organic loading, poor solids control allowing sediment, scale, and corrosion products to accumulate, plus operational conditions that increase water age and stagnation. Cooling tower guidance emphasizes that scale, corrosion, sediment controls, and system cleaning, plus maintaining disinfectant residual, prove critical for tower microbial management.

**Excessive SRB develops where oxygen becomes depleted.** SRB proliferate under biofilm and slime layers, under sludge deposits, in dead legs, low-flow zones, and offline equipment, plus areas with heavy sediment, poor filtration and blowdown, or long idle periods. Cooling system guidance explicitly notes this "SRB habitat" description when addressing SRB control.

## How Control Strategies Differ

**When TVC/HPC exceeds limits,** operators focus on restoring bulk-water microbiological control. They verify disinfectant feed, control, and measurement while ensuring control at representative points. They improve solids management through better blowdown, filtration, and basin cleanliness. They address operating conditions that increase water age and stagnation, using TVC/HPC as a trend indicator to verify improvement.

**When SRB exceeds limits,** operators target eliminating anaerobic niches and stopping MIC conditions. SRB control focuses less on adding more oxidizer to bulk water and more on removing the protected habitat. This requires removing deposits and biofilm reservoirs through mechanical cleaning of basins, sumps, and low-flow zones while restoring proper housekeeping. SRB commonly live under slime and sludge, making deposit removal decisive.

Operators must fix conditions creating anaerobic microzones by restoring circulation in low-flow areas, eliminating dead legs, and preventing offline equipment from becoming stagnant reservoirs. They rebalance solids control through improved filtration and blowdown to prevent rapid re-deposition. Because SRB generate sulfide and link strongly to MIC, inspection and monitoring often includes corrosion indicators and targeted checks at historically corroding components.

## Special Considerations for Aerosolizing Systems

SRB themselves primarily create a corrosion and biofilm issue. In aerosolizing equipment, the immediate public health focus often centers on organisms like Legionella. Control strategies emphasize managing sediment, biofilm, water age, and disinfectant residual to reduce that risk.

## Key Takeaway

High TVC/HPC indicates a broad process control issue where overall conditions favor growth. High SRB signals a deposit, biofilm, and anaerobic niche issue with elevated MIC concern, requiring physical cleaning plus hydraulic and niche correction rather than simply increasing chemical treatment.
