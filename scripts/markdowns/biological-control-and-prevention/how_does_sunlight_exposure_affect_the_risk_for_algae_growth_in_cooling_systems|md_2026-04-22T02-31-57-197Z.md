# How does sunlight exposure affect the risk for algae growth in cooling systems?

## Executive Summary

Sunlight dramatically increases algae growth risk in cooling systems because algae use light for photosynthesis. Basin waterlines, splash zones, louvers, and drift eliminators exposed to sunlight can develop thick algae mats that increase disinfectant demand and create sheltered zones for bacterial growth.

## Overview

A cooling tower basin that looks clean in winter can turn green within days once spring sunlight hits the waterline. Algae don't just create unsightly films—they consume your biocide, trap sediment, and create protected microenvironments where bacteria thrive. The wetted surfaces that get the most light become the first battleground in your biological control program.

## Why sunlight increases algae risk

Algae require light to grow through photosynthesis. More light generally increases growth potential when other conditions align—nutrients, suitable temperature, and sufficient water residence time.

Sunlit zones warm quickly and concentrate growth conditions. Shallow, wet surfaces heat faster than bulk water, increasing biological activity rates. Evaporation at exposed surfaces concentrates nutrients locally, supporting thicker mats at the air-water interface where algae access both light and dissolved nutrients.

## Where algae forms first in cooling systems

**Basin waterline and splash zones** experience high light exposure plus intermittent wetting. This combination supports attached algae and the characteristic green or brown films that appear at the water's edge.

**Louvers and drift eliminators** maintain constant moisture from droplet impingement while remaining exposed to light and air. These components often show the first signs of algae establishment in spring and summer.

**Fill edges and distribution areas with uneven wetting** create wet-dry cycling conditions. This promotes algae mats that resist shear forces and become difficult to remove once established.

## How algae increases overall biological control risk

Algae biomass becomes food for bacteria when it dies or sloughs off surfaces. This adds organic carbon and particulates to the system, creating more substrate for bacterial growth.

Algae mats reinforce biofilm formation and trap solids. The mats hold sediment and create sheltered micro-zones where bacteria can establish and multiply, protected from disinfectants and shear forces.

More biomass means higher disinfectant and biocide demand. Algae consume oxidizers before they can maintain effective residuals, creating under-treated zones where biological growth rebounds quickly.

## Best early detection methods

Focus visual inspection on known sunlit "first-growth" zones during weekly checks in warm seasons. Basin waterlines, louvers, eliminators, and fill edges typically show algae before it spreads to other system components.

Monitor trend indicators that shift with algae onset. Adenosine triphosphate levels and heterotrophic plate counts often increase after algae establishes, reflecting higher organic content and biofilm activity. Rising differential pressure or reduced distribution performance can signal algae and slime fouling screens and nozzles.

## Prevention and control actions

Reduce sunlight exposure where practical by covering or shading exposed basins and sunlit wetted components. Basin covers prove especially effective for systems with direct sun exposure. Maintain louvers and eliminators by replacing damaged sections that allow abnormal light entry.

Improve solids control through side stream filtration and routine basin cleaning to reduce seed habitat for algae establishment. Prevent process leaks that introduce organics, which serve as additional growth substrates.

Maintain stable oxidizing biocide residual when used, avoiding low-residual periods during peak sunlight seasons. Use supplemental strategies when algae recurs, including targeted slug treatment and cleaning of algae-prone surfaces before mats become established.

## Key takeaways

Check sunlit waterlines and eliminators weekly during warm months—algae establishment here predicts system-wide biological control challenges. Maintain oxidizing biocide residuals above 0.5 ppm free chlorine equivalent in sunlit zones. Consider basin covers or targeted shading for systems with persistent algae problems, especially where basins receive direct sunlight for more than 4 hours daily.
