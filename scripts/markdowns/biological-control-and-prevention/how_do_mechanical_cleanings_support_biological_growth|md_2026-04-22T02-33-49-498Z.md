# How does mechanical cleaning support biological growth?

## Executive Summary

Mechanical cleaning can inadvertently accelerate biological growth by breaking biofilms into viable fragments that reseed downstream surfaces, releasing trapped nutrients into the water, creating fresh attachment sites through surface roughening, and temporarily disrupting disinfectant control.

## Overview

A properly executed basin cleaning can trigger the worst biofilm outbreak a cooling tower has ever seen. When maintenance teams brush, scrape, or pressure-wash contaminated surfaces, they often scatter living biofilm fragments throughout the system while simultaneously feeding them with newly released organics. The cleaned area looks pristine, but heat exchanger fouling accelerates within days.

## How Mechanical Cleaning Increases Biological Growth

### Breaking Biofilm Into Seed Particles

Brushing, scraping, and high-velocity flushing shear off biofilm chunks that remain viable after detachment. These fragments reattach quickly to new surfaces, often in less accessible locations like heat exchanger channels or distribution piping dead legs. The system appears cleaner at the cleaned point while biofouling accelerates downstream where removal becomes much more difficult.

### Releasing Growth-Fueling Nutrients

Biofilm matrices contain organic polymers that dissolve into the bulk water when disturbed. Sludge layers trap organics and nutrients that become immediately available to microorganisms when agitated. Without proper filtration and blowdown, microbial activity spikes after cleaning as these newly available carbon sources fuel rapid regrowth.

### Creating Fresh Attachment Sites

Aggressive brushing and abrasive cleaning create micro-scratches and pits that shelter early colonizers from shear forces. Cleaning also removes stable protective films and passive surface layers, exposing "fresh" reactive surfaces that microorganisms colonize more easily. These exposed surfaces corrode more actively, generating corrosion products that form an ideal conditioning layer for biofilm establishment.

### Disrupting Disinfectant Control

Released organics, reduced metals, and disturbed sludge consume oxidizing biocides rapidly, causing residual levels to collapse even when feed rates remain unchanged. Cleaning operations also create temporary low-flow zones through valve isolation and equipment bypassing, allowing these areas to lose disinfectant residual and become rapid regrowth reservoirs.

### Leaving Protected Remnants

Mechanical cleaning cannot reach crevices in gaskets, threaded joints, valve cavities, and areas beneath remaining scale deposits. These protected remnants regrow faster than before because competitors have been removed from adjacent surfaces while the surviving organisms face less competition for freshly cleaned, easily colonized surfaces.

## System-Specific Risk Areas

### Cooling Towers

Basin sludge disturbance releases organics and suspended solids directly into the recirculating loop. Partially cleaned fill sheds biomass that plugs strainers and heat exchangers. Drift eliminator washing creates aerosol transport risks and moves biomass to previously clean wet surfaces.

### Heat Exchangers

High-velocity flushing pushes solids into condenser tubes or plate channels when strainers lack adequate protection. Plate-and-frame heat exchangers with narrow passages trap dislodged fragments where biological regrowth concentrates around lodged solids.

### Closed Loop Systems

Strainer cleaning without simultaneous system filtration redistributes biomass and debris during restart. Draining and refilling introduces oxygen and new microorganisms that accelerate biofilm and corrosion when inhibitor and biocide sequencing fails.

## Prevention and Control Strategies

### Immediate Containment and Removal

Run continuous side-stream or portable filtration during cleaning circulation to capture dislodged material. Increase controlled blowdown and use basin vacuuming rather than stirring deposits and leaving them suspended in the system.

### Proper Sequencing

Follow the detach-remove-disinfect sequence rather than relying on disinfection alone. Apply dispersants to lift deposits, remove suspended material through filtration and blowdown, then disinfect with verified concentration and contact time at the worst-circulated system point.

### Maintaining Circulation

Plan valve lineups and pump operations to avoid creating isolated branches filled with warm water lacking disinfectant residual. Temporary stagnation during cleaning creates ideal conditions for rapid biological rebound.

### Chemistry Restoration

Re-establish corrosion inhibitor and pH control in closed loops immediately after cleaning. Restore stable biocide residual and deposit control programs in open systems before biological populations can recover.

## Key Takeaway

Monitor biological indicators, residual stability, and heat transfer performance weekly for two weeks following any mechanical cleaning, and always pair physical cleaning with immediate filtration, controlled blowdown, and verified disinfectant contact time to prevent biological rebound that often exceeds pre-cleaning contamination levels.
