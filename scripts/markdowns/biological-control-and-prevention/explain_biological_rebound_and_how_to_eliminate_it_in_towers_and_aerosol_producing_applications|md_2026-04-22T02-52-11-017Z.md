# Why do bacteria keep returning after "treatment" and what can be done to prevent this return in Cooling Towers and other Evaporative / Aerosol Causing Applications?

## Executive Summary

Bacteria return after cooling tower treatment because most treatments only kill free-floating organisms while protected biofilm reservoirs continuously re-seed the system, combined with inconsistent disinfectant residuals that leave dead zones untreated.

## Overview

A properly executed biocide treatment can drop bacterial counts to near zero, yet within days the same system shows high counts again. This frustrating cycle happens because cooling towers create perfect conditions for bacterial hideouts that standard treatments can't reach. The most telling diagnostic pattern reveals the real culprit: when counts drop immediately after treatment but rebound within days, you're dealing with biofilm reservoirs and inconsistent residual distribution, not new contamination from outside sources.

## Why Bacteria Keep Returning After Treatment

### Biofilm and Deposits Were Not Removed

Biofilm, sludge, and scale protect organisms from biocides and act as a continuous source of re-growth. These protective matrices shield bacteria from chemical treatment, allowing them to survive and multiply once conditions return to normal.

**To Prevent Return:**
- Pair chemical treatment with mechanical cleaning of basins, sumps, strainers, and fill where fouled
- Improve solids control through sidestream filtration where appropriate plus effective blowdown so loosened biomass gets removed, not redistributed

### Disinfectant Residual Is Not Consistently Achieved Throughout the System

Short "slug doses" may drop counts briefly but can leave dead zones and biofilm protected if not done properly. Many systems achieve good residual readings at one sampling point while other areas receive inadequate treatment.

**To Prevent Return:**
- Verify feed equipment and control logic
- Confirm residual at representative points throughout the system, not just one sampling spot
- Control water chemistry that reduces oxidant effectiveness, especially pH rise and oxidant demand shifts

### Water Age and Low-Flow Zones Create Growth Niches

Idle cells, low flow areas, and intermittent operation increase water age and allow biofilm reservoirs to rebound. Stagnant water provides perfect conditions for bacterial growth even when the main system maintains proper treatment levels.

**To Prevent Return:**
- Reduce dead legs and low-flow zones; manage cell isolation practices
- Maintain circulation and turnover during low load periods
- Flush stagnant legs per your water management program

### Rapid Re-inoculation from External Sources

Towers operate as open systems exposed to environmental contamination. Incoming makeup water and airborne debris continuously seed microbes, while maintenance practices can introduce additional contamination sources.

**To Prevent Return:**
- Inspect and correct basin covers, screens, louvers, drift eliminator condition, and debris control
- Verify makeup water protections including air gaps, backflow prevention, and avoid temporary hose connections without controls

### Treatment Approach Mismatched to the Problem

Systems with established biofilm often need a biofilm strategy involving dispersants, cleaning agents, and biocides rather than simply increasing oxidizer levels. Industry guidance commonly uses non-oxidizing biocides as part of controlling biofouling, including biofilm-associated growth.

**To Prevent Return:**
- Use a planned program that includes oxidizing control plus a structured non-oxidizing component per your vendor program and local discharge constraints
- Time biodispersant and non-oxidizer use with filtration and blowdown so released solids get removed

### Startups, Shutdowns, and Shock Events Aren't Controlled

After shutdown or idle periods, systems can rebound quickly unless restart, cleaning, and disinfection steps are executed and verified. Many programs lack formal protocols for these critical transition periods.

**To Prevent Return:**
- Formalize startup and layup procedures including cleaning and disinfection where required
- Document verification steps and ensure they're consistently followed

## Practical Prevention Package

### Physical Cleanliness First

Address basin, sump, strainer, and fill condition. Remove existing sludge and biofilm, then implement measures to prevent deposits from re-accumulating. Physical cleaning must precede chemical treatment for maximum effectiveness.

### Stable Residual Control

Establish verified measurement and control systems, preferably automated. Confirm adequate residual levels at multiple representative points throughout the system, not just convenient sampling locations.

### Solids Management

Implement proper blowdown control plus filtration where needed. This prevents the common problem where treatment loosens biomass that then redeposits elsewhere in the system.

### Hydraulics and Water Age Control

Minimize low-flow zones and manage intermittent operation and idle cells. Good hydraulic design and operational practices prevent the stagnant conditions that promote bacterial growth.

### Program Structure

Develop a comprehensive water management program that sets control limits, establishes monitoring protocols, and defines predetermined corrective actions when limits aren't met. Document everything for consistency and regulatory compliance.

### Appropriate Organism Indicators

Use heterotrophic plate count and total viable count for trending purposes. Add targeted testing where your water management program or authority having jurisdiction requires validation.

## Key Takeaway

The diagnostic pattern that reveals the true cause: when bacterial counts drop immediately after treatment but rebound within days, you're dealing with biofilm reservoirs and inconsistent residual distribution rather than new contamination. Address this by implementing weekly mechanical cleaning of basins and strainers, maintaining 0.5-1.0 ppm free chlorine residual at three representative system points, and adding monthly non-oxidizing biocide treatments timed with increased blowdown to remove loosened biomass.
