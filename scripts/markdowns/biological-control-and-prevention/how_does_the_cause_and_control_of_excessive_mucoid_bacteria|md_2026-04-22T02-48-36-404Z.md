# How does the cause and control of excessive mucoid bacteria differ from total viable plate count?

## Executive Summary

Mucoid bacteria indicates biofilm formation and slime production, while total viable plate count measures overall culturable microorganisms in bulk water. Each requires different control strategies—mucoid bacteria needs physical cleaning and biofilm disruption, while high total counts need improved oxidizer control and solids management.

## Overview

A cooling tower can show normal bacterial counts in the main circulation while harboring slimy, persistent colonies in dead zones and deposits. These mucoid colonies produce up to 90% more extracellular slime than typical bacteria, creating protected reservoirs that standard biocide treatments often miss. The difference between these two measurements reveals whether you're dealing with a system-wide microbial bloom or localized biofilm establishment.

## What Each Result Actually Tells You

**Total viable plate count (TVC/HPC)** serves as a non-specific indicator of live, culturable heterotrophic microorganisms in the sample. Water treatment professionals use this test as a general water quality and microbial condition indicator. Research shows that increases in HPC correlate with greater biofilm formation in building water systems, making it useful as an overall trend indicator.

**Mucoid bacteria** appear as slimy, viscous colonies caused by high production of extracellular polymeric substances (EPS)—the hydrated matrix that supports biofilms. Mucoid variants in bacteria like *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* overproduce viscous polysaccharides such as alginate, creating a biofilm-adapted phenotype. These colonies signal strong slime and EPS production, indicating biofilm propensity even when bulk-water counts remain normal.

## How the Causes Differ

**High TVC/HPC** typically results from system-wide bloom drivers affecting bulk water conditions. Inadequate oxidizer exposure and residual control allow microorganisms to multiply freely. Increased organic load and nutrients provide food sources for growth. Poor blowdown and solids control concentrate these nutrients while warm water combined with high water age creates ideal conditions for culturable heterotrophs to flourish throughout the system.

**Excessive mucoid colonies** develop under conditions that favor surface growth and EPS protection. Persistent deposits and slime on basin walls, fill, sumps, and strainers provide attachment surfaces. Low-flow and dead zones allow mucoid biofilm communities to outcompete planktonic bacteria. Intermittent or poorly distributed biocide exposure selects for more protected phenotypes, since EPS provides the key scaffold that shields biofilms from treatment chemicals.

## Control Strategy Differences

**When TVC/HPC runs high**, focus on restoring bulk-water microbiological control. Verify oxidizer feed, controller function, and measurement accuracy. Confirm residual levels at representative points throughout the system, not just one location. Improve suspended solids control through enhanced blowdown, filtration performance, and strainer maintenance. Address operating conditions that increase water age and stagnation, such as cell isolation and intermittent operation.

**When mucoid colonies become excessive**, prioritize biofilm removal and niche elimination. Conduct mechanical cleaning and deposit removal where slime accumulates. Pair biocide treatments with biofilm disruption using biodispersants, ensuring loosened material gets removed through filtration and blowdown to prevent re-seeding. Fix hydraulic dead zones and low-flow areas where slime layers persist. The EPS matrix significantly increases persistence and tolerance—simply raising bulk oxidizer levels without removing deposits often provides only temporary improvement while leaving the protected reservoir intact.

## Practical Interpretation Matrix

**High TVC + high mucoid** indicates system-wide loss of control plus established slime and biofilm. Prioritize cleaning, solids removal, and biofilm-focused response first, then restore steady residual control.

**High TVC + low mucoid** suggests primarily planktonic bloom or upset. Focus on oxidizer control, blowdown and solids management, and operating conditions.

**Normal TVC + high mucoid** points to localized slime niches or selection pressure that a single bulk sample might miss. Inspect and clean targeted locations, then re-sample from dead zones versus main flow areas.

## Key Takeaway

Test for both parameters weekly during biofilm episodes, targeting mucoid counts below 10% of total colonies. When mucoid bacteria exceed this threshold, conduct physical cleaning of basin surfaces and dead zones before increasing biocide dosing. Consider switching to biodispersant-enhanced biocide programs that can penetrate the EPS matrix more effectively than standard oxidizers alone.
