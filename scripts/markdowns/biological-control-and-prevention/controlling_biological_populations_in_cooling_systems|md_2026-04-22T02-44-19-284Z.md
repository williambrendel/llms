# How do I Control Biological Populations in Cooling Towers?

## Executive Summary

Controlling biological populations in cooling towers requires continuous oxidizing biocide, physical cleaning, and solids management working together. The most common failures happen when operators rely only on chemical treatment while ignoring sediment removal, dead zones, or biofilm buildup in basins and fill media.

## Overview

A cooling tower running at 0.5 ppm chlorine residual can still develop dangerous microbial growth if the basin contains sludge or the sidestream filter hasn't been serviced in months. Biofilm creates protected environments where bacteria survive even aggressive chemical treatment, while deposits and scale provide the rough surfaces that accelerate new growth.

## Physical Cleaning and Deposit Control

Keep the cooling tower physically clean through routine maintenance and sidestream filtration. Remove sediment and biofilm from basins, fill media, and strainers before they create protected habitats for microorganisms.

Sidestream filtration removes suspended solids that would otherwise settle and create biofilm refuges. Maintain these filters consistently - a clogged or bypassed sidestream filter eliminates this critical defense against biological growth.

Perform thorough cleaning and disinfection after seasonal shutdowns or extended idle periods. Stagnant water creates ideal conditions for microbial establishment and biofilm formation.

## Chemical Treatment Program

Maintain an oxidizing biocide program using halogen, bromine, or chlorine dioxide. Choose between continuous feed at 0.5 to 1.0 ppm halogen or slug feed targeting 5 ppm free halogen for 2 hours, with a 7 ppm maximum and 3 ppm minimum.

Test residual levels at multiple representative points throughout the system. Single-point testing misses dead zones where biological growth concentrates.

Supplement oxidizing biocides with non-oxidizing biocides on a scheduled rotation. This combination penetrates established biofilm and prevents resistance development that occurs when microorganisms adapt to single treatment methods.

Add dispersants or biodispersants to help remove existing biofilm and prevent redeposition. Pair dispersant treatment with increased filtration and blowdown to actually remove the loosened material from the system.

## Water Chemistry and Operational Controls

Control scaling, corrosion, and concentration cycles through proper water chemistry management. Maintain appropriate pH, conductivity, and inhibitor levels because both scale and corrosion create rough surfaces that accelerate biofilm attachment and growth.

Ensure blowdown systems function properly and match the target cycles of concentration. Poor blowdown control concentrates nutrients and suspended solids that feed biological growth.

Minimize water age and eliminate dead zones through consistent circulation. During intermittent operation or low-load conditions, prevent prolonged stagnation by operating pumps periodically, flushing low-flow piping legs, and managing cooling cell isolation properly.

## Monitoring and Response

Monitor oxidant residual and pH at consistent locations because biocide efficacy depends heavily on pH levels. Track biological trends using Heterotrophic Plate Count or BART testing to establish site-specific alert and action levels.

Watch for visual and operational indicators including increased turbidity, basin contamination, rising filter differential pressure, unusual odors, and foaming that signal biological activity.

When microbial indicators trend upward, respond systematically. First, confirm residual levels, feed system function, and controller calibration. Then shock dose with oxidizer according to program requirements, add or rotate non-oxidizing biocides with dispersants, and physically remove deposits through basin cleaning and improved filtration. Finally, verify recovery through continued trend monitoring.

## Common Failure Points

Address these frequent problem areas first for immediate improvement. Dirty basins or inadequate solids removal create biofilm refuges that resist chemical treatment. Single-point residual testing misses dead zones where growth concentrates. Adding biocide without cleaning or filtration kills planktonic cells while leaving protective biofilm intact. High pH reduces halogen efficacy and may require water chemistry adjustment before biocide programs can work effectively.

## Key Takeaways

Test oxidant residual weekly at multiple tower locations, maintaining 0.5-1.0 ppm continuous or 5 ppm for 2-hour slug doses. Clean basins and service sidestream filters monthly, switching to non-oxidizing biocides plus dispersants when Heterotrophic Plate Counts exceed 10,000 CFU/mL or site-specific action levels.
