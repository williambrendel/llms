# Why do bio-dispersants help oxidizers work better in cooling towers and other evaporative systems?

## Executive Summary

Bio-dispersants dramatically improve oxidizer performance by breaking down biofilm's protective matrix, allowing chlorine, bromine, and chlorine dioxide to reach and kill organisms like Pseudomonas that would otherwise survive treatment.

## Overview

Cooling towers often show a frustrating pattern: total plate counts drop briefly after oxidizer treatment, then bounce right back within days. The culprit isn't weak chemistry—it's biofilm acting as a fortress that shields bacteria from disinfection. Some biofilms can reduce antimicrobial effectiveness by 1000-fold compared to free-floating cells.

## How Biofilm Defeats Oxidizers Alone

Biofilm creates a nearly impenetrable defense system. Cells embed themselves in an extracellular polymeric substance (EPS) matrix that acts as both physical barrier and chemical buffer against oxidative stress. This protective coating explains why Pseudomonas and other strong biofilm formers persist despite aggressive chlorination.

Cooling systems make the problem worse. Biofilm establishes itself in dead ends, low-flow zones, and areas where treatment chemicals barely reach. After oxidizer dosing, planktonic bacteria die off quickly, but the biofilm reservoir remains intact and continuously releases new cells into the bulk water.

## Bio-Dispersant Mechanisms That Create Synergy

Different bio-dispersant products use varying chemistries—surfactant-type dispersants, polymeric dispersants, and enzyme-assisted formulations—but they share three critical mechanisms.

**Disrupting the protective outer layer** represents the primary mode of action. Major water treatment suppliers describe bio-dispersants as breaking down biofilm's surface barrier, allowing biocides to penetrate previously protected areas. The EPS matrix that once deflected oxidizers becomes permeable.

**Reducing adhesion and increasing detachment** transforms surface problems into removable solids problems. Bio-dispersants loosen biofilm from surfaces so deposits can be carried out through filtration and blowdown rather than remaining as protected reservoirs.

**Improving wetting and access to micro-niches** ensures uniform cleaning effects on surfaces where deposits create micro-environments. Industrial reviews emphasize how surfactant properties help oxidizers reach areas that would otherwise remain untreated.

The net effect on Pseudomonas and total plate counts becomes dramatic. Pseudomonas persists specifically because its biofilm phenotype resists disinfectants, but dispersal exposes cells and weakens the protective matrix. Total plate counts fall more sustainably when the biofilm reservoir shrinks rather than just killing planktonic cells.

One critical operational requirement: the system must remove what gets detached. Without proper blowdown, filtration, or cleaning, bio-dispersants temporarily increase bulk counts and turbidity as biofilm strips away.

## Open vs Closed Cooling System Approaches

**Open recirculating systems** like cooling towers and evaporative condensers continuously import microbes and nutrients while concentrating them through evaporation. This creates ideal conditions for extensive fouling.

Bio-dispersant plus oxidizer typically operates as periodic "biofilm cleaning events" combined with ongoing preventive dosing. The strategy must couple with aggressive solids removal through sidestream filtration, basin cleaning, and controlled blowdown.

**Closed loop systems** usually face lower nutrient loading but develop persistent problems from construction debris, oils, corrosion products, and under-deposit niches. Frequent makeup water, oxygen ingress, and low-flow segments compound these issues.

Bio-dispersant use becomes more project-focused in closed systems—cleanup and conditioning phases or targeted treatment of known problem areas. Chronic high suspended solids create operational problems, so filtration and side-stream cleanup of mobilized material becomes critical.

## New Systems: Commissioning Cleanup Strategy

Systems less than 12 months old typically struggle with residual oils, greases, construction fines, mill scale, flux, and startup corrosion products. These materials create immediate biofilm establishment and oxidizer demand that overwhelms standard treatment.

Treat this as commissioning cleanup rather than routine maintenance. Use bio-dispersant to mobilize organics and slime while exposing clean surfaces, then apply oxidizer to inactivate organisms as they become vulnerable. Run aggressive solids removal during and after treatment through filtration, blowdown, and strainer cleaning.

Expect short-term spikes in turbidity and total plate counts as biofilm strips away. These increases indicate successful removal rather than treatment failure. After cleanup, shift to stable residual control plus routine cleaning protocols.

## Established Systems: Breaking Entrenched Biofilm

Well-established systems with chronic high total plate counts or recurring Pseudomonas face mature biofilm in dead legs, low-flow zones, fill packs, and under deposits. Treatment chemicals either don't reach these areas or get consumed before penetration occurs.

Use bio-dispersant as a biofilm access tool to disrupt the outer matrix and improve penetration, followed by oxidizer as the kill step. Combine with targeted physical cleaning of known reservoirs including basin sludge, fouled fill, and strainers. Dispersants plus biocide alone won't reduce fouling rates if deposits remain.

Address fundamental "reach" problems by correcting low-flow zones, intermittent circulation, and poor monitoring locations. Biofilm establishes where treatments can't reach consistently.

Without removing deposits, increased oxidizer dosing often creates a sawtooth pattern where total plate counts drop then rebound. Biocides reduce numbers but don't reduce fouling unless dosage, frequency, and biofilm control align properly.

## Practical Implementation Guidelines

Ensure export capacity for mobilized biomass through adequate blowdown, filtration, and strainer cleaning plans. Expect temporary fouling loads that will plug filters and strainers while increasing basin solids during biofilm stripping.

Verify oxidizer effectiveness at operating pH and representative system points. Monitor total plate counts, Pseudomonas levels, and biofilm indicators rather than just residual chemical levels.

## Key Takeaway

Bio-dispersants work by dismantling biofilm's protective matrix rather than killing organisms directly—that's the oxidizer's job. Test total plate counts weekly during treatment, maintain 0.5-1.0 ppm free chlorine residual throughout the system, and consider enzyme-enhanced dispersants for systems with recurring Pseudomonas problems above 1000 CFU/mL.
