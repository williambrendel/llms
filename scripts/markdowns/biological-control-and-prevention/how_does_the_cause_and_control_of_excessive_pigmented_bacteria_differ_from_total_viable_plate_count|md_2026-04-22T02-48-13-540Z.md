# How Does the Cause and Control of Excessive Pigmented Bacteria Differ from Total Viable Plate Count?

## Executive Summary

Total Viable Plate Count (TVC) and pigmented bacteria measure different aspects of microbial activity, so high results require different control strategies. TVC indicates overall microbial biomass and system-wide conditions, while excessive pigmented bacteria typically signals biofilm formation and ecological selection in protected niches.

## Overview

A cooling tower showing high TVC with low pigmented bacteria points to a planktonic bloom from recent system upsets. But normal TVC with high pigmented bacteria suggests established biofilm in dead zones that standard oxidizer programs can't reach. This distinction changes everything about how you respond.

## What Each Measure Reveals

**Total Viable Plate Count (TVC)** provides a broad, non-specific count of live, culturable heterotrophs, typically reported as CFU/mL. TVC serves as a general indicator of overall microbial biomass and activity, reflecting changes in system conditions across the entire cooling water system.

**Pigmented bacteria** represents a visual subset of colonies that produce visible pigments like red, orange, yellow, or brown/black colors. This measurement isn't a separate standardized microbiological category but rather a diagnostic clue noted because certain pigment producers commonly thrive in wet biofilms. The results depend more heavily on media, incubation conditions, light, and temperature than TVC measurements.

## How Causes Differ Between High TVC and Excessive Pigmented Bacteria

**High TVC typically stems from system-wide drivers** that create favorable conditions for broad microbial growth. Insufficient oxidizer exposure or poor residual control allows bacteria to multiply throughout the system. Elevated nutrients from makeup water, process contamination, or environmental loading feed this growth. High suspended solids and deposits create biofilm habitat, while poor circulation creates dead zones where bacteria accumulate. Scale, corrosion, and sediment provide surface roughness that protects biofilm from disinfectants.

**Excessive pigmented bacteria indicates ecological selection rather than simply more bacteria overall.** These conditions favor biofilm-dominant growth, where organisms adapt to surfaces and intermittent disinfectant exposure. Longer water age in stagnant niches allows specific organisms to outcompete others. Localized deposits and slime favor particular genera, many of which are strong biofilm formers that also produce pigments. Temperature and incubation effects can also influence pigment expression during testing.

Pigment production signals a pattern suggesting biofilm niche development and selection pressure rather than purely planktonic growth throughout the system.

## Practical Interpretation Matrix for Field Use

**High TVC + High Pigmented Bacteria** provides strong evidence of broad biofouling combined with established biofilm. Verify oxidizer performance, improve cleaning and filtration, and implement a comprehensive biofilm control program.

**High TVC + Low Pigmented Bacteria** indicates a likely planktonic bloom from recent system upset, nutrient influx, or low oxidizer exposure. Focus on oxidizer control optimization and improving blowdown and solids removal.

**Normal TVC + High Pigmented Bacteria** suggests protected niches with established biofilm or potential method artifacts. Inspect and clean targeted areas, then repeat sampling to confirm results.

**Normal TVC + Normal Pigmented Bacteria** indicates effective control. Continue routine monitoring and maintenance programs.

## Control Response Strategies

**When TVC runs high, focus on reducing overall microbial growth conditions.** Verify oxidizer control by checking feed equipment, controllers, and test methods to ensure residual maintenance at representative monitoring points. Improve solids and deposit control through basin cleaning, strainer maintenance, sidestream filtration optimization, and blowdown control. Optimize your biocide program by coordinating oxidizer programs with non-oxidizing biocide rotation as designed in your treatment plan, paired with dispersants where specified. Address operational factors by reducing stagnation through improved pump scheduling and cell isolation practices.

**When pigmented bacteria levels spike disproportionately, treat it as a biofilm signal requiring targeted intervention.** Find and eliminate niches including dead legs, low-flow zones, basin sludge accumulation, fouled fill media, and sidestream filter problems. Prioritize mechanical cleaning over simply increasing biocide doses, since pigmented colonies commonly persist in protected biofilm matrices that chemicals can't penetrate effectively.

Implement biofilm-focused treatment steps according to your program specifications: apply biodispersants paired with biocides, then remove loosened solids through enhanced filtration, blowdown, and physical cleaning. Check for sampling artifacts by confirming media, incubation conditions, and timing, then repeat sampling from the same location and a contrasting point to determine if the problem is localized.

## Key Takeaway

Monitor both TVC and pigmented bacteria weekly during normal operations, monthly during stable periods. When TVC exceeds 10,000 CFU/mL, verify oxidizer residuals and increase blowdown. When pigmented bacteria represent more than 30% of total colonies, inspect dead zones and low-flow areas for biofilm accumulation, then implement mechanical cleaning before adjusting chemical programs.
