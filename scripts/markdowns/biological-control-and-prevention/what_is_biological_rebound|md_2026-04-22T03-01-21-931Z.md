# Why Do Bacteria Come Back After Water Treatment?

## Executive Summary

Bacteria populations rebound after water treatment because low-dose oxidants kill free-floating organisms but leave biofilm communities intact, which then reseed the water within 24-72 hours.

## Overview

A cooling tower treated with hypobromous acid can show bacterial counts dropping from 1.4 million to 8,000 CFU/mL in one day, only to surge back to 110,000 CFU/mL six weeks later. This dramatic rebound reveals how biofilms act as protected bacterial reservoirs that standard oxidant treatments cannot penetrate effectively.

## The Biological Rebound Mechanism

### Initial Kill Phase

Hypobromous acid (HOBr) effectively reduces planktonic bacteria in bulk water at low concentrations. These free-floating organisms have no protective barriers and succumb quickly to oxidation. However, this initial success masks a deeper problem.

### Biofilm Protection

Biofilm communities survive the treatment because penetration into biofilm layers remains poor without biodispersants. The extracellular polymeric substances (EPS) that surround biofilm bacteria create a protective matrix. Low oxidant doses meet insufficient oxidative demand, allowing organisms deeper in the biofilm structure to survive and potentially develop increased tolerance through sublethal exposure.

### Reservoir Effect

Surviving biofilm bacteria continuously reseed the bulk water with new planktonic organisms after oxidant levels decay. Many cooling tower bacteria species, including Pseudomonas, coliforms, and Legionella, double their population every 20-60 minutes under favorable conditions. Once the hypobromous acid residual drops within hours, this rapid growth rate enables explosive population recovery.

### Rebound Amplification

The rebound often exceeds original bacterial levels because the treatment creates selective pressure favoring resistant species rather than achieving complete eradication. Nutrient cycling from dead bacteria and organic matter fuels regrowth after oxidant dissipation. The intact biofilm matrix continues preventing deep oxidant penetration while serving as a continuous contamination source.

## Control Benchmarks for Well-Run Systems

Industry best practices from CTI, ASHRAE, and major treatment vendors establish clear bacterial control ranges:

**Total Plate Count (TPC)**
- Good control: <10,000 CFU/mL  
- Upper action limit: 100,000 CFU/mL
- Above 100,000 indicates poor control and high biofilm risk

**Coliforms**
- Target: Non-detectable
- Action threshold: >100 per 100 mL
- Any detection indicates contamination pathways or biofilm release

**Pseudomonas aeruginosa**  
- Target: Non-detectable
- Well-controlled: <100 CFU/mL
- 1,000 CFU/mL signals significant biofilm activity
- 10,000 CFU/mL indicates major reservoir problems

## Case Study Analysis

The treatment data reveals classic under-treatment patterns:

**Before Treatment (June 24):**
- TPC at 1.4 million indicated severe biofouling
- Coliforms at 1,000 exceeded acceptable limits by 10-fold  
- Pseudomonas at 79,000 showed well-established biofilm reservoirs

**1 Day Post-Treatment:**
- TPC dropped to 8,000 (within target range)
- Coliforms unchanged at 1,000 (no effective kill)
- Pseudomonas reduced 90% to 7,000 but remained 70 times above acceptable levels

**6 Weeks Post-Treatment:**
- TPC rebounded to 110,000 (poor control territory)
- Coliforms doubled to 2,000 
- Pseudomonas reached 85,000 (worse than baseline)

## Comprehensive Treatment Protocol

### Phase 0: Immediate Controls (Days 0-3)

| Step | Action | Dose/Setpoint | Contact Time/Frequency | Target | Notes |
|------|--------|---------------|----------------------|---------|-------|
| 1 | pH adjustment | 8.2-8.4 | Continuous | Stable pH band | Above 8.6 reduces HOBr efficacy |
| 2 | Biodispersant shock | 20-30 mg/L | Daily × 3 days, 60-90 min before oxidant | Visual turbidity increase | Use polymeric/surfactant, not quat |
| 3 | HOBr shock | 5-7 mg/L as HOBr | 2 hours with recirculation | ≥3 mg/L residual at all points | Re-dose if residual drops below 3 mg/L |
| 4 | Solids capture | Side-stream 5-10%, ≤10 µm filtration | Continuous | Filter loading expected | Physical removal during biofilm release |
| 5 | Physical cleaning | Basin skimming, strainer cleaning | Days 1-3 | Complete debris removal | Non-negotiable during sloughing |

### Phase 1: Stabilization (Weeks 1-3)

| Step | Action | Dose/Setpoint | Frequency | Target | Notes |
|------|--------|---------------|-----------|---------|-------|
| 1 | Continuous HOBr | 0.5-1.0 mg/L Br₂ residual | 24/7 | Stable residual near and far | Calibrate analyzer for bromine |
| 2 | Programmed slugs | 3-5 mg/L HOBr | M/W/F, 2h each | ≥2 mg/L end residual | Maintains biofilm stress |
| 3 | Biodispersant maintenance | 10-15 mg/L | 2×/week, 60 min before oxidant | Decreasing particulate release | Maintain sequence order |
| 4 | Non-oxidizer rotation A | Isothiazolinones 5-10 mg/L | Weekly, 4-6h contact | ≥90% Pseudomonas reduction | Check metals first |
| 5 | Non-oxidizer rotation B | DBNPA 10-20 mg/L | Alternate weeks, 2-3h | Low TPC between oxidizer slugs | Separate dosing days |
| 6 | Metals control | Fe + Cu ≤0.3 mg/L | Continuous monitoring | Trending ≤0.3 mg/L | Protects biocide efficacy |

### Phase 2: Maintenance (Week 4+)

Reduce continuous HOBr to 0.3-0.7 mg/L, maintain weekly 3 mg/L slugs, and rotate non-oxidizers every other week. Biodispersant frequency drops to weekly, then biweekly only after eight weeks of stable control.

## Monitoring and Action Limits

Critical parameters require specific monitoring frequencies and response triggers:

**Bromine Residual:** Daily monitoring in Phase 1, targeting 0.5-1.0 mg/L. If residual drops below 0.3 mg/L for more than 4 hours, immediately slug to 3-5 mg/L and investigate demand.

**Bacterial Counts:** Test TPC twice weekly, targeting <10,000 CFU/mL. At ≥100,000 CFU/mL, run dispersant plus 5-7 mg/L HOBr for 2 hours same day.

**Pseudomonas:** Weekly testing with target of non-detect or <100 CFU/mL. At ≥1,000 CFU/mL, add non-oxidizer cycle next day and repeat in 7 days.

## Key Operational Rules

Sequence biodispersant 60-90 minutes before biocide application - never co-feed these chemicals. Verify contact time with residual measurements at both near and far sampling points during every treatment. Maintain pH between 8.2-8.4 since HOBr efficacy drops significantly above 8.6. Control metals and solids as root causes, not secondary issues, since high iron/copper and suspended solids neutralize biocides and shelter organisms.

## Takeaway

Prevent biological rebound by combining biodispersants with oxidants weekly, maintaining 0.5-1.0 mg/L bromine residual continuously, and rotating non-oxidizing biocides every two weeks. Target Pseudomonas counts below 100 CFU/mL as your primary success metric, testing weekly until you achieve two consecutive non-detect results.
