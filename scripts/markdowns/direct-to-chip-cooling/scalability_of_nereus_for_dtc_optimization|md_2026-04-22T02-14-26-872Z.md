# What's the economic case for Nereus's DTC optimization system?

## Executive Summary

Nereus's DTC optimization system generates $925,000 in annual customer value through a 50% reduction in fluid-related failures and 60% reduction in fluid-related downtime, with a 1.42-year payback period at the revised $1.17 million hardware price plus $100,000 annual service fee.

## Overview

A $1.17 million water treatment system that saves customers nearly a million dollars annually sounds too good to be true. The Nereus DTC optimization system achieves this by targeting a specific but critical failure mode: coolant-quality problems that can shut down entire data centers. While the system only addresses 5-10% of total site downtime risk, it eliminates 1,000 hours of operator work per year and prevents $350,000 in maintenance costs.

## Economic Framework

The revised pricing model balances profitability with market accessibility. Hardware costs $585,000 to manufacture and sells for $1.17 million, achieving the target 50% gross margin. Annual service costs $50,000 to deliver and sells for $100,000, maintaining the same 50% margin. This combined approach produces a 50% year-one gross margin while spreading the investment across hardware and ongoing service.

The 100 gallons per minute treatment rate works deliberately on prevention rather than rapid cleanup for million-gallon cooling systems. The value comes from staged filtration, side-stream ultrafiltration, and continuous monitoring of chemistry, particles, biology, and pressure drop to catch problems before they cascade into system failures.

## Customer Value Analysis

**Reliability Improvement:** The system reduces fluid-related failures by 30-70%, with a best estimate of 50%. This improvement stems from preventing fouling and contamination through early detection and intervention, though it only addresses coolant-quality issues rather than the full spectrum of potential outages.

**Downtime Reduction:** Fluid-related downtime drops by 40-80% (best estimate: 60%), translating to a 2-15% reduction in total site downtime (best estimate: 5-10%). The modest total impact reflects the system's focused scope—it cannot prevent power failures, hardware malfunctions, or network issues.

**Operator Time Savings:** The system eliminates 500-2,000 hours of manual work annually (best estimate: 1,000 hours), equivalent to 0.5 full-time employees. At loaded labor costs of $120,000-$180,000 per position, this generates $60,000-$90,000 in annual savings through reduced monitoring, testing, and reactive maintenance.

**Maintenance Cost Avoidance:** Annual maintenance savings range from $175,000-$750,000 (best estimate: $350,000) through reduced fouling response, avoided contamination cleanup, fewer reactive flushes, and fewer branch or cold-plate service events.

**Avoided Downtime Value:** The economic impact of prevented outages varies dramatically by facility type, ranging from $100,000-$2,000,000 annually (best estimate: $500,000). High-value AI and hyperscale facilities experience the upper end of this range due to revenue density and operational criticality.

## Value Scenarios by Case

**Low Case ($315,000 annual value):** Combines $40,000 in operator savings, $175,000 in maintenance avoidance, and $100,000 in downtime prevention. Hardware payback extends to 3.71 years, or 5.44 years including service fees.

**Base Case ($925,000 annual value):** Combines $75,000 in operator savings, $350,000 in maintenance avoidance, and $500,000 in downtime prevention. Hardware payback drops to 1.27 years, or 1.42 years including service fees.

**High Case ($2.9 million annual value):** Combines $150,000 in operator savings, $750,000 in maintenance avoidance, and $2,000,000 in downtime prevention. Hardware payback falls to 0.4 years, or 0.42 years including service fees.

## Market Salability Assessment

Overall salability ranges from 55-75% with a best estimate of 65%. Four factors support adoption: reduced hardware price improves capital approval odds, moderate recurring service costs, sustainable 50% service margins, and commercially respectable combined margins.

**Hyperscale and High-Value AI Sites (70-85% likelihood):** High outage costs and cooling reliability sensitivity justify the $1.17 million investment. These facilities typically operate in the high-value scenario range.

**Large Enterprise and Colocation Sites (50-70% likelihood):** Moderate coolant problems make this a plausible reliability purchase when fluid-quality issues are documented and visible to operations teams.

**Sites Without Quantified Problems (20-35% likelihood):** Precautionary purchases remain difficult to justify without demonstrated pain points like contamination history, fouling events, or high labor burden.

## Operational Impact Summary

The system delivers measurable improvements across four operational areas. Reliability improves through 30-70% reduction in fluid-related failures (best estimate: 50%). Downtime decreases through 40-80% reduction in fluid-related outages (best estimate: 60%), though total site impact remains modest at 5-10%. Labor efficiency gains 500-2,000 hours annually (best estimate: 1,000 hours), equivalent to half a full-time position. Maintenance burden drops by $175,000-$750,000 annually (best estimate: $350,000) through proactive intervention.

## Implementation Considerations

The system remains attractive for customers in base and high-value scenarios but struggles in low-value cases where payback exceeds five years. Success requires buyers with real pain points: contamination history, plugging or fouling events, high labor burden, significant outage costs, or documented reliability drift.

The technology addresses only coolant-related failures within the broader outage risk profile. While this limitation constrains total impact, it also creates a focused value proposition for facilities where cooling system reliability drives operational risk and cost.
