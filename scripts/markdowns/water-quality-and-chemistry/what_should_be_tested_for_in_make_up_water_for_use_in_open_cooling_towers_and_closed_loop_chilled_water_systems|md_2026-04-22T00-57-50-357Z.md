# Can reclaimed water be safely used in data center cooling towers?

## Executive Summary

Reclaimed water can be effectively used in data center cooling towers when properly analyzed and treated, but requires comprehensive testing of 30+ parameters including scaling minerals, corrosive ions, nutrients, and pathogens to prevent equipment damage and health risks.

## Overview

Data centers using reclaimed water in their cooling systems face a surprising challenge: a single untested parameter like barium can create rock-hard scale deposits that are nearly impossible to remove. While reclaimed water offers significant cost and sustainability benefits, its complex chemistry demands far more rigorous analysis than traditional makeup water sources.

## Critical Water Quality Parameters for Cooling Tower Assessment

### Scaling and Corrosion Control

Calcium and magnesium drive the most common scaling problems in cooling towers. Calcium combines with carbonate and phosphate to form deposits that reduce heat transfer efficiency. Magnesium creates alkaline scales, particularly magnesium hydroxide, that build up at higher pH levels.

**What it measures:** Calcium carbonate scaling potential based on water conductivity

Formula:
```
TDS (mg/L) ≈ 0.65 × Conductivity (µS/cm)
```

**Example:** Water with 1,500 µS/cm conductivity contains approximately 975 mg/L total dissolved solids, indicating moderate scaling potential that requires chemical treatment.

Barium and strontium create particularly problematic scales. Barium sulfate forms extremely hard deposits that resist most cleaning methods. These metals often go undetected in standard water analysis but can shut down cooling systems when they precipitate.

Chloride poses the greatest corrosion threat to stainless steel and copper components. Even moderate concentrations accelerate pitting corrosion, particularly in the presence of oxygen and elevated temperatures typical in cooling towers.

### Chemical Treatment Compatibility

Silica becomes increasingly problematic at high pH and temperature conditions. It forms glassy deposits on heat exchange surfaces that severely impact thermal performance. Silica scaling intensifies as water concentrates through evaporation in cooling towers.

Phosphate management requires careful balance. Orthophosphate can precipitate with calcium to form scale, while polyphosphates serve as effective corrosion inhibitors but gradually revert to orthophosphate over time.

Ammonia creates dual problems in cooling systems. It directly corrodes copper alloys through complexation reactions and serves as a nutrient for nitrifying bacteria that can destabilize water chemistry.

## Reclaimed Water Regulatory Standards

### United States Framework

U.S. reclaimed water follows strict EPA and state guidelines, with California's Title 22 setting performance standards for pathogen removal and disinfection. TSS and BOD are controlled below 10-30 mg/L for high-quality reuse applications. Nitrogen and phosphorus undergo advanced treatment to reduce nutrient loads.

Industrial discharges to reuse streams face tight regulation, and frequent public reporting ensures transparency. E. coli must be non-detectable, and turbidity stays within strict limits.

### Biological Control Considerations

BOD indicates the organic load that fuels biofilm growth in cooling systems. High BOD water requires increased biocide demand and more aggressive biological control programs.

Total suspended solids create mechanical fouling and provide surfaces for microbial attachment. They also contribute to under-deposit corrosion by creating oxygen concentration cells on metal surfaces.

Nutrients like nitrogen and phosphorus support extensive biofilm development. Total Kjeldahl Nitrogen measures organic nitrogen and ammonia that bacteria readily consume for growth.

## Pathogen and Safety Management

E. coli serves as the primary indicator of fecal contamination in reclaimed water. While these bacteria typically don't survive long in cooling towers due to temperature and biocide exposure, their presence indicates treatment adequacy.

Giardia and Cryptosporidium represent protozoan pathogens that resist standard disinfection. Though not viable in properly maintained cooling systems, their detection suggests inadequate source control.

Cyanide and hydrogen sulfide require monitoring due to their toxicity and corrosive properties. Hydrogen sulfide promotes sulfide-producing bacteria that accelerate corrosion rates.

## Implementation Requirements

Successful reclaimed water use in cooling towers demands testing all 30+ parameters before system startup. Missing even one critical parameter like barium or boron can lead to equipment failure or regulatory violations.

Treatment programs must account for the complex interactions between scaling, corrosion, and biological control. Standard cooling tower chemicals may require modification when dealing with reclaimed water's unique chemistry.

Monitoring frequency should increase compared to traditional makeup water sources. Weekly testing of key parameters like pH, conductivity, and biocide residuals becomes essential, with monthly comprehensive analysis of all scaling and corrosion indicators.

## Key Takeaways

Test reclaimed water for all 30+ parameters monthly, maintain chloride below 500 mg/L to prevent pitting corrosion, and consider membrane pretreatment systems when calcium exceeds 200 mg/L or silica surpasses 150 mg/L to ensure reliable cooling tower operation.
