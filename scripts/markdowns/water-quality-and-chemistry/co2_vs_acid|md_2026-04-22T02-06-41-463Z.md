# Does using CO2 instead of acid for pH control actually reduce carbon footprint?

## Executive Summary

CO2 typically produces a lower overall carbon footprint than sulfuric acid for pH adjustment, especially when the CO2 comes from captured industrial sources and when you account for reduced corrosion and scaling impacts.

## Overview

A million gallons of treated water might seem like a drop in the bucket for climate impact, but the choice between CO2 and sulfuric acid for pH control can swing your facility's carbon footprint by hundreds of kilograms annually. The math gets interesting when you dig past the obvious irony of adding CO2 to reduce emissions.

## Carbon Source and Manufacturing Impact

CO2 used for water treatment often comes from captured industrial processes like ammonia or ethanol production. This recycled CO2 carries an embodied carbon footprint of just 0.1–0.3 kg CO2e per kg used. Sulfuric acid tells a different story—the energy-intensive contact process that burns sulfur or pyrite generates 1.3–1.8 kg CO2e per kg of acid produced.

The direct emissions comparison flips the script. Adding CO2 to water releases no additional emissions when the gas comes from captured sources. You're essentially moving existing CO2 from one place to another. Sulfuric acid doesn't directly emit CO2 during use, but its manufacturing footprint follows every drop to your facility.

## Water Chemistry Differences Drive Long-term Impact

CO2 dissolves into carbonic acid, which dissociates to release hydrogen ions without adding problematic dissolved solids. The reaction creates bicarbonate ions that your system can handle easily. Sulfuric acid delivers the same pH drop but loads your water with sulfate ions that increase total dissolved solids, conductivity, and scaling potential.

This chemistry difference cascades through your entire system. Higher TDS from sulfuric acid means more frequent blowdown cycles, increased corrosion rates, and shorter equipment life. Each of these factors multiplies the indirect carbon impact through additional chemical use, energy consumption, and premature equipment replacement.

## Real-World Carbon Calculation

For a facility treating 1 million gallons annually and removing 65 ppm alkalinity, the numbers break down like this:

**CO2 Requirements:**
- Amount needed: 572 lbs CO2 per year
- Embodied emissions: 52 kg CO2e annually

**Sulfuric Acid Requirements:**  
- Amount needed: 32 lbs H2SO4 per year
- Embodied emissions: 33 kg CO2e annually

**Formula for CO2 requirement:**
```
CO2 needed (lbs) = Alkalinity removal (ppm) × 8.8 lb/Mgal
```

**Example:** For 65 ppm alkalinity removal: 65 × 8.8 = 572 lbs CO2 per million gallons

The raw calculation shows sulfuric acid slightly ahead on direct carbon intensity. But this narrow view misses the bigger picture of system-wide impacts.

## The ESG and Asset Life Advantage

CO2 treatment qualifies for carbon reuse credits in many sustainability frameworks. Your facility can claim points for circular economy practices when using captured CO2. Sulfuric acid carries the regulatory burden of an industrial chemical with documented environmental impacts.

Equipment longevity shifts the carbon math decisively toward CO2. Lower TDS means less scaling, reduced corrosion, and longer asset life. A cooling tower that runs five extra years before replacement can offset thousands of kilograms of embodied carbon in new equipment.

## Operational Impact Assessment

| **Factor** | **CO2** | **H2SO4** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source | Often captured from industrial processes (e.g., ammonia or ethanol production) – may be reused CO₂ | Produced via energy-intensive contact process (burning sulfur or pyrite) |
| Direct CO₂ Emissions | None added if CO₂ is re-used; Net positive only if from fossil source | Indirect emissions from energy-intensive manufacture |
| Scope 3 Transport Emissions | Moderate – bulk gas or cryo liquid transport | Similar or slightly lower due to higher concentration by mass |
| Water Chemistry Impact | Adds DIC (H₂CO₃ ⇌ H⁺ + HCO₃⁻) without adding ions like Na⁺ or SO₄²⁻; no increase in TDS | Adds SO₄²⁻; increases TDS, conductivity, and scaling risk |
| Embodied CO₂ (approx.) | ~0.1–0.3 kg CO₂e per kg used (if captured) | ~1.3–1.8 kg CO₂e per kg H₂SO₄ |
| Total Kg Needed for 1 mol H⁺ | 44 g CO₂ (1 mol) | ~49 g of H₂SO₄ (1 mol H⁺) |
| Regulatory/ESG Impact | May qualify for carbon reuse credits or sustainability scores | Viewed as industrial chemical with higher environmental burden |

## Net Footprint Verdict

| **Assessment** | **Verdict** |
| --- | --- |
| Direct CO₂ emissions | CO₂ may appear worse, unless it's captured or reused |
| Embodied footprint | H₂SO₄ has a larger embodied energy and emissions profile |
| Net footprint with ESG/asset impacts | CO₂ wins, especially if carbon reuse credits, lower TDS, reduced corrosion, and improved asset longevity are factored in |

## Making the Switch Work

CO2 delivers the lowest carbon footprint when your supply chain includes captured or recycled sources and your analysis accounts for reduced scaling, corrosion, and chemical use downstream. Track your TDS levels monthly, monitor corrosion rates quarterly, and calculate the avoided carbon from extended equipment life. Most facilities see payback within two years when they factor in reduced maintenance and longer asset cycles alongside the direct carbon benefits.
