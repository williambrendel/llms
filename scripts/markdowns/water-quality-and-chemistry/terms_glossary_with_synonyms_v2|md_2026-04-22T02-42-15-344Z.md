# What are the essential water and energy terms everyone should know?

## Executive Summary

Understanding water and energy systems requires fluency in 500 core terms spanning treatment processes, cooling systems, heat exchangers, and biological controls. These terms form the foundation for effective communication between technical professionals and curious adults seeking to understand how water and energy infrastructure actually works.

## Overview

Water treatment plants process millions of gallons daily using equipment most people never see. A single cooling tower can evaporate 10,000 gallons per hour while removing heat equivalent to melting 15 tons of ice. The language describing these systems often sounds like technical jargon, but each term represents a specific process, measurement, or piece of equipment that keeps our infrastructure running safely.

## Water Treatment Fundamentals and Processes

Water treatment begins with **raw water** — untreated source water entering a plant or building. This becomes **potable water** for drinking or **process water** for manufacturing. **Makeup water** replaces losses from evaporation and bleed, while **reuse water** gets treated for beneficial applications.

Different water types require different approaches. **Brackish water** contains more salt than freshwater but less than **seawater**. **Hard water** forms scale due to calcium and magnesium, while **soft water** has these minerals removed. **Total dissolved solids** (TDS) measures all dissolved minerals, typically ranging from 50 mg/L in pure water to 35,000 mg/L in seawater.

**What it measures:** The relationship between water conductivity and dissolved solids content

Formula:
```
TDS (mg/L) ≈ 0.65 × Conductivity (µS/cm)
```

**Example:** Water with 1,000 µS/cm conductivity contains approximately 650 mg/L dissolved solids — acceptable for most industrial uses but requiring treatment for high-purity applications.

Key water quality parameters include **pH** (acid-base level), **alkalinity** (acid-neutralizing capacity), and **hardness** (scale-forming minerals). **Turbidity** measures cloudiness from suspended particles, while **dissolved oxygen** affects biological activity and corrosion rates.

## Treatment Technologies

**Coagulation** destabilizes fine particles using chemicals like aluminum sulfate. **Flocculation** follows with gentle mixing to form larger, settleable particles. **Sedimentation** removes these particles by gravity, while **filtration** captures remaining solids through sand, cartridges, or membranes.

**Membrane filtration** includes four main types: **microfiltration** removes bacteria, **ultrafiltration** removes viruses, **nanofiltration** removes some dissolved salts, and **reverse osmosis** removes most dissolved contaminants. RO systems typically operate at 150-600 psi and can achieve 95-99% salt rejection.

**Ion exchange** swaps unwanted ions for more acceptable ones. **Water softening** replaces calcium and magnesium with sodium. **Mixed bed deionization** uses both cation and anion resins to produce ultra-pure water with resistivity exceeding 10 megohm-cm.

**Activated carbon adsorption** removes chlorine, organics, and taste/odor compounds. **Granular activated carbon** (GAC) operates in fixed beds, while **powdered activated carbon** (PAC) gets added directly to water for temporary treatment.

## Cooling Towers and Evaporative Systems

**Cooling towers** remove heat by evaporating 1-2% of recirculating water. A typical tower handling 1,000 GPM with a 10°F **range** (temperature drop) and 7°F **approach** (difference from wet-bulb temperature) removes approximately 15 million BTU/hour.

**Crossflow towers** move air horizontally across falling water, while **counterflow towers** move air upward against downward water flow. **Induced draft towers** use fans pulling air through, while **forced draft towers** push air in.

**Fill** increases contact between water and air. **Film fill** spreads water in thin sheets, while **splash fill** breaks water into droplets. **Drift eliminators** capture water droplets before they leave with exhaust air.

**Cycles of concentration** show how much dissolved solids have concentrated compared to makeup water. Operating at 4 cycles means the tower water contains 4 times the dissolved solids of incoming makeup water. Higher cycles save water but increase scaling and corrosion potential.

**What it measures:** Water conservation achieved by operating at higher concentration cycles

Formula:
```
Water Savings (%) = [(Cycles - 1) / Cycles] × 100
```

**Example:** Operating at 5 cycles instead of 3 cycles saves 13% makeup water — for a 1,000 GPM system, this reduces makeup from 83 GPM to 72 GPM.

## Heat Exchangers and Heat Transfer Equipment

**Shell-and-tube heat exchangers** put one fluid inside tubes while the other flows around them in a shell. **Plate-and-frame heat exchangers** use corrugated plates with gaskets creating alternating flow channels. **Brazed plate heat exchangers** offer compact design for smaller applications.

**Countercurrent flow** (fluids moving opposite directions) provides the most efficient heat transfer. **Fouling factors** account for deposit buildup that reduces performance over time. Typical fouling factors range from 0.0001 hr-ft²-°F/BTU for clean water to 0.005 for dirty cooling water.

**Approach temperature** describes how closely the outlet temperature of one stream approaches the inlet temperature of the other. Closer approaches require larger heat exchangers but improve efficiency.

## Biological Control and Biocides

**Biofilm** forms when microorganisms attach to wet surfaces and create protective slime layers. Once established, biofilm protects bacteria from disinfectants and can harbor **Legionella** bacteria that cause serious respiratory illness.

**Oxidizing biocides** like **chlorine** and **bromine** kill organisms through strong oxidation. **Free chlorine** works best at pH 6.5-7.5, where most exists as **hypochlorous acid** (HOCl). At pH 8.5, effectiveness drops 80% because chlorine shifts to less active **hypochlorite** ion.

**Non-oxidizing biocides** like **glutaraldehyde** and **isothiazolines** kill through specific toxic mechanisms. These work at higher pH and don't react with organics like oxidizers do.

**Shock feeding** delivers high biocide doses for 2-6 hours to quickly reduce biological activity. **Continuous feeding** maintains lower residual levels for ongoing control.

## Scale Inhibition and Deposit Control

**Scale inhibitors** prevent mineral precipitation through **threshold inhibition** — small amounts (2-10 mg/L) delay crystal formation. **Phosphonates** like **HEDP** and **ATMP** are common choices for calcium carbonate and calcium sulfate control.

**Dispersants** keep particles suspended so they leave the system rather than depositing. **Polyacrylates** and **polymaleic acid** serve this function in many cooling systems.

**What it measures:** Calcium carbonate scaling potential based on pH, alkalinity, calcium, and temperature

Formula:
```
Langelier Saturation Index = pH - pHs
```

**Example:** LSI = +0.5 indicates moderate scaling tendency; LSI = -0.5 indicates moderate corrosion tendency. Most cooling systems target LSI between -0.2 and +0.2.

## Corrosion Control

**General corrosion** removes metal uniformly, while **pitting corrosion** creates deep localized holes. **Galvanic corrosion** accelerates when dissimilar metals connect electrically in water.

**Oxygen scavengers** like **sodium sulfite** remove dissolved oxygen that drives corrosion. **Filming inhibitors** create protective barriers on metal surfaces. **Azoles** like **tolyltriazole** specifically protect copper and brass.

Typical corrosion rates range from 0.1 mils per year (excellent) to over 5 mils per year (severe). Most cooling systems target rates below 2 mils per year for steel and 0.2 mils per year for copper.

## Legionella Prevention

**Legionella** bacteria grow best at 77-108°F in stagnant water with biofilm and sediment. **Legionnaires' disease** occurs when people inhale contaminated **aerosols** from cooling towers, hot tubs, or building water systems.

**Water management plans** identify **control points** where temperature, disinfectant residual, and flow can be monitored. **Hot water return loops** maintain circulation to prevent stagnation. **Mixing valves** blend hot and cold water to safe delivery temperatures while keeping storage tanks hot enough to control Legionella.

## Key Takeaways

Implement monthly testing of cooling tower water for Legionella bacteria, maintain free chlorine residuals above 0.5 mg/L in building hot water systems, and target cooling tower cycles of concentration between 3-6 to balance water conservation with deposit control. Consider upgrading to automated chemical feed systems when manual dosing cannot maintain consistent residuals within ±20% of target levels.
