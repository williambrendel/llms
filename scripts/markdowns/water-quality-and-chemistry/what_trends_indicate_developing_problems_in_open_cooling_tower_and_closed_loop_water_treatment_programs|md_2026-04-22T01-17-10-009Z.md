# What trends indicate developing problems in open cooling tower and closed loop water systems?

## Executive Summary

Open cooling towers show developing problems through unstable oxidizer residuals, rising biological counts, and increasing cycles with higher pH. Closed loop systems reveal issues primarily through increased makeup water demand, rising total iron levels, and declining inhibitor residuals even when conductivity appears stable.

## Overview

A cooling tower that required biocide dosing once weekly might suddenly need it twice weekly to maintain the same residual levels. This shift signals that biofilms have gained a foothold, consuming more oxidizer and creating protected niches for bacteria. The same pattern of increasing chemical demand applies across all water treatment programs—what worked yesterday requires more effort today when problems develop.

## Biological Control Breakdown in Open Cooling Towers

Weakening biological control announces itself through unstable oxidizer residuals and rising organism counts. Oxidizer residual drops to near-zero more frequently, with larger day-to-day swings, especially at remote points rather than just at the basin. BART, HPC, and dip slide trends climb or show faster "rebound" after dosing, even when occasional spot results appear acceptable.

Increasing biocide demand becomes evident when the same feed rate produces lower measured residuals, or when residuals decay faster due to biofilm, organics, and sediment consumption. Operational symptoms follow: sliming, musty odors, nozzle plugging, fill fouling, and rising filter pressure drops.

## Scale and Deposition Risk Escalation

Cycles creep upward as conductivity rises above target levels, while chloride ratios indicate higher true concentration cycles. pH drifts higher—common in towers—while alkalinity and calcium remain elevated, increasing calcium carbonate scale tendency.

Turbidity and total suspended solids rise and stay elevated, creating more nucleation sites and "stickier" deposits. Chiller and condenser approach temperatures increase at similar loads, providing an early fouling signal as deposits reduce heat transfer efficiency.

## Accelerating Corrosion Patterns

Total iron trends upward, especially when paired with turbidity increases. Coupon corrosion rates measured in mils per year (mpy) or pit depth increase, with pitting depth often more critical than general corrosion rates for assessing risk.

Chemistry becomes more aggressive over time as chloride, sulfate, and conductivity trend upward, or pH trends downward. Under-deposit corrosion manifests through tubercles, rust staining, and localized attack in low-flow areas and basin zones.

## System Cleanliness Deterioration

Basin sludge accumulation rates increase, requiring more frequent strainer cleaning. Side-stream filter pressure drops rise more quickly than previously observed. The CDC emphasizes sediment and biofilm control plus cleaning as essential components of effective Legionella control programs.

## Closed Loop System Warning Signs

Closed loops should maintain chemical stability. Developing problems first appear as makeup water ingress, oxygen intrusion, corrosion byproducts, and deposits. The loop is no longer truly "closed."

**What it measures:** Makeup water demand indicates system integrity loss and oxygen ingress rates.

Formula:
```
Makeup Rate (% system volume/month) = (Monthly Makeup Volume / Total System Volume) × 100
```

**Example:** A 10,000-gallon chilled water system requiring 500 gallons monthly makeup operates at 5% monthly replacement rate, suggesting significant leakage requiring investigation.

Makeup water trends provide the highest-yield early warning indicator. Conductivity slowly increases from repeated makeup and inhibitor salt concentration, but inhibitor residuals don't track proportionally. NIH research confirms that oxygen ingress, makeup water, and leaks drive corrosion, while high turbidity indicates active corrosion processes.

## Corrosion and Deposit Transport

Total iron levels rise, often providing the earliest laboratory indicator of developing problems. Turbidity and cloudiness increase as black magnetic sludge (magnetite) appears in strainers. Strainer cleaning frequency increases, coil and heat exchanger pressure drops rise, and control valves begin sticking.

## Microbiology and MIC Development

BART, total plate counts, and heterotrophic plate counts trend upward after remaining stable. Anaerobe indicators like iron-reducing bacteria (IRB) and sulfate-reducing bacteria (SRB) screens turn positive, or reaction times shorten during diagnostic testing.

"Clean bulk water" paired with increasing sludge and black deposits suggests biofilm niches rather than planktonic organism problems. These protected environments foster microbiologically influenced corrosion (MIC) under deposits.

## Chemical Control Drift

Nitrite and molybdate residuals drift downward or become unstable even when conductivity appears stable—common when systems rely on indirect conductivity control rather than direct inhibitor measurement. pH drifts outside the treatment program's intended range, and corrosion coupons show rising mpy rates or pit depths.

## Early Warning Dashboard

**Open Cooling Water Systems:**
- Residual/ORP at basin plus remote monitoring points
- Conductivity plus monthly chloride testing to validate cycles
- pH, alkalinity, calcium hardness, turbidity/TSS, and total iron
- Approach temperature trends, filter pressure drops, and basin inspection scores

**Closed Loop Systems:**
- Makeup volume, dissolved oxygen (when feasible), pH, and conductivity
- True inhibitor residual concentrations, total iron, and turbidity
- Strainer solids accumulation trends and coupon/pit depth measurements

## Key Takeaways

Monitor makeup water volumes in closed loops weekly and oxidizer residuals at remote tower locations daily. Investigate when makeup exceeds 2% of system volume monthly, or when oxidizer residuals swing more than 0.5 mg/L daily. Consider switching from conductivity-based to direct inhibitor monitoring when closed loop conductivity rises above 1,500 µS/cm without proportional inhibitor increases.
