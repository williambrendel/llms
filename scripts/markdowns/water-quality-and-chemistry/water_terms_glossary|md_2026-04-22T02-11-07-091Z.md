# What Are the Most Important Water Treatment Terms for Building Operators?

## Executive Summary

Building operators need to understand roughly 500 water treatment terms to manage cooling towers, boilers, and domestic water systems safely and effectively. These terms span basic water chemistry, equipment operation, biological control, and regulatory compliance — with Legionella prevention now driving many facility management decisions.

## Overview

A single cooling tower malfunction can shut down an entire building's air conditioning. A boiler chemistry upset can destroy expensive heat exchangers. A Legionella outbreak can trigger lawsuits and regulatory action. Yet most building operators learn water treatment vocabulary on the fly, often after problems emerge. This comprehensive glossary covers the 500 terms that separate confident system management from reactive crisis response.

## Water Treatment Fundamentals

Water treatment starts with understanding what you're working with. **Raw water** enters your facility carrying dissolved minerals, suspended particles, and microorganisms. Your job is converting it into **potable water** for occupants, **process water** for manufacturing, or **utility water** for cooling and heating systems.

The chemistry matters immediately. **Total dissolved solids** tells you how much mineral content will concentrate in recirculating systems. **Hardness** from calcium and magnesium determines scaling potential. **pH** affects corrosion rates and chemical effectiveness. **Alkalinity** buffers pH changes but can contribute to scale formation.

**Turbidity** measures suspended particles that can foul equipment and harbor microorganisms. **Conductivity** provides a quick estimate of dissolved ion content. These basic measurements guide every treatment decision.

## Cooling Systems and Heat Rejection

Cooling towers dominate water use in most commercial buildings. These systems work by evaporating roughly 1% of recirculating water to reject heat. As water evaporates, dissolved minerals concentrate in the remaining water, creating scaling and corrosion challenges.

**Cycles of concentration** measures how much minerals have built up compared to fresh makeup water. Most towers run 3-6 cycles before **bleed** or **blowdown** removes concentrated water. **Makeup water** replaces losses from evaporation, drift, and bleed.

**Fill** provides contact area between water and air. **Film fill** spreads water in thin sheets for maximum efficiency. **Splash fill** breaks water into droplets. Both can foul with scale, slime, or debris, reducing heat transfer.

**Drift eliminators** remove water droplets from leaving air. Excessive **drift** wastes water and can spread Legionella bacteria to surrounding areas.

## Biological Control and Legionella Prevention

Legionella bacteria thrive in warm, stagnant water with biofilm protection. Building water systems create ideal conditions unless actively managed.

**Biofilm** forms when bacteria attach to surfaces and produce protective slime. Standard disinfectants struggle to penetrate established biofilms, making prevention crucial.

**Biocides** fall into two categories. **Oxidizing biocides** like chlorine, bromine, and chlorine dioxide kill by destroying cell walls and proteins. **Non-oxidizing biocides** like glutaraldehyde and isothiazolins target specific cellular processes.

**Free chlorine** provides the most cost-effective disinfection in most systems. **Hypochlorous acid** is the active killing species. Effectiveness drops rapidly as pH rises above 7.5.

**Shock feeding** delivers high biocide doses to knock down established growth. **Continuous feeding** maintains residual protection. Most systems need both approaches.

## Scale and Corrosion Control

Mineral scale reduces heat transfer and restricts flow. **Calcium carbonate scale** forms when pH, temperature, or concentration increases. **Calcium sulfate scale** forms when concentration exceeds solubility limits.

**Scale inhibitors** prevent crystal formation and growth at very low doses through **threshold inhibition**. **Phosphonates** like HEDP and ATMP are workhorses for hardness control. **Polymers** like polyacrylates disperse particles and modify crystal structure.

Corrosion destroys expensive equipment and creates leak points. **Oxygen scavengers** like sodium sulfite remove dissolved oxygen that drives corrosion. **Filming inhibitors** create protective barriers on metal surfaces. **Azoles** like tolyltriazole specifically protect copper alloys.

**Galvanic corrosion** accelerates when dissimilar metals connect electrically in conductive water. **Pitting corrosion** creates deep holes that can quickly penetrate metal. **Under-deposit corrosion** develops aggressive local chemistry beneath scale or debris.

## Membrane Systems and Advanced Treatment

**Reverse osmosis** removes dissolved salts by forcing water through semi-permeable membranes under high pressure. **Antiscalants** prevent mineral precipitation that would foul membrane surfaces.

**Microfiltration** and **ultrafiltration** remove particles and many microorganisms. **Nanofiltration** falls between ultrafiltration and reverse osmosis, removing some dissolved salts while passing others.

**Ion exchange** replaces unwanted ions with more acceptable ones. **Water softening** exchanges calcium and magnesium for sodium. **Deionization** removes all dissolved ions for high-purity applications.

## Heat Transfer Equipment

**Shell-and-tube heat exchangers** put one fluid inside tubes while the other flows around them. **Plate heat exchangers** use corrugated plates to create compact, efficient heat transfer.

**Fouling factors** account for expected deposit buildup that reduces performance. **Approach temperature** measures how closely the exchanger reaches theoretical limits.

**Tube fouling** from scale, biological growth, or corrosion products reduces heat transfer and increases pressure drop. Regular cleaning maintains performance and prevents permanent damage.

## Evaporative Cooling and Humidification

**Swamp coolers** or **evaporative coolers** work best in dry climates where water can readily evaporate into air. **Cooling pads** provide surface area for evaporation. **Rigid media pads** offer better performance than traditional **aspen pads**.

**Humidifiers** add moisture to indoor air. **Steam humidifiers** boil water or inject steam. **Adiabatic humidifiers** evaporate water without external heat. **Electrode steam humidifiers** use water conductivity to generate steam.

**Scale formation** clogs humidifier surfaces. **Blowdown** controls mineral concentration in reservoir water.

## Key Takeaways

Monitor conductivity weekly in cooling towers to track cycles of concentration and bleed effectiveness. Maintain 0.5-1.0 ppm free chlorine residual continuously, with 5-10 ppm shock doses monthly. Replace evaporative cooler pads annually and flush humidifier reservoirs monthly during operating season.

Understanding these 500 terms transforms water treatment from mysterious chemistry into manageable facility maintenance. The vocabulary investment pays off in equipment longevity, energy efficiency, and regulatory compliance.
