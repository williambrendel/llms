# Why Does My Cooling Tower Keep Corroding Despite Chemical Treatment?

## Executive Summary

Cooling tower corrosion persists when the chloride-to-sulfate mass ratio (CSMR) exceeds critical thresholds—above 1.0 for stainless steel and above 1.5 for mild steel—because chloride ions overpower chemical inhibitors and destroy protective metal films.

## Overview

A cooling tower with perfect water chemistry can still experience catastrophic corrosion failure within months. Systems running at CSMR levels above 2.5 face total chemical treatment failure, where even advanced filming amines cannot bridge the electrochemical potential driving galvanic attack at joints and welds.

## Understanding Chloride-to-Sulfate Mass Ratio

The Chloride-to-Sulfate Mass Ratio measures the electrochemical balance between pitting-aggressive chloride ions and the less aggressive sulfate ions in cooling water. This ratio predicts galvanic corrosion potential, particularly in systems with lead-bearing solder, copper alloys, or mixed metallurgy.

**What it measures:** The competitive balance between aggressive chloride ions and protective sulfate ions on metal surfaces

Formula:
```
CSMR = Chloride Concentration (mg/L) ÷ Sulfate Concentration (mg/L)
```

**Example:** Water with 150 mg/L chloride and 300 mg/L sulfate yields CSMR = 0.5, indicating effective chemical treatment potential.

## Technical Failure Mechanisms

### Mild Steel Failure at CSMR > 1.5

When chlorides significantly outweigh sulfates, the protective sulfate-scale becomes porous and non-protective. This triggers rapid galvanic-like attack on mild steel, especially at threaded joints. Chemical inhibitors cannot stabilize a protective film when CSMR exceeds 1.5, leading to out-of-control general corrosion.

### Stainless Steel Failure at CSMR > 1.0

For Type 304 stainless steel, CSMR above 1.0 means aggressive chloride ions win the competition for the metal surface against less aggressive ions. This breaks down the passive layer rapidly. No bulk-water chemical treatment can reliably prevent pitting on 304/316 stainless steel once chloride concentration dominates the ratio.

### Galvanic Acceleration

AWWA guidelines identify high CSMR as the primary driver for de-alloying and galvanic corrosion between steel and copper components. When the ratio exceeds 2.0, the electrochemical potential difference becomes too great for standard molecular inhibitors like Azoles to suppress.

## Operational Strategy

Water treatment experts recommend [increasing sulfate levels](https://www.veoliawatertechnologies.com/en/services/chemicals-cooling-water-treatment) via sulfuric acid for pH control when CSMR exceeds 1.0. Alternative approaches include high-performance dispersants to prevent under-deposit environments where local CSMR could spike to extreme levels.

## Key Takeaway

Monitor CSMR weekly in cooling systems with mixed metallurgy. Maintain ratios below 1.0 by adding sulfuric acid to increase sulfate levels, or switch to high-performance filming amines when ratios approach the 1.5 failure threshold for your specific metal components.
