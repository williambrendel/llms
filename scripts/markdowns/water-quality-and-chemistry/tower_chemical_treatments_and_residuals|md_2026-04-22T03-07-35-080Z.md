# What are the typical dosages for common water treatment compounds in cooling towers?

## Executive Summary

Common cooling tower corrosion inhibitors range from 1-2 ppm for copper-protecting azoles to 5-25 ppm for phosphate programs, with zinc systems targeting around 0.5 ppm insoluble zinc and molybdate programs running 3-6 ppm.

## Overview

A cooling tower running at just 0.5 ppm zinc difference can mean the gap between protected carbon steel and costly pitting corrosion. Water treatment professionals face dozens of chemical options, each with specific dosage windows that balance protection against waste and environmental limits. The challenge intensifies because these dosages represent active ingredients in circulating water, not the concentrated products being fed.

## Copper Protection Chemistry

Azole compounds protect copper and copper alloys through two main formulations. Traditional azoles like tolyltriazole (TTA) and benzotriazole (BZT) operate effectively at 1-2 ppm TTA or around 1 ppm BZT in recirculating water. These compounds form protective films on copper surfaces, preventing corrosion in heat exchangers and piping.

Halogen-resistant azoles (HRA) serve the same copper protection role but maintain effectiveness under chlorination or bromination programs. These proprietary chemistries typically require 1-3 ppm in circulation. Standard azoles break down when exposed to oxidizing halogens, creating higher chemical demand and reduced protection. HRA formulations resist this degradation.

Both azole types can include prefilming procedures for copper alloys, though this occurs as part of broader pretreatment rather than ongoing dosage management.

## Carbon Steel Protection Programs

Zinc-based programs target approximately 0.5 ppm insoluble zinc in recirculating water for carbon steel protection. This measurement reflects zinc that has precipitated onto metal surfaces rather than dissolved zinc ions. Zinc solubility decreases as pH rises, and many facilities face discharge limits that constrain zinc use.

**What it measures:** The amount of zinc required to establish protective films on carbon steel during system startup

Formula:
```
Prefilming: 30-60 ppm zinc + 300-600 ppm phosphate for 4-6 hours
```

**Example:** A 1000-ton cooling tower might require 150 gallons of 10% zinc solution plus 900 gallons of phosphoric acid solution during a 6-hour prefilming procedure before normal operation begins.

Molybdate programs protect carbon steel through different chemistry, typically requiring 3-5 ppm molybdate when used with zinc, or 3-6 ppm in stabilized phosphate programs. Molybdate reduces pitting corrosion but costs more than traditional inhibitors. Many programs blend molybdate with other chemicals to balance performance and economics.

## Phosphate-Based Systems

Stabilized phosphate programs maintain 3-6 ppm orthophosphate as a control residual, though some formulations run 5-25 ppm total phosphate depending on system requirements. The stabilization prevents calcium phosphate scaling while maintaining corrosion protection on ferrous metals.

Phosphate prefilming uses much higher concentrations than normal operation. Systems require 300-600 ppm phosphate combined with 30-60 ppm zinc for 4-6 hours to establish protective films. This high-concentration treatment occurs only during startup or after system cleaning.

Higher phosphate levels increase calcium phosphate scaling risk depending on calcium content, pH, and dispersant effectiveness. Each system requires individual evaluation of these factors.

## Supporting Chemistries

Phosphonates like AMP and HEDP typically dose at 3-5 ppm in zinc programs, providing both corrosion inhibition and scale control. These compounds chelate metal ions and disperse deposits while supporting multi-metal protection programs.

Dispersant polymers operate at 4-6 ppm in typical zinc programs, controlling deposits that cause under-deposit corrosion. These polymers work indirectly by preventing the conditions that accelerate localized attack on metal surfaces.

## Key Takeaways

Monitor corrosion inhibitor residuals weekly using appropriate test methods for each chemistry type, maintain zinc programs below 0.5 ppm insoluble zinc to balance protection with discharge compliance, and consider halogen-resistant azoles when chlorine or bromine demand exceeds 2-3 ppm daily in systems with significant copper alloy components.
