# How effective and cost-competitive is CO₂ for pH control in cooling towers?

## Executive Summary

CO₂ provides effective alkalinity control in cooling towers and often proves cost-competitive with sulfuric acid, especially when you factor in safety, corrosion prevention, and operational costs. While sulfuric acid costs 50-66% less per ton, CO₂'s gentler chemistry and non-corrosive properties can offset the higher chemical costs through reduced maintenance and safer operations.

## Overview

CO₂ dissolves in water to form carbonic acid, creating a mild buffering system that prevents the sharp pH drops and localized corrosion risks that plague sulfuric acid systems. At facilities running high cycles with alkalinity levels around 780 ppm, CO₂ can reduce scaling risk while eliminating acid burns and hazmat handling requirements entirely.

## How CO₂ Works in Cooling Towers

CO₂ dissolves in water to form carbonic acid through this reaction:

**What it measures:** The chemical pathway that creates mild acidity from CO₂ gas

Formula:
```
CO₂ + H₂O → H₂CO₃ → H⁺ + HCO₃⁻
```

**Example:** When CO₂ gas contacts cooling water, it forms carbonic acid that gently lowers pH from 8.5 to 8.2 while consuming alkalinity

This mild acid lowers pH and consumes alkalinity similar to sulfuric acid, but with softer chemistry. You're rebalancing the carbonate system rather than adding strong mineral acids.

## Effectiveness Compared to Sulfuric Acid

| **Feature** | **CO₂** | **Sulfuric Acid** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cost | Higher | Lower |
| Corrosion risk | Low | High (can cause localized corrosion) |
| Safety | Safer (non-hazardous gas) | Hazardous, requires PPE and containment |
| pH control | Smooth, buffered | Easy to overshoot, sharp pH drops |
| Equipment | Needs gas handling, injectors | Needs acid tanks, pumps, safety systems |
| Environmental | No acid discharges | May require permits and spill containment |

CO₂ lowers pH effectively to target ranges of 8.2–8.5 and reduces M-alkalinity over time by shifting carbonate equilibrium. The non-corrosive feed eliminates localized corrosion and acid burns while providing self-buffering that makes pH overshoot nearly impossible.

CO₂ works especially well where alkalinity and calcium run high, and you need to reduce scaling risk without over-acidifying sensitive system components.

## Cost Effectiveness Analysis

| Chemical | Cost (delivered) | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CO₂ (gas) | ~$100–150/ton | Varies by volume, storage setup |
| Sulfuric Acid (93%) | ~$150–200/ton | Cheaper per ton but more dangerous and corrosive |

Sulfuric acid costs 50-66% less than CO₂ on a pure chemical basis. However, CO₂ becomes competitive where safety concerns dominate, in sensitive systems with stainless or aluminum components, or when operational downtime and acid handling costs add up.

CO₂ requires more pounds per pound of alkalinity neutralized due to its lower acid strength, but savings in safety, maintenance, and corrosion protection often offset the higher volume requirements.

## System Requirements and Efficiency

CO₂ systems need gas storage tanks, flow control and injection equipment, and pH automation integration. Initial capital costs run higher than sulfuric acid systems, but long-term operational and maintenance costs often prove better.

**What it measures:** Chemical demand for alkalinity reduction

Formula:
```
CO₂: 1 ppm alkalinity (CaCO₃) ≈ 0.88 ppm CO₂
H₂SO₄: 1 ppm alkalinity (CaCO₃) ≈ 0.49 ppm 92% H₂SO₄
```

**Example:** To reduce 280 ppm alkalinity in a 1000-ton cooling tower, you need roughly 246 ppm CO₂ versus 137 ppm sulfuric acid

## When CO₂ Delivers Maximum Value

CO₂ proves most cost-effective at high-cycle operations where pH and alkalinity climb significantly, requiring substantial acid demand. Sites with strict safety regulations, acid-handling restrictions, or systems prone to acid overdosing see the biggest benefits.

Facilities with onsite CO₂ generation—breweries, food plants, or chemical manufacturers—often find CO₂ the clear economic winner due to reduced transportation and storage costs.

## PHX 15 Makeup Water Example

| Parameter | Value |
| --- | --- |
| pH | 7.69 |
| M-Alkalinity | 156 ppm as CaCO₃ |
| Target Cycles | 5 |
| Target Tower pH | ~8.4–8.5 (natural drift) |
| Target pH w/ CO₂ | ~8.2–8.3 |

At 5× concentration, this makeup water creates tower alkalinity around 780 ppm. CO₂ injection can reduce this to 500 ppm while maintaining pH at 8.2–8.3, preventing carbonate scaling without the corrosion risks of strong acid addition.

## Key Takeaways

Monitor alkalinity levels weekly and target reductions to 500 ppm or below to prevent scaling. CO₂ becomes cost-competitive when total system costs—including safety equipment, maintenance, and downtime—exceed the 50-66% chemical cost premium. Consider CO₂ injection systems for any cooling tower operating above 4 cycles with makeup alkalinity exceeding 150 ppm as CaCO₃.
