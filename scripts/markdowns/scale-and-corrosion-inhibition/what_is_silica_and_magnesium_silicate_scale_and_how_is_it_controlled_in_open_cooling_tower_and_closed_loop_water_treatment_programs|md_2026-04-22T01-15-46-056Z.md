# What is silica scale and magnesium scale and how is it controlled in open cooling tower and closed loop water systems?

## Executive Summary

Silica scale forms hard, glassy deposits that are extremely difficult to remove, while magnesium scale typically means magnesium silicate deposits created when magnesium hardness combines with silica under high pH and temperature conditions. Control requires managing concentration cycles, pH control, specialized dispersants for open towers, and eliminating chronic makeup for closed loops.

## Overview

A single silica scaling event can shut down a heat exchanger for days because these deposits resist conventional cleaning methods. Open cooling towers concentrate silica through evaporation until it polymerizes into glass-like formations, while closed loops that should never scale often suffer recurring deposits due to hidden makeup sources importing fresh minerals.

## Understanding Silica and Magnesium Scale Formation

Silica scale creates a hard, glassy deposit when silica (SiO₂) precipitates or polymerizes and deposits on heat-transfer surfaces. These silica-related deposits include polymerized silica and metal silicates such as magnesium silicate, calcium silicate, and iron/aluminum silicates. The deposits prove problematic because they resist removal and severely reduce heat transfer efficiency.

Magnesium scale in cooling-water practice most commonly means magnesium silicate scale, which represents a specific form of silica scale. This scale forms when magnesium hardness combines with silica under conditions that favor silicate precipitation, particularly at high pH and high temperature or skin temperature conditions.

## What Drives Silica and Magnesium-Silicate Scaling

Several factors drive scaling in both open towers and closed loops. High silica content in makeup or fill water provides the raw material for scale formation. High pH and high surface temperatures accelerate silica polymerization and metal-silicate precipitation at heat transfer surfaces. High dissolved metals (iron, aluminum) and suspended solids provide reactants and nucleation sites for metal-silicate scaling and deposition.

## Control Strategies for Open Cooling Towers

### Control Concentration First

Open towers concentrate dissolved solids through evaporation, making silica the limiting factor at higher cycles. The most direct scale control approach involves operating at subsaturated conditions, which often requires controlling cycles and pH. Set a silica operating ceiling based on site-specific chemistry, then manage it through blowdown and reliable conductivity control validated periodically with laboratory silica testing.

### Maintain pH Control

Silica and metal-silicate precipitation risk increases with higher pH levels. Maintaining precise pH control serves as a core lever for scale control in cooling systems. Tighten the pH control band and avoid high-pH excursions, especially near chemical injection points.

### Apply Silica-Focused Dispersants

When silica becomes the limiting factor, field and pilot work demonstrate that certain silica dispersants and antiscalants can increase the practical operating window. These products allow higher silica concentrations before fouling occurs, enabling higher cycles without silica deposition when properly applied. Use products specifically validated for silica and magnesium-silicate control, dosing based on silica level, cycles, and heat flux rather than conductivity alone.

### Reduce Nucleation Sites

Deposits form oxygen differential cells and accelerate corrosion. Keeping systems clean reduces locations where scale can form and adhere. Improve side-stream filtration, basin cleanliness, and iron control to reduce seeding opportunities.

### Consider Makeup Treatment

When silica levels prove too high to manage through chemistry and blowdown, consider makeup treatment through reverse osmosis, blending, or alternative water sources. This approach often provides the only durable solution when raw-water silica is high and water conservation requires high cycles.

## Control Strategies for Closed-Loop Systems

Closed loops normally should not experience recurring mineral scaling unless the loop is not truly closed or the fill water creates problems.

### Eliminate Chronic Makeup

Recurring makeup continuously imports silica, hardness, and metals while creating deposition conditions. The principle of eliminating oxygen ingress is widely accepted, but the key lies in quantifying actual makeup rates to address root causes.

### Verify Scale Identity

Closed loops often suffer from iron corrosion-product deposition that gets mistaken for scale. All deposits reduce heat transfer and promote under-deposit corrosion. Conduct laboratory identification using SEM-EDS or XRD analysis on deposits when they recur.

### Control Local High pH and Metal Availability

Metal-silicate scaling can result from reactions involving metals (iron, aluminum) and silicate. Acidification serves as a preventive measure in contexts with metal-silicate scaling potential. Avoid localized high pH at chemical injection points, control corrosion to prevent iron release, and maintain effective filtration.

## Treatment When Silica Scaling is Confirmed

When makeup cannot be eliminated and silica levels remain high, consider pretreating fill water through reverse osmosis or blending. Alternatively, implement periodic controlled cleaning combined with improved filtration and careful inhibitor and passivation management.

## Essential Monitoring Requirements

Both open and closed systems require monitoring of reactive and total silica when possible, magnesium hardness, pH, conductivity, and alkalinity. Track iron and aluminum levels, especially when silica scale is suspected. Trend approach temperature and heat exchanger pressure drop as early fouling signals. Conduct periodic deposit identification when scaling recurs rather than guessing at the cause.

## Key Takeaways

Monitor silica levels weekly in open towers and monthly in closed loops, maintaining silica below 150 ppm in open systems and pH below 8.5 to prevent polymerization. When conventional chemistry fails to control silica scaling, switch to reverse osmosis makeup treatment or specialized silica dispersants validated for your specific operating conditions.
