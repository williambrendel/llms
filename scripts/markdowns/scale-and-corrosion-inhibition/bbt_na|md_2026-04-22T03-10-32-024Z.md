# What is BBT.Na?

## Executive Summary

Sodium Butyl Benzotriazole (BBT.Na) is a copper corrosion inhibitor that forms a protective molecular film on copper and brass surfaces in cooling tower systems, providing stronger and longer-lasting protection than traditional benzotriazole compounds.

## Overview

BBT.Na stands out from other corrosion inhibitors because it maintains effectiveness even in harsh conditions that would degrade standard benzotriazole. This enhanced stability makes it particularly valuable in high-chloride waters and oxidizing environments where copper corrosion typically accelerates. The compound's sodium salt form also provides superior solubility compared to its predecessors.

## Chemical Properties and Classification

BBT.Na belongs to the benzotriazole family but features a butyl group modification that significantly enhances its performance characteristics. This chemical modification transforms a basic corrosion inhibitor into a more robust solution for challenging water conditions.

The sodium salt form ensures high solubility in water systems, eliminating the precipitation issues that sometimes plague other benzotriazole compounds. This solubility advantage becomes critical in high-TDS cooling tower applications where dissolved solids concentrations can interfere with chemical performance.

## Protection Mechanism in Cooling Systems

BBT.Na protects copper and brass through surface adsorption, creating an invisible molecular barrier between the metal and corrosive water constituents. This film blocks oxygen, chlorides, and other aggressive ions from reaching the metal surface.

The protection extends beyond simple surface coverage. BBT.Na specifically prevents pitting corrosion, which can create pinhole leaks in copper tubing and heat exchanger components. It also reduces galvanic corrosion between copper and steel components in mixed-metal cooling systems.

The compound maintains its protective film even under the oxidizing conditions created by chlorine dioxide, bromine, or other biocides commonly used in cooling tower water treatment.

## Operational Parameters and Dosing

| Parameter | Value / Range |
| --- | --- |
| Target dosage | 1–5 ppm active BBT |
| pH stability | Effective between 6.5–9.5 |
| Metal protection | Primarily copper/brass |
| Solubility | High (due to sodium salt form) |

BBT.Na demonstrates superior stability across alkaline pH ranges compared to traditional benzotriazole. This stability proves essential in cooling towers operating at higher pH levels for scale control or environmental compliance.

The dosing range allows for flexibility based on system metallurgy and water quality. Systems with extensive copper components or aggressive water conditions typically require dosing toward the higher end of the range.

## Chemical Compatibility and Synergistic Effects

BBT.Na works synergistically with other water treatment chemicals rather than interfering with their performance. Common combinations include phosphonates like PBTC for steel corrosion protection, polyacrylates for dispersion, and zinc or molybdate compounds for additional corrosion control.

The compound maintains compatibility with oxidizing biocides, though sustained high biocide concentrations can gradually degrade the protective film. This compatibility allows for effective biological control without sacrificing corrosion protection.

## Advantages Over Traditional Inhibitors

BBT.Na outperforms regular benzotriazole (BZT) and tolyltriazole (TTA) in several key areas. It forms stronger, more durable films that resist degradation from chloride attack and oxidizing conditions. This enhanced durability translates to more consistent copper protection and reduced inhibitor consumption.

The compound proves particularly effective in reclaimed water applications and high-chloride makeup water scenarios where traditional benzotriazoles often fail. These challenging water sources are becoming more common as facilities seek to reduce freshwater consumption.

## Key Takeaway

Monitor copper levels in cooling tower blowdown weekly when using BBT.Na, maintaining dosage between 2-4 ppm for optimal protection. If copper levels exceed 1.0 ppm in the circulating water, increase BBT.Na dosage or consider switching to a more robust corrosion inhibitor package that includes molybdate or zinc for enhanced protection.
