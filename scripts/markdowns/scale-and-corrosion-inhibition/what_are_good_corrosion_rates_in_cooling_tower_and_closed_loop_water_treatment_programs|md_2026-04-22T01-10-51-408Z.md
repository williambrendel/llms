# What are acceptable corrosion rates for Mild Steel, Copper, and SS in Open cooling tower and closed loop systems?

## Executive Summary

Excellent corrosion rates target less than 1.0 mpy for mild steel in open cooling towers and less than 0.2 mpy in closed loops, while copper and stainless steel should stay below 0.1 mpy in both systems.

## Overview

A cooling tower that corrodes mild steel at 2 mpy will eat through a quarter-inch pipe wall in just 125 years, but the same rate in a closed loop system signals poor water treatment. Closed systems demand five times stricter corrosion control than open towers because they lack the natural flushing action that carries away corrosion byproducts.

## Open Recirculating Systems (Cooling Towers)

Open cooling towers expose metal surfaces to constant air contact, concentrate dissolved solids through evaporation, and introduce contaminants from the atmosphere. These harsh conditions make some corrosion inevitable, but proper water treatment keeps rates within acceptable bounds.

**Mild steel (carbon steel)** shows the widest tolerance range in open systems. Rates below 1.0 mpy indicate excellent corrosion control, while 1.0–3.0 mpy represents good performance. Fair control allows 3.0–5.0 mpy, but anything above 5.0 mpy signals poor water treatment that will cause premature equipment failure.

**Copper and copper alloys** demand much tighter control. Excellent performance requires rates below 0.1 mpy, with good control maintaining 0.1–0.3 mpy. Fair control extends to 0.5 mpy, but rates above this threshold indicate poor water chemistry management.

**Stainless steel** offers superior corrosion resistance but still requires monitoring. Excellent performance stays below 0.1 mpy, while anything above this rate indicates poor conditions that could lead to localized pitting despite the material's general corrosion resistance.

## Closed-Loop Systems

Closed cooling, chilled water, and hydronic systems operate in controlled environments without atmospheric exposure or evaporative concentration. This protection allows much stricter corrosion rate targets.

**Mild steel** in closed systems should achieve excellent performance below 0.2 mpy—five times better than open tower requirements. Good control maintains 0.2–0.5 mpy, fair control allows 0.5–1.0 mpy, and anything above 1.0 mpy indicates poor water treatment.

**Copper and copper alloys** follow similar patterns to open systems but with tighter tolerances. Excellent performance stays below 0.1 mpy, good control maintains 0.1–0.2 mpy, and fair control extends to 0.3 mpy. Rates above 0.3 mpy signal inadequate corrosion inhibition.

**Stainless steel** maintains the same 0.1 mpy threshold for excellent performance, with rates above this level indicating potential problems despite the material's inherent corrosion resistance.

## Measurement and Limitations

These targets assume general uniform corrosion measured by weight-loss coupons over extended exposure periods. The standard unit of mils per year (mpy) equals 0.001 inches per year or 0.0254 millimeters per year, providing a direct measure of metal thickness loss.

**What it measures:** The rate at which metal thickness decreases due to uniform corrosion across exposed surfaces

Formula:
```
1 mpy = 0.001 in/year = 0.0254 mm/year
```

**Example:** A mild steel heat exchanger tube with 0.065-inch wall thickness experiencing 2.0 mpy corrosion will lose half its wall thickness in 32.5 years, requiring replacement before structural failure.

Weight-loss coupon testing provides reliable data for general corrosion but cannot predict localized pitting, crevice corrosion, or galvanic attack. These localized forms can cause equipment failure even when overall corrosion rates meet excellent targets.

## Key Takeaways

Monitor corrosion rates monthly using weight-loss coupons positioned in high-velocity, high-temperature locations where corrosion typically peaks. Target rates below 1.0 mpy for mild steel in open towers and below 0.2 mpy in closed systems, while keeping copper and stainless steel below 0.1 mpy in both applications. Supplement weight-loss measurements with regular visual inspections and electrochemical monitoring to catch localized corrosion that coupon testing might miss.
