# What are azoles and how do you use them in closed-loop water systems?

## Executive Summary

Azoles are organic compounds that protect copper and brass components in closed-loop systems by forming thin protective films on metal surfaces, with benzotriazole and tolyltriazole being the most common types used at 3-10 ppm concentrations.

## Overview

A single corroded copper heat exchanger can shut down an entire chilled water system, yet most corrosion inhibitors focus only on protecting steel. Azoles fill this critical gap by specifically targeting yellow metals like copper and brass that traditional inhibitors leave vulnerable.

## Understanding Azoles as Corrosion Inhibitors

Azoles are a class of organic compounds that serve as specialized corrosion inhibitors for yellow metals like copper and brass. The most common azoles used in water treatment are:

- Benzotriazole (BZT)
- Tolyltriazole (TTA)
- Mercaptobenzothiazole (MBT) (used less often now due to environmental concerns)

## Why Azoles Matter in Closed-Loop Systems

Closed loops often contain a mix of metals: carbon steel, copper, brass, and sometimes aluminum. While nitrite, molybdate, or phosphate may protect steel effectively, azoles are the go-to solution for protecting copper alloys.

Azoles work through a specific mode of action that addresses the unique challenges of mixed-metal systems:

1. Formation of a thin protective film on the metal surface
2. Preventing galvanic corrosion between dissimilar metals
3. Providing targeted protection in chilled water and hot water loops, especially where copper or brass heat exchangers and components are present

## Dosage Guidelines for Azoles

Different azole types require specific concentration ranges for effective protection:

| Azole Type | Target Concentration (mg/L or ppm) |
| --- | --- |
| Benzotriazole (BZT) | 5–10 ppm (as active ingredient) |
| Tolyltriazole (TTA) | 3–10 ppm (as active) |

For initial passivation or new system start-up, higher doses (up to 25–50 ppm as active) may be used temporarily, then dialed back to maintenance levels.

## Key Takeaways

Monitor azole levels monthly using colorimetric test kits, maintain BZT at 5-10 ppm or TTA at 3-10 ppm for ongoing protection, and consider switching to tolyltriazole if environmental regulations restrict benzotriazole use in your area.
