# How Does Enhanced Polymaleic Acid (EPMA) Prevent Scale in Cooling Water Systems?

## Executive Summary

Enhanced Polymaleic Acid (EPMA) prevents mineral scale formation through lattice distortion, threshold effect, and particle dispersion, requiring only 5-15 ppm dosing in most recirculating cooling water systems. EPMA outperforms standard carboxylate and sulfonate polymers by resisting calcium-polymer gel formation and maintaining effectiveness at higher temperatures and alkalinity levels.

## Overview

A single pound of EPMA can stabilize thousands of pounds of dissolved calcium and magnesium that would otherwise cement onto heat exchanger surfaces as rock-hard scale. Unlike older polymer chemistries that fail when cooling systems push higher cycles of concentration, EPMA actually thrives in the aggressive water conditions that modern facilities demand for water conservation.

## How EPMA Works

EPMA prevents scale through three distinct chemical mechanisms working simultaneously in cooling water systems.

**Lattice Distortion** occurs when EPMA molecules adsorb onto developing scale crystals like calcium carbonate. The active carboxylic acid (-COOH) groups chelate with calcium and magnesium ions at crystal growth points, distorting the normal crystal structure. Instead of forming hard, interlocking calcite scales, the minerals form soft, non-adherent crystals (aragonite or vaterite) that remain suspended in water.

**Threshold Effect** allows EPMA to prevent precipitation of scale-forming minerals even when present far beyond their normal solubility limits. A very small concentration (sub-stoichiometric) of the polymer can stabilize a much larger concentration of scale-forming ions.

**Dispersion** works by EPMA wrapping around small particles and imparting a negative charge. This creates electrostatic repulsion between particles, preventing them from agglomerating into heavy deposits.

## Dosage Requirements

The common dosage for EPMA in recirculating cooling water systems varies by water quality conditions:

**Maintenance Dosing:** 5–15 ppm (mg/L) of active polymer handles typical cooling tower operations.

**High-Stress Systems:** Systems with very high calcium hardness or alkalinity may require 20–50 ppm to maintain system cleanliness.

**Low-Level Maintenance:** Cleaner waters may need only 1–3 ppm to control basic calcium carbonate scale.

## Advantages Over Standard Polymers

EPMA delivers specific performance benefits compared to carboxylate and sulfonate polymers in modern cooling tower programs.

**Higher Tolerance for Calcium and Alkalinity** sets EPMA apart from carboxylate polymers like polyacrylic acid (PAA), which form insoluble calcium-polymer gels in water with high hardness or alkalinity. EPMA resists this gelling, allowing systems to operate at higher cycles of concentration without the polymer itself becoming a foulant.

**Superior Thermal Stability** means EPMA maintains its structural integrity and inhibitory effectiveness at the higher skin temperatures found in heat exchangers, while standard carboxylates can degrade or lose effectiveness as temperatures rise.

**Oxidizing Biocide Stability** gives EPMA an edge in cooling towers that use chlorine or bromine for microbial control. EPMA remains stable in the presence of these oxidizing biocides compared to some sulfonated polymers, which may lose performance when exposed to high halogen levels.

**Synergistic Corrosion Control** occurs when EPMA combines with zinc salts or phosphonates to act as a secondary corrosion inhibitor by helping control the formation of protective films on metal surfaces. Pure carboxylates or sulfonates do not perform this function as effectively.

**Environmental Profile** advantages include EPMA's phosphorus-free and non-toxic formulation, aligning with environmental regulations that increasingly restrict phosphate discharge in cooling tower blowdown.

## Key Takeaway

Monitor EPMA residuals weekly at 5-15 ppm active polymer concentration, with system performance confirmed by maintaining calcium carbonate saturation ratios below 2.5. Consider switching from standard polyacrylic acid to EPMA when operating above 6 cycles of concentration or when heat exchanger skin temperatures exceed 140°F.
