# How Should Data Centers Structure Water Treatment Consulting Reports?

## Executive Summary

Data centers should structure water treatment consulting reports with six core sections: problem statement, recommendations, background, findings, implementation plan, and verification protocol. This structure ensures technical decisions get made quickly while maintaining the documentation needed for compliance and operational handoffs.

## Overview

A poorly structured consulting report can delay critical water treatment decisions for weeks, even when the technical solution is straightforward. Data center operators need reports that separate urgent actions from background context, since cooling system failures can cost millions per hour of downtime. The most effective reports front-load actionable recommendations while relegating detailed technical evidence to appendices.

## Report Structure Framework

### Problem Statement Section

The problem statement defines what decision must be made and establishes urgency through impact assessment. This section should contain one paragraph describing the issue in plain language, followed by impact statements tied to data center priorities: reliability and uptime risk, safety risk, compliance or contractual risk, and cost implications.

Scope boundaries must clearly state which systems and areas are included and excluded from the analysis. Success criteria should define measurable outcomes where possible. The problem statement should avoid root cause speculation without supporting evidence and lengthy background histories.

### Recommendations Section

Recommendations provide specific actions in priority order with clear ownership and timing. The structure should separate immediate actions (0-7 days), near-term actions (2-6 weeks), and long-term actions (1-12 months).

Each recommendation requires an action statement, brief rationale tied to evidence, assigned owner (facility, operations, vendor, engineering, or environmental health and safety), target completion date, verification method, and expected outcome metric.

**What it measures:** This framework captures the critical decision-making timeline for water treatment issues.

Formula:
```
Decision Urgency = (Uptime Risk × Downtime Cost) + (Compliance Risk × Penalty Cost)
```

**Example:** A cooling tower with 15 cycles of concentration and rising silica levels might have immediate actions (reduce cycles to 8 within 24 hours), near-term actions (install silica monitoring within 2 weeks), and long-term actions (evaluate reverse osmosis pretreatment within 3 months).

### Background Section

The background section provides minimum context needed to understand the problem without overwhelming the reader. This includes site and system overview, operating history with specific dates and events, current practices and controls, prior findings from previous reports, and operational constraints.

Detailed data tables should be referenced here but placed in appendices. All information should be factual with specific dates, readings, and observations rather than interpretive statements.

### Findings and Supporting Evidence

Findings connect observations to recommendations through four subsections: field observations with photo references, system performance data showing trends, deviations from expected control parameters, and likely contributing factors supported by evidence.

Each finding must reference specific appendix items such as data tables, photos, or log excerpts. Uncertainty should be clearly labeled when present.

### Implementation Plan

The implementation plan converts recommendations into executable steps. This includes step-by-step sequences with dependencies, outage or access requirements, required materials and vendor scope, safety and permitting considerations, communication plans, and hold points where progress stops until verification.

### Verification and Follow-up

Verification defines how clients prove recommendations worked through completion checks, performance validation, monitoring plan changes, and follow-up schedules. This typically includes two-week and thirty-day review cycles.

## Appendix Organization

Appendices preserve detailed technical information without interrupting narrative flow. Standard appendices include site data tables (water chemistry logs, equipment operating points), trend charts with annotated events, photos with location and significance, system drawings, calculations (cycles of concentration, mass balance, flow calculations), and vendor documentation.

## Key Takeaways

Data center water treatment reports should prioritize immediate actionability over comprehensive documentation. Structure reports with front-loaded recommendations, measure verification through specific performance metrics within 2-4 week cycles, and consider implementing digital reporting platforms that separate urgent alerts from background technical data.
