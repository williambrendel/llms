# How do I compare various vendors and treatment approaches in open cooling tower and closed chilled water systems?

## Executive Summary

Use a standardized scorecard that weighs technical fit (25-35%), control capability (20-30%), proven outcomes (20-30%), service execution (10-20%), and total cost of ownership (10-20%) across identical system specifications and evaluation periods.

## Overview

Most facility managers compare water treatment vendors by chemical price alone, missing 60-80% of the true cost picture. A cooling tower running at 3 cycles instead of 5 cycles wastes $15,000+ annually in water costs for a typical 500-ton system, while closed-loop iron transport from poor oxygen control can destroy a chiller in 18 months.

## Step-by-step comparison process

### Step 1: Standardize your "basis of design"

Create one-page definitions for each system to prevent vendors from proposing to different problem statements.

**Open cooling tower specifications:**
- Tonnage/heat load profile
- Recirculation flow
- Metallurgy
- Makeup water quality (at least conductivity, alkalinity, hardness, chloride, silica)
- Target cycles strategy
- Blowdown control type
- Current issues (scale, corrosion, bio, drift, fouling)

**Closed chilled water specifications:**
- System volume estimate
- Operating temperatures
- Metallurgy
- Treatment makeup history
- Air removal equipment
- Filtration
- Current issues (iron transport, coil plugging, low ΔT, leaks)

### Step 2: Require identical deliverables from each vendor

For both system types, require:
- Treatment program description and setpoints/control limits
- Monitoring plan (frequency, tests, methods, sample points)
- Equipment plan (controllers, sensors, feed pumps, side-stream filtration if proposed) and calibration method
- Response protocols (excursions, high bacteria, contamination, leak events)
- Reporting format (trend charts, KPIs, action tracking)
- Cost proposal broken out by chemical, service, equipment rental/lease, lab, and extras

### Step 3: Score using a weighted rubric

**Technical fit (25-35%):**
- Correct approach for your metallurgy and operating envelope
- Clear control limits (not vague "keep in range")
- Realistic cycles targets for your makeup water (open towers)
- Closed-loop oxygen/leak control strategy (closed loops)

**Control & verification (20-30%):**
- Automated control where it materially reduces risk (tower conductivity + blowdown, biocide control/verification, alarming)
- Defined field test methods and QA/QC
- Instrument calibration and data integrity plan

**Outcomes evidence (20-30%):**
- Corrosion monitoring plan (coupons/probes), target metrics, trend reporting
- Deposit/fouling monitoring method (approach temp, UA trend, inspections)
- Micro program effectiveness (open tower): monitoring + response discipline
- References/case studies in comparable systems (same industry, water, climate)

**Service execution (10-20%):**
- Technician qualifications, coverage, escalation path
- On-time service rate, corrective action closure process
- Documentation quality (sample chain-of-custody, logs)

**Commercials/TCO (10-20%):**
- Transparent pricing and assumptions
- Guarantees and exclusions
- Expected water/energy impacts quantified (with assumptions)

## Open cooling tower comparison criteria

### Scale/corrosion inhibitor philosophy

Compare these common approaches:

**Phosphate + polymer programs:** Scale inhibition/dispersancy; can be effective but may increase phosphate discharge considerations.

**All-organic/polymer programs:** No phosphate; can work but must be proven on your makeup water and cycles.

**High-cycle programs:** Maximize water efficiency; requires careful scale control and often better filtration and monitoring.

Compare vendors on ability to run your target cycles without scaling and their excursion tolerance and recovery plan.

### Microbiological control approach

**Biocide strategy:** Evaluate oxidizing + non-oxidizing rotation vs single-product dependence, including feed verification (flow proof, ORP control only if appropriate, slug vs continuous).

**Monitoring plan and response protocol:** What triggers what action.

**Mechanical support elements:** Compare vendors on whether they assess and optimize blowdown control hardware and sensor placement, side-stream filtration sizing and maintenance plan, and tower condition items tied to bio risk (dead legs, basins, fill condition, drift eliminators).

## Closed chilled water system comparison criteria

### Inhibitor chemistry strategy

Common approaches include nitrite-based, molybdate-based, and other blends (often with azole for copper). Compare on:
- Fit to metallurgy and operating temperature range
- Compatibility with glycol (if present)
- Required testing frequency and sensitivity to makeup

### Oxygen ingress and cleanliness strategy

Closed loops fail mainly from oxygen and debris. Compare vendors on:
- Makeup measurement plan and leak response expectations
- Air removal/expansion tank evaluation
- Filtration strategy (side-stream filtration, magnetic separation where appropriate)
- Commissioning/repair flushing standards and how they control contractor tie-ins

### Verification metrics

Compare vendors on:
- Coupon/probe program design and targets
- Iron/copper trend monitoring, turbidity/particle control
- Evidence-based triggers for cleaning or filtration upgrades

## Total cost of ownership comparison

Build a simple annualized model for each vendor:

**Open tower costs:**
- Water + sewer cost impact from cycles/blowdown
- Chemical + service
- Energy impact (kW/ton drift tied to fouling/cleanliness)
- Maintenance impacts (cleanings, strainer plugging, basin cleanouts)

**Closed loop costs:**
- Chemical + service
- Maintenance impacts (coil cleaning, filter changes, plugged strainers)
- Leak-related costs (makeup water, corrosion, repairs, downtime)

If a vendor claims savings (higher cycles, lower kW/ton), require assumptions (makeup chemistry, load profile, baseline performance) and measurement method and verification plan.

## Red flags when comparing vendors

Watch for these warning signs:
- No defined control limits or no plan for excursions
- No corrosion monitoring (or "we don't need coupons")
- Heavy reliance on one micro tactic in towers without verification
- Promised water/energy savings without a measurement plan
- Pricing that hides "extras" (lab fees, additional visits, cleaning support)

## Key takeaway

Produce a 1-2 page decision summary per system containing scorecard totals and top differentiators, risks and mitigations, a 90-day validation plan (what must improve, what data proves it), and exit criteria (what triggers corrective action or vendor change). Evaluate vendor performance monthly using corrosion coupon data, conductivity trends, and actual water/energy consumption vs. promises.
