# How do lab analyses support treatment programs in open cooling tower and closed chilled water systems?

## Executive Summary

Lab analyses support treatment programs by providing defensible, repeatable measurements that validate program performance, diagnose root causes of problems, and document conditions when field tests prove too variable or non-specific.

## Overview

While field testing gives you day-to-day control, lab analysis becomes your program's insurance policy when stakes get high. A single misdiagnosed deposit can cost tens of thousands in unnecessary chemical changes, and failed Legionella control can shut down entire facilities. Lab testing cuts through the uncertainty that field instruments can't resolve, especially when you're dealing with mixed contamination or need to defend your decisions to regulators.

## Validating Microbiological Control and Legionella Program Performance

Routine Legionella testing over time represents the CDC's identified best method to validate that a water management program controls Legionella effectively. When culture methods are used, ISO 11731 specifies culture methods to isolate and enumerate Legionella in water, and the standard applies to biofilms and sediments beyond just bulk water samples.

Lab results provide defensible documentation to support response decisions and program adjustments, particularly important for cooling towers where regulatory scrutiny runs high. Field biology tools like ATP, dipslides, BART, and rapid antigen screens prove useful for trending, but they cannot reliably replace lab enumeration and speciation when decisions carry high stakes or require defensibility.

## Quantifying Solids Loading That Drives Deposits and Corrosion

### Total Suspended Solids Analysis

Total Suspended Solids (TSS) measurement follows standardized methods under Standard Methods 2540 (Section 2540D). TSS testing confirms whether blowdown control, basin cleaning, filtration, and dispersant programs actually reduce suspended solids levels. The analysis helps explain operational symptoms like spray nozzle plugging, strainer loading, fill fouling, heat exchanger approach increases, and biological persistence where solids and biofilm provide shelter.

### Particle Size Distribution Analysis

Particle Size Distribution (PSD) analysis follows ISO 13320 guidance for particle size analysis by laser diffraction. When paired with TSS results, PSD explains whether solids consist mainly of fine particles and colloids (harder to capture, more likely to pass strainers and deposit on heat transfer surfaces) or larger particles (more likely to settle and be captured with coarser filtration).

This analysis supports selecting and optimizing side-stream filtration type and micron rating, while diagnosing why filters plug or why deposition continues despite treatment efforts.

## Deposit Analysis for Root Cause Identification

Deposit analysis identifies the deposit type so corrective action targets the right problem. Deposits in cooling systems can be scale, sludge, dirt, corrosion products, or biological matrix. ASTM guidance explicitly notes deposits may be classified this way, with composition determined by chemical and spectrographic methods and specific constituents identified by microscopy or XRD.

Common lab approaches include XRD for crystalline compound identification (CaCO₃, CaSO₄, silica phases), SEM/EDS for morphology plus elemental composition (distinguishing scale from corrosion products from dirt and ash), and XRF, ICP, or wet chemistry for elemental totals and fractions.

Deposit analysis confirms whether the right corrective lever involves adjusting cycles, blowdown, or pH; increasing or altering phosphonate, polymer, or dispersant programs; improving filtration and housekeeping; changing corrosion control strategy; or cleaning mechanically or chemically with the appropriate method.

## Corrosion Analysis and Mechanism Determination

### Corrosion Coupon Testing

Corrosion coupon testing determines corrosion mechanism and verifies inhibitor performance. ASTM G4 covers conducting corrosion coupon tests in field applications under operating conditions, while ASTM G1 covers preparing, cleaning, and evaluating corrosion test specimens, emphasizing mass loss and pitting measurements.

Coupon testing provides corrosion rate trending and visual evidence, distinguishing uniform corrosion from pitting and under-deposit corrosion. The results confirm whether inhibitor residual targets and oxygen control prove adequate, especially in closed loops.

### Corrosion Product Analysis

Corrosion product analysis commonly uses SEM/EDS and XRD to identify corrosion products and support root cause diagnosis. This analysis differentiates likely mechanisms including oxygen ingress-driven corrosion in closed loops, under-deposit corrosion often driven by solids plus poor dispersion or filtration, galvanic effects from mixed metallurgy, and MIC-suspected conditions requiring correlation with deposits, biology, and system conditions.

## Application by System Type

### Open Cooling Towers

Open cooling towers use lab testing more frequently because towers face exposure to air, solids, biological loading, and chemistry swings. The most common program support includes routine Legionella testing over time to validate the water management program, TSS plus PSD analysis to manage deposition drivers and optimize filtration and blowdown, deposit analysis using XRD, SEM, and EDS to confirm what fouls equipment and why, and coupons plus lab evaluation to verify corrosion control and detect pitting risk.

### Closed-Loop Chilled Water Systems

Closed-loop chilled water systems typically use lab testing on an event-driven basis since loops remain stable when truly closed, but lab analysis provides high value when problems occur. After makeup intrusion, drain and refill operations, construction tie-ins, stagnation events, or abnormal differential pressure and solids conditions, common program support includes TSS plus PSD analysis to confirm contamination and guide filtration and cleanup, coupons plus ASTM G1 and G4 evaluation to confirm corrosion protection, and deposit or corrosion product analysis when sludge, magnetite, or pitting appears.

## Strategic Lab Testing Timeline

Baseline and commissioning testing establishes normal ranges, including solids and deposits if issues exist. Routine validation provides periodic confirmation that field trends reflect reality, especially Legionella monitoring in cooling towers. After upsets or when symptoms appear—plugging, rising approach temperatures, visible deposits, ATP spikes, persistent biofouling, unexpected corrosion indicators—lab testing diagnoses root causes. When field results conflict with each other, lab analysis becomes the tie-breaker and creates the defensible record.

## Key Takeaway

Implement routine Legionella testing every 30-90 days for cooling towers, maintain TSS below 25 mg/L through confirmed lab measurement, and conduct deposit analysis via XRD/SEM whenever fouling symptoms appear rather than guessing at chemical adjustments.
