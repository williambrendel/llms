# When should formal lab testing be used in place of field testing?

## Executive Summary

Formal lab testing should replace field testing when you need defensible, comparable, decision-grade results—especially for Legionella detection—and when the consequences of being wrong are high. Field tests work best for rapid trending and day-to-day operational decisions.

## Overview

A cooling tower's ATP reading spikes overnight, but your dip slides show normal counts. Your closed chilled water system returns from a summer shutdown with cloudy makeup water, but handheld readings seem fine. These scenarios highlight a critical decision point: when do you move beyond quick field tests to formal laboratory analysis that can definitively tell you what's happening in your water system?

## When Formal Lab Testing Is Essential

### Validation of Water Management Programs

Establish a baseline and periodically confirm that controls are working, especially in higher-risk buildings and systems. The CDC frames routine testing as a way to check whether a water management program is functioning and provides structured guidance on sampling plans and response actions.

### Regulatory, Contractual, or Third-Party Compliance

When results may be reviewed by regulators, auditors, insurers, or customers, lab methods are typically expected. These situations require documented chain of custody and standardized methods that field testing cannot provide.

### Response to Elevated Risk Conditions or System Upsets

Examples include loss of biocide feed, prolonged stagnation, dirty tower or basin conditions, high ATP trends, major maintenance events, seasonal startup and shutdown, abnormal makeup water events, disinfection events, and drift eliminator failures in towers. CDC's routine testing guidance emphasizes having a plan to respond when Legionella is detected.

### When Field Test Trends Indicate Loss of Control

Rapid rise in ATP, repeated positive or short time-lag BART results, consistently high dipslides, or chemistry drifting out of control limits all signal the need for lab testing to confirm severity and guide corrective actions.

### When Decisions Are High-Stakes

Examples include deciding to take a tower offline, perform hyperhalogenation, notify stakeholders, change treatment programs, or respond after a near-miss event. Lab results provide stronger defensibility for these critical decisions.

### When You Need Organism Specificity

ATP, dip slides, and BART do not identify Legionella specifically. Use lab testing when you need to distinguish between Legionella species versus L. pneumophila, or when you need enumeration using recognized methods.

### When Closed-Loop Concentrations Are Low or Results Are Ambiguous

Closed chilled loops often have low biomass, putting field methods near their detection limits. When field results are "noisy" or inconsistent, lab testing provides the better answer.

### When Results from Different Methods Disagree

Examples include high site ATP but low dipslides, handheld readings that differ from controller instrumentation, or rapid onsite Legionella screens that contradict operational indicators. Lab testing serves as the tie-breaker.

## System-Specific Considerations

### Open Cooling Towers

Cooling towers are more variable due to biofilm, solids, organic loading, and aerosol generation. Lab testing is most justified for:

- Baseline establishment and periodic validation as routine program checks
- Seasonal startup and shutdown or return-to-service after outages
- Any control upset including biocide failure, high ATP, or visible fouling
- After corrective actions to confirm system recovery

### Closed Chilled-Water Systems

True closed loops are typically lower risk and more stable. Lab testing is most justified:

- After drain and refill, major repairs, or contamination and makeup events
- When warm zones or stagnation exist in the system
- When MIC or biofouling is suspected, evidenced by sludge, plugged strainers, or differential pressure increases
- When field results show persistent biological activity despite treatment

## Practical Implementation

Use field tests for daily and weekly operational trending and early warning. Reserve lab tests for quarterly baseline and validation checks, post-upset confirmation, compliance requirements, and high-impact decisions. This approach balances the speed and cost-effectiveness of field methods with the accuracy and defensibility of laboratory analysis.
