# How do Audits Evaluate Treatment Programs in Open Cooling Tower and Closed Chilled Water Systems?

## Executive Summary

Audits evaluate treatment programs by measuring key performance indicators including water quality parameters, chemical dosing effectiveness, and system efficiency metrics to determine if the treatment is preventing corrosion, scaling, and biological growth while optimizing water and energy use.

## Overview

A single poorly managed cooling tower can waste thousands of gallons of water daily while driving up energy costs by 15-30%. Treatment program audits serve as the diagnostic tool that reveals whether your water treatment strategy is actually working or just burning through chemicals and budget. The difference between an effective program and a failing one often comes down to measuring the right parameters at the right frequency.

## Water Quality Parameter Assessment

Audits begin with comprehensive water quality testing across multiple sampling points. Conductivity measurements reveal concentration ratios, indicating whether the system operates within optimal ranges. pH levels must stay within narrow bands specific to each system type - typically 6.5-8.5 for open towers and 8.5-10.5 for closed systems.

Total dissolved solids (TDS) readings provide direct insight into blowdown efficiency. Elevated TDS levels signal inadequate blowdown, leading to scaling and reduced heat transfer. Auditors compare makeup water quality against circulating water to calculate actual concentration ratios versus target ranges.

Microbiological testing identifies bacterial counts, particularly Legionella in open systems. Effective treatment programs maintain heterotrophic plate counts below 10,000 CFU/mL in cooling towers and near-zero levels in closed systems.

## Chemical Treatment Effectiveness

Auditors evaluate chemical dosing accuracy by measuring residual levels of biocides, corrosion inhibitors, and scale inhibitors. Inconsistent residuals indicate dosing equipment problems or inadequate chemical selection.

Corrosion coupon analysis provides objective data on metal loss rates. Properly treated systems show corrosion rates below 2 mils per year for carbon steel and 0.2 mils per year for copper alloys. Higher rates indicate treatment program failure requiring immediate adjustment.

Scale formation assessment involves examining heat exchanger surfaces and measuring deposit thickness. Effective programs prevent visible scale buildup while maintaining heat transfer coefficients within 10% of clean conditions.

## System Performance Metrics

Energy efficiency measurements reveal treatment program impact on operational costs. Auditors track approach temperatures, heat rejection capacity, and pump energy consumption. Scale buildup increases approach temperatures and reduces efficiency, while biological fouling restricts water flow and increases pumping energy.

Water consumption audits compare makeup water usage against theoretical requirements based on evaporation, drift, and blowdown calculations. Excessive makeup water indicates system leaks, over-blowdown, or inadequate treatment allowing higher blowdown rates.

Equipment condition assessments document physical system integrity. Auditors inspect cooling tower fill, distribution systems, and heat exchangers for signs of corrosion, scaling, or biological fouling that indicate treatment program deficiencies.

## Key Takeaways

Conduct comprehensive treatment program audits quarterly, measuring water quality parameters weekly and maintaining corrosion rates below 2 mils per year for optimal system performance. Consider automated monitoring systems with real-time chemical feed control to maintain consistent treatment levels and reduce audit-identified variations in program effectiveness.
