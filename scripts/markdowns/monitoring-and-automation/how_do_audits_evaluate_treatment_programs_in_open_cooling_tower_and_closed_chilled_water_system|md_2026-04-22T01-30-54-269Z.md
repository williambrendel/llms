# How do audits evaluate treatment programs in open cooling tower and closed chilled water systems?

## Executive Summary

Audits evaluate treatment programs by systematically verifying design fit, execution, control, and outcomes across four critical areas: scale risk, corrosion control, microbiological management, and system performance. Both open cooling towers and closed chilled-water loops require connecting what is being done (procedures and chemistry) to what is being achieved (reliability and cost control).

## Overview

A single failed audit can expose millions of dollars in equipment risk, yet most facilities treat water treatment audits as routine paperwork exercises. The difference between a passing and failing audit often comes down to whether makeup water increases by just 10% in closed systems or whether cooling tower corrosion rates exceed 2 mils per year. These seemingly small deviations signal fundamental breakdowns that compound into catastrophic failures.

## What an Audit Delivers

Audits produce four essential outputs that facilities can act on immediately. **Risk findings** identify scale risk, corrosion risk, microbiological risk, and system-specific vulnerabilities like Legionella management gaps in open systems or oxygen ingress in closed systems. **Performance findings** reveal trends indicating fouling or instability before they become visible problems.

The **action plan** provides prioritized corrective actions with designated owners and firm deadlines. Without this accountability structure, audit findings become suggestions that facilities ignore. The **verification plan** establishes specific KPIs and inspection points that will confirm improvement, creating measurable success criteria rather than subjective assessments.

## Open Cooling Tower Audit Focus

### System Inventory and Operating Conditions

Audits first verify that the treatment program matches the actual system rather than outdated specifications. This requires current piping and equipment schematics with accurate volumes, complete metallurgy documentation covering mild steel, copper alloys, stainless steel, and galvanized components, and detailed heat load profiles including operating hours and seasonal variations.

The audit examines makeup water sources and their variability, whether city water, well water, or blended sources, plus the blowdown control method and its reliability. **Pass condition:** the treatment approach appropriately matches the system's metallurgy and operating envelope.

### Control of Concentration and Key Chemistry

Chemistry control verification focuses on whether concentration control and scale/corrosion inhibition actually function as designed. Auditors examine target versus actual **cycles of concentration**, tracking methods using chloride or conductivity measurements, and control stability over time.

Critical parameters include pH, alkalinity, calcium hardness, and silica levels maintained within range, plus inhibitor residual control for phosphate, molybdate, polymer, or azole programs as applicable. Controller calibration, sensor condition, setpoints, and alarm functionality receive detailed inspection, along with feed equipment verification covering pumps, injection points, and backpressure valves.

**Pass condition:** chemistry stays consistently within defined control limits, excursions occur rarely, and all alarms and corrective actions carry complete documentation.

### Deposit and Fouling Evidence

Physical evidence of cleanliness requires both performance trending and direct inspection. Trend analysis covers condenser approach temperatures, tower approach temperatures, kW/ton efficiency where measurable, strainer differential pressure, and heat exchanger differential pressure patterns.

Physical inspections examine basin sludge accumulation, fill condition, distribution deck cleanliness, spray nozzle function, and drift eliminator condition. Side-stream filtration systems require flow verification, differential pressure trending, and filter maintenance record review. Auditors document scaling evidence, under-deposit corrosion, and biofilm formation.

**Pass condition:** performance trends remain stable and inspections demonstrate low deposit accumulation.

### Corrosion Control Verification

Corrosion measurement and management require systematic verification of monitoring placement, handling procedures, and response protocols. Corrosion coupon and probe placement must provide suitable flow conditions and representative system locations. Coupon handling procedures and exposure time discipline prevent measurement errors that mask real corrosion rates.

Results require trending by metallurgy type with correlation analysis during chemistry excursions. Visual inspections check for rust staining, tuberculation, and pitting on accessible surfaces throughout the system.

**Pass condition:** corrosion rates meet internal targets and remain stable; when rates worsen, facilities identify and correct root causes promptly.

### Microbiological Control and Risk Management

Microbiological control auditing examines both measurable outcomes and process discipline with particular attention to public health risks. The biocide program review covers chemical types, feed frequency, and verification methods including pump output confirmation, timer function, and flow proof systems.

Microbiological monitoring requires consistent methodology, appropriate frequency, and trend analysis using heterotrophic plate counts or dipslide methods. Response protocols must specify exact actions when counts spike, with complete documentation of interventions and outcomes.

Cleaning and disinfection schedules require completion evidence rather than just planned schedules. Tower risk management elements include drift eliminator condition, splash containment measures, and safe access procedures for maintenance personnel.

**Pass condition:** monitoring occurs on schedule, responses follow consistent protocols, and no uncontrolled microbiological events or chronic high count conditions exist.

### Documentation and Accountability

Service reports must demonstrate completeness and consistency in readings, adjustments, and corrective actions. Chemical usage reconciliation compares actual consumption against expected usage based on system parameters. Corrective actions require closure verification rather than repeated monthly notation of the same problems.

**Pass condition:** repeatable processes with traceable records demonstrate vendor and standard operating procedure compliance.

## Closed Chilled-Water Audit Focus

### System Tightness and Makeup Control

Closed system audits prioritize makeup control since these systems should require minimal water addition. Makeup meters must be present, functional, and regularly monitored with trend analysis of any consumption spikes and thorough investigation of causes.

Leak logs require documentation with repair closure rates that demonstrate proactive maintenance. Expansion tank condition, air separator operation, and venting practices directly affect oxygen ingress and corrosion acceleration.

**Pass condition:** makeup water consumption remains consistently low and oxygen ingress stays controlled through proper system maintenance.

### Chemistry Control and Contamination Checks

Stable chemistry control requires verification that inhibitor strategies function as designed. pH control and buffering capacity must remain within specification, while inhibitor residuals for nitrite, molybdate, azole, or program-specific chemicals require consistent measurement and maintenance.

Conductivity and key ion trending, particularly chloride and sulfate levels, reveals contamination sources before they cause system damage. Glycol concentration monitoring, where present, includes inhibitor compatibility verification. Sampling points must represent actual system conditions rather than dead leg locations.

**Pass condition:** tight chemistry control with minimal parameter swings and no evidence of chronic contamination.

### Corrosion and Metals Transport

Low corrosion rates require appropriate monitoring programs with proper coupon and probe placement. Trend analysis by metallurgy type reveals developing problems before they become visible. Iron and copper level monitoring, combined with turbidity and particle load trending, indicates corrosion product transport throughout the system.

Evidence of magnetite or sludge accumulation, particularly in low-flow zones, signals corrosion control breakdown and requires immediate attention.

**Pass condition:** low corrosion rates with minimal metals transport and controlled solids levels throughout the system.

### Cleanliness and Hydronic Performance Indicators

Closed loop fouling differs from open systems, typically involving corrosion products, biofilm formation in some cases, and construction debris. Strainer and side-stream filter differential pressure monitoring with maintenance record review reveals fouling patterns.

Coil and heat exchanger performance complaints tied to deposit formation manifest as temperature differential problems or low flow from plugging. Pump energy changes and valve or coil control symptoms serve as screening indicators for developing fouling problems.

**Pass condition:** minimal plugging events with stable temperature differential and flow performance consistent with original design specifications.

### Program Governance

Standard operating procedures must cover makeup water addition, glycol addition, and chemical addition processes. Start-up and layup procedures for seasonal equipment or idle systems prevent contamination during transitions.

Work control procedures specify how pipe repairs, system tie-ins, and flushing operations are managed to prevent contamination. Recordkeeping and corrective action processes ensure controlled changes and disciplined operations.

**Pass condition:** controlled change management and disciplined operations prevent repeat contamination events.

## Key Takeaway

Implement monthly makeup water tracking for closed systems and weekly microbiological monitoring for cooling towers, with immediate investigation protocols when makeup exceeds 0.5% of system volume monthly or microbial counts exceed 10,000 CFU/mL. Consider automated monitoring systems with alarm capabilities for pH, conductivity, and inhibitor levels to catch excursions within 24 hours rather than waiting for weekly manual testing.
